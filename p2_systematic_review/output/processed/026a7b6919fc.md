## Abstract summary
The paper presents a framework for modeling options on hypothetical quantum money by combining classical optimisation and path-integral methods to capture quantum features using only classical (super)computers. It applies Adaptive Simulated Annealing (ASA) to fit parameters of the Statistical Mechanics of Financial Markets (SMFM) model and uses PATHINT/qPATHINT to propagate short-time (quantum) path-integral probability distributions, demonstrating feasibility and discussing performance and scaling considerations for hybrid classical-quantum computations in finance.
## Methodology
The paper develops and applies a hybrid classical-quantum computational methodology implemented entirely on classical high-performance computers. Parameter fitting is performed using Adaptive Simulated Annealing (ASA) to fit short-time conditional probability distributions of a Statistical Mechanics of Financial Markets (SMFM) model. Time-sequential evolution of the distributions (and quantum generalizations) is computed with histogram-kernel path-integral algorithms: PATHINT for classical systems and qPATHINT (variables/functions treated as complex) for quantum-money analogues. The approach uses Lagrangian/path-integral representations equivalent to Fokker–Planck and Langevin formulations, with midpoint (Stratonovich/Feynman) discretization and Riemannian-curvature corrections when diffusions are non-constant. Numerical propagation is done by constructing banded kernel matrices from short-time Gaussian kernels; meshes are chosen using Δq ≈ (Δt g^{ii})^{1/2} and tiled diagonal meshes for multivariate factors. For options pricing the model uses a two-factor SDE system for underlying price S and stochastic volatility σ with a piecewise multiplicative noise function F(S,S',S*,x,y) (including forms that recover Black–Scholes in limits). American-option style epoch resets (wavefunction decoherence) are modeled by resetting time at the start of each epoch. Performance and scaling are benchmarked across dimensions using cloned 1-D systems; implementation details include C-code bases, gcc -O3 compilation, and runs on XSEDE supercomputers (Expanse).

**Algorithms used:** Adaptive Simulated Annealing (ASA), PATHINT (path-integral histogram kernel propagation), qPATHINT (quantum generalization of PATHINT), PATHTREE / qPATHTREE, Histogram kernel evaluation of short-time propagator

**Experimental setup:** Classical HPC environment (XSEDE Expanse) running custom C codes: ASA (global optimization C-code) and PATHINT/qPATHINT implementations. Code compiled with gcc -O3; example timing: 1-D run with dt=0.0002, 10 foldings executes in ~0.002s. qPATHINT modifications involved converting variables/functions to complex (~7500 lines changed).

**Results summary:** The paper describes a modeling and computational approach that uses classical implementations of path-integral algorithms (PATHINT/qPATHINT) together with Adaptive Simulated Annealing (ASA) to fit and propagate short-time conditional probability distributions for financial options models (SMFM) and related systems. The author reports having generalized PATHINT to N dimensions and to complex-valued quantum versions (qPATHINT), applied these to prior studies (neocortical interactions and options), and demonstrates that kernel-based path-integral propagation can include serial shocks that are less convenient for Monte Carlo methods. Performance benchmarks show rapid growth in kernel size and computation with increasing dimensionality (e.g., 2-D ≈ 200× 1-D), and a small 1-D SMNI benchmark run-time is reported on XSEDE hardware. The work is presented as a classical emulation of hybrid classical-quantum workflows aimed at preparing for potential future quantum-money markets; claims about the arrival and market adoption of quantum money and consequent option markets remain speculative.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The paper does not demonstrate any quantum computational advantage; instead it shows how quantum-like models (qPATHINT) can be emulated and run on classical (super-)computers and discusses future relevance if quantum money/markets arise. Performance and scaling challenges for qPATHINT are highlighted, and no empirical claim of quantum speedup or advantage is presented.
## Limitations
- Quantum money (QM) implementation and exchange with classical money is not addressed; there is no clear proposal for how QM would be implemented or exchanged with classical money (author-stated).
- Quantum computing remains infeasible for many systems even when combined with classical optimizers (author-stated/cited).
- Numerical imaginary-time (Wick) rotations can leave no audit trail back to imaginary time in multiple foldings of the path integral, limiting interpretability of numerical quantum solutions (author-stated).
- Monte Carlo / importance-sampling methods are not convenient for time-sequential serial random shocks, necessitating kernel-based approaches (author-stated).
- Severe computational scaling: kernel (qPATHINT/PATHINT) sizes grow very rapidly with dimensionality (author-stated), e.g., 2-D is ~200x 1-D and higher D explode exponentially.
- Multivariate diffusions cause mesh-coupling problems (the mesh of S depends on sigma) and complicate PATHINT grids; available transformations produce cumbersome boundary conditions (author-stated).
- Oscillatory quantum wavefunctions require much larger off-diagonal spreads in the kernel, increasing computational cost (author-stated).
- Treatment of American options assumes epochal decoherence/collapse at each early-exercise measurement which is a modeling assumption that may not capture all quantum measurement dynamics (author-stated/inferred).
- [inferred] The project uses classical (super-)computers to emulate hybrid classical-quantum systems; results may not capture true quantum-hardware-specific phenomena such as entanglement, hardware noise, or real quantum speedups.
- [inferred] High sensitivity of some Greeks (higher-order derivatives) to shocks indicates potential numerical instability or model fragility for risk measures.
- [inferred] Large number of ASA options and quench parameters implies substantial tuning effort and potential difficulty in robust, automated model fitting across market regimes.
- [inferred] Approximations such as tiling diagonal meshes for multivariate problems are ad hoc and may reduce accuracy in strongly coupled regions.
## Open questions
- How will quantum money actually be implemented, standardized, and exchanged with existing classical money?
- How will financial markets value quantum money and integrate quantum instruments into existing market structures?
- Will classical-only simulations (qPATHINT running on classical supercomputers) adequately reflect behavior of real quantum financial systems or quantum hardware?
- What is the practical threshold for quantum advantage in derivative pricing and related financial computations (cited external work raises this question)?
- How can the exponential computational scaling of kernel-based path-integral methods be mitigated for realistic multi-factor (D>2) financial models?
- What numerical or methodological approaches can better handle mesh coupling in multivariate multiplicative-diffusion systems without introducing unwieldy boundary conditions?
- How should decoherence and quantum measurement (e.g., American option early exercise) be modeled most realistically in quantum-option pricing?
- How to reconcile imaginary-time numerical artifacts (loss of audit trail) with requirements for reliable quantum path-integral pricing?
- What are the implications of serial random shocks in quantum-option wavefunctions for hedging and Greeks, and how robust are pricing/hedging strategies under such shocks?
- How generalizable are the presented qPATHINT kernel methods to a broader class of quantum financial instruments and market microstructures?
