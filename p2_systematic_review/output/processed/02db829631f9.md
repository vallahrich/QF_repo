## Abstract summary
This conference paper investigates the application of the hybrid quantum-classical Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) to portfolio optimization, formulating risk-return tradeoffs as a Hamiltonian and implementing VQE via Qiskit. On a 20-asset test case VQE produced slightly lower variance, higher expected return and Sharpe ratio than classical Mean-Variance Optimization and Black-Litterman benchmarks, while requiring longer convergence time on current noisy hardware; the paper also discusses scalability, noise, and future prospects for quantum finance.
## Methodology
The study applies a hybrid quantum-classical approach to portfolio optimization centered on the Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE). The methodology comprises data collection and preparation (historical daily closing prices from public finance sources, calculation of returns, expected returns vector and covariance matrix, and inclusion of practical constraints such as transaction costs and liquidity), classical benchmarking (solving the mean-variance optimization and Black-Litterman models as baselines), formulation of the portfolio objective as a Hamiltonian combining portfolio variance and expected return with tunable hyperparameters (α and β), implementation of VQE circuits using Qiskit on simulators and subsequently on IBM Quantum hardware, and a hybrid optimization loop where a classical optimizer iteratively updates quantum circuit parameters based on measurement outcomes until convergence. Performance evaluation includes comparison of optimized portfolios by variance, expected return, Sharpe ratio and computational/convergence time, plus sensitivity/robustness tests under varied returns/volatility and noisy conditions. The experiments start with small portfolios (e.g., 20 assets) on QASM simulators and then consider running on real NISQ devices, with discussion of scalability and future extensions (larger asset sets, QAOA and ML integrations).

### Process
1) Construct classical baseline portfolios via MVO and Black-Litterman. 2) Map portfolio objective to a Hamiltonian H(w)=α w^T Σ w - β w^T r. 3) Encode portfolio weights/decision variables into a parameterized quantum ansatz (VQE circuit) using Qiskit. 4) Run quantum circuit on QASM simulator to obtain measurements (probability distributions). 5) Use a classical optimizer to update ansatz parameters based on measured expectation values; iterate until convergence. 6) Evaluate resulting portfolio performance (variance, expected return, Sharpe ratio) and convergence time; perform sensitivity/noise robustness tests and compare to classical baselines. Iterations continue until optimizer convergence; experiments start small (20 assets) and plan to scale.
