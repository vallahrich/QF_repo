## Abstract summary
This paper investigates hybrid quantum-classical approaches—principally QAOA and VQE—applied to large-scale combinatorial and convex optimization problems, presenting a layered architecture suitable for NISQ devices. Empirical results on benchmark problems indicate improved solution quality and computational efficiency (notably for problem sizes >50 variables), while noting persistent challenges from hardware noise, limited qubit counts, and the need for error mitigation and scalable encodings.
## Methodology
The authors propose a hybrid quantum-classical optimization framework targeting large-scale combinatorial and convex problems. The pipeline comprises four main stages: (1) Problem encoding — mapping optimization objectives and constraints into a cost Hamiltonian (e.g., HC = sum_{<i,j> in E} w_{ij} (1 - Z_i Z_j)/2) with variables represented as qubits; (2) Quantum circuit construction — building parameterized variational circuits using VQE or QAOA style unitaries U(γ⃗, β⃗) = ∏_{k=1}^p e^{-i β_k H_M} e^{-i γ_k H_C} where H_M is typically a Pauli-X mixer and γ⃗, β⃗ are tunable; (3) Hybrid optimization loop — execute the circuit on a quantum resource (simulator or NISQ device), measure to estimate the expectation value <H_C>, and update quantum parameters with a classical optimizer (examples cited: Nelder–Mead, COBYLA, gradient descent) until convergence; and (4) Solution extraction — repeated measurements produce bitstrings that are post-processed to decode candidate solutions (e.g., graph cuts, binary assignments). The architecture is presented as a five-layer modular stack (Input, Encoding, Quantum Processing, Classical Optimization, Output) designed to run on current NISQ hardware and to scale via decomposition of large problems into quantum-compatible subproblems. Empirical evaluation is reported on benchmark combinatorial (Max-Cut, TSP) and convex optimization tasks using virtual and physical NISQ devices; metrics reported include expectation values, approximation ratios, convergence behavior, qubit-scaling effects, and execution time comparisons against classical baselines such as simulated annealing and gradient-based methods.

**Algorithms used:** QAOA, VQE

**Experimental setup:** The paper reports experiments carried out on both virtual simulators and physical NISQ devices (unnamed). Benchmark combinatorial problems (Max-Cut, Travelling Salesman) and convex optimization tasks were used. The hybrid loop ran parameterized circuits (QAOA/VQE) with classical optimizers; convergence was typically reached by iteration 30–40. Studies varied qubit count up to ~100 to observe noise-induced degradation beyond that scale.
