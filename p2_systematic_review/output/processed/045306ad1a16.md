## Abstract summary
The paper proposes a Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR)-based Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) framework to solve generalized assignment problems for user association in handoff-aware vehicular networks. The hybrid quantum-classical method integrates a CVaR-tailored cost function to improve convergence and robustness on NISQ devices, and demonstrates a reported 23.5% improvement over a DNN benchmark in simulations.
## Methodology
The authors address a vehicular-network user-association optimization (a generalized assignment problem, GAP) by formulating it as a QUBO and solving it with a hybrid variational quantum-classical approach. They convert the GAP (maximize weighted sum-rate with per-BS capacity and single-association constraints) into a QUBO objective by adding quadratic penalty terms for the constraints (weights λ1 and λ2) and map the QUBO to an Ising Hamiltonian. Input features (flattened SINR matrices) are encoded into S-qubit states via a feature map U_x. A parametrized variational quantum circuit (ansatz) with layers of Y-rotations and controlled-Z entangling gates (circuit depth p) prepares |Ψ(θ)⟩ = G_θ U_x |0⟩. Measurements in the Pauli-Z basis produce bitstrings whose QUBO costs are computed; energies from K samples are used to estimate expectation values. To improve robustness and convergence on NISQ devices, they adopt a CVaR (Conditional Value at Risk) loss: rather than minimizing the mean energy, they sort sampled energies and minimize the average of the lowest α fraction (α ∈ {0.25,0.5,1.0}). Classical optimization updates the circuit parameters to minimize CVaR_α(E(θ)). The trained CVaR-VQE is compared to (i) the optimal solution and (ii) a classical DNN benchmark (PyTorch) with specified architecture and training hyperparameters. Experiments explore sensitivity to number of qubits, number of base stations, circuit depth p, and CVaR α, and report metrics in terms of average data rate (normalized sum rate) with reported improvement of 23.5% over the DNN baseline.

**Algorithms used:** VQE, CVaR-VQE, QUBO mapping, Ising Hamiltonian mapping, Parametrized Quantum Neural Network (QNN), Classical DNN benchmark
**Frameworks:** Qiskit, PyTorch

**Experimental setup:** Simulations implemented in Python: DNN experiments in PyTorch; CVaR-VQE experiments using IBM Qiskit. Synthetic vehicular-network data generated (AVs randomly distributed on a 1000m, 4-lane highway). Network/physical parameters include THz molecular absorption coefficient ka=0.05 m^-1 (1% water vapor, 1.0 THz center), RF frequency 2.1 GHz, path-loss exponent 2.5, antenna gains (THz: 25 dB, RF: 0 dB), transmit power 1 W, noise power -70 dBm. Typical experimental configuration uses 4 AVs and 4 BSs; input feature vector is a 16-dimensional flattened SINR matrix.
## Experiment details
### Input
Synthetic simulation-generated vehicular network data. AV user positions randomized along a 1000 m single-direction 4-lane highway. For reported experiments: 4 AVs and 4 BSs (leading to a 16-dimensional flattened SINR input). Preprocessing: sum-rate normalized by transmission bandwidth; associations encoded as binary variables; SINR matrices used as input to feature map. No external or financial datasets used.

### Process
1) Formulate GAP objective (maximize weighted rates with handover penalty) and constraints; convert to QUBO by adding quadratic penalty terms (λ1, λ2) for equality/inequality constraints. 2) Map QUBO to Ising Hamiltonian via xi -> (1+σz_i)/2. 3) Encode input feature vector x into S-qubit quantum state |ψ_x⟩ = U_x |0⟩. 4) Apply parametrized variational ansatz G_θ consisting of Y-rotation gates and controlled-Z entanglers with depth p (layers). 5) Measure qubits in Pauli-Z basis to obtain K sample bitstrings; compute per-sample QUBO energy E_k. 6) Sort sampled energies and compute CVaR_α as average of lowest αK energies (α in {0.25,0.5,1.0}); use CVaR as loss. 7) Use a classical optimizer to update parameters θ and iterate for multiple epochs (reported up to 100 epochs). 8) Evaluate performance across variations: number of qubits (2–6), number of BSs, circuit depth p (1–3) and CVaR α. 9) Compare against an optimal solution and a classical DNN baseline (5-layer network trained with epochs=100, batch_size=128, lr=1e-4). Shots K and specific classical optimizer for VQE are not specified in the paper.

### Output
Outputs are association decisions (binary assignments) and the resulting performance metric: average data rate / normalized sum-rate. Evaluation plots report average data rate over epochs (convergence), and sensitivity curves over number of qubits, number of BSs, and circuit depth. Baselines: optimal solver and a DNN benchmark. Reported quantitative outcome: CVaR-VQE achieves a 23.5% improvement over the DNN baseline in average reward/data rate.

- [supported] Using CVaR (selecting the lowest α fraction of sampled energies) improved training efficiency and convergence behavior compared to using all samples.
- [supported] Different CVaR confidence levels exhibit different convergence characteristics: α = 1.0 gives fastest initial convergence but later instability; α = 0.5 yields more stable convergence (≈40–50 epochs to near-optimal); α = 0.25 is more conservative and stable.

- [supported] Increasing problem size (number of qubits from 2 to 6, or number of base stations >4) degrades average data rate for all methods; CVaR-VQE maintained closer proximity to the optimal solution than the DNN across tested sizes.

**Results summary:** The paper introduces a hybrid quantum-classical optimization method (CVaR-enhanced VQE) for the vehicle-to-base-station assignment GAP in vehicular networks by mapping the problem to QUBO/Ising form. Simulation experiments using IBM Qiskit compare CVaR-VQE (with α ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1.0}) to a classical DNN baseline and an optimal solver. CVaR-VQE shows better convergence properties for appropriate α settings, achieves closer-to-optimal average data rates across varying qubit counts and base station numbers, and reports a 23.5% improvement over the DNN benchmark. The study finds shallow circuit depth (p = 1) is preferable, while larger problem sizes and deeper circuits reduce performance.

- For α = 1.0, the probability of choosing an initial ground state increased to ~5% on average in the experiments reported.
- CVaR-VQE was tested with α values {0.25, 0.50, 1.00} and demonstrated different trade-offs between speed and stability across these settings.
