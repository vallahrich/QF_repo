## Abstract summary
The paper applies Pauli Correlation Encoding (PCE) to enable variational, gate-based quantum portfolio optimization by encoding multiple binary variables per qubit and using an iterative market-graph bipartitioning strategy. The approach scales to real S&P 500 data instances with over 250 assets, is benchmarked against QAOA (small instances) and classical Estimation of Distribution Algorithms, and demonstrates improved scalability, competitive Sharpe ratios, and practical runtimes (≈1 hour on statevector simulation).
## Methodology
The authors formulate portfolio optimization as a graph partitioning problem over a market graph G where nodes are assets and weighted edges encode pairwise Pearson correlations above a threshold. They apply Pauli Correlation Encoding (PCE) to map multiple binary decision variables into a reduced number of qubits: each optimization variable xi is taken as the sign of the expectation value of a chosen Pauli-string Πi (xi = sgn(<Πi>)). A hardware-efficient variational ansatz (HEA) is prepared with p layers (p = floor(m/n)) on n qubits (n chosen so that the chosen PCE order k can encode m variables). The loss combines a graph cut objective (Cut(G,x) = sum wij xi (1 − xj)) and the PCE variational correlation loss L = sum_{(i,j) in E} wij tanh(α <Πi>) tanh(α <Πj>) + L_reg with α tuned to α = n * floor(k/2). The core procedure is an iterative recursive bipartitioning (Algorithm 1): at each iteration the current subgraph is bipartitioned by (i) preparing the HEA on n qubits, (ii) evaluating Pauli-string expectations (statevector measurement), (iii) computing binary assignments via sign of expectations, and (iv) minimizing the loss with a classical optimizer (COBYLA). The graph is split nsplits times to obtain nsplits+1 clusters; from each cluster the representative asset is chosen as the asset with the highest historical return (computed on the training split). Experiments use real S&P 500 historical price data (5 years) with an 80/20 train/test split. Benchmarks compare the PCE-based variational bipartitioning against (i) QAOA (one-hot encoding) for the smallest instance (m=10, QAOA p=5) and (ii) a classical Estimation of Distribution Algorithm (EDA) for larger instances. Evaluations include portfolio value evolution (a $1000 initial investment), Sharpe ratio on train and test, gate-count comparisons, and per-iteration runtimes (statevector simulation).

**Algorithms used:** Pauli Correlation Encoding (PCE), Hardware-Efficient Ansatz (HEA), Variational optimization (PCE loss), Recursive bipartitioning (graph cut), Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA), Estimation of Distribution Algorithm (EDA), Classical optimizer: COBYLA

**Experimental setup:** Statevector quantum simulator used for expectation-value measurements and runtime experiments. HEA circuits prepared with p = floor(m/n) layers; PCE orders k ∈ {2,3} evaluated; QAOA runs for a small instance with p = 5 layers. Classical optimizer: COBYLA. Train/test split 80/20 on historical S&P 500 data.

**Dataset:** Historical S&P 500 stock data (5 years) from the Kaggle dataset 'camnugent/sandp500'. The dataset columns correspond to individual S&P 500 companies; experiments use the first m columns for each tested m.

## Findings
- [supported] Pauli Correlation Encoding (PCE) encodes multiple optimization variables per qubit, enabling representation of m variables with far fewer qubits than one-hot encodings.
- [supported] Using PCE and a variational ansatz (HEA) the authors implemented a portfolio partitioning pipeline and applied it to market-graph instances up to m = 250 assets.
- [supported] In the authors' experiments (statevector simulation) PCE required far fewer quantum gates than QAOA on dense market graphs (e.g., PCE ≈ 715–730 gates vs QAOA p=2 ≈ 42,646 gates for m=250).
- [supported] In the conducted experiments PCE-based portfolios outperformed the S&P 500 baseline (unoptimized full-asset baseline) and showed higher Sharpe ratios than the baseline in most cases on both training and test splits.
- [supported] PCE produced stable portfolio growth trajectories across tested problem sizes, often outperforming a classical Estimation of Distribution Algorithm (EDA) baseline in these simulations.
- [supported] QAOA (one-hot encoding) is limited in scalability for the dense instances studied; the authors used QAOA only for the smallest case (m=10) and show gate counts and runtime that scale unfavorably.
- [supported] Runtime: on a statevector simulator a single bipartition iteration with PCE could solve instances with m ≥ 200 in about one hour (authors' reported runtime).
- [speculative] The approach 'opens new possibilities for quantum-enhanced financial applications' and could be scaled further (possibly to full S&P 500) by increasing qubit count; this is suggested but not demonstrated on hardware.
- [speculative] The paper asserts that PCE's circuit depth does not grow with the number of problem edges (unlike QAOA), implying better scalability for dense graphs; this is a methodological/theoretical claim supported by their design and simulation but not proven as a general speedup over classical methods.
- [speculative] The authors propose that higher PCE order (k) can improve performance as m increases (citing that cubic order may outperform quadratic for larger m), a claim based on prior work and their parameter choices but not fully explored across all instances here.
- [supported] The pipeline uses recursive graph bipartitioning on a correlation-based market graph, selecting one representative asset per cluster (highest historical return) to form the final portfolio.
- [speculative] Future directions noted (alternative ansatz, improved representative selection, analysis of noise resilience) are proposed avenues and not evaluated in this work.

**Results summary:** The paper introduces and evaluates a Pauli Correlation Encoding (PCE) based variational pipeline for graph-based portfolio optimization. By encoding multiple binary decision variables per qubit and using a hardware-efficient ansatz, the authors demonstrate statevector-simulated experiments on S&P 500-derived market graphs up to m = 250 assets. Empirically, PCE required substantially fewer quantum gates than QAOA on dense graphs, achieved faster runtimes in simulation (reportedly solving m ≥ 200 instances in ≈1 hour per bipartition step), and produced portfolios that outperformed an unoptimized S&P 500 baseline and a classical EDA heuristic in their experiments, including higher Sharpe ratios on train and test splits. The work is simulation-based and does not claim a general or provable quantum advantage on real devices or across all classical baselines.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The authors do not claim a provable or demonstrated quantum advantage. Results are based on statevector simulations showing that PCE reduces qubit/gate requirements compared to QAOA and can produce competitive portfolios versus a classical heuristic in their specific experiments. However, these are simulation results on a particular pipeline; no general advantage over state-of-the-art classical algorithms or experiments on noisy quantum hardware is demonstrated, so any claim of practical quantum advantage remains speculative.
## Limitations
- Experiments limited to problem sizes up to m = 250 assets and n = 9 qubits (author-stated).
- All quantum experiments were performed with a statevector simulator rather than on real noisy quantum hardware (author-stated).
- QAOA benchmarking was only performed on the smallest instance (m = 10) due to qubit/encoding limits, limiting comparative conclusions versus QAOA (author-stated).
- PCE implementation used a Hardware-Efficient Ansatz (HEA); alternative ansätze were not explored (author-stated).
- The partitioning and representative selection procedure is based on correlation structure and then picks the single asset with highest historical return per cluster — other selection criteria were not used (author-stated).
- Gate-count for QAOA scales rapidly with graph size and depth, making QAOA impractical for large dense instances tested (author-stated / evidenced by gate-count table).

## Open questions
- How does PCE perform on real quantum hardware under realistic noise, limited shots, and connectivity constraints?
- What is the optimal choice of PCE order k and the scaling rule for number of qubits n as m grows for different problem instances?
- How sensitive are results to hyperparameters such as the correlation threshold λ, α in the loss, regularization Lreg, ansatz depth p, and classical optimizer settings (e.g., COBYLA)?
- Can alternative ansätze or problem-inspired circuits improve solution quality or noise resilience compared to the HEA used here?
- How robust is the bipartition + representative selection pipeline across different markets, time periods, and asset universes (generalizability)?
- How to incorporate realistic portfolio constraints (cardinality, min/max positions, transaction costs, turnover) into the PCE-based pipeline effectively?
- What are better criteria or algorithms for selecting representatives from each cluster that balance return and risk rather than using max historical return?
- How does PCE compare against strong classical optimization and graph-partitioning algorithms on dense market graphs in terms of solution quality and runtime?
- What is the impact of using shot-based measurements (versus statevector) on the binary mapping xi = sgn(<Πi>) and on overall optimization performance?
- How does the PCE variational landscape behave (e.g., presence of barren plateaus, local minima) as m increases and more variables are mapped per qubit?
- Can the method be scaled to the full S&P 500 (m ≈ 500) in practice on near-term or future quantum hardware, and what are the resource trade-offs?
- How does partitioning strategy (balanced splits vs incremental node separation) influence final portfolio stability and performance, and can it be controlled or optimized?

**Future work:**
- Explore alternative quantum circuit architectures (ansätze) tailored to this problem instead of the Hardware-Efficient Ansatz.
- Investigate enhanced methods for selecting representative assets from each correlated group to improve portfolio construction (beyond max historical return).
- Analyze quantum noise resilience of the PCE approach (evaluate performance on noisy devices and with realistic measurement schemes).
- Potentially scale the approach to the full S&P 500 or larger asset sets, accounting for trade-offs in qubit count n.
- Generalize measurement strategies from statevector to shot-based measurements and real devices as suggested in PCE literature.
