## Abstract summary
The paper presents an end-to-end quantum algorithm that solves the Kolmogorov forward (Fokker–Planck) PDE for local-volatility models by mapping the non-unitary dynamics to a Hamiltonian simulation problem using the Schrödingerisation technique. It details state preparation, Hamiltonian simulation (with a clock dimension for time dependence), and option-price recovery via a swap test, reporting a polynomial advantage in spatial grid size for single-option instances and an exponential speedup in dimension for high-dimensional (basket) options.
## Methodology
The paper develops an end-to-end quantum algorithmic framework to price European vanilla options under local-volatility (LV) models by solving the Kolmogorov forward (Fokker–Planck) PDE on a quantum computer. The methodology: (1) amplitude-encode the spatial probability distribution p(x,0) (initial delta at spot S0 approximated by a sharply peaked Gaussian or computational basis state) into an n-qubit main register; (2) rewrite the forward PDE as a non-Hermitian pseudo-Hamiltonian evolution ∂τ|p⟩ = −i H_LV(τ)|p⟩ and apply the Schrödingerisation technique to embed the non-unitary dynamics into unitary Schrödinger dynamics by introducing an auxiliary 'w' qumode (nw qubits) with a smooth initial wavefunction ψ(pw) (choices like ζ(pw)e−pw, mollifier/erf constructions for optimal precision scaling); (3) remove explicit time dependence of the resulting Hamiltonian by adding a clock (y) qumode (ny qubits) and promoting time to a position operator ˆx_y, yielding a time-independent Hamiltonian H acting on Hp⊗Hw⊗Hy; (4) discretize continuous operators (ˆx, ˆp, ˆp^2 etc.) on uniform grids with periodic boundary conditions and finite-difference stencils (second-order central differences, alternatives up to fourth-order discussed), build efficient block-encodings of the sparse Hamiltonian terms (LCU used to combine separable low-rank representations of σ(s,t)), and simulate e^{-iHt} using optimal sparse Hamiltonian simulation approaches (quantum-walk / QSP-style algorithms with the stated query/gate complexity bounds); (5) after unitary evolution, recover the target amplitude-encoded solution |p(T)⟩ by post-selecting positive momentum eigenstates of the Schrödingerisation register and optional amplitude amplification to improve success probability; (6) prepare the payoff function state |C0⟩ via an efficient piecewise-polynomial state-preparation method (theorem provided) and compute the option price as an expected value using swap tests between |p(T)⟩ and |C0⟩ (and a swap test against the uniform |+⟩^⊗n state to obtain normalization Np), deriving closed-form expressions for call/put prices in terms of measured overlaps and known scaling factors; (7) provide resource/error analyses: initial-state preparation precision (ϵ_prep), Schrödingerisation error (ϵ_Schr), Hamiltonian simulation tolerance (ϵ_evol), shot noise in swap tests (ϵ_V0), bounds on ∥H∥_max (dominated by discrete momentum term ∼2^{2n}) and sparsity s, and complexity scaling (polynomial speedup in single-dimension grid size vs classical PDE solvers and exponential advantage in the number of underlying assets/dimensions under the amplitude-encoding + swap-test retrieval). Key assumptions and implementation choices are documented: LV σ(s,t) approximated as a low-rank separable sum of 1D factors (R small) or polynomial in (s,t), periodic boundary conditions on a sufficiently large domain, amplitude encoding of p(x) (not Qsample), and use of block-encoding primitives to avoid unspecified black-box Hamiltonian oracles.

**Algorithms used:** Schrödingerisation (warped phase transform), Clock-dimension conversion for time-dependent Hamiltonians, Amplitude encoding, Block-encoding, Sparse Hamiltonian simulation (quantum-walk / QSP-style optimal simulation), Linear Combination of Unitaries (LCU), Quantum Fourier Transform (for basis switches), Post-selection on momentum (measurement), Amplitude amplification (optional), Piecewise-polynomial quantum state preparation, Swap test (overlap estimation)

**Results summary:** This preprint proposes a theoretical, end‑to‑end quantum algorithm for pricing European vanilla options under local‑volatility models by solving the Kolmogorov forward PDE. The approach maps the non‑unitary forward PDE dynamics to unitary Hamiltonian evolution via Schrödingerisation, augments the Hilbert space with auxiliary and clock registers to remove non‑unitarity and time dependence, and uses block‑encoding Hamiltonian simulation plus postselection to obtain a quantum state encoding the asset price distribution at maturity. Payoffs are evaluated via SWAP tests on amplitude‑encoded states. The authors derive detailed complexity bounds for state preparation, Hamiltonian simulation, and information retrieval, argue a polynomial quantum speedup in single‑asset grid size (roughly O(N^2 polylog N) vs classical O(N^3)), and claim exponential benefits with respect to dimension for multi‑asset/basket pricing under separability/low‑rank assumptions, while also acknowledging practical limitations arising from the large norm of discrete momentum operators.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** theoretical

The authors present theoretical complexity analyses showing a polynomial speedup in the number of spatial grid points for single‑asset discretizations and argue that the quantum algorithm mitigates the curse of dimensionality (claiming exponential advantage in dimension under separability/low‑rank assumptions). These are analytical/theoretical claims without empirical/experimental demonstrations; the paper also highlights limiting factors (large momentum operator norm) that can reduce practical advantage, so the advantage remains a theoretically derived promise rather than an experimentally validated result.
## Limitations
- Quantum advantage for a single option instance is at best polynomial in the number of spatial grid points; the method does not claim exponential speedup for single-option pricing.
- The discretized momentum operator induces a large operator norm (∥P^2∥_max ∼ 2^{2n}), which dominates the Hamiltonian norm and severely restricts achievable speedups—this limits the practical quantum advantage unless mitigations (energy cutoffs / alternative representations) are applied.
- The local-volatility surface σ(s, t) must be represented in a small-rank separable form for implementation (σ_lr(s,t)=∑_{m=1}^R α_m r_m(s) q_m(t)); a generic bivariate polynomial transform cannot be implemented directly with current QSVT/PET techniques.
- The Schrödingerisation embedding and the time-removal via a clock dimension enlarge the Hilbert space and add ancilla registers, increasing resource requirements.
- Post-selection success probability for retrieving |p(T)⟩ depends on the norm ratio ∥p(0)∥^2/∥p(T)∥^2 (and the auxiliary-profile L_+), which can be large and increase cost; amplitude amplification is required to improve this factor.
- The theoretical query complexity bounds (e.g., Theorem III.2) rely on abstract oracle models; constructing explicit oracles / matrix-element circuits can be resource-intensive and the paper acknowledges that practical gate-level constructions are nontrivial.
- Information retrieval (swap test) for the expected payoff requires O(1/ϵ^2) shots to reach additive accuracy ϵ (sampling/shot-noise limitation); this multiplicative sampling cost can be significant.
- Initial conditions that are Dirac deltas must be approximated (e.g., by sharply peaked Gaussians or basis states), introducing approximation errors and practical preparation costs.
- The block-encoding and Hamiltonian simulation complexity is multiplicatively sensitive to the grid resolution (N) via ∥H∥_max, limiting asymptotic improvements: although gate complexity is improved relative to classical O(N^3), it still scales as poly(N) (e.g., O(N^2 polylog N)), not exponentially better in N.
- [inferred] The use of periodic boundary conditions to obtain simple anti-Hermitian derivative operators may not be appropriate for all option domains and could introduce modeling artifacts or limitations in applicability.
- [inferred] Success of the approach in practice depends on several constant and prefactor values (σ_max, domain bounds a,b, choice of ancilla profiles ψ), so achieving theoretical scalings in realistic market parameter regimes may be challenging.
- [inferred] The claimed exponential advantage with respect to problem dimension hinges on favorable assumptions (e.g., efficient separable encoding of payoffs and σ); these assumptions may not hold for arbitrary multi-asset payoffs or volatility surfaces.
## Open questions
- What effective regimes (parameter ranges, energy cutoffs, momentum cutoffs) allow the restrictive ∥P^2∥-driven scaling to be relaxed so that a practical quantum advantage is realized?
- How to construct explicit, resource-efficient circuit-level oracles / block-encodings for complex Hamiltonian coefficients (including σ(x,t) and its derivatives) with realistic gate counts and ancilla usage?
- Can one implement non-separable or high-rank bivariate local-volatility surfaces σ(s,t) directly (or with small overhead) without resorting to a small-rank separable approximation?
- What are the precise trade-offs (in gates, qubits, and shots) between discretization error (∆x), Schrödingerisation error (ϵ_Schr), simulation tolerance (ϵ_evol), and sampling noise (shots) in realistic option-pricing scenarios?
- How large is the post-selection overhead (or how effective is amplitude amplification) in practice for typical market distributions p(T), and what are the concrete resource costs to reach acceptable success probabilities?
- How does the method perform in practice (gate counts, wall-clock time, noise resilience) on near-term or early fault-tolerant quantum hardware for moderate grid sizes and low-to-moderate dimensions?
- What are the optimal choices for the auxiliary-profile ψ(p_w) and for the clock-register encoding to minimize resources while preserving accuracy?
- What is the precise scaling and comparison (both asymptotic and prefactor-level) between classical multi-asset PDE solvers and the proposed quantum approach for high-dimensional basket options—i.e., under which dimensionality and accuracy regimes does the quantum method outperform classical counterparts?
- How to extend the forward-PDE Schrödingerisation approach to non-deterministic (stochastic) volatility models or to models with jumps, while keeping the simulation efficient?
- How robust is the approach to payoff discontinuities (kinks at strike) or PDE stiffness, and how should resources (grid resolution, timestep, ansatz) be adapted accordingly?
