## Abstract summary
This review examines the application of quantum computing to portfolio optimization, focusing on encoding the problem as a QUBO and solving it with quantum annealing and QAOA. It discusses potential computational speedups and practical limitations—such as limited qubit counts, noise/decoherence, I/O bottlenecks, and integration challenges—while calling for further research and hybrid quantum-classical development for finance.
## Methodology
This paper is a conceptual review and methodology/proposal paper rather than an empirical study. The authors perform a literature-based review of portfolio optimization and quantum computing methods, introduce quantum computing fundamentals (qubits, gates, basic algorithms), and synthesize prior work to derive a high-level quantum solution pipeline for portfolio optimization. Methodologically they (1) reformulate the Markowitz mean-variance portfolio optimization as a Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) problem, (2) describe how to construct the corresponding cost Hamiltonian (H_C) from the QUBO matrix Q and a mixing Hamiltonian (H_B) using Pauli operators, (3) propose solution preparation either by quantum annealing / adiabatic evolution or by a QAOA-style parameterized alternating operator ansatz, (4) specify an initial equal-superposition state, an annealing schedule A(t), B(t) for adiabatic evolution or sequences of U_C(γ) and U_B(β) for QAOA, and (5) obtain candidate solutions by measuring the final state in the computational basis and selecting the best feasible allocation satisfying target-return and budget constraints. The paper includes high-level pseudo-code (Python/IBM Qiskit style) to illustrate the pipeline, presents theoretical computational-complexity and speedup discussion, and surveys practical limitations (noise, decoherence, scalability, I/O bottlenecks, integration and talent gaps). No empirical experiments, datasets, or numerical benchmarking are reported — the contribution is a methodological synthesis and algorithmic proposal with illustrative pseudo-code.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum Annealing (adiabatic quantum computation), Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA), QUBO formulation (Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization)
**Frameworks:** Qiskit, Cirq, QuTiP
