## Abstract summary
This preprint introduces a QSVT-based method to construct the state-preparation operator for Quantum Amplitude Estimation applied to credit risk (VaR/CVaR) under a multivariate Gaussian conditional-independence model. The authors propose an amplitude-loading strategy that avoids expensive in-circuit arithmetic, achieves linear gate-scaling in the number of counterparties for fixed polynomial degree, and reuses a single polynomial approximation to reduce preprocessing. They provide an end-to-end implementation and simulation results demonstrating accurate CDF and VaR estimation and reduced circuit depth compared to earlier approaches.
## Methodology
The authors propose and implement an end-to-end quantum workflow for Value at Risk (VaR) estimation that replaces costly in-circuit arithmetic with an amplitude-loading strategy combined with Quantum Singular Value Transformation (QSVT). The uncertainty model U (encoding multivariate Gaussian conditional-independence scenarios on a counterparties register C and an auxiliary Gaussian register Z) is concatenated with an amplitude-loading operator A that maps a (additive) function θ of each counterparty loss li into the rotation angle of a target qubit T via controlled Ry rotations. The resulting amplitude sin(θ0 + θ(Lj)) for scenario j is then filtered by applying a QSVT polynomial approximation P (even, |P|≤1) of a rescaled threshold function kTµ to the operator ˜Π A Π (with Π,˜Π projectors on target-qubit basis states). The composed operator QU encodes (up to scale k^2) the cumulative distribution function (CDF) for a chosen loss threshold as the probability of measuring particular states on registers T and B. Phases and polynomial coefficients for QSVT are computed with QSPPACK (MATLAB). Iterative Quantum Amplitude Estimation (IQAE) is then applied to the constructed state-preparation circuit to estimate the filtered CDF value; an outer bisection search (using the same precomputed polynomial when possible) is used to find VaR. The methodology achieves logical depth scaling O(d·n) (d = polynomial degree, n = number of counterparties) because A is implemented as n controlled rotations and QSVT requires d alternating blocks. The paper also outlines variants to extract expected loss and CVaR using small-angle linearization and other polynomial choices.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum Singular Value Transformation (QSVT), Quantum Signal Processing (QSP), Iterative Quantum Amplitude Estimation (IQAE), Amplitude loading via controlled Ry rotations
**Frameworks:** Python (authors' implementation), QSPPACK (MATLAB)

**Experimental setup:** Simulated end-to-end experiments. Polynomial coefficients and QSVT phases were computed using QSPPACK in MATLAB; the quantum circuits and IQAE orchestration were implemented in Python. Key operational parameters used in simulations: polynomial degree d = 1000 for approximating the threshold function, number of shots = 2048, iterative amplitude estimation (IQAE) used for amplitude estimation, counterparties n = 4, 2 qubits per Gaussian factor, Gaussian truncation ±2.

**Dataset:** Anonymized and perturbed real bank dataset (not synthetic). The experiments used a small, representative subset consisting of 4 counterparties/assets with associated intrinsic probabilities of default (PDs), sensitivities to risk factors (ρ), loss-given-default (LGD) values, and a correlation-with-risk-factors matrix. Values were explicitly reported in the paper (see Table I).

## Findings
- [speculative] A QSVT-based amplitude-loading approach is proposed that, for a fixed polynomial degree d, reduces the logical depth of the VaR state-preparation circuit to scale linearly with the number of counterparties (O(d · n)), avoiding the exponential-controlled-rotation construction used in prior amplitude-encoding proposals.
- [supported] An end-to-end implementation (code released) and numerical simulations with a 4-asset test instance demonstrate the proposed QSVT-based pipeline can reproduce cumulative distribution function (CDF) values that closely match classical benchmark CDFs (Table II).
- [supported] The authors successfully computed QSVT phases and a polynomial threshold approximation (degree d = 1000) with the QSPPACK tool and used that polynomial in a simulated circuit to estimate CDF values via IQAE.
- [speculative] The amplitude-loading design avoids in-circuit quantum arithmetic by encoding per-counterparty losses into controlled rotations on an amplitude qubit and then applying a polynomial filter via QSVT to select loss thresholds.
- [speculative] The approach generalizes to expected-loss and CVaR estimation (appendix): they present constructions based on linearizing sin^2 around π/4 and variant polynomials for CVaR, but these are theoretical constructions not extensively benchmarked here.
- [speculative] The necessity and scaling of the polynomial degree d with respect to desired precision and number of counterparties remains an open question left for future work (authors explicitly state this).
- [supported] Iterative Quantum Amplitude Estimation (IQAE) was used in the workflow to avoid additional counting registers, and the authors detail how the k-scaling in their QSVT filter affects the IQAE precision requirement (ϵ_IQAE = ϵ · k^2).
- [supported] Practical experimental parameters used in simulations are provided (e.g., 4 assets, 2 qubits per Gaussian, shots = 2048, polynomial degree = 1000), and simulation outcomes (CDF estimates) are reported in detail.
- [speculative] The authors claim that the multiplicative amplitude-scaling factor k (<1) introduced to make the polynomial feasible for QSVT only results in a constant multiplicative increase in the number of IQAE samples required (via 1/k^2), rather than an asymptotic change.
- [speculative] While the method is argued to be a step toward practical quantum risk analysis, the paper acknowledges that a full resource/T-depth analysis in a fault-tolerant regime and further optimization of polynomial degree are necessary before claims of practical advantage can be established.

**Results summary:** The paper proposes a QSVT-based method to implement the state-preparation operator used in quantum amplitude-estimation-based credit risk analysis. By encoding per-counterparty loss contributions into amplitudes via controlled rotations and then applying a QSVT polynomial filter approximating a threshold function, the authors argue the depth of the filtering module scales as O(d · n) (d = polynomial degree, n = number of counterparties) rather than exponentially. They implemented an end-to-end simulation (4 assets, 2 qubits per Gaussian, 2048 shots) using a degree-1000 polynomial computed with QSPPACK and IQAE for amplitude estimation; simulated CDF estimates closely matched classical benchmarks. The paper highlights that while the approach avoids quantum arithmetic and empirically reproduces expected CDF behavior on small instances, the dependence of polynomial degree on precision and the full resource requirements for large-scale/fault-tolerant implementations remain open.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** theoretical

The paper relies on Quantum Amplitude Estimation (QAE), which provides a theoretical quadratic speed-up over classical Monte Carlo for estimating expectation values. The authors' contribution is a QSVT-based state-preparation to reduce circuit resource scaling (theoretical O(d · n) depth for fixed polynomial degree). However, no empirical demonstration of a practical quantum advantage on hardware is presented; results are limited to simulations on small instances and the dependence of polynomial degree and full fault-tolerant resource costs remain unresolved. Therefore any speed-up or practical advantage is at present theoretical/speculative.
## Limitations
- Classical preprocessing for QSVT (computing polynomial approximations and QSP/QSVT phases) can be computationally intensive and time-consuming (the authors used MATLAB QSPPACK).
- The polynomial approximation requires a (potentially) large degree d; the relation between d and number of counterparties n (and required precision) is not established and may cause resource blow-up.
- Circuit depth scales as O(d * n). If d grows with precision or n, total circuit depth may become prohibitively large.
- The method introduces a multiplicative amplitude scaling factor k < 1 (rescaling the filtered amplitude). This reduces signal amplitude and increases the number of IQAE samples required (roughly by a factor 1/k^2).
- No T-depth or detailed fault-tolerant resource analysis was provided, so the benefit in a fault-tolerant regime is unquantified.
- [inferred] Experiments were limited to noiseless simulation; no experiments on real (noisy) quantum hardware or noisy simulators are reported, leaving practical NISQ feasibility untested.
- [inferred] Demonstrations are small-scale (four assets/counterparties in experiments); scalability to large, production-size portfolios is unproven.
- [inferred] The authors chose conservative parameter (θ, βm, βM) settings to reuse a single polynomial across bisection steps; these safe choices may be suboptimal in performance and accuracy.
- [inferred] The requirement to approximate a bounded polynomial (|P(x)| ≤ 1) forces rescaling (kT_µ), introducing approximation error and additional sampling overhead.
- [inferred] The approach relies on a structure where ˜Π O Π is real and diagonal (or at least admits an SVD-friendly structure); applicability to more general or non-diagonal encodings is unclear.
- [inferred] If the threshold (VaR guess) changes during bisection, recomputing polynomials/phases may be needed; the classical overhead and practicality of repeated preprocessing is an open practical concern.
## Open questions
- How does the polynomial degree d scale with the required approximation precision and with the number of counterparties n?
- Under what conditions (if any) can d be made independent of n?
- What is the full T-depth and fault-tolerant resource count for the proposed QSVT-based amplitude loading approach, and does it yield net benefits in the fault-tolerant regime?
- Can the QSVT amplitude-loading methodology be extended to more sophisticated uncertainty models beyond the multivariate Gaussian conditional independence model?
- What are the practical classical preprocessing costs (time, memory) for computing high-degree polynomial approximations and corresponding phase sequences at realistic problem sizes?
- How resilient is the proposed circuit and QAE pipeline to realistic noise on NISQ hardware (error rates, decoherence, gate infidelity)?
- What is the practical impact of the amplitude scaling factor k on total runtime, sample complexity, and end-to-end cost for achieving target error bounds?
- Can the polynomial / QSVT phase data be reused efficiently across bisection or other iterative procedures (i.e., avoid repeated expensive preprocessing), and what are the trade-offs?
- How does the method scale (qubits, depth, sampling) for large portfolios (hundreds or thousands of counterparties) and is it implementable with foreseeable hardware?

**Future work:**
- Further enhancements concerning the resources required for the scaling of the circuit related to state preparation.
- Study the detailed growth of the polynomial degree d with respect to the required precision and the number of counterparties (i.e., formalize dependence of d on precision and n).
- Perform a complete T-depth analysis to assess the effective benefit of the amplitude loading scheme in the fault-tolerant regime.
- Extend the proposed methodologies to other, more sophisticated uncertainty models of interest in risk management.
## Key ideas
- #idea:quantum-advantage — Use of QSVT to implement a polynomial filter on an amplitude-loaded state enables encoding of the loss CDF and VaR estimation via IQAE; simulated results (n=4) closely match classical benchmark CDFs and authors report reduced circuit depth compared to prior in-circuit-arithmetic approaches.
- #idea:hybrid-approach — Classical precomputation (QSPPACK) of QSVT/QSP phases and reuse of a single polynomial across bisection steps reduces online quantum preprocessing; the pipeline combines classical computation (phase coefficient generation, bisection orchestration) with quantum state preparation and IQAE.
- #idea:near-term-feasibility — The authors present an end-to-end implementation avoiding counting registers (IQAE) and amplitude-loading via controlled Ry rotations; demonstrated on a small, realistic 4-counterparty dataset, indicating feasibility at small scales but with caveats on resource scaling.
- #limitation:qubit-count — Experiments use only 4 counterparties and 2 qubits per Gaussian factor; scaling to realistic portfolios would require substantially more qubits and resources.
- #limitation:data-encoding — Amplitude-loading is implemented as n controlled rotations and QSVT requires polynomial degree d (authors use d=1000), so although in-circuit arithmetic is avoided the cost of encoding and large-degree polynomials increases circuit depth and gate count.
- #limitation:simulation-only — All results are from classical simulation (with polynomial phases computed classically); no real-hardware experiments or noise-tailored results are reported.
- #limitation:no-empirical-validation — The method is validated against a classical reconstruction benchmark on the same small dataset, but there is no empirical hardware validation or large-scale benchmarking to establish practical advantage.
## Contradictions
- Paper asserts reduced circuit depth vs earlier arithmetic-based approaches, but requires a high polynomial degree (d=1000) and reports logical depth scaling O(d·n); the large d used in experiments raises scalability concerns and may negate the claimed depth advantages for larger, realistic portfolios.
