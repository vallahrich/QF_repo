## Abstract summary
This review examines how traditional risk management tools (e.g., portfolio diversification, VaR, stress testing) are being complemented by modern technologies such as AI/ML, big data analytics, and blockchain to improve real-time monitoring, predictive analytics, and transactional transparency. It highlights case studies demonstrating measurable benefits, discusses implementation challenges, and identifies emerging trends—most notably quantum computing and decentralized finance (DeFi)—as transformative forces for future financial risk management.
## Methodology
The paper employs a conceptual, review-based methodology to develop a comprehensive risk management framework for volatile financial markets. The authors synthesize extant literature and case-study evidence to (1) identify and categorise principal risk types (systemic, credit, market, operational), (2) specify and evaluate risk assessment tools using both quantitative approaches (e.g., Value-at-Risk, Monte Carlo simulation) and qualitative approaches (e.g., SWOT, Delphi), and (3) describe how emerging technologies (AI/ML predictive analytics, anomaly detection, big-data stress-testing, and blockchain/smart-contract automation) can be integrated into assessment and mitigation processes. They further outline practical implementation considerations including phased adoption, legacy-system integration, data-privacy/compliance constraints, and organizational agility measures (real-time analytics, automated trading triggers, scenario planning and contingency frameworks). The methodology also uses illustrative case studies from banking and trading to demonstrate practical applications and outcomes of the proposed framework. Overall, the approach is integrative and conceptual—combining literature synthesis, technology capability mapping, framework design, and case-based illustration rather than empirical experimentation.

## Limitations
- Traditional risk tools (e.g., VaR, stress testing) have gaps in predictive accuracy and often fail to capture extreme tail risks or black swan events
- Portfolio diversification cannot eliminate systemic risk
- Many quantitative models rely heavily on historical data and static assumptions that may not reflect future or rapidly changing market conditions
- Scalability and integration issues: traditional models struggle to process and analyse vast volumes of real-time data
- Technical and operational challenges integrating AI and blockchain with legacy infrastructures
- High costs of technology adoption, continuous maintenance, and updates, making deployment financially challenging (especially enterprise-wide AI systems)
- Shortage of skilled personnel to implement and maintain advanced systems, disproportionately disadvantaging smaller institutions
- Data privacy and compliance complexity when using diverse data sources (e.g., GDPR, CCPA)
- Interoperability and lack of standardization across AI, blockchain, and other risk-management platforms leading to fragmented systems
- Implementation risks from reliance on automation—errors in automated processes (e.g., smart contracts) can cause rapid failures
- Quantum computing is at an early stage; practical financial applications are limited by technical and scalability challenges
- Decentralized finance (DeFi) introduces regulatory uncertainty and cybersecurity vulnerabilities (e.g., smart contract exploits)

## Open questions

- How should AI and blockchain be integrated with legacy systems to minimize disruption and maximize benefit?
- What governance, privacy, and compliance frameworks are needed to safely leverage diverse data sources while meeting regulations such as GDPR and CCPA?
- How can algorithmic bias in AI-driven risk models be detected, measured, and mitigated to avoid unfair or discriminatory outcomes?
- What does explainability (XAI) look like in high-stakes financial risk decisions, and how can XAI be operationalized for regulators and practitioners?
- How can interoperability and standardization be achieved across diverse risk-management platforms and vendor solutions?
- What are the practical paths and timelines for applying quantum computing to real-world financial risk tasks, and which problems will it most effectively solve?
- How should regulators approach DeFi to balance innovation with consumer protection, systemic risk mitigation, and cross-border enforcement?
- What are effective scalable frameworks for modelling and managing risks across global, interconnected markets with diverse regulatory regimes?
- How can smaller institutions access or share advanced risk-management capabilities (e.g., via partnerships, federated models, or marketplaces) without compromising data privacy?
- What new systemic risks might emerge from widespread deployment of automation, DeFi, and quantum-enhanced analytics, and how can they be monitored?
