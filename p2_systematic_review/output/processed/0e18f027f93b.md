## Abstract summary
The paper proposes a quantum-assisted trading framework that combines Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage and hierarchical clustering for uncorrelated asset selection, an entropy-regularised genetic algorithm for weight optimisation, and a novel QUBO formulation of rebalancing scheduling solved by QAOA in a walk-forward setting. Backtests on a survivorship-free S&P 500 universe (train 2010–2024, test 2025) show the GA+QAOA approach achieves higher Sharpe and lower transaction costs than classical baselines, providing preliminary empirical evidence of advantage for variational quantum optimisation in combinatorial scheduling.
## Methodology
The study constructs a survivorship-bias-free S&P 500 universe (M=422 tickers) using adjusted daily close prices from Jan 1, 2010 to Dec 31, 2025, splits data into training (2010–2024) and test (2025) periods, and selects n=10 uncorrelated assets via Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage covariance, angular-distance conversion of the shrinkage correlation matrix, Ward hierarchical clustering into 10 clusters, and choosing the cluster representative with highest training-period annualised Sharpe. Portfolio weights are derived using three methods: an entropy-regularised Genetic Algorithm (GA) that maximises annualised Sharpe plus a normalised entropy term (λ_ent=0.05) on GPU, a closed-form Minimum-Variance (MinVar) solution from the Ledoit–Wolf covariance (projected to nonnegative weights), and equal weights; an ensemble is formed by averaging GA, MinVar and equal weights. Rebalancing scheduling is formulated as a QUBO: for W candidate dates per window the authors compute drifted weights, estimate marginal Sharpe gain g_k (benefit of rebalancing over the local return window minus an annualised transaction cost penalty), set diagonal Q_kk = -λ1 g_k + λ2 c · n and off-diagonals Q_kl = λ3 exp(-|t_k - t_l|/Δt) to penalise closely spaced rebalances; Q is normalised to [-1,1]. The QUBO is mapped to an Ising Hamiltonian; QAOA (p=2) is used to approximate its ground state with multi-restart variational optimisation (R=5 restarts) using COBYLA, with measurement-based estimation of expected energy (2,048 shots during optimisation, 4,096 shots for final evaluation). A walk-forward scheme divides the test year into K=3 windows, builds and solves a QUBO per window using only in-window returns (to avoid lookahead), and applies the most-probable measured bitstring (lowest-energy run) as the schedule for that window. Backtesting accounts for transaction costs (c=0.001 per unit, applied as L1 distance between drifted and target weights), computes portfolio evolution, and evaluates performance with return, annualised Sharpe, Sortino, max drawdown, Calmar ratio, rebalance count and total transaction cost. Comparative baselines include buy-and-hold, periodic rebalancing (1d, 5d, 10d, 21d), and a 5%-threshold rule.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA), Genetic Algorithm (entropy-regularised), Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage covariance estimator, Ward hierarchical clustering (agglomerative), Minimum Variance closed-form portfolio, COBYLA (constrained optimisation), QUBO-to-Ising mapping, Periodic and threshold rebalancing heuristics
**Frameworks:** Qiskit (Qiskit-Aer), PyTorch, PyGAD, scikit-learn, yfinance

**Experimental setup:** Experiments run on Google Colab with an NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPU (16 GB) for GPU-accelerated GA evaluations via PyTorch. QAOA circuits are simulated on the Qiskit Aer statevector simulator (CPU). Software stack includes Python 3.12, PyTorch 2.x (CUDA), Qiskit 1.2.4, Qiskit-Aer 0.15.1, Qiskit-Algorithms 0.3.1, PyGAD, scikit-learn and yfinance.

**Dataset:** Survivorship-bias-free S&P 500 universe constructed from current index constituents reversed to the training cutoff (Dec 31, 2024), daily adjusted close prices for Jan 1, 2010–Dec 31, 2025. After removing tickers with missing data, M=422 assets remain. Training period: Jan 2010–Dec 2024 (T_train = 3,774 trading days). Test period: Jan–Dec 2025 (T_test = 249 trading days).

### Process
1) Asset selection: Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage covariance → angular distance → Ward clustering → choose 10 cluster representatives by highest training Sharpe. 2) Weight optimisation: run entropy-regularised GA (population=300, generations=200, uniform crossover, mutation rate=15%, weight bounds [0.01,1], entropy term λ_ent=0.05) on GPU to maximise annualised Sharpe + normalised entropy; compute MinVar closed-form weights from Σ_LW (pseudo-inverse, project negatives to zero); compute equal weights and ensemble average. 3) Define rebalancing candidate dates: per walk-forward window W=8 candidate dates, K=3 windows across test period. For each candidate date compute drifted weights and marginal Sharpe gain g_k (SR improvement over local window minus annualised transaction cost penalty). 4) Build QUBO: Q_kk = -λ1 g_k + λ2 c · n, Q_kl = λ3 exp(-|t_k - t_l|/Δt) for k ≠ l, normalise Q to [−1,1]. 5) Map QUBO to Ising Hamiltonian (h_i, J_ij) and construct QAOA ansatz with depth p=2 on W qubits. 6) Optimise variational angles (γ, β) using COBYLA (max 150 iter) with S=2,048 measurement shots estimating expected energy during optimisation; perform R=5 random restarts and keep the run with lowest measured energy. 7) Final evaluation: sample S=4,096 shots from the best parameters, take the most probable bitstring as schedule for that window. 8) Walk-forward: repeat per window using only in-window returns, concatenate schedules. 9) Backtest: apply schedule, rebalance to target weights on selected dates, apply transaction cost c=0.001 (10 bp per unit) applied as L1 norm between drifted and target weights, update portfolio value, compute performance metrics.

## Findings
- [supported] Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage combined with hierarchical angular-distance clustering was used to select n=10 relatively low-correlation stocks from a survivorship-bias-mitigated S&P 500 universe.
- [supported] An entropy-regularised Genetic Algorithm (GA) run on GPU produced GA weights with high in-sample Sharpe (reported training Sharpe = 1.445) while avoiding degenerate (sparse) allocations.
- [supported] Closed-form Minimum Variance (MinVar) and equal-weight portfolios were implemented as classical baselines and used in an ensemble.
- [supported] The rebalancing timing problem was formulated as a QUBO incorporating marginal Sharpe gain, a transaction-cost penalty and an exponentially-decaying over-frequency penalty, and Q was normalised to stabilise optimisation.
- [supported] A walk-forward framework was used so scheduling decisions (QUBO construction and QAOA optimisation) used only window-local information, removing lookahead from the scheduling step.
- [supported] QAOA (p=2) with 5 restarts and multi-shot evaluation (2,048 shots during optimisation, 4,096 shots final) produced concentrated measurement distributions: the top bitstring >7% of shots and top-5 bitstrings >25% of shots in the reported runs.
- [supported] In the 2025 out-of-sample backtest (n=249 trading days), the GA + QAOA strategy achieved Sharpe=0.588 and total return=10.10%, outperforming their best classical baseline (GA periodic 10d: Sharpe=0.575, return=9.77%).
- [supported] GA + QAOA executed 8 rebalances (total transaction cost 6.1 basis points) versus GA periodic/10d which executed 24 rebalances (11.0 basis points), a reported 44.5% reduction in transaction costs while improving risk-adjusted performance.
- [supported] MinVar weights underperformed in the 2025 test year (low Sharpe), indicating weight-selection method materially affected out-of-sample results in this environment.
- [supported] The simple ensemble (equal blend of GA, MinVar, Equal) diluted GA’s signal and underperformed GA alone in the 2025 test.
- [speculative] The authors claim the observed QAOA probability concentration provides empirical evidence of quantum advantage for the combinatorial scheduling subproblem.
- [speculative] The paper asserts that the QAOA circuit (8 qubits, p=2) and the QUBO formulation are compatible with near-term NISQ hardware and with quantum annealers for scale-up.
- [speculative] The QUBO construction choices (specific λ penalties and exponential decay interaction) are presented as generally effective priors for penalising over-frequent rebalancing in production settings.
- [speculative] The claim that joint weight-and-schedule QUBO formulations and scaling to W ≥ 20 are feasible with hardware or tensor-network simulators is forward-looking and not demonstrated in the paper.
- [speculative] The literature claim that 'no prior work has formulated rebalancing timing as a QUBO' is stated by the authors as a novelty claim (requires independent verification).

**Results summary:** The authors present a hybrid classical-quantum portfolio construction and rebalancing system: Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage plus hierarchical clustering to pick 10 uncorrelated stocks, entropy-regularised GA (GPU) for weights, and a novel QUBO formulation of rebalancing timing solved by QAOA inside a walk-forward backtest. In the 2025 out-of-sample year (249 trading days) their GA + QAOA pipeline produced Sharpe=0.588 and 10.10% return, outperforming the best classical periodic rebalancing baseline (GA Rebal/10d: Sharpe=0.575, return=9.77%) while using fewer rebalances (8 vs. 24) and materially lower transaction costs. QAOA measurement distributions showed concentrated probability mass on a small set of low-energy bitstrings in their simulated 8-qubit experiments, which the authors interpret as evidence of effective variational optimisation for the scheduling subproblem.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The authors report concentrated QAOA measurement distributions and a simulated performance improvement when using QAOA-selected schedules on an 8-qubit problem (classical statevector simulation). However, the experiments are small-scale (W=8), simulated (Qiskit Aer statevector), and compared against simple classical scheduling rules; no demonstration on physical quantum hardware or against stronger classical combinatorial optimisers is provided. Therefore the claim of quantum advantage is preliminary and speculative rather than demonstrated.
## Limitations
- Single-year out-of-sample test (2025) — results validated on only one test year, limiting robustness claims (author-stated).
- QAOA window size limited to W = 8 due to simulation cost; experiments do not demonstrate performance for larger scheduling horizons (author-stated).
- MinVar weight method underperformed in the 2025 momentum regime, indicating sensitivity of outcomes to weight-selection method (author-stated).
- QUBO / QAOA experiments performed using the Qiskit Aer statevector simulator (exact simulation) rather than noisy, physical quantum hardware — hardware noise and finite-coherence effects are not evaluated [inferred].
- [inferred] The small QUBO instance (W = 8) is classically tractable (2^8 = 256), so the claimed empirical advantage on this instance does not establish advantage at realistic, larger problem sizes.
- [inferred] The transaction cost model is simplistic (fixed per-unit cost c = 0.001) and ignores slippage, market impact, bid/ask spreads, and execution constraints, which could materially affect net performance.
- [inferred] Candidate rebalancing dates are equally spaced and limited to a fixed set per window; continuous-time or more flexible scheduling was not considered.
- [inferred] The GA hyperparameters (population size, generations, mutation rate, entropy weight λ_ent) and their sensitivity are not explored; results may be sensitive to these choices.
- [inferred] Removing tickers with missing data across the full period may introduce survivorship-like or selection biases despite efforts to reconstruct a survivorship-free universe.
- [inferred] Walk-forward partitioning used only K = 3 windows (≈83 days each); granularity and sensitivity to the number/length of windows are not analysed.
- [inferred] QAOA depth (p = 2), number of restarts (R = 5), and shot budgets were fixed; no systematic study of how these algorithmic settings affect solution quality or resource trade-offs is reported.
- [inferred] The ensemble weighting method is a naive equal blend of three weight schemes; more sophisticated ensemble or model-selection methods were not tested.
- [inferred] Risk-return metric optimisation was limited to Sharpe/Sortino-type objectives; alternative objectives (tail risk measures, transaction-aware utility) were not considered.
## Open questions
- Does QAOA (or other quantum optimisation methods) deliver a practical advantage for rebalancing scheduling at realistic, larger scales (W >> 8) and in multi-asset, multi-window formulations?
- How do results generalise across multiple years and different market regimes (i.e., is the GA + QAOA outperformance stable out-of-sample beyond 2025)?
- How robust are the QAOA solutions to realistic hardware constraints: gate errors, decoherence, limited connectivity, finite measurement shots, and calibration overhead?
- How does QAOA compare, in solution quality and wall-clock time, to state-of-the-art classical combinatorial optimisers (e.g., simulated annealing, tabu search, integer programming solvers, or specialised heuristic schedulers) on larger instances?
- What is the sensitivity of outcomes to QUBO hyperparameters (λ1, λ2, λ3), the transaction cost parameter c, and the choice of decay scale ∆t used to penalise frequent rebalancing?
- Can joint optimisation of portfolio weights and rebalancing schedule (a single, larger QUBO) yield materially better performance than the sequential approach used here?
- What is the impact on net performance when using more realistic transaction and execution models (market impact, slippage, partial fills), and can the QUBO be adapted to include such considerations?
- How well do angle parameters and optimisation strategies transfer across walk-forward windows — could parameter reuse or warm-starting reduce optimisation cost without degrading solutions?
- Are quantum annealers (e.g., D-Wave) practically competitive for this scheduling QUBO at larger scales, and how would embedding and analog noise affect solutions?
- What QAOA depth p and qubit counts are required to obtain high-quality solutions as W grows, and what are the corresponding resource and error-correction requirements?

**Future work:**

- #limitation:simulation-only — All QAOA experiments are performed on the Qiskit Aer statevector simulator (no noisy QPU experiments), so hardware noise and device constraints are not evaluated.
- #limitation:no-empirical-validation — No runs on real quantum hardware or noise-aware/error-mitigated experiments; empirical claims about quantum benefit are based solely on classical simulation.
- #limitation:qubit-count — The QUBO per window uses W=8 qubits (K=3 windows across test year), a small problem size which limits evidence about performance on larger candidate-date sets or more complex scheduling instances.
- #idea:hybrid-approach — Walk-forward design uses only in-window returns to avoid lookahead; the final schedule per window is taken as the most-probable measured bitstring from QAOA sampling and applied in backtests.
## Contradictions
- #contradiction:classical-vs-quantum — The paper attributes performance gains to the GA+QAOA approach but uses an ensemble averaging GA, MinVar and equal weights and relies on a classical GA for weight optimisation; because QAOA is simulated (small W) and combined with strong classical methods, it is unclear how much of the reported improvement is attributable to the quantum component versus classical processing.
- #contradiction:scalability — Demonstration uses an 8-qubit QUBO per window simulated on a statevector simulator; claims or implications about broader quantum superiority are not supported for larger problem sizes or realistic noisy hardware, so scalability to production-scale rebalancing problems is unproven.
