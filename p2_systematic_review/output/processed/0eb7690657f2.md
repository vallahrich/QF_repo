## Abstract summary
This preprint proposes an end-to-end quantum algorithm that combines PDE-based option valuation (via QSVT) with quantum-parallel Monte Carlo, QPCA and QPE to estimate portfolio Value-at-Risk (VaR) and Conditional VaR (CVaR) for European options, claiming a circuit-depth scaling of roughly ˜O(max{¯t, T−¯t} ϵ_d^{-1/2} ϵ^{-4}). The authors also analyze American option pricing and present a quantum 'no-go' result for the amplitude-comparison step in their approach, indicating that alternative strategies are required for American options.
## Methodology
This is a theoretical / algorithmic paper that designs an end-to-end quantum workflow to estimate Value-at-Risk (VaR) and Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) for portfolios of European options. The methodology combines a backward PDE discretization for option fair values with quantum linear-algebra and quantum sampling subroutines, and a forward Monte Carlo stock-price simulation implemented in quantum parallel. Key components: (1) Discretize the option pricing PDE on a time grid (∆τ) and asset grid {S_j} and express the implicit time-step as V^{t-∆τ} = [I + M]^{-1} V^t where M is a 3-sparse tridiagonal matrix. (2) Prepare the terminal payoff state |V_T> via Grover–Randolph state-prep and use Quantum Singular Value Transformation (QSVT) / block-encoding to implement the matrix function g(˜M) ≈ ˜M^{-(T−t)/∆τ} to produce an amplitude-encoded quantum state |V_{¯t}> of option values at target time ¯t. Polynomial approximation degree and QSVT query complexity scale as O((T−¯t)∆τ^{-1} ||˜M||_2 log(1/ϵ)). (3) Prepare many Monte Carlo stock-price trajectories in quantum superposition using iterative controlled update unitaries (CC-CU_S and their inverses) that implement an Euler discretization St+∆τ = St(1 + µ∆τ + α √{St} ∆Z_t), producing a uniform superposition |ϕ_{¯t}> = (1/√L) ∑_k |k>|Ŝ_{¯t,k}> with m qubits used to represent each price to precision 2^{-m}. (4) Merge asset-price and option-value information by using amplitude-encoded |V_{¯t}> to form a density matrix ρ (QPCA approach) and perform density-matrix exponentiation (via the swap operator) and Quantum Phase Estimation (QPE) to map |Ŝ_{¯t,k}>|0> -> |Ŝ_{¯t,k}>|ĤV_{¯t}(Ŝ_{¯t,k})> (estimated option value amplitudes). (5) Run a bisection search over candidate VaR thresholds using a controlled comparison unitary to flag samples ≤ threshold and use quantum mean/ amplitude estimation to estimate probabilities p(0) = Pr(ĤV ≤ V_bar) to find the VaR and then compute CVaR from the conditioned expectation using overlap / swap-test based estimation. The paper analyzes errors and resource scaling including discretization error ϵ_d, amplitude-approximation errors ϵ1, QPCA/QPE error ϵ3 and the overall circuit-depth / sample complexities. It also presents a negative result (a 'no-go' theorem) showing that enforcing the pointwise max(payoff, continuation) step required for American options (amplitude-wise maximum) would require Ω(2^n) copies and thus is quantum-hard under the presented encoding.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum Singular Value Transformation (QSVT), Block-encoding, Grover–Rudolph state preparation, Quantum Principal Component Analysis (QPCA), Quantum Phase Estimation (QPE), Quantum Fourier Transform (QFT), Density-matrix exponentiation via swap operator, Quantum amplitude/mean estimation, Swap test, Quantum bisection search (via amplitude estimation for probabilities)
**Frameworks:** QSPPACK

**Results summary:** The preprint develops a theoretical, end-to-end quantum algorithmic pipeline to estimate VaR and CVaR for portfolios of European options. The approach computes option values on a discretized grid by QSVT-based backward PDE propagation, simulates underlying asset paths in quantum parallel, uses QPCA/QPE to link simulated prices to precomputed option-value amplitudes, and then performs quantum bisection plus amplitude/overlap estimation to obtain VaR and CVaR. The authors present complexity formulas (polynomial-in-inverse-error and time-horizon factors) suggesting potential quantum advantages under their data-access assumptions, and they prove a hardness/no-go result for a direct extension to American options based on the difficulty of constructing an amplitude-wise maximum state.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The authors claim a potential quantum speed-up for European-option VaR/CVaR estimation because their pipeline avoids exhaustive classical post-processing/readout of high-dimensional PDE solutions by working in amplitude-encoded quantum states (using QSVT, QPCA, QPE and amplitude estimation). However, the claim is theoretical (no empirical demonstration), depends on idealized data-access oracles and block-encoding assumptions, and relies on polynomial factors in inverse error that may be large; moreover, a direct extension to American options is argued to be infeasible by a presented hardness result.
## Limitations
- Algorithm is designed for European options; the current approach cannot price American options (author-stated 'quantum no-go' theorem).
- Theorem 1 shows the amplitude-maximum step required for American option PDEs would require Ω(2^n) samples, i.e., no quantum advantage for that component (author-stated).
- High resource requirements: circuit depth and sample complexity scale unfavorably with precision. The paper reports depth ~ Õ(max{¯t, T−¯t} ϵ_d^{-1/2} ϵ^{-4}) and sample complexity factors like O(ϵ^{-3}) and O(1/ϵ) in subroutines (author-stated).
- Accuracy depends on discretization: discretization error ϵ_d (from ∆τ) contributes to end-to-end error; finite-difference discretisation induces O((∆τ)^2) error (author-stated).
- Relies on strong data-access and oracle assumptions: efficient block-encoding of the PDE matrix ˜M (UR, Uc), efficient Grover–Rudolph state preparation for |V_T⟩, and efficient arithmetic oracles for F and F^{-1} (author-stated assumptions).
- QSVT/QSP polynomial degree depends on matrix norm and time horizon; this can produce large-degree polynomials and deep circuits in practice (author-stated / implied in complexity expressions).
- QPCA + QPE subroutines require many copies of input states and long evolution times (τ = O(2^m)) making Step 3 resource intensive (author-stated).
- The worked examples and constructions assume a single-asset underlying per option; multi-asset PDEs (high-dimensional) are not implemented and would face the curse of dimensionality classically (author-stated).
- Modeling simplifications: for illustration the paper often specializes to a specific local volatility (σ(S,t)=α/√S) and other realistic market features (stochastic volatility, jumps, rough vol) are not integrated into the end-to-end resource analysis (author-stated/in-text choices).
