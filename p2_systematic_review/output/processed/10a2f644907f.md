## Abstract summary
This survey reviews quantum-inspired evolutionary algorithms (QIEAs) applied to feature subset selection, highlighting that QIEAs use qubit-based probabilistic representations to improve diversity and balance exploration and exploitation. The authors collected 56 papers, analyzed algorithmic novelties, heuristics, objective functions and rotation-gate variants, presented a taxonomy, and identified open research problems and directions for future work.
## Methodology
This paper is a systematic literature survey of quantum-inspired evolutionary algorithms (QIEAs) applied to feature subset selection (FSS). The authors carried out a structured search across multiple bibliographic sources (Web of Science, ScienceDirect, IEEE Xplore, MDPI, Emerald, Springer and Google Scholar) for publications from 2002 through 2024 using keyword combinations such as "quantum-inspired metaheuristic", "quantum-inspired + attribute selection", "quantum + feature selection", and "quantum-inspired + evolutionary algorithm". Inclusion criteria required relevance to QIEAs and feature subset selection; exclusion criteria removed publications in unknown journals or duplicate/redundant reports. The authors collected 56 relevant papers, extracted and tabulated key metadata (algorithm family, encoding, objective function, rotation/quantum gates used, classifier in wrappers, validation method, sequential/parallel implementation, and implementation environment), and performed quantitative analyses (year-wise and publisher-wise trends, algorithm counts). They also synthesized building blocks across studies (objective functions, rotation-gate variants and lookup tables), produced a taxonomy of approaches (by encoding, objective type, EC algorithm, heuristic type and implementation), and identified open research gaps. No original empirical experiments were run by the survey authors; the work aggregates, categorizes and critiques existing empirical studies reported in the literature.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum Genetic Algorithm (QGA) and variants (QIGA, NQGA, QCGA, ClusterQGA, etc.), Quantum Particle Swarm Optimization (QPSO) and variants (QBPSO, GQPSO, iBQPSO, EBQPSO, HI-BQPSO, CQPSO, KPLS-QPSO, ALQPSO), Quantum Differential Evolution (QDE, Q-inspired DE), Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA), Quantum Grasshopper Optimization Algorithm (QGAO / QGOA), Quantum Whale Optimization Algorithm (QWOA), Quantum Immune/Immune Clonal Algorithms (QVICA, QICO), Quantum Moth Flame Optimization Algorithm (QMFOA), Quantum Hummingbird Algorithm (QAHA), Quantum Ant Bee Colony / Quantum Equilibrium Optimizer with ABC (SQEOABC), Quantum Squirrel Inspired Algorithm, Quantum Owl Search Algorithm (QIOSA), Quantum Avian Navigation Optimizer (QANA, BQANA, IBQANA), Quantum Dwarf Mongoose Optimization (QDMO), Quantum Honey Badger Algorithm (QCHBA), Quantum Fruit Fly Algorithm (QFFA), Quantum Grey Wolf Optimizer (QCGWORS, EQI-GWO), Quantum Gravitational Search Algorithm (QBGSA, BQIGSA), Quantum Shuffled Frog Leaping Algorithm (QSFLAR), Hybrid/graph-based quantum-inspired methods (e.g., quantum graph-theoretic feature selection)
**Frameworks:** Qiskit (mentioned / used in at least one reviewed study), D-Wave (referenced as a quantum platform possibility)

**Results summary:** This review synthesizes 56 studies on quantum-inspired evolutionary algorithms (QIEAs) for feature subset selection, showing that QIEAs (implemented on classical machines) commonly use Q-bit encodings and rotation gates to guide search. Accuracy and combinations of accuracy with subset cardinality are the most used objectives. Eighteen rotation-gate variants (static and adaptive) and many lookup-table schemes were identified; adaptive angles (including chaotic maps and generation schedules) are often reported to improve convergence and escape local optima. Empirical claims across works indicate improved accuracy and feature reduction compared to non-quantum counterparts on specific benchmarks, though QIEAs mostly mimic quantum principles without leveraging true quantum hardware or demonstrating genuine quantum speedups. The authors highlight gaps (limited use of other quantum gates, under-exploited probabilistic Q-bit aspects, lack of true parallel/quantum implementations) and propose several speculative future research directions.

**Performance claims:**
- Wei & Ye [17]: reduced >70% of features with improved accuracy >80% (text categorization).
- Aranian et al. [21]: reduced features by 19–49% and improved accuracy by 7–31% (Persian handwritten letters OCR).
- Zhang & Wang [20]: QGA improved recognition rate to 86% (rolling bearing failure recognition).
- Ahmad et al. [32]: 90% reduction in deep feature vector with classification accuracy ≈99% (white blood cell leukemia detection).
- Nijaguna et al. [62]: QFFA achieved sensitivity 99.26% and accuracy 99.04% on reported medical diagnosis experiments.
- Ghosh et al. [56]: reported achieving 100% accuracy on many datasets with large reduction in selected features (prostate cancer gene selection) — dataset-specific claim.
- QDMO-EDLID [60]: reported 1–6% higher accuracy than standalone deep models (CRN/DBN/DAE) on intrusion-detection benchmarks.
- Some studies report error-rate or F1 improvements in the order of 0.08–0.14 F1 (Ng et al. [48] reported mean F1 improvements of 0.08–0.14 versus certain wrappers on scRNA-seq gene selection).
## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** not-applicable

The surveyed methods are 'quantum-inspired' algorithms implemented on classical hardware; while many papers report empirical improvements over classical metaheuristics for specific FSS problems, no reviewed work demonstrates genuine quantum computational advantage (speedup from quantum hardware or provable exponential gains). Claims that conflate quantum parallelism or exponential efficiency with benefits of these classical QIEAs are not substantiated by the surveyed evidence.
## Limitations
- Majority of QIEA studies use only the rotation gate; other quantum gates and richer quantum operations are largely unexplored.
- There is no unified rule or standard for choosing rotation gate magnitude or sign; diverse lookup tables and ad hoc schemes are used across studies.
- The probabilistic nature of Q-bit representations is not fully exploited—many works reduce Qubits to simplified two-dimensional representations and mostly use a single gate.
- Most reviewed algorithms are implemented and evaluated on classical computers; none of the surveyed FSS QIEAs were demonstrated on real quantum hardware.
- Parallel implementations are absent in the literature reviewed despite being included in the proposed taxonomy; reported 'parallel' methods are typically not true distributed/parallel implementations.
- Many QIEA variants rely on static (user-tuned) parameters or lookup tables for rotation angles, making them non-adaptive and sensitive to parameter choices.
- Objective functions are not standardized; many studies use simple accuracy or weighted combinations of accuracy and cardinality which may not be adequate across domains.
- Evaluation methodologies vary widely (different classifiers, validation protocols, datasets), hindering direct comparability of results across studies.
- Most works focus on single-objective optimization; multi-objective formulations (e.g., accuracy vs. cardinality vs. cost) are underrepresented.
- The literature seldom leverages advanced quantum principles such as entanglement or multi-qubit gates for FSS; quantum-inspired approaches are largely limited in scope.
- [inferred] The heavy reliance on wrapper approaches in many QIEA studies likely incurs high computational cost and may limit scalability to large/high-dimensional datasets.
- [inferred] The absence of standardized benchmarks and reproducible codebases for QIEA-based FSS makes replicability and fair comparison difficult.
- [inferred] Limited exploration of online/streaming feature selection settings—most algorithms target static datasets.
- [inferred] Handling of imbalanced datasets is not consistently addressed; few studies explicitly tackle class imbalance in the FSS context.
- [inferred] Many adaptive rotation schemes depend on problem-specific tuning (e.g., chaotic maps, bespoke schedules), reducing generalizability across problem domains.
## Open questions
- Which other quantum gates (beyond rotation, Hadamard, NOT, CNOT) can be effectively incorporated into QIEAs to improve FSS performance?
- How can quantum entanglement concepts be meaningfully and tractably integrated into quantum-inspired EAs for feature subset selection?
- What are principled, adaptive strategies for setting rotation angles and directions that generalize across datasets and algorithms?
- Can QIEAs be parallelized and distributed effectively to scale to very high-dimensional or large-scale datasets, and what architectures/frameworks best support that?
- How can reinforcement learning (including deep RL) be hybridized with QIEAs to dynamically manage exploration-exploitation trade-offs in FSS?
- How can QIEAs be extended from offline/static FSS to online/streaming feature selection (both data and feature streams)?
- What methods make QIEAs robust to class imbalance and irregular real-world data distributions?
- How feasible and beneficial is it to implement QIEAs on actual quantum computers (e.g., via Qiskit or D-Wave), and what are the practical obstacles?
- Are there standard, domain-agnostic objective functions or multi-objective formulations that better capture the trade-offs in FSS for QIEAs?
- How can the full probabilistic characterization of Q-bits (beyond simplified 2D/Bloch reductions) be leveraged in classical simulations to improve optimization?
- [inferred] What benchmark datasets, experimental protocols, and open-source implementations are needed to enable reproducible and comparable evaluation of QIEAs for FSS?
- [inferred] Can hybrid approaches combining multiple quantum-inspired operators (e.g., entanglement-inspired coupling + diverse gates) yield consistently better results than single-operator designs?

**Future work:**
- Employing other evolutionary algorithms (novel EAs) in quantum-inspired variants and evaluating them for feature subset selection.
- Hybridizing EAs with quantum operators to create QIEA hybrids that better balance exploration and exploitation.
- Investigating and employing other quantum gates (beyond the rotation gate) within QIEAs for FSS.
- Incorporating quantum entanglement principles into evolutionary search to exploit richer quantum behaviors (noting memory/intensity trade-offs).
- Hybridizing reinforcement learning (including deep RL) with QIEAs to improve dynamic decision-making for exploration and exploitation.
- Developing online quantum-inspired FSS algorithms for streaming data and streaming features.
- Designing QIEA approaches that explicitly handle unbalanced datasets more effectively than traditional EAs.
- Porting and developing evolutionary algorithms for execution on actual quantum computers (e.g., Qiskit, D-Wave) to realize genuine quantum advantages.
- Developing adaptive rotation-angle mechanisms (e.g., chaotic maps, generation-aware schedules, fitness-based adaptive rules) to reduce parameter sensitivity.
- Exploring true parallel and distributed implementations of QIEAs to improve scalability for big data FSS tasks.
- Creating standardized benchmarks, reproducible codebases, and consistent evaluation protocols for QIEA-based FSS to facilitate fair comparisons.
## Key ideas
- #idea:quantum-advantage — Survey reports many empirical claims in the literature that QIEA variants (quantum-inspired evolutionary algorithms) outperform non-quantum counterparts on benchmark datasets in terms of accuracy and feature reduction, but these claims are dataset- and study-dependent and lack standardised benchmarking.
- #idea:hybrid-approach — QIEAs are frequently combined with classical heuristics (filters, clustering, cooperative/co-evolutionary schemes) and wrapped with classifiers; rotation-gate strategies (18 variants identified) and adaptive angle schedules are common hybrids that reportedly improve convergence and avoid premature stagnation.
- #contradiction:classical-vs-quantum — The survey identifies instances where papers attribute quantum parallelism or exponential efficiency to QIEAs despite all implementations being executed on classical hardware, and flags such claims as misleading and unsupported by the evidence.
- #contradiction:scalability — The literature lacks true parallel/distributed implementations and standardised scalability studies, contradicting broad claims of superior scaling or efficiency; the survey calls out the absence of evidence for scaling to real-world high-dimensional problems.
- #limitation:simulation-only — All reviewed QIEA implementations were classical (quantum-inspired) rather than run on quantum hardware, so no QPU-based validation exists in the surveyed body of work.
- #limitation:no-empirical-validation — The survey itself did not run original experiments; it aggregates heterogeneous empirical reports from 56 studies, which limits the ability to draw definitive performance conclusions across methods and datasets.
## Contradictions
- Several surveyed papers attribute 'quantum parallelism' or exponential speedups to QIEAs, but this is contradicted by the fact that all implementations were run on classical hardware and no evidence of real quantum exponential advantage is presented.
- Claims of QIEA superiority are contradicted by heterogeneity in datasets, objectives, rotation-angle schemes and evaluation protocols across studies; lack of standardized benchmarks and absence of distributed/parallel implementations undermine broad scalability claims.
