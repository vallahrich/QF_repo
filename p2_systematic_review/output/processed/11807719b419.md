## Abstract summary
The authors generalize Spekkens' notion of noncontextuality to a principle of univalence, formalized via linear embeddings and simulations between generalized probabilistic theories (GPTs). They prove structural results on exact and approximate embeddability into quantum theory — in particular showing that only special Euclidean Jordan-algebraic state spaces are exactly embeddable — and derive robustness bounds (using Bell-type arguments) that enable experimental tests. Building on theory-agnostic tomography, they propose an experiment to challenge quantum theory by identifying effective GPTs that cannot be univalently simulated by quantum mechanics, without assuming tomographic completeness or device calibration.
## Methodology
This is a theoretical/mathematical study developing a framework and proofs rather than an empirical or simulation-based methodology. The authors adopt the prepare-and-measure operational setting and model systems as generalized probabilistic theories (GPTs). They formalize the notion of one GPT simulating or embedding into another via linear, positive, normalization-preserving maps (exact and approximate ε-embeddings), and introduce the additional constraint of univalence (single-valued simulation) as a generalization of Spekkens’ generalized noncontextuality. The methodology is largely mathematical: (i) precise definitions are given for states, effects, cones, unrestricted GPTs, ε-simulations, and ε-embeddings; (ii) structural lemmas and theorems are proved about embeddings (linearity, transitivity, existence of projectors, positivity, relations between duals); (iii) convex-geometric constructions are used to show that any finite GPT admits a measurement-univalent approximate classical simulation (construction of polytopes between shrunk and original convex sets; use of Hausdorff approximation bounds); (iv) algebraic/operator methods and results from the literature (Jordan algebra classification, Effros–Størmer results, Kadison’s inequality) are applied to characterize all unrestricted GPTs that admit exact unital embeddings into finite-dimensional complex quantum theory (result: special Euclidean Jordan-algebraic GPTs and their direct sums); (v) analytic tools from quantum information (gentle measurement lemma, Dini’s theorem, trace and operator-norm estimates, Tsirelson bound, robustness of entanglement) are used to derive quantitative bounds ruling out approximate embeddings of certain GPTs (e.g. gbits) into quantum theory and to relate approximate embeddability to violations of Bell inequalities; (vi) they formulate an operational experimental protocol (theory-agnostic tomography on a chosen finite set of preparations and measurements, reconstruction of an experimental GPT A'', estimating an ε such that A'' ε-embeds into the full effective GPT A', and certifying nonembeddability via numerical/analytic Bell-functional based criteria and bounds involving self-entangleability). The approach mixes rigorous constructive existence proofs, classification theorems, convex approximation bounds, and derivation of quantitative error thresholds that would permit experimental falsification under the assumption of univalence.

## Findings
- [theoretical] Introduced a general formalism of one GPT (generalized probabilistic theory) simulating another via ε-simulations, and defined univalence (preparation- and/or measurement-univalent simulations) as a generalization of Spekkens’ noncontextuality.
- [theoretical] Proved that univalent simulations are equivalent to linear (approximate) embeddings (Lemma 2).
- [theoretical] Showed transitivity of embeddings: embeddings compose and approximation errors add (Lemma 3).
- [theoretical] Proved that every finite-dimensional GPT admits measurement-univalent ε-simulations by finite classical systems (and hence by quantum systems) with a bound on required classical dimension n that scales polynomially in 1/ε (Lemma 1).
- [theoretical] Characterized all unrestricted GPTs that admit exact unital embeddings into finite-dimensional complex quantum theory: they are exactly the special Euclidean Jordan-algebraic state spaces (Theorem 2).
- [theoretical] Demonstrated that only classical probability theory and complex quantum theory with superselection rules can arise as fixed-point GPTs of physically realizable completely positive decoherence/coarse-graining channels (Corollary 3; Lemmas 10 and 11).
- [theoretical] Proved that embeddings into countably infinite-dimensional quantum systems can be approximated arbitrarily well by embeddings into finite-dimensional quantum systems (Lemma 12).
- [theoretical] Developed quantitative constraints on approximate embeddability into quantum theory by connecting to Bell-nonlocality: if a GPT admits bipartite self-correlations that violate quantum bounds, this yields explicit lower bounds on the embedding error ε (Theorem 3).
- [theoretical] Produced explicit numerical lower bounds ruling out high-accuracy quantum embeddings for the gbit: an argument yielding ε >= ~0.101416 for any finite-dimensional quantum embedding (geometry-based), and a Tsirelson-based bound giving ε >= ~0.00915 (Examples in Section VI).
- [theoretical] Proven equivalence between the discrete ontological (ontic) models formulation of Spekkens-style noncontextuality and exact univalent simulations/embeddings into finite classical systems (Theorem 1 and Corollary 1).
- [speculative] Proposed an experimental protocol (theory-agnostic tomography + checking non-embeddability under the univalence assumption) to test quantum theory experimentally while avoiding the tomographic-completeness loophole, arguing that multivalent simulations are implausible outside adversarial scenarios.

**Results summary:** This work formalizes how an effective probabilistic theory (GPT) can be explained by a more fundamental GPT via ε-simulations, generalizes Spekkens’ noncontextuality into the univalence requirement (unique simulating state/effect), and shows univalent simulations are linear embeddings. It proves classification results: unrestricted GPTs exactly embeddable into finite-dimensional complex quantum theory are precisely the special Euclidean Jordan-algebraic state spaces (including real/quaternionic/spin-factor cases and their direct sums). It establishes that every finite GPT admits approximate (measurement-univalent) classical/quantum simulation with dimension bounds, that decoherence/coarse-graining do not force multivalence, and provides quantitative, Bell-inequality-based constraints ruling out approximate quantum embeddings for theories with post-quantum self-correlations. The paper also proposes an experimental test of quantum theory based on theory-agnostic tomography and the univalence assumption.

**Performance claims:**
- [theoretical] Lemma 1: For any finite-dimensional GPT A and any ε>0 there exists a measurement-univalent ε-simulation by Cn (hence Qn) with n bounded by n ≤ O(ε^{-(dim A - 2)/2}) (explicit constant c depends only on ΩA).
- [theoretical] Theorem 2: An unrestricted GPT admits an exact embedding into finite-dimensional complex quantum theory iff it corresponds to a special Euclidean Jordan algebra (classification claim; no empirical data).
- [theoretical] Example / bound (gbit, geometry-based): any quantum embedding of the gbit must have error ε ≥ 0.101416 (approx.).
- [theoretical] Example / bound (gbit, Tsirelson-based): using CHSH/Tsirelson arguments the gbit cannot be ε-embedded into any Qn or Q∞ if ε ≤ 0.00915 (approx.).
- [theoretical] Theorem 3 (general bound): If A admits a Bell functional B with maximum over A A greater than the quantum maximum BQ, then any ε-embedding of A into quantum theory requires ε ≥ (BAA - BQ) / (4 |B| (1 + 2 R(A))).
- [theoretical] Lemma 12: If A can be ε-embedded into countably infinite-dimensional quantum theory Q∞, then for any δ>0 there exists finite n such that A can be (ε+δ)-embedded into Qn (approximation to finite dimensions).

## Limitations
- The experimental test proposed relies on the assumption of univalence (a generalization of noncontextuality); this assumption is plausible but novel and requires further justification and analysis.
- The nonlocality-based certification (Theorem 3 / steps 5.1–5.3) is not optimal and can be computationally demanding; searching for post-quantum Bell inequality violations on two copies of the system is in general computationally hard.
- The nonlocality test may fail to produce nontrivial bounds for some GPTs that are not embeddable into quantum theory; it is unknown whether the method yields nonzero bounds for all nonembeddable GPTs.
- The approach does not straightforwardly generalize to more than two parties: correlations from maximal tensor products for three or more systems can exceed standard quantum correlations, so the same techniques do not directly apply.
- It is unclear whether the absence of a Bell-functional violation (via the nonlocality test) guarantees embeddability in the limit ε → 0; this converse remains an open problem.
- Statistical aspects are underspecified: the paper leaves unspecified detailed statistical procedures for estimating the embedding error parameter ε from finite experimental data and for confidence statements about embeddability.
- Practical implementation details are incomplete: data-analysis methods (e.g., for highly restricted or high-dimensional experimental systems like BECs) need extension and tailoring; a concrete implementation is deferred to future work.
- There are topological and technical subtleties for embeddings into infinite-dimensional quantum systems; while Lemma 12 offers finite-dimensional approximations, practical ramifications are not exhaustively explored.
- Only special Euclidean Jordan algebras are exactly embeddable into finite-dimensional quantum theory; exotic Jordan structures (e.g., exceptional H3(O)) cannot be embedded — limiting the class of exactly embeddable unrestricted GPTs.
- Complete decoherence or coarse-graining processes that are physically realizable (completely positive) produce only classical theory or standard complex quantum theory with superselection rules; other Jordan-algebraic GPTs arise only via positive but not completely positive projections, which may be physically inaccessible.
- The paper assumes non-adversarial experimental scenarios and 'fair' selection of preparation/measurement sets; adversarial or engineered finetuning could produce multivalent simulations that the proposal is not designed to detect or rule out. [inferred]
- The computational complexity of the numerical search over bipartite states and Bell functionals (step 5.1) is not characterized and may be infeasible for many realistic experimental GPTs. [inferred]
- The Holevo-type classical simulations show that multivalent simulations can reproduce many GPTs; distinguishing plausible (univalent) from implausible (multivalent) explanations can be subtle in practice, especially when experimental constraints lead to restricted GPTs. [inferred]
- The proposed test gives only falsification under the additional univalence assumption; it does not provide an unconditional falsification of quantum theory from experimental data alone. [inferred]
## Open questions
- Is the assumption of univalence (generalized noncontextuality for arbitrary GPT simulations) a physically necessary constraint in non-adversarial experimental contexts? If so, can it be rigorously justified beyond plausibility arguments?
- Does failure to find any Bell-functional on A''A'' that violates the quantum bound imply that the effective theory A admits an embedding into quantum theory in the limit ε → 0?
- Does Theorem 3 produce nontrivial bounds for all GPTs that are not exactly embeddable into quantum theory, or are there counterexamples where post-quantum self-correlations do not exist but the GPT is still nonembeddable?
- Can the nonlocality-based certification technique be improved to yield tighter, more practical bounds on ε-embeddability, and can more efficient, possibly polynomial-time algorithms be found?
- How can the statistical estimation of ε (the embedding error) from finite experimental data be performed robustly, and how should hypothesis testing be formulated to give reliable confidence statements?
- Which singular (non-full-rank image) quantum channels produce effective GPTs that are embeddable, and which produce nonembeddable restricted GPTs? The paper states this is not currently known.
- Can the framework be generalized to multipartite scenarios with three or more copies, given that maximal-tensor-product correlations can exceed quantum correlations for >2 parties?
- What is the precise relation between Jordan-algebraic GPTs and Bell inequalities? Is there a deeper mathematical structure connecting embeddability, Jordan algebras, and Bell-nonlocality that can be exploited?
- Can the approach be extended to test embeddings into fundamentalt heiries other than classical or quantum (i.e., other GPTs), and how would one adapt the Bell-correlation technique in those cases?
- How to adapt the framework to the recently proposed 'GPT fragments' setting, where preparations may succeed with probability less than one?
- What are the resource-theoretic monotones or free operations appropriate for contextuality/univalence as a resource, and can one develop a full resource theory for multivalence?

**Future work:**
- Develop computationally more efficient and (ideally) optimal algorithms for certification of nonembeddability into quantum theory; improve the nonlocality-based tests and numerical search methods.
- Extend and formalize statistical analysis methods for fitting a GPT to experimental data and for estimating the embedding error parameter ε with confidence (accounting for finite statistics and experimental imperfections).
- Implement and test the proposed experimental scheme in concrete laboratory settings (e.g., analyze existing Bose-Einstein condensate data or design new experiments), adapting data analysis methods to restricted/high-dimensional systems.
- Investigate the plausibility assumption of univalence more deeply: provide conceptual analysis, derive theorems showing robustness of univalence, and develop a resource-theoretic perspective that formalizes when multivalence can or cannot arise naturally.
- Explore mathematical relations between Jordan algebras and Bell inequalities; study the singular role of special Euclidean Jordan algebras in embeddability and seek deeper structural insights in operator algebras.
- Generalize the approach to GPT fragments and other scenarios (e.g., multipartite generalizations, non-classical target theories) and study the limits of the technique for more general embeddings.
- Design and validate more practical algorithms and heuristics for step 5 (searching for Bell functionals) that are computationally tractable for realistic experimentally reconstructed GPTs.
- Provide a more detailed elaboration and implementation plan for the proposed application to Bose-Einstein condensate experiments, including necessary adaptations for restricted GPTs and collective measurements.

## Contradictions
- The paper contradicts the expectation that any generalized probabilistic theory (GPT) can be exactly represented within finite-dimensional quantum theory: it proves that only special Euclidean Jordan-algebraic GPTs admit exact unital embeddings.
- The paper provides explicit lower bounds on embedding error for post-quantum correlations (e.g., the gbit), contradicting the view that quantum approximations can always approach arbitrary GPTs arbitrarily closely without nontrivial error — some GPTs remain separated from quantum theory by finite ε thresholds.
