## Abstract summary
The paper presents a novel quantum algorithm to approximate the price of discrete floating-strike Asian options using a valuation-tree representation of the underlying. Paths are encoded into a qubit register with amplitudes set by state preparation to reflect path probabilities, and the payoff and an indicator for positive payoff are implemented via amplitude-based arithmetic and a gearbox-style unit step approximation. The construction is compatible with quantum amplitude estimation, potentially providing a quadratic speed-up over classical Monte Carlo methods while reducing circuit size by allowing different discretizations across time steps.
## Methodology
The paper develops a quantum-circuit-based algorithm to approximate the fair price of a discrete floating-strike Asian option using a valuation tree approximation of the underlying. Methodologically, the authors (1) discretize the underlying in time and value to obtain a scenario/valuation tree; (2) map each possible path through the tree to computational-basis states of an N-qubit state register (N = ceil(log2(product of per-time-node counts)); in the binomial example they use one qubit per time step); (3) load path probabilities into amplitudes via a quantum state-preparation operator P (or by simple Hadamard preparation when probabilities are equal); (4) encode scaled asset values Sk_norm(ti) in amplitudes using uniformly-controlled RY rotations (Möttönen-style construction) and build operators Gti/GM/G that produce amplitudes encoding the mean and terminal value information; (5) implement the positive-part (indicator) function q(x) using an amplitude-based gearbox circuit GbD that approximates a unit step via a Fourier-series-based series of RY rotations (parameterized by approximation level D and number of Fourier terms n_f); (6) combine amplitudes (addition/subtraction) and multi-controlled gates (Toffoli/multi-control X) to form a final target qubit whose measurement probability is proportional (after known rescaling) to the expected payoff; (7) the final circuit Z is compatible with Quantum Amplitude Estimation (AE) in principle, though AE was not executed in their simulations due to circuit-depth constraints; (8) to reduce depth and gate-count they split computations into subcircuits (e.g., separate circuits for g+ and g- and for Fourier terms) and recombine classically. The algorithm is validated on a simulated Rendleman–Bartter binomial model (risk-neutral up/down p=1/2) with classical precomputation of node values and scaling to [0,1], and results are obtained by executing the constructed circuits on the Qiskit simulator with large numbers of measurement shots to estimate output probabilities and compare to classical binomial pricing.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum state preparation (amplitude loading operator P), Uniformly-controlled rotations (Möttönen et al. style) for loading values, Amplitude arithmetic (addition/subtraction via controlled rotations), Gearbox / amplitude-based unit-step approximation (GbD) implemented via Fourier-series of RY rotations, Quantum Amplitude Estimation (AE) (algorithm is made compatible with AE; not executed in simulations)
**Frameworks:** Qiskit

**Experimental setup:** Simulations were executed on the Qiskit simulator (no physical QPU). Classical preprocessing computed the valuation tree node values and normalized them (Sk_norm := Sk / M with M = max Sk). For the binomial example, parameters used: T=1, S(0)=1, r=0, sigma=0.2, time-steps n (example n=5), u and d per standard binomial formulas. State-register mapping used one qubit per time step (n qubits). The gearbox approximation uses approximation level D and number of Fourier terms n_f; circuits were split into subcircuits per Fourier term and for g+ / g-. Per-path circuit evaluations used 1e7 shots; full-superposition pricing runs used 1e6 shots per circuit execution; ensembles of 100 independent circuit runs were collected for histograms.

**Dataset:** Synthetic/analytical valuation tree data generated from a Rendleman–Bartter (equal-probability binomial) model. No external financial dataset was used; node values for S(t_i) were computed classically from model parameters (T, n, sigma, r, S0).

## Findings
- [speculative] The authors develop a novel quantum algorithm to price discrete floating-strike Asian options using valuation trees by encoding entire paths into a qubit state register and loading path probabilities via quantum state preparation.
- [speculative] The algorithm composes the option expectation from three components—path probabilities f(xk), scaled payoff g(xk), and an indicator q(xk)—implemented as separate quantum subroutines and recombined into amplitudes of an output qubit.
- [supported] The authors implemented and simulated the algorithm (Qiskit simulator) on a binomial model with n=5 time steps, demonstrating the approach on a small example and reporting numerical results.
- [supported] Encoding the underlying as probabilities (amplitude encoding) rather than bitwise values allows different discretizations per time step and can reduce required qubits per time step in the illustrated example.
- [speculative] The positive-part (indicator) function is implemented via an amplitude-based unit-step-like construction (gearbox/Fourier approximation) that maps RY rotation angles to a qubit probability approximating 0/1 for the sign test.
- [supported] The authors split the global circuit into subcircuits (e.g., per Fourier term and g+/g−) to reduce circuit depth and to avoid some arithmetic scaling factors, and provide resource counts for these subcircuits.
- [supported] Simulation experiments explored approximation level D (gearbox depth) and number of Fourier terms n_f, showing trade-offs: higher D reduces bias near zero but requires more Fourier terms (higher circuit complexity).
- [supported] The paper provides explicit resource estimates (cX-gate counts and total qubit widths) for various n (2..6) and parameter settings (D and n_f), demonstrating linear/quasi-exponential cost drivers for uniformly controlled rotations.
- [speculative] The proposed circuit is in principle compatible with quantum amplitude estimation (AE), which would yield a theoretical quadratic speed-up over classical Monte Carlo (O(1/Ns) vs O(1/sqrt(Ns))).
- [supported] In practice the authors did not run AE due to circuit depth restrictions of their simulator/hardware constraints; amplitude estimation was not demonstrated experimentally in this work.
- [speculative] The method is generalizable to other path-dependent derivative pricing problems (e.g., cliquet options) and could reduce qubit requirements relative to fully bit-based encodings.
- [supported] Each module of the algorithm (state preparation/probability loading; payoff arithmetic; positive-part indicator) can be executed individually on existing hardware or simulators, while the full end-to-end circuit is currently too deep for noisy devices.
- [speculative] Using amplitude-based arithmetics and an amplitude-based unit step may lead to smaller quantum circuits compared to bit-encoding approaches for some valuation-tree-based derivatives.

## Limitations
- Evaluations performed on a quantum simulator only; not demonstrated on real quantum hardware (author-stated).
- Cannot employ amplitude estimation (AE) in the presented simulations due to circuit depth restrictions of the simulator (author-stated).
- Construction of the probability-loading operator P (quantum state preparation) for arbitrary distributions requires an exponential number of gates in general; the paper assumes an appropriate P is available (author-stated).
- Approximation of the positive-part indicator uses a gearbox/Fourier approximation that introduces bias and requires tuning (approximation level D and number of Fourier terms n_f); trade-offs between approximation error and circuit complexity are observed (author-stated).
- Circuit depth and two-qubit (cX) gate counts are high for nontrivial instances; uniformly controlled rotations dominate resource counts and may exceed current NISQ device capabilities (author-stated).
- The method relies on precomputing and classically supplying the valuation-tree node values S(ti) and transition probabilities (author-stated).
- Simplifying modelling assumptions used in experiments (zero interest rate, assumed risk-neutral measure Q = P for clarity) reduce realism of demonstrated experiments (author-stated).
- Assumes valuation tree structure where every node in a previous level is connected to every node in the next level (author-stated assumption that limits class of trees directly handled).
