## Abstract summary
This thesis (Chapter 2) analyzes approximation and generalization properties of parameterized quantum circuits (PQCs). It proves that, with simple input rescaling, PQCs can uniformly approximate functions in Lp, C0 and Sobolev Hk spaces, and derives generalization bounds that relate discretized Sobolev-type loss functions (including derivative information) to target distances such as Lp and supremum norms. The work explains why differential machine learning losses can improve generalization for PQC models and illustrates the results with numerical experiments.
## Methodology
The chapter develops a theoretical and numerical study of the approximation and generalization capacities of parameterized quantum circuits (PQC). Theoretical methodology: PQCs are modeled as expectation values f_theta(x)=<0|U^†(x;theta) M U(x;theta)|0> and expressed as generalized trigonometric series. The authors define a universal Hamiltonian family (tensor-product Pauli Hamiltonians as an example) and prove universality/approximation theorems in multiple function spaces (Lp for 1<=p<infty, C0 on compact subsets, and Sobolev H^k) by connecting PQC representable truncated Fourier/Fejér means to classical Fejér/Cèsaro summation theorems and Sobolev extension theorems. They derive generalization bounds relating discrete empirical losses that include derivative terms (DML / discretized H^k losses) to continuous target distances (L^p, C^0, H^k) using Rademacher complexity, Talagrand's lemma, and compact Sobolev embeddings (Rellich–Kondrachov). Practical recommendations (methodological prescriptions) include min–max feature scaling of inputs to the interval [-π/2, π/2]^N so PQCs can approximate in the stronger senses. Numerical methodology: small-scale numerical simulations illustrate theory. A layered PQC architecture with data-encoding Rx(omega x) rotations and variational Ry(theta) rotations per qubit is trained (Pauli-Z measurements) to approximate a 1D synthetic target function. Training experiments compare loss choices (mean-squared ℓ_{l2} vs. derivative-inclusive ℓ_{h1}) and different input normalizations, using the Adam classical optimizer, multiple trials, and plotting percentile bands to illustrate bias/variance and boundary/Gibbs-like effects.

**Algorithms used:** Parameterized Quantum Circuits (PQC), Data-reuploading / repeated data-encoding via Rx(omega * x), Fejér / Cèsaro summation (theoretical construction), Parameter-shift rule (for derivative evaluation, discussed), Adam optimizer (classical)

**Experimental setup:** Classical simulation of PQC experiments (simulator/software unspecified). Architecture: layered PQC with per-qubit Rx(omega * x) data-encoding gates and Ry(theta) variational gates; measurements in Pauli-Z basis. Experiments used 10 training sample points (plus derivative values when applicable), training repeated 100 times, 100 epochs of Adam optimizer per run. Loss functions compared: empirical l2 (mean squared) and h1 (sum of MSE on function values and first derivatives). Input normalization variants tested: min-max scaling to [-π/2, π/2], [-π, π], and [-2π, 2π].

**Dataset:** Synthetic 1D function: f*(x) = x / (2π) on domain x in [-π, π] (and normalized variants). Training points uniformly sampled; in derivative experiments both function values and derivative values are provided at training points.

## Findings
- [speculative] Parameterized quantum circuits (PQCs) can be expressed as generalized trigonometric series whose accessible frequency spectrum is determined by the data-encoding Hamiltonian and whose coefficients are controlled by trainable gates.
- [speculative] For a suitable ("universal") family of encoding Hamiltonians, PQC model families are universal approximators in Lp([0,2π]^N) for all 1 ≤ p < ∞ (Theorem 2.2).
- [speculative] For functions continuous on a domain U compactly contained in [0,2π]^N, PQCs (with the stated encoding assumptions) can approximate the function uniformly (C0 convergence) to arbitrary precision (Theorem 2.3).
- [speculative] PQCs can approximate target functions in Sobolev spaces H^k (i.e., approximating function values and derivatives up to order k) under the same encoding/universality conditions (Theorem 2.4).
- [speculative] A simple min–max feature scaling (per-dimension linear map) that maps input features into the interval [−π/2, π/2]^N improves PQC expressivity and enables the above approximation results in practice (recommended normalization strategy).
- [speculative] New generalization bounds are derived that relate empirical losses based on derivatives (discretized Sobolev losses Dh_k) to continuous target distances (Lp, Hk, and C0) for PQC model classes built from truncated trigonometric series (Theorems 2.5–2.7).
- [speculative] In particular, minimizing the empirical Dh_k loss (sum of squared errors of function values and derivatives up to order k) yields control over the C0 (supremum) norm in the regime k > N/2 (uniform convergence), via compact embeddings (Rellich–Kondrachov).
- [speculative] Theoretical results explain why differential machine learning (DML) losses that include derivative terms (ℓ_hk) can be consistent with stronger target metrics (e.g., C0) and thus can lead to better approximations for some problems.
- [supported] Numerical simulations (1D experiments approximating the linear function f*(x)=x/2π with a small PQC) show that: (a) normalizing inputs to [−π/2, π/2] gives markedly better approximation than larger input ranges, (b) using the ℓ_h1 loss reduces variance and can reduce bias under the appropriate normalization, and (c) using ℓ_h1 under an inappropriate normalization (e.g., [−π,π]) can worsen approximation of function values because the model balances function and derivative errors.
- [speculative] The discretized Sobolev (ℓ_hk) empirical loss requires more data and more circuit evaluations (scales with the number of derivatives), implying a practical curse-of-dimensionality for high input dimension and high k (exponential growth in mixed derivatives).
- [supported] The work cites and connects to earlier empirical DML successes (e.g., prior 7D Basket option example) to motivate that derivative-based losses can be helpful in practice.

**Results summary:** This chapter develops a theoretical and partly empirical analysis of the approximation and generalization capacities of parameterized quantum circuits (PQCs). Building on prior work that represents PQC expectation values as generalized trigonometric series, the authors prove that, for suitable ("universal") encoding Hamiltonians, PQCs can approximate functions in Lp (1 ≤ p < ∞), uniformly approximate continuous functions on compact subsets (C0), and approximate functions in Sobolev spaces H^k (including derivatives) to arbitrary precision. They show that a practical per-dimension min–max scaling of inputs to [−π/2, π/2] is effective to meet the domain assumptions of the theorems. The chapter also derives new generalization bounds linking empirical discretized Sobolev losses (which include derivative information) to target distances (Lp, Hk, C0), giving a theoretical rationale for differential machine learning (DML) losses. Small-scale numerical simulations in 1D corroborate that proper normalization and derivative-aware losses can improve approximation quality, while improper normalization can harm it; the authors note practical costs (data and circuit evaluations) and dimensionality limitations for derivative-based losses.

## Limitations
- Universality proofs rely on a universal Hamiltonian family and frequency-spectrum coverage; practical encodings may not realize the required frequency sets.
- Theoretical universality assumes trainable circuit blocks can implement arbitrary global unitaries (or prepare arbitrary states), which in practice may require circuit depth that scales exponentially with the number of qubits.
- Results are proved on domains mapped to [0, 2π]^N (periodic setting); non-periodic target functions require careful extension and can suffer Gibbs-like boundary effects.
- To get uniform (C0) or Sobolev (Hk) guarantees the input must be normalized (min–max scaling to [−π/2, π/2]^N); inappropriate normalization can produce very poor approximations.
- The generalization bounds connecting ℓ_hk (discrete Sobolev-style losses) to target distances require access to derivative data; if derivatives are unavailable the bounds/utilities do not apply.
- The ℓ_hk loss and its evaluation impose high data and computational demands: the number of derivative terms grows combinatorially with k and N, and derivative evaluation (e.g., parameter-shift) requires many PQC runs (scales as I(1+2N) for H1).
- The C0 generalization guarantee needs derivatives of order k > N/2; for high-dimensional inputs this is a strong requirement.
- Generalization bounds shown relate empirical risk to population risk but do not by themselves guarantee both tend to zero (i.e., they do not automatically ensure convergence of the target distance to zero without further conditions).
- The presented numerical experiments are simple (linear target, small circuits, few training points); scalability and empirical performance on realistic/high-dimensional tasks are not demonstrated.
- [inferred] The asymptotic theoretical results may hide large finite-resource gaps: achieving the frequencies, coefficients and state preparations required may be impractical on noisy, shallow NISQ devices.
- [inferred] The proofs and bounds depend on choices of frequency set Ω and bounds B, ˜B determined by encoding and measurement; selecting these in practice for complex targets is nontrivial and not automated.
- [inferred] Use of parameter-shift for derivative evaluation may be prohibitively expensive for larger circuits or high-dimensional inputs, reducing practical applicability.
- [inferred] The reliance on periodic extensions and Fejér summation may limit straightforward applicability to problems with irregular domains or with strong nonperiodic features (e.g., discontinuities).
