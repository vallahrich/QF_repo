## Abstract summary
This preprint applies the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) combined with Zero Noise Extrapolation (ZNE) to an 88-variable, multi-objective carbon credit portfolio optimization problem for the Brazilian Cerrado, encoding carbon, biodiversity and social metrics into a QUBO with a cardinality constraint (k=28). Seven independent runs on IBM Quantum hardware (ibm_torino and ibm_fez) using a warm-start XY-mixer QAOA (p=1) and ZNE (noise scales λ=1,2,3) produced an extrapolated mean portfolio score of 58.47±6.98, a 31.6% improvement over a Greedy baseline (44.42) with strong statistical significance (p=0.0009), and demonstrated temporal stability across calibration cycles.
## Methodology
The study formulates a multi-objective carbon-credit portfolio selection as a QUBO with linear terms for carbon, biodiversity and social impact and quadratic terms encoding spatial/biodiversity/social synergies and a quadratic penalty for a cardinality constraint (n = 88, k = 28). The QUBO is sparsified (threshold |Qij|>=0.01), globally scaled by Qmax, and converted into a cost Hamiltonian for QAOA. A warm-start was used (initial computational basis state set to a Greedy heuristic solution) and an XY-type mixer preserving the total excitation number was implemented to respect cardinality. A fixed-parameter, single-layer QAOA (p = 1) with parameters γ = 0.05 and β = 0.20 was executed on IBM Quantum Heron-generation processors (ibm_torino, ibm_fez) for three noise-amplification scale factors λ ∈ {1,2,3}. Controlled noise amplification used two-qubit gate folding to implement ZNE; each λ job used 8,192 shots. Extrapolation to the zero-noise limit was performed using linear, quadratic and Richardson extrapolation and uncertainty quantified with a nonparametric percentile bootstrap (B = 100, seed = 42). Classical baselines (Greedy heuristic, Simulated Annealing with fixed CPU budget, Random Search) were run for comparison. Statistical inference used paired one-sided t-tests, Wilcoxon signed-rank as confirmatory analysis, effect sizes (Cohen’s d), bootstrap CIs, and leave-one-out sensitivity checks. The pipeline (QUBO construction → warm-start QAOA p=1 → measurements at λ=1,2,3 → ZNE extrapolation → statistical analysis) was made reproducible with public code and data, IBM job IDs and recorded backend calibration metadata.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA), Zero Noise Extrapolation (ZNE), XY-mixer (cardinality-preserving mixer), Gate folding (for ZNE noise amplification), Greedy heuristic (warm-start and baseline), Simulated Annealing (baseline), Random Search (baseline)
**Frameworks:** Qiskit, Qiskit Runtime, Qiskit Aer, NumPy, SciPy, Pandas, Matplotlib

**Experimental setup:** Executed on IBM Quantum Heron-generation superconducting processors (ibm_torino — Heron r1 and ibm_fez — Heron r2). Problem width: 88 active qubits. QAOA ansatz: p = 1, γ = 0.05, β = 0.20, warm-started from a Greedy solution and using an XY-type mixer. Three noise-scaling factors λ = 1,2,3 implemented via two-qubit gate folding; 8,192 shots per λ (24,576 shots per ZNE run). Seven independent runs performed across Jan 17–Feb 2, 2026 (total shots 172,032). QUBO sparsified with threshold 0.01; global scaling Q' = Q/Qmax with Qmax = 47.23; penalty weight λ_penalty = 100 before scaling. Transpilation level 3 with SABRE routing. Circuit depths ≈ 190–210 gates at λ=1 and ≈ 600–750 gates for folded λ=2,3.
## Experiment details
### Input
Empirical environmental dataset (not financial): initial pool 246 municipalities reduced to 128 by data completeness then to n = 88 by ranking normalized carbon sequestration scores. Per-municipality normalized scores in [0,1] for carbon (ci), biodiversity (bi), and social impact (si). Three 88×88 synergy/adjacency matrices: territorial adjacency A (binary), biodiversity synergy Bsyn, social synergy Ssyn. Sources: MapBiomas, GEDI LiDAR, PRODES, Brazilian demographic census, INCRA, ICMBio; biomass model from atlas-biomassa-goias repository. Preprocessing: min–max normalization to [0,1], sparsification of QUBO terms with |Qij|<0.01 removed, QUBO global scaling by Qmax, cardinality constraint encoded as quadratic penalty. Final instance: n=88, k=28; retained 132 non-zero QUBO coefficients (88 diagonal + 44 off-diagonal).

### Process
1) Construct multi-objective QUBO: combine weighted linear terms (wC=0.33, wB=0.33, wS=0.34), quadratic synergy terms scaled by λC=0.15, λB=0.25, λS=0.20, and quadratic penalty for cardinality with λ_penalty=100 (then global scaling by Qmax=47.23). 2) Sparsify QUBO (threshold 0.01). 3) Warm-start: initialize state |x_greedy⟩ from Greedy heuristic. 4) Prepare QAOA ansatz with p=1, mixer = XY-type (cardinality-preserving), parameters γ=0.05, β=0.20. 5) Transpile (level 3, SABRE) and map to 88 active qubits on selected backend. 6) Execute circuits at three noise-amplification levels λ ∈ {1,2,3} via two-qubit gate folding; collect 8,192 shots per λ. 7) For each run, compute expectation value of the cost observable; apply extrapolation methods (linear, quadratic regression across λ, and Richardson combination) to estimate E(λ=0). 8) Quantify uncertainty with percentile bootstrap (B=100). 9) Compare mitigated mean scores to classical baselines (Greedy, SA, Random Search) using paired one-sided t-test and Wilcoxon signed-rank, compute Cohen's d and bootstrap CIs; perform LOO sensitivity and Jaccard overlap analyses on resulting portfolios.

### Output
Outputs were expectation-value estimates of the portfolio objective extrapolated to zero noise (per-run E(λ=0) via linear/quadratic/Richardson ZNE) and diagnostic raw measurements at λ={1,2,3}. Reported metrics: mean ± SD of ZNE-mitigated portfolio scores across 7 runs (primary endpoint), raw QAOA scores, valid-shot (feasible) rate (% of bitstrings satisfying cardinality constraint), Jaccard similarity (overlap) with Greedy portfolio, statistical tests (paired one-sided t-test p-value, Wilcoxon signed-rank p-value), effect size (Cohen’s d), bootstrap 95% CIs, and per-run job metrics (QPU times). Classical baselines: Greedy score (44.42), Simulated Annealing (fixed 2.0 s budget), Random Search (10k iterations). Results delivered as aggregated numeric summaries and selected mode bitstrings (mode at λ=1 used for discrete portfolio composition).

## Findings
- [supported] QAOA combined with Zero Noise Extrapolation (ZNE) outperformed a deterministic Greedy baseline on the tested carbon-credit portfolio instance: mean mitigated score 58.47 ± 6.98 vs Greedy 44.42.
- [supported] The improvement reported is 31.6% on average and is statistically significant (one-sided paired t-test p = 0.0009; Cohen's d = 2.01).
- [supported] QAOA+ZNE exceeded the Greedy baseline in all seven independent hardware runs (7/7 success rate under the experimental protocol).
- [supported] Raw (unmitigated) QAOA measurements averaged 43.55 ± 1.54 (≈98.0% of Greedy) and did not consistently beat the Greedy baseline without ZNE.
- [supported] Zero Noise Extrapolation was executed with gate-folding noise scale factors λ ∈ {1,2,3}, 8,192 shots per scale, and extrapolation via linear, quadratic and Richardson methods; quadratic extrapolation produced the primary endpoint (58.47).
- [supported] A conservative linear extrapolation produced a lower-bound mean estimate of 49.12 ± 1.82, which still exceeds the Greedy baseline (≈10.6% improvement).
- [supported] The feasible-shot (valid portfolio) rate averaged ≈15.9% across runs (shots satisfying the cardinality constraint k = 28).
- [supported] Experiments were performed on IBM Heron processors (ibm_torino and ibm_fez) over a 17-day period; no statistically significant monotonic temporal trend in ZNE scores was observed, supporting temporal stability under the tested conditions.
- [supported] Problem formulation and hardware choices: n = 88 candidate municipalities, target cardinality k = 28, QAOA depth p = 1, warm-start initialization from the Greedy solution, XY-mixer to preserve cardinality, and sparsification of QUBO to reduce circuit depth.
- [supported] Classical baselines tested: Greedy (44.42), Simulated Annealing under a short computational budget (41.96), Random Search mean (18.54).
- [speculative] The authors propose that the workflow (QAOA+ZNE with warm-start and sparsification) provides a scalable methodological template for high-precision environmental conservation policies.
- [speculative] The recurring selection of certain municipalities (e.g., Chapadão do Céu) is interpreted as evidence that the quantum approach captures territorial synergies missed by myopic classical heuristics.
- [supported] ZNE produces extrapolated expectation-value estimates rather than a guaranteed discrete, noise-free bitstring portfolio; reported portfolios are derived from mode valid bitstrings at base noise level.

**Results summary:** The preprint reports an empirical study applying QAOA with Zero Noise Extrapolation to a real-world multi-objective carbon-credit portfolio selection problem (n=88, k=28) using IBM superconducting quantum hardware (ibm_torino, ibm_fez). Over seven independent hardware executions (24,576 shots per run for ZNE), ZNE-extrapolated QAOA expectation values (quadratic extrapolation) yielded a mean portfolio score of 58.47 ± 6.98, which the authors report as a statistically significant 31.6% improvement over a deterministic Greedy baseline (44.42) with p = 0.0009 and large effect size (Cohen's d = 2.01). The protocol used warm-start initialization, p=1 QAOA with an XY-mixer, QUBO sparsification, and gate-folding ZNE at λ={1,2,3} with 8,192 shots per scale. A conservative linear extrapolation still exceeded Greedy (mean 49.12). The authors emphasize reproducibility (job IDs, public repo) and interpret results as evidence of empirical quantum utility for this specific environmental optimization instance under NISQ constraints, while noting they do not claim formal quantum advantage.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** not-applicable

The authors explicitly refrain from claiming formal quantum advantage. They present 'empirical quantum utility' for the tested problem instance—i.e., ZNE-extrapolated QAOA expectation values outperform specific classical heuristics (Greedy, short-budget SA) on this instance and hardware—but do not claim a provable or general quantum computational advantage. The manuscript acknowledges the difference between empirical utility in a constrained experimental regime and formal complexity-theoretic quantum advantage.
## Limitations
- Does not demonstrate formal quantum advantage; results show empirical quantum utility relative to the tested classical heuristics only.
- Zero Noise Extrapolation (ZNE) produces extrapolated expectation values rather than directly yielding a noise-free discrete portfolio; final portfolio composition is derived from the most frequent valid bitstring at the base noise level (λ = 1).
- QAOA was run with a single shallow layer (p = 1) and fixed parameters (γ = 0.05, β = 0.20); a full variational optimization loop on hardware was avoided, limiting circuit expressivity and generalizability.
- Warm-start initialization used the Greedy solution; while simulation ablation was performed, a full hardware ablation (warm-start removed on device) was not executed.
- Sample size and experimental scope are limited: seven independent hardware runs (n = 7) across two IBM Heron backends—constrained by hardware access and cost.
- No comparisons were made against solver-grade classical optimizers (e.g., Gurobi, CPLEX), so the absolute optimality gap is not quantified.
- Feasible-shot rate (percentage of measured bitstrings satisfying the cardinality constraint) is low (~15.9% on average), meaning reliance on large shot budgets to obtain sufficient valid samples.
- ZNE increases estimator variance and is sensitive to the chosen extrapolation model; quadratic extrapolation fits three points exactly (zero residual degrees of freedom), making it sensitive to noise and outliers.
- Sparsification of the QUBO (discarding |Qij| < 0.01) and global scaling introduce small approximation errors (~<1%) and may alter problem structure.
- Results depend on an empirically calibrated QUBO formulation (penalty weights, λpenalty, λC, λB, λS) and on parameter choices derived from small-instance calibration; these design choices may not generalize across instances.
- [inferred] Gate folding was applied only to two-qubit gates (CNOT and RZZ); other noise sources (single-qubit errors, readout errors, crosstalk) may not be fully amplified or corrected by the ZNE procedure.
- [inferred] Study is limited to IBM Heron superconducting processors (ibm_torino, ibm_fez); results may not transfer directly to other hardware platforms or qubit technologies.
- [inferred] Use of warm-start initialization could mask how much of the performance gain is due to quantum exploration versus starting-state bias on real hardware.
- [inferred] The ZNE extrapolation produces expectation-value estimates but does not provide a principled way to extrapolate per-variable selection probabilities or to propagate extrapolated expectations into a discrete, verifiable portfolio without additional assumptions.
