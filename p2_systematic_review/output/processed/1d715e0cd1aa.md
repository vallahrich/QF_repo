## Abstract summary
This preprint proposes HQFS, an end-to-end hybrid quantum–classical pipeline that uses a variational quantum circuit with a small classical head to jointly forecast next-step returns and a volatility proxy, maps constrained portfolio allocation to a QUBO solved by quantum annealing (with a classical QUBO fallback), and signs each rebalance with a post-quantum signature for auditability. Experiments on an S&P 500 dataset report reduced return and volatility prediction errors and improved out-of-sample Sharpe and lower maximum drawdown compared with tuned classical baselines.
## Methodology
The authors propose HQFS, an end-to-end hybrid quantum–classical pipeline that (1) pre-processes daily OHLCV market data into normalized sequence inputs (log-returns, rolling volatility proxy computed over window W, lagged features, volume and range features), using chronological train/val/test splits, winsorization (training quantiles) and feature standardization (training statistics); (2) embeds each length-L sequence into a compact quantum feature vector via a variational quantum circuit (VQC) U(X,θ) that produces Q observable measurements z_q = <0|U† O_q U|0>, with trainable parameters θ (the circuit is differentiable via the parameter-shift rule) and a small classical head f_ϕ that jointly predicts next-step return and next-step volatility proxy; the joint training minimizes an MSE-style loss L_pred = MSE_return + β MSE_volatility + λ||θ||_2^2 over mini-batches and epochs (optimizer unspecified, learning-rate symbol α noted), with hyperparameter tuning on validation (30 trials, early stopping) and repeated seeds (S=5); (3) at each rebalance date Treb, selects a tradable universe St (top-M by liquidity and signal), forms expected returns μ̂_t and covariance Σ̂_t (from predicted returns and volatilities plus recent correlations), and encodes discrete portfolio weights with B binary bits per asset using a normalized binary decoding wi = (1/Z) sum_{b=0}^{B-1} 2^{-b} x_{i,b}; (4) maps the constrained mean–variance objective (min w^T Σ̂ w − η μ̂^T w, 1^T w = 1, w≥0) into a QUBO x^T Q x plus penalties for constraint violations (penalty weight ρ_c), solves the QUBO via a quantum annealer interface if available or classical QUBO heuristics (simulated annealing) as a fallback, then decodes the binary solution to real weights; (5) creates an auditable, post-quantum-signed record per rebalance by hashing model state and allocation (mt = Hash(θ,ϕ,w_t,t)) and signing with a post-quantum private key σ_t for later public verification. Experimental protocol includes multiple baselines (ARIMA, LSTM, GRU, TCN, Transformer for forecasting; EW, MV-PG, SA-QUBO for allocations), transaction-cost-aware backtests, solver timing comparisons and ablation studies.

**Algorithms used:** Variational Quantum Circuit (VQC), Parameter-shift rule (for VQC gradients), Quantum annealing (QUBO formulation), Classical QUBO solver / Simulated annealing (SA-QUBO), Projected gradient mean–variance optimizer (MV-PG), ARIMA, LSTM, GRU, TCN, Transformer encoder, Post-quantum digital signature (hash-and-sign)

**Dataset:** Kaggle S&P 500 stock data (camnugent) — daily OHLCV time series for multiple S&P 500 constituents. Primary series: adjusted close (or close if adjusted unavailable).

## Findings
- [supported] HQFS (hybrid VQC + classical head) achieves better joint forecasting accuracy for next-step return and a volatility proxy compared to multiple classical baselines on the used S&P500 dataset.
- [supported] The paper reports specific forecast improvements: return MAE 0.0107±0.0002 and volatility MAE 0.0086±0.0002 for HQFS on the test split, with volatility correlation 0.63.
- [supported] Joint training of next-step return and volatility provides consistent moment inputs (ˆµt, ˆΣt) to the downstream optimizer, improving decision stability versus separate/mismatched estimates.
- [supported] Mapping the discrete allocation problem to a QUBO enables use of a quantum-annealing interface while retaining a classical QUBO solver as a reproducible deployment fallback.
- [supported] In the reported backtest, HQFS-produced allocations yield higher risk-adjusted performance (reported Sharpe = 0.91±0.03) and lower drawdown (MDD = 0.192±0.005) versus baselines, after transaction costs.

- [supported] Ablation experiments indicate the VQC encoder contributes materially to improved volatility correlation (0.63 vs 0.58) and higher Sharpe when compared to a classical encoder alternative.
- [speculative] The paper positions HQFS as 'quantum-ready' for future hardware (quantum annealers and larger VQCs) and suggests improved scaling and runtime when such hardware becomes available.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The authors report empirical improvements using a VQC encoder and an annealer interface in their experiments, but the paper does not demonstrate a general, hardware-level quantum advantage on large-scale, production quantum hardware. The approach is hybrid with a classical fallback (classical QUBO solvers and heuristics), results are dataset-limited, and timing/solver gains appear experiment-specific; therefore claims of practical quantum advantage are suggestive/speculative rather than demonstrably proven.
## Limitations
- Quantum annealer availability and practical quantum hardware limitations; pipeline relies on a classical QUBO fallback when annealing hardware is not available.
- Not evaluated under explicit stress regimes or high-volatility market windows (experiments are limited to the chosen historical split).
- Covariance estimation is basic in the current study and may be ill-conditioned at larger scale; shrinkage/factor methods were not implemented.
- Trading-realistic constraints (sector caps, lot sizes, slippage, market impact) were not included in the optimization experiments.
- Post-quantum signature choices and audit reporting were not exhaustively evaluated; only a lightweight signing prototype was implemented.
- [inferred] Limited dataset and experimental scope: results are reported on a Kaggle S&P 500 dataset and backtests rather than live trading or multiple diverse market regimes.
- [inferred] Scalability concerns: QUBO dimensionality grows with top-M and bits-per-asset (MB), so scaling to much larger universes may increase solve time and memory demand.
- [inferred] Unclear quantum advantage boundary: it's not established for which problem sizes or constraint mixes the annealer outperforms classical QUBO heuristics reliably.
- [inferred] Impact of quantum hardware noise and VQC training instability on forecasting performance was not studied (simulations/idealized emulation appear used).
- [inferred] Dependence on hyperparameter tuning and sensitivity: stability across seeds is reported but sensitivity to many design choices (VQC depth, bit budget B, penalty weights) is not fully characterized.
- [inferred] Covariance construction from predicted volatilities plus recent correlations may misestimate joint risk, especially during regime shifts.
- [inferred] Regulatory and operational integration complexity: while signing adds auditability, practical compliance integration (workflow, key management, verification) was not demonstrated end-to-end.
## Open questions
- How does the VQC forecasting advantage scale with universe size, longer lookbacks, and more heterogeneous assets?
- Under what market regimes (stress, crises, low liquidity) does the hybrid VQC + QUBO pipeline maintain its benefits versus classical baselines?
- What is the practical quantum annealer threshold (problem size, constraint complexity) beyond which annealing yields a meaningful runtime or solution-quality benefit over classical QUBO solvers?
- How robust are allocations when the predicted returns and volatilities are mis-specified or when prediction uncertainty increases?
- How should covariance matrices be constructed from VQC-predicted moments to remain well-conditioned and robust (e.g., which shrinkage/factor models work best)?
- How do realistic trading constraints (sector caps, lot sizes, minimum fills, slippage, market impact) affect QUBO formulation size, solve time, and solution quality?
- How sensitive are results to VQC design choices (embedding, depth D, number of measured observables Q) and to classical head architecture?
- What is the impact of real quantum hardware noise on VQC feature quality and on end-to-end performance, and what mitigation strategies are effective?
- How should penalty weights and bit-budget (B) be chosen or adapted to balance allocation granularity, enforceability of constraints, and solver tractability?
- Which post-quantum signature schemes provide the best trade-off between signature size, verification cost, and regulatory acceptance for auditability?
- How to operationalize key management, replay protection, and long-term verifiability for signed allocation records in production settings?
- Can the QUBO mapping be extended to support dynamic or multi-period optimization (e.g., turnover-aware or transaction-cost-aware multi-period rebalancing) efficiently?
- What are the reproducibility and portability concerns when moving between different classical QUBO solvers and annealing hardware (consistency of solutions, seed dependence)?
- How does the pipeline perform in live execution with market microstructure, partial fills, and latency constraints?

**Future work:**
- Test stress regimes (high-volatility windows) and implement drift-aware retraining to keep signals stable under change.
- Strengthen covariance estimation with shrinkage and factor updates so the QUBO stays well-conditioned at larger scale.
- Add trading-realistic constraints (sector caps, lot sizes, slippage) and measure their impact on QUBO size and solve time.
- Evaluate more post-quantum signature choices and generate a compact audit report that ties each allocation to its input window, forecasts, and solver settings.
