## Abstract summary
The paper proposes a novel Quantum-Enhanced Neural Network (QENN) architecture that integrates a quantum layer, classical layer, and a quantum-classical interface to enable hybrid quantum-classical algorithms based on quantum neural networks (QNNs). The design is evaluated for optimization tasks and claims improvements in speed, accuracy, and scalability, with potential applications in finance, logistics, and drug discovery, while noting current hardware and noise limitations.
## Methodology
The paper describes a conceptual, design-oriented methodology for building a hybrid quantum-classical system (Quantum-Enhanced Neural Network, QENN). The methodological workflow begins with problem definition and complexity assessment, followed by the co-design of a quantum neural network (QNN) layer and a complementary classical neural network (CNN) layer. A dedicated Quantum–Classical Interface (QCI) is specified to handle data encoding/decoding, quantum state preparation, measurement, and noise mitigation; the architecture supports iterative hybrid training loops in which quantum circuits produce features or cost estimates and classical optimizers update parameters. Training and optimization methods discussed include hybrid variational approaches (VQAs), quantum gradient descent and quantum backpropagation for quantum parameters, and standard classical optimizers (gradient descent, SGD, Adam, RMSprop) for classical parameters; circuit compilation, quantum error mitigation/correction, and resource-aware design (qubit count, circuit depth, shots) are explicitly noted as design considerations. Performance evaluation is described at a conceptual level (accuracy, convergence speed, computational efficiency, standard ML metrics and quantum-state fidelities), together with iterative refinement, scalability assessment and implementation/validation on either quantum simulators or physical QPUs via a quantum development framework.
