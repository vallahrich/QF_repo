## Abstract summary
This review surveys Markov chains and random walks, tracing their development from Pearson's 1905 formulation to modern quantum random walks, and discusses recurrence properties across dimensions. It summarizes applications in knowledge representation learning, Markov Decision Processes for reinforcement learning, neuroimaging-based brain network analysis, and waste-recycling path planning, and highlights future directions linking quantum computing and big data to more advanced random-walk models.
## Methodology
This paper is a non‑empirical literature review and theoretical analysis. The author surveys historical and recent work on Markov chains and random walks, presents analytic criteria for classifying states (recurrent vs. transient) based on return probability sums (Gii = sum_n P_nii), and derives/provides arguments for recurrence/transience in various settings (one‑ and two‑dimensional symmetric random walks, three‑dimensional transience via asymptotic probability bounds and Green's formula, higher dimensions, and asymmetric walks). The paper also analyzes the absorbing‑barrier random walk by setting up and solving recurrence relations for absorption probabilities. In addition to the theoretical derivations, the paper reviews applications across domains (knowledge representation via random‑walk + LSTM embeddings, Markov Decision Processes in reinforcement learning, neuroimaging/brain network embedding via random walks, and random‑walk based path planning for waste recycling) and cites related algorithmic developments in quantum walks (Szegedy, Magniez). No computational experiments, datasets, or hardware/simulator studies are reported; the methodology is based on literature synthesis and mathematical argumentation.

## Findings
- [supported] Markov chains and random walks are fundamental memoryless probabilistic models used to describe transitions between states and underpin many applications in natural language processing, finance, and bioinformatics.
- [supported] A state in a Markov chain is recurrent if the sum of its return probabilities diverges (G_ii = ∞) and transient if that sum is finite (G_ii < ∞); communicating states share recurrence/transience status.
- [supported] One-dimensional and two-dimensional symmetric random walks are recurrent; three-dimensional symmetric random walks are transient (classical mathematical result).
- [supported] One-dimensional asymmetric random walks are recurrent only in the unbiased case (p = q = 1/2); biased 1D walks are transient.
- [supported] Random-walk-based techniques underpin practical algorithms and applications such as PageRank, computer vision (e.g., lazy random walks for superpixel segmentation), and social-network analysis.
- [supported] Szegedy introduced a quantum variant of Markov chains and Magniez et al. extended Szegedy's method to a broader class of ergodic Markov chains (established theoretical quantum-walk algorithmic work).
- [speculative] Quantum random walks are expected to offer higher computational efficiency and enhanced security and to enable novel optimization approaches across domains including finance, healthcare, and energy.
- [supported] Integrating random walks with neural models (e.g., random-walk sampling + LSTM) can produce richer knowledge-graph embeddings and improve training efficiency compared to some traditional triple-focused models (as reported in cited work).
- [supported] Random-walk sampling strategies can be used to generate near-optimal, computationally efficient solutions for combinatorial path-planning problems such as waste recycling collection routing (as reported in cited work).
- [supported] Random-walk-based embedding approaches can capture higher-order relationships in structural brain networks and have potential utility for characterizing abnormalities relevant to psychiatric disorders (as argued and cited).
- [speculative] The paper projects that as quantum computing, big data, and AI mature, random-walk models (including quantum variants) will be increasingly integrated into predictive and optimization systems for domains like traffic forecasting, urban planning, epidemic modeling, and marketing.

## Limitations
