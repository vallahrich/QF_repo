## Abstract summary
The authors develop and evaluate a hybrid quantum-classical GAN (HQGAN) with a quantum circuit generator and a classical neural network discriminator to produce synthetic financial data for a Brazilian bank. They compare HQGAN to a fully classical GAN using loss convergence and MSE distance, finding that the HQGAN failed to achieve sufficient accuracy on the complex financial dataset, highlighting current limitations of quantum-inspired methods in this domain.
## Methodology
The authors developed and evaluated a hybrid quantum-classical Generative Adversarial Network (HQGAN) to produce synthetic financial data for the minority (approved withdrawals) class from a Banco BV dataset. The HQGAN used a parameterized quantum circuit as the generator and a classical neural network as the discriminator. The quantum generator employed an angle-embedding feature map (Ry rotations) to encode latent vectors, a parameterized ansatz (same Ry structure) with linear CZ entangling gates, and an ancilla qubit plus partial measurement to introduce nonlinearity. Because the quantum generator can only produce 2^{NG} output values for NG data qubits, the authors set NG=4 (data qubits) and NA=1 (ancilla) and padded features as necessary to match this constraint. The classical discriminator was a small feed-forward neural network with two hidden layers and a sigmoid output for binary discrimination. For comparison, a fully classical DCGAN-style network (dense layers, batch normalization, ReLU/Tanh activations) was implemented as a baseline. Both models were trained with Binary Cross-Entropy loss and compared using training loss dynamics and the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between generated and original real-data features. Data preprocessing comprised ordinal encoding for a single categorical feature and min-max scaling to [0,1]. Implementations used PyTorch (Torch) and PennyLane, and experiments were executed on a local quantum simulator cluster (Kuatomu). The HQGAN did not match the classical GAN in terms of MSE convergence and exhibited unstable/erratic behavior in training, leading the authors to conclude current limitations for this approach on the tested problem.

**Algorithms used:** Hybrid Quantum-Classical GAN (HQGAN) - quantum generator + classical discriminator, Quantum Neural Network (QNN) parameterized circuit / ansatz, Angle embedding (Ry rotations) feature map, Controlled-Z linear entanglement, Partial measurement with ancilla qubit for nonlinearity, Deep (classical) Convolutional / Dense GAN (DCGAN-like) as baseline, Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD), Adam optimizer, Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss
**Frameworks:** PyTorch (Torch), PennyLane

**Experimental setup:** Simulations implemented in Python using PyTorch and PennyLane, executed on the Kuatomu quantum simulator cluster (SENAI-CIMATEC) with 192 CPU cores / 384 threads, 3 TB RAM, and 4 NVIDIA V100 32 GB GPUs. No physical QPU was used; quantum circuits were run on a simulator.

**Dataset:** Banco BV dataset of clients requesting FGTS withdrawals (April 2022 to July 2022). Total >13 million samples; minority class (approved withdrawals) = 54,490 samples (~0.4%). Dataset originally had 16 features; target and time were discarded, one categorical feature ordinal-encoded, min-max normalization applied, and zero-padding applied to reach feature length required by the quantum generator.

## Findings
- [supported] The authors implemented a hybrid quantum-classical GAN (HQGAN) with a quantum circuit as generator and a classical neural-network discriminator and trained it on a real financial dataset from Banco BV.
- [supported] The dataset comprised over 13 million samples with only 54,490 (~0.4%) belonging to the minority (approved) class, motivating synthetic-data generation to rebalance classes.
- [supported] The HQGAN consistently underperformed a fully classical GAN in these experiments: the classical GAN showed decreasing MSE relative to real data and stable loss convergence, while the HQGAN exhibited erratic MSE behavior and a divergence in losses partway through training.
- [supported] The HQGAN showed a mid-training divergence (generator loss increased and diverged from discriminator loss) and did not converge to the data distribution as measured by MSE over the training runs reported.
- [supported] The quantum generator design used NG = 4 data qubits and NA = 1 ancilla (leading to a 2^NG capacity constraint), required padding features to match the generator output dimensionality, and used angle-embedding Ry rotations with a CZ linear entanglement ansatz.
- [supported] Model and training hyperparameters: HQGAN generator had 30 parameters and discriminator 2,145 parameters (latent size 5), trained with SGD (generator LR 0.3, discriminator LR 0.01), batch size 10, for up to 5,449 epochs; the classical GAN had a much larger parameter count (generator ~805,600, discriminator ~183,969), latent size 100, trained with Adam (LRs 0.0002).
- [speculative] Architectural differences such as the lack of batch normalization in the quantum generator may have driven instability and poor convergence of the HQGAN.
- [speculative] Other potential causes for HQGAN failure include incomplete preprocessing, suboptimal choice/optimization of the partial measurement operation used to introduce nonlinearity, and general limitations of the chosen quantum circuit architecture (ansatz depth, embedding, entanglement pattern).
- [speculative] The authors suggest future research directions including trying a quantum discriminator or fully quantum GANs as alternate hybrid architectures.
- [speculative] The paper cites theoretical work that quantum generative models might offer exponential advantage over classical counterparts, but their empirical results do not demonstrate such an advantage and instead highlight current limitations on real financial datasets.

**Results summary:** The paper reports an empirical study comparing a hybrid quantum-classical GAN (quantum generator + classical discriminator) against a fully classical GAN for synthetic financial data generation using a large, highly imbalanced dataset (13M samples, 0.4% positive class) from Banco BV. The classical GAN consistently achieved better empirical performance: its loss curves behaved stably and the MSE between generated and real data decreased and stabilized. The HQGAN, implemented with 4 data qubits and 1 ancilla qubit, showed erratic MSE behavior and a mid-training divergence in losses, failing to accurately capture the data distribution. The authors identify possible causes (architecture differences such as missing batch normalization, preprocessing choices, measurement optimization, and quantum circuit limitations) as speculative explanations and recommend exploring alternative hybrid/fully quantum GAN architectures in future work.

## Limitations
- HQGAN did not achieve sufficient accuracy to learn the complex patterns in the financial dataset (author-stated).
- The quantum GAN exhibited unstable training dynamics: erratic behavior, divergence partway through training, and higher MSE versus the classical GAN (author-stated).
- The quantum generator's capacity is constrained by the number of data qubits, forcing dataset padding and limiting representational capacity (author-stated).
- Quantum circuit operations are unitary/linear, requiring partial measurements and ancilla qubits to introduce nonlinearity; selecting and optimizing these measurements is nontrivial (author-stated).
- Severe class imbalance in the dataset (minority class ≈ 0.4%) creates a challenging regime for learning and evaluation (author-stated / problem context).
- The HQGAN generator lacked Batch Normalization while the classical GAN used it, raising concerns that this architectural difference harmed convergence (author-stated concern).
- Overfitting due to excessive training iterations was identified as a possible cause of divergence in the quantum GAN (author-hypothesized).
- [inferred] The quantum generator had very few trainable parameters and a small latent vector (30 parameters, latent size 5) compared to the classical generator (805,600 parameters, latent size 100), indicating limited model capacity.
- [inferred] The experimental evaluation used a simulator (Kuatomu) rather than real noisy quantum hardware, so results may not reflect device-level noise/limitations.
- [inferred] The quantum circuit depth and expressibility were limited (ansatz depicted with depth k=1); shallow circuits may have constrained learning.
- [inferred] Architectural and hyperparameter differences (e.g., optimizer choice SGD for HQGAN vs Adam for classical GAN, learning rates) add confounding factors that may have impacted comparative performance.
