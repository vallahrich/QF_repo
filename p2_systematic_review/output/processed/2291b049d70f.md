## Abstract summary
The paper proposes a new quantum combinatorial optimization algorithm, Quantum Mix Optimization Algorithm (QMOA), for discrete portfolio optimization, building on prior QAOA/QAOAz/QWOA approaches. It introduces a lattice partitioning into cycles to contract the solution space and reduce required iterations, provides algorithmic descriptions and code, and presents theoretical analyses claiming improved efficiency over existing quantum methods; no empirical quantum hardware results are reported due to access limitations.
## Methodology
The paper is theoretical and methodological: starting from Markowitz-style portfolio construction, the authors map discrete portfolio selection (N assets) to a geometric graph/lattice where nodes are assets and weighted edges represent pairwise covariances (correlations). They introduce lattice partitioning into cycles (and an induced tadpole/path-graph structure to represent preselected assets), derive combinatorial counts for lattice paths and iteration bounds (binomial expressions, principle of optimality), and use these to motivate contraction of the valid-solution subspace. Building on prior QCO work (QAOA, QAOAz, QWOA), they propose a new hybrid quantum-combinatorial scheme called Quantum Mix Optimization Algorithm (QMOA). QMOA encodes each asset position using a two-qubit encoding (Choice U / Choice D), defines the search subspace size M(N,A) analytically, and prescribes an iterative classical-quantum selection routine: in each cycle compute the minimum edge weights, append minima to a candidate set S*, apply swap operations across cycles when later minima are smaller than prior minima, use a CNOT-style thresholding step to guarantee a minimum number of assets F, and employ an unbiased estimator operator Oπ to re-evaluate covariances after swaps. The manuscript contains formal proofs, bounds and complexity comparisons (search-space and gate-complexity) between QAOA, QAOAz, QWOA and the proposed QMOA, derives theoretical ranges/suprema for the number of algorithm iterations p, and provides pseudocode (algorithmic description) for the QMOA selection/indexing routine. No empirical quantum experiments or numerical benchmarks are reported; the authors recommend simulation frameworks (e.g., QuOPMPI) and financial-data sources (e.g., Yahoo Finance via Python API) for future implementation.

**Algorithms used:** QAOA, Quantum Alternating Operator Ansatz (QAOAz), Quantum Walk Optimization Algorithm (QWOA), Quantum Mix Optimization Algorithm (QMOA) (proposed)
**Frameworks:** QuOPMPI (recommended)

## Quantum advantage claim

## Contradictions
- The manuscript asserts broadly that 'quantum algorithms are provably strong and possess the ability to solve NP-hard problems in polynomial time.' This contradicts established complexity-theory consensus and literature on quantum algorithms (no known general polynomial-time quantum algorithms for NP-hard problems).
- Claims of reduced search space and fewer iterations for QMOA are not supported by empirical benchmarks; the paper simultaneously states improved search-space scaling while reporting higher gate-complexity (QMOA O(2N^2)) than some alternatives, creating tension between gate-cost and search-space advantage claims.
- The reported search-space/gate-complexity entries contain apparent typos/inconsistencies (e.g., '22N' likely intended as 2^{2N}) undermining the internal consistency of claimed asymptotics and making comparisons to QAOA/QWOA inconclusive without further clarification or empirical validation.
