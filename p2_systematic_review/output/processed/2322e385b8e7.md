## Abstract summary
The paper presents a hybrid quantum-classical approach to crypto-currency intra-exchange arbitrage by formulating the problem as a QUBO/Ising Hamiltonian and solving it with VQE using a differential evolution (DE) optimizer. The authors show DE improves convergence to the global optimum in problems with many local minima compared to common optimizers like COBYLA, and report experiments on simulators and IBM quantum hardware (up to 127 qubits), including a three-currency run that converged on ibm_geneva in 417 steps (~12 hours).
## Methodology
The authors formulate intra-exchange cryptocurrency arbitrage as a binary quadratic optimization problem: they build a transit (exchange rate) matrix, take logarithms, impose closed-cycle and single-use constraints, convert inequalities to equalities with slack variables, binarize slack variables, and convert constrained quadratic model into an unconstrained QUBO by adding quadratic penalty terms (DocPLEX/CPLEX was used to model the quadratic problem). The QUBO is mapped to an Ising Hamiltonian (Pauli Z/I decomposition). They use a VQE hybrid quantum-classical workflow: a parameterized ansatz (Real Amplitudes / SCA or circular entanglement, small repetition counts) is prepared; expectation values of the problem Hamiltonian for parametrized circuits are evaluated via Qiskit Runtime (Estimator/Sampler) and those expectation values are minimized by classical optimizers. Two optimizer families are compared: COBYLA (local, gradient-free) and a custom Differential Evolution (DE) global optimizer that was implemented by combining scipy.optimize.differential_evolution with batched circuit evaluation in Qiskit Runtime. Hyperparameter search was performed for ansatz entanglement and repetition count; DE hyperparameters (tolerance, population size, maximum interactions) were tuned to reach global minima in cases where COBYLA became trapped in local minima. Experiments were run on simulators (Qiskit simulator_mps) and on IBM Quantum backends (Falcon and Eagle processors). For real-world five-asset data they normalized transition matrices (per-asset normalization coefficients) before QUBO construction to accommodate widely varying asset scales. Performance metrics collected included expectation (energy), realized profit rate of discovered arbitrage cycles, number of optimization generations/iterations to convergence, and wall-clock time.

**Algorithms used:** Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE), Differential Evolution (DE) for VQE / Minimum Eigensolver, COBYLA (classical optimizer), QUBO formulation / Ising Hamiltonian mapping, Classical quadratic modeling with DocPLEX / CPLEX
**Frameworks:** Qiskit, DocPLEX (IBM CPLEX for Python), Qiskit Runtime Estimator and Sampler, scipy.optimize (differential_evolution)

**Experimental setup:** Small-scale simulated and real intra-exchange cryptocurrency experiments. Problems with 3, 4 and 5 currencies were used. QUBO → Ising Hamiltonian → VQE workflow with Real Amplitudes ansatz (SCA/circular entanglement, typically 1 repetition). Expectation values evaluated in batched runs via Qiskit Runtime and minimized with COBYLA or a custom Differential Evolution wrapper. Shots per circuit: 4000. Batches: 64 circuits (3-currency on 27-qubit machine) or 256 circuits (25-qubit ansatz runs). Experiments run on simulator_mps and IBM Quantum backends (ibm_geneva, ibm_hanoi, ibm_washington, ibm_brisbane).

**Dataset:** Simulated exchange-rate (transit) matrices for 3- and 4-currency toy problems; a real intra-exchange dataset of five cryptocurrency assets (unspecified exchange/source) for the 5-asset experiment. For real data the transition matrix was normalized per-asset to avoid very small exchange rates.

## Findings
- [supported] A differential-evolution (DE) based optimizer integrated with VQE was implemented (custom implementation in Qiskit) and is usable as a global optimizer for the arbitrage QUBO-to-Hamiltonian pipeline.
- [supported] In small-scale simulated experiments (3-currency), DE and COBYLA both found the global optimum in some settings, but COBYLA exhibited gradient-descent-like behavior and could get trapped in local minima depending on ansatz/entanglement choices.
- [supported] DE demonstrated more global-search behavior (population-based exploration) and in the reported tests reliably converged to the global optimum where some ansatz/optimizer combinations yielded suboptimal local solutions.
- [supported] For the 3-currency real-device experiment on ibm_geneva (27-qubit device) the 9-qubit ansatz with DE converged in 417 generations (~12 hours) using batches of 64 circuits with 4000 shots each (≈104 s per generation).
- [supported] For the 5-currency problem the approach was feasible on an MPS simulator (simulator_mps) using a 25-qubit ansatz: DE converged after 469 generations taking ≈47 hours, using batches of 256 parametric circuits.
- [supported] Experiments on multiple real IBM QPUs (ibm_hanoi, ibm_washington, ibm_brisbane) were executed as partial runs (did not reach convergence within <24 hours); reported per-generation runtimes: ibm_hanoi ≈525 s, ibm_brisbane ≈926 s, ibm_washington ≈1147 s for 256 circuits × 4000 shots.
- [supported] Real-world crypto exchange-rate matrices require normalization (per-asset scaling) before encoding into QUBO/Hamiltonian to avoid numerical issues due to very small rates and price-inequalities.
- [supported] Choice of ansatz entanglement and repetition count materially affects whether VQE finds global or local optima; the authors favored a circular/SCA ansatz with one repetition for mapping/topology fit on current hardware.
- [supported] DE requires many circuit evaluations (higher quantum workload) compared to single-run local optimizers, but it enables parallel execution of circuits in a generation (potential hardware-level parallelism benefit).
- [speculative] The authors assert novelty: this is the first application of quantum optimization with differential evolution to the arbitrage problem (stated "to the best of our knowledge").
- [speculative] The results "suggest the potential for achieving a quantum advantage in solving increasingly complex problems" — i.e., DE+VQE could yield advantage as quantum hardware and parallelism improve.
- [speculative] Future improvements (encoding/representation changes to reduce qubit counts and hyperparameter tuning of DE) could materially improve scalability and convergence speed.
- [supported] Classical solutions for illustrative toy instances: reported profit rates were 23 (3-currency) and 118.99 (4-currency) for the classical optimizer on the constructed examples.

**Results summary:** The paper implements a Differential Evolution (DE) optimizer within a VQE workflow (custom Qiskit integration) to solve intra-exchange cryptocurrency arbitrage encoded as QUBO → Ising Hamiltonian. In simulator and small real-device tests the DE-VQE pipeline found global optima for 3- and 4-currency toy instances and for a 5-currency instance on a simulator, while DE required many generations and substantial wall-clock time (tens of hours). Partial runs on multiple IBM QPUs (27- and 127-qubit devices) validated feasibility but did not reach convergence within limited real-device time budgets. The authors report that DE provides more robust global search than local optimizers like COBYLA at the cost of much higher circuit evaluation counts, and highlight practical needs (normalization of real data, ansatz/topology matching) and future directions (encoding improvements, parallel execution).

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The authors claim potential for quantum advantage as hardware improves and parallelization becomes available, but they do not demonstrate a provable or empirical quantum advantage (no speedup or asymptotic comparison vs classical algorithms is provided); current real-device runs are feasibility/validation experiments that are time-consuming and do not show advantage.
## Limitations
- Current quantum hardware resource limits: many relevant quantum applications demand resources beyond current hardware capacity (author-stated).
- Long run-times on real devices: differential evolution VQE required many hours (e.g., 12 hours for 3-currency on ibm_geneva; tens of hours or aborted runs for 5-currency experiments) and often did not converge within 24 hours on several QPUs (author-stated).
- High circuit-evaluation cost of Differential Evolution: DE achieves global minima but at the expense of a large number of quantum circuit evaluations per generation (author-stated).
- Noise and device errors hinder performance: real device error rates, coherence times, and readout errors limit effective execution and convergence (author-stated).
- Mapping/topology constraints: circuit mapping (avoiding swap gates) and ansatz placement influence depth and accumulated noise; larger machines help with routing but may have lower CLOPS, increasing runtime (author-stated).
- Need for ansatz and optimizer hyperparameter tuning: results depend strongly on choice of ansatz (entanglement, repetitions) and DE hyperparameters (author-stated).
- Data preprocessing requirements: real exchange-rate matrices needed normalization to avoid near-zero transitions and numerical issues (author-stated).
- Implementation gaps in tooling: Qiskit lacked a built-in Differential Evolution VQE optimizer, necessitating a custom implementation (author-stated).
- [inferred] Real-world trading constraints not modeled: latency, order book dynamics, market impact, minimum trade sizes, slippage, and transfer/withdrawal times are not addressed and may invalidate intra-exchange assumptions in practice.
- [inferred] Scalability limits in qubit count: QUBO conversion combined with naive encoding leads to rapid growth in qubit requirements as asset count increases, constraining problem sizes solvable on current hardware.
- [inferred] Economic viability and timeliness: even if a global minimum is found, the time-to-solution on present hardware likely makes extracted arbitrage profits infeasible in fast-moving markets.
- [inferred] Sensitivity to normalization and preprocessing choices: different normalization strategies could materially change optimization results, but robustness was not fully analyzed.
- [inferred] Limited benchmarking against classical/global optimizers: while comparisons to COBYLA were made, broader benchmarking (other global heuristics, classical exact/approx methods) is lacking.
## Open questions
- Can a clear quantum advantage for crypto-currency arbitrage be demonstrated in problem sizes and time-to-solution that are relevant to real market practice?
- What encoding/representation techniques (Hamiltonian/QUBO) can reduce qubit requirements while preserving problem fidelity?
- How can differential evolution (and other global optimizers) be best configured (strategy, population size, tolerance, etc.) for VQE to minimize quantum circuit evaluations and convergence time?
- To what extent do device noise and error rates change the landscape of local minima and the optimizer's ability to find the global optimum on hardware versus simulators?
- How feasible is parallel execution of DE populations across multiple quantum processors in practice, and what orchestration/latency challenges would that introduce?
- How sensitive are solutions to normalization and preprocessing choices for real exchange-rate matrices, and what are principled normalization methods for heterogeneous asset prices?
- What is the impact of real-world trading frictions (fees, minimum lot sizes, latency, transfer times) on the profitability of quantum-found arbitrage loops?
- Which ansatz structures and circuit depths strike the best balance between expressivity (to represent optimal states) and noise-resilience on near-term devices?
- How do alternative quantum optimization algorithms (QAOA variants, other evolutionary or population-based VQE hybrids) compare to DE-VQE for this problem class?
- What are practical strategies for mapping and routing the ansatz on large processors to minimize swaps and accumulated error while keeping runtime acceptable?

**Future work:**
- Develop novel encodings of the problem to the Hamiltonian to represent more QUBO variables using fewer qubits.
- Perform fine tuning of differential evolution hyperparameters to improve convergence and reduce the number of generations required.
