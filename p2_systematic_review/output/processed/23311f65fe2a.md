## Abstract summary
The paper proposes a quantum-based approach to feature selection by formulating the problem as a QUBO and solving it with a CVaR-optimized QAOA. The authors introduce a hybrid initialization strategy combining Trotterized Quantum Annealing (TQA) and Diﬀerential Evolution (DE) to improve QAOA parameter selection and demonstrate the method on 11 benchmark datasets, showing improved classification performance over prior techniques. This paper introduces DarkMDE, a self-supervised framework for monocular depth estimation in nighttime scenes that leverages cross-domain supervision. The method uses a CycleGAN to generate synthetic nighttime images from daytime captures, employs daytime images for pose estimation and photometric loss computation, and enforces a pseudo-depth consistency constraint between paired day/night images. Experiments on the Oxford RobotCar dataset demonstrate that DarkMDE reduces artifacts common in night depth maps and achieves competitive performance versus state-of-the-art methods.
## Methodology
The authors reformulate the feature-selection task as a Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) / Ising problem using Pearson-correlation based Q-matrix entries (off-diagonals r(fi,fj) and diagonals -r(fi,y)) and enforce the fixed-cardinality constraint (select exactly k features) via a squared-penalty term. They map the resulting QUBO into a Hamiltonian H_f for QAOA. To overcome nonconvexity and local optima in QAOA parameter optimization, they (1) estimate a suitable Trotterized-Quantum-Annealing (TQA) time T to derive a time-step Δt = T/p and use the Trotter decomposition as a guide for initial QAOA parameters, (2) run a Differential Evolution (DE) search within ranges informed by TQA (γ_j ∈ [j/p·Δt, 2π], β_j ∈ [(1−j/p)·Δt, 2π]) to produce strong initial parameters, and (3) reﬁne those parameters with a classical optimizer (COBYLA) on the QAOA expectation. They sample bitstrings from the optimized QAOA state and apply Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) as the objective (i.e., minimize the average of the lowest-α% energies) to focus on low-energy (good) solutions; the α used is very small (0.01%). The method returns the selected feature subset (from the best bitstrings) and evaluates downstream classiﬁcation performance with classical ML models (LR, XGBoost, HistGradientBoosting). Experiments are run on the Qiskit Aer matrix-product-state simulator (100 qubits) and on 11 real-world benchmark datasets to compare accuracy vs prior approaches. The provided document is a conglomeration of conference papers on topics such as human emotion and pose recognition, knee osteoarthritis diagnosis, skin disease classification, indoor positioning, smart parking with Jetson platforms, a RISC-V SoC with a DNN accelerator, a blockchain e-voting protocol employing zk-STARKs, and nighttime monocular depth estimation. **Algorithms used:** QAOA, Trotterized Quantum Annealing (TQA) for initialization, Differential Evolution (DE) for parameter initialization, COBYLA (classical optimizer for parameter reﬁnement), CVaR (Conditional Value-at-Risk) objective for low-tail optimization
**Frameworks:** Qiskit Aer (matrix product state simulator)

**Experimental setup:** QAOA experiments were performed on the Qiskit Aer matrix-product-state simulator with capacity to emulate 100 qubits. QAOA depth p was explored (p ∈ {1..10}) and Trotterized Quantum Annealing (TQA) was used to select a suitable total time T, with Δt = T/p used to bound initial γ_j and β_j ranges. Differential Evolution (DE) searched for initial parameters within those ranges; COBYLA was used as the local classical optimizer to further optimize the QAOA expectation. CVaR α = 0.0001 (0.01%) was employed to select low-energy solutions from sampled bitstrings.

**Dataset:** Eleven real-world datasets mainly from the UCI repository and one additional dataset used in prior work: Wilt, Wireless Indoor Localization, Raisin grain, Rice, Accent, Stability, HTRU2, Occupancy, Room Occupancy Estimation, seeds, and pv fault. These datasets cover classiﬁcation tasks with varying numbers of features and classes and were used to evaluate selected feature subsets with classical ML models.
