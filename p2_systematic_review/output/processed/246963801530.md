## Abstract summary
This paper presents a systematic literature review of classical and quantum machine learning approaches applied to mutual fund portfolio optimization, analyzing papers from 2003–2023 with a focus on equity markets. It compares classical ML methods and emerging quantum techniques (including QAOA, VQE, quantum annealing and QUBO/hybrid approaches), summarizes benchmarks and gaps, and highlights challenges and opportunities for QML in the NISQ era, such as limited device performance and a lack of mutual-fund-specific studies.
## Methodology
This work is a systematic literature review (SLR) conducted using a PRISMA-inspired methodology over roughly the past 20 years. The authors searched major bibliographic and preprint sources (Scopus, arXiv.org and other academic search engines, SSRN) and selected papers from prominent journals (e.g., Nature, Quantum, Physical Review Research, Quantum Information Processing). The review focused on studies relevant to mutual funds and equity markets (with exceptions for two highly cited milestone papers). The authors collected, screened and analyzed literature to answer two research questions about (1) prominent classical and quantum ML work addressing NP-hard mutual fund portfolio optimization and (2) critical research gaps. The review compiled and categorized papers by topic (classical ML vs quantum ML), algorithms and hardware used in the literature, benchmarks and evaluation approaches, and summarized trends (publication year distribution, author-country distribution). Figures and tables (e.g., methodology flowchart, publication-year trend, problem-type tables, and research-gap tables) were used to present selection and synthesis outcomes. The SLR emphasizes mapping the landscape rather than conducting new empirical experiments.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA), Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE), Harrow-Hassidim-Lloyd algorithm (HHL), NISQ-HHL, Quantum Walk Optimization Algorithm, Quantum Circuit Born Machines (QCBM), Quantum Annealing (including Reverse Quantum Annealing), QUBO formulation, Simulated Annealing, Genetic Algorithm, Restricted Boltzmann Machine (RBM), Quantum-inspired Tensor Networks
**Frameworks:** Gekko (Python optimizer), QUBO formulation (modeling framework)

**Dataset:** The reviewed studies used a variety of financial datasets in the literature: equity mutual fund data, equity/stock datasets (including S&P 500 constituents), ETFs and adjusted closing prices, Fund-of-Funds data, and other real-world market datasets. The review itself did not generate a new experimental dataset.

## Open questions
- Which quantum or quantum-assisted algorithms (QAOA, VQE, quantum walks, quantum annealing, QCBMs, etc.) consistently outperform classical baselines for mutual fund portfolio optimization in realistic settings?
- How can mutual fund–specific constraints (tax efficiency, turnover limits, liquidity, regulatory mandates) be encoded efficiently into quantum-native problem representations (e.g., QUBO) without prohibitive overhead?
- What are the concrete criteria and standardized benchmarks to evaluate QML approaches for portfolio optimization to allow fair cross-study comparisons?
- To what extent do quantum linear-algebra techniques (HHL and variants) apply to practical financial datasets, and when do their prerequisites break down?
- How can the curse of dimensionality be mitigated for ML/DL and QML approaches in high-dimensional portfolio optimization problems?
- What is the optimal hybrid partitioning of portfolio optimization workflows between classical and quantum resources for near-term devices?
- How can noisy quantum outputs be validated and made reliable enough for high-stakes financial decision making?
- How large and noise-resilient must quantum devices become before pure-quantum PO solutions outcompete classical or hybrid methods in practice?
- How can QML methods incorporate real-time macroeconomic and market-impact factors into portfolio rebalancing decisions at scale?
- What are the best practices for mapping NP-hard PO problems to quantum annealers or gate-model quantum hardware while preserving realistic constraint handling and solution quality?

**Future work:**
