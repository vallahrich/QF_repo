## Abstract summary
The paper introduces QOPLE (Quantum Option Profit and Loss Encoding) and a Quantum Option Risk Assessment (QORA) model to encode option profit-and-loss distributions and compute Value at Risk for European call options on quantum hardware. By using MLE-based amplitude estimation and binary search, the approach targets NISQ devices, reducing ancillary qubit and controlled-operation requirements while retaining a quadratic speedup over classical Monte Carlo; Qiskit simulations report up to 99% accuracy and at least 15% fewer ancillary qubits compared to existing quantum algorithms.
## Methodology
The paper proposes a circuit-level methodology for option risk assessment based on a new Quantum Option Profit and Loss Encoding (QOPLE) operator A = V P L and an associated Quantum Option Risk Assessment (QORA) workflow. L loads a truncated-and-discretized Black–Scholes–Merton (log-normal) distribution of the underlying asset price into an n-qubit register using Qiskit uncertainty-modeling circuits; P computes option payoff (PnL) by comparing each discretized price to the strike K and encoding the zero/non-zero payoff via an auxiliary qubit; V performs a controlled comparison of each payoff to a threshold l and flips a target qubit when PnL ≤ l, thereby encoding the cumulative distribution P[PT ≤ l] into the target amplitude. Amplitude (probability) of the target qubit is estimated using an amplitude-amplification sampling strategy combined with maximum-likelihood estimation (MLE) rather than phase-estimation-based QAE: multiple Grover-style amplification counts fq (exponentially increasing sequence) are applied, each followed by Nq projective measurements; the collected counts form a likelihood function L(h; θa) which is maximized to recover the amplitude a = sin^2 θa. A classical binary search over l (with bounds set to PnL min/max) is used, calling the quantum CDF-estimation subroutine repeatedly until the smallest l with P[PT ≤ l] ≥ α is found (VaR at confidence α). Experiments are simulations implemented in IBM Qiskit: option distributions are truncated to ±3σ, discretized with 2^n equidistant samples, and loaded into n-qubit registers; parameters reported include n (sampling qubits varied e.g. 3..7), MLE amplification schedule m = 6 producing fq = {0,1,2,4,8,16,32}, Nq = 100 per fq, α = 0.95, and classical Monte Carlo baseline with 10,000 samples. Results reported include estimated VaR, relative error vs classical Monte Carlo, CDF plots and resource comparisons (ancillary qubits and controlled-amplification counts) between MLE-based amplitude estimation and standard QAE.

**Algorithms used:** QOPLE (Quantum Option Profit and Loss Encoding), QORA (Quantum Option Risk Assessment), Amplitude amplification (Grover-style), MLE-based Amplitude Estimation (Amplitude estimation without phase estimation), Quantum Amplitude Estimation (QAE) (as baseline / comparison), Binary search (classical) for VaR
**Frameworks:** Qiskit

**Experimental setup:** Simulation using IBM Qiskit (uncertainty-modeling module) on classical simulator; no physical QPU specified. Simulated European call options generated from Black–Scholes–Merton log-normal distribution, truncated to ±3σ and discretized into 2^n samples.

**Dataset:** Synthetic option price distributions generated from the Black–Scholes–Merton model (log-normal ST), truncated to ±3 standard deviations around the mean and discretized into 2^n equidistant samples. Two example European call option parameterizations were tested (S0=2.0, σ=0.4, T=40/365, r=0.05) with strikes K=1.896 and K=1.5.

**Results summary:** The paper proposes QOPLE, a circuit construction to encode option profit-and-loss distributions into quantum states (operators L,P,V), and combines it with a QORA pipeline that uses MLE-based amplitude estimation plus binary search to compute VaR for European call options. ## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** theoretical

The paper claims potential quadratic speedup (O(1/ε) vs O(1/ε^2)) inherited from amplitude-estimation techniques, but provides no empirical runtime or hardware demonstration of end-to-end quantum speedup on real quantum devices — only Qiskit simulations and analytic resource comparisons. Therefore the claimed advantage remains theoretical rather than experimentally demonstrated.
## Limitations
- NISQ hardware limitations (noise, limited qubit counts and gate depth) restrict near-term realization of true quantum advantage (author-stated).
- Demonstrations are performed on Qiskit simulations rather than noisy quantum hardware, so results do not reflect realistic device noise or calibration errors [inferred].
- Model ignores transaction costs, margins, premiums and other real-world trading frictions for simplicity (author-stated).
- Work focuses on European call options only; other option types (American, path-dependent, exotic) are not treated (author-stated).
- The algorithm relies on the Black–Scholes–Merton log-normal assumption to load the underlying price distribution, which may be invalid for markets with fat tails or non-lognormal behaviour (author-stated and [inferred]).
- Distribution truncation (three standard deviations around the mean) may under-represent extreme tail events relevant to risk measurement (author-described truncation; [inferred] limitation).
- Scalability is unclear: experiments use small numbers of sampling qubits (examples up to n=7); performance and resource requirements for practical, large-scale problems are not demonstrated (author-stated experimental scope; [inferred]).
- Although the L operator is argued to be loadable with structured circuits, parts of the paper refer to O(2^n) complexity for distribution loading—potential exponential resource growth for naïve implementations ([inferred] ambiguity/limitation).
- Circuit depth and controlled operations required for multi-factor or high-dimensional derivative models can exceed current hardware capabilities (author-stated).
- Binary-search-based VaR search and repeated amplitude estimations imply multiple queries and measurements, introducing classical/quantum query overhead that may be significant in practice ([inferred]).
- The MLE-based amplitude estimation reduces ancilla and controlled operations compared to phase-estimation QAE, but still requires careful choice of amplification sequence and many measurements; robustness to sequence design and measurement noise is not fully characterized ([inferred]).
- Resource claims (e.g., ≥15% ancilla reduction) are based on comparisons in the paper but may not generalize across all option instances and larger problem sizes ([inferred]).
## Open questions
- How robust is QORA/QOPLE to noise, decoherence, gate errors and readout errors on real NISQ devices? (How do results degrade on hardware vs ideal simulation?)
- Can the QOPLE encoding and QORA model be extended to American options, path-dependent/exotic derivatives, or multi-period/multi-factor risk models?
- How to incorporate real-world trading frictions (premiums, transaction costs, margin requirements) into the quantum encoding and VaR estimation pipeline?
- How sensitive are results to violations of the Black–Scholes log-normal assumption (e.g., heavy tails, stochastic volatility, jumps)?
- What are the detailed resource (qubit, gate count, depth) scalings for practical problem sizes (realistic strikes, maturities, portfolio sizes) and the crossover point where quantum advantage appears in practice?
- How to efficiently implement the distribution-loading operator L for large n in practice while controlling circuit depth and noise accumulation?
- How should the amplitude amplification sequence and MLE measurement schedule be optimally designed for a given precision/noise budget?
- Can the theoretical quadratic speedup (O(1/epsilon) vs O(1/epsilon^2)) be observed end-to-end in realistic, noisy hardware and end-to-end financial workflows?
- How to integrate QORA into existing risk-management systems, including data pipelines, stress-testing, and regulatory reporting?
- How does the algorithm perform for portfolio-level VaR with many instruments and correlated underlyings, including conditional or scenario-based VaR?

- #idea:near-term-feasibility — Introduces an MLE-based amplitude-estimation protocol (Grover-style amplification counts + classical MLE) designed to reduce ancillary-qubit and controlled-operation overheads relative to phase-estimation-based QAE, targeting NISQ-era feasibility.
- #idea:hybrid-approach — Uses a hybrid pipeline: quantum circuits to encode truncated/discretized log-normal asset distributions and to mark payoffs; classical MLE for amplitude recovery and classical binary search to find VaR thresholds.
- #limitation:simulation-only — All experiments are classical Qiskit simulations on discretized synthetic Black–Scholes samples (n up to 7); no runs on physical QPUs.
- #limitation:data-encoding — State preparation relies on truncation and discrete loading (Qiskit uncertainty-modeling); cost and scaling of accurate distribution loading are practical limitations noted by the methodology.
- #limitation:qubit-count — Demonstrations are small-scale (sampling qubits n up to 7) so practical qubit requirements for realistic option portfolios / higher-resolution discretizations remain untested.
