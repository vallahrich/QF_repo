## Abstract summary
The paper proposes a hybrid quantum-classical transfer learning (QTL) model for credit risk classification that combines a frozen classical feature extractor (MLP) with a trainable 4-qubit variational quantum circuit. Evaluated on a real-world Kaggle credit risk dataset, the QTL model substantially outperforms a standalone QNN, matches or slightly improves upon a classical neural network, and notably increases minority-class (defaulter) recall from 0.16 to 0.62 while achieving ~91% accuracy and an AUC around 0.88.
## Methodology
The study implements a hybrid quantum-classical transfer learning (QTL) pipeline for binary credit-risk classification. A pretrained classical 3-layer feedforward neural network (MLP) with ReLU and Tanh activations is used as a frozen feature extractor; the activations of its final hidden layer are reduced to a 4-dimensional feature vector z ∈ R^4. Each scalar zi is encoded into a single qubit via angle encoding using an RY rotation, generating a 4-qubit input state. A variational quantum circuit (VQC) composed of initial Hadamard gates, entangling operations and parameterized unitaries U(θq) processes the encoded state. The circuit output is measured as the expectation of the Z observable; that expectation is fed to a small classical output layer (linear weights and bias) followed by a sigmoid to produce a probability. During QTL training only the quantum circuit parameters θq and the final classical output layer parameters are optimized using the Adam optimizer with binary cross-entropy loss, while the classical feature extractor weights remain frozen. For baseline comparisons, the authors evaluate a standalone QNN trained directly on the original dataset and a conventional classical neural network. Evaluation uses an 80/20 stratified train/test split, and metrics reported include accuracy, AUC, precision, recall and F1 (per class). The VQC implementation is built with the PennyLane framework.

**Algorithms used:** Variational Quantum Circuit (VQC), Quantum Neural Network (QNN), Quantum Transfer Learning (QTL), Angle encoding (RY), Feedforward Neural Network (MLP)
**Frameworks:** PennyLane

**Experimental setup:** PennyLane-based variational quantum circuit implemented in simulation (no specific quantum backend or QPU specified). **Results summary:** On a normalized Kaggle credit risk dataset (32,580 instances, 80/20 stratified split), the authors implemented a hybrid quantum-classical transfer learning pipeline consisting of a frozen classical MLP feature extractor (reduced to d=4 features) feeding a 4-qubit variational quantum circuit. Empirically, the Hybrid QTL model outperformed a standalone QNN (accuracy 0.9108 vs 0.8097; AUC 0.8797 vs 0.7446) and matched or slightly exceeded the classical neural network baseline (accuracy 0.9108 vs 0.9090; AUC 0.8797 vs 0.8477). Notably, the Hybrid QTL achieved much better minority-class recall (0.62) than the standalone QNN (0.16). The authors argue these results indicate hybrid transfer learning can mitigate some shortcomings of pure QNNs, though claims about reduced quantum overhead and environmental benefits are not empirically validated and the paper contains an internal numeric inconsistency for AUC.

## Contradictions
- contradiction:classical-vs-quantum — Although the paper frames the hybrid as a quantum-enabled improvement, the hybrid's overall accuracy is nearly identical to the classical MLP (0.9108 vs 0.9090) and AUC improvement is modest; therefore claims of clear quantum superiority over classical methods are not strongly supported by the reported results.
- contradiction:scalability — The paper promotes reduced quantum overhead but evaluates only a 4-qubit VQC in a simulator with underspecified circuit depth and training hyperparameters; scalability to larger feature sets, larger models, or real QPU execution is untested and thus claims about practical scaling remain unverified.
