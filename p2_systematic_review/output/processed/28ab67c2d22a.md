## Abstract summary
The authors propose a one-dimensional cyclic driver Hamiltonian for the fermionic QAOA (FQAOA) that enforces constraints via fermion number conservation while reducing the number of quantum gates compared to prior ladder-lattice drivers. Using portfolio optimization as an application, they show in noiseless simulation comparable performance to previous FQAOA and experimentally demonstrate on a trapped-ion QPU (IonQ Aria via Amazon Braket) that the cyclic driver yields improved robustness to noise and higher probability of finding low-cost solutions.
## Methodology
The authors design and evaluate a Fermionic QAOA (FQAOA) variant that uses a one-dimensional cyclic tight-binding (hopping) driver Hamiltonian to enforce constant Hamming-weight constraints as fermion-number conservation. The methodology includes: mapping the constrained portfolio optimization into a fermionic unary-encoding representation (each integer variable encoded by D binary sites mapped to fermionic number operators); constructing the cyclic driver Hamiltonian H_cyc_hop (and comparing to a previously used D-leg ladder H_lad_hop) whose ground state is a single Slater determinant used as the FQAOA initial state; deriving explicit mixing unitaries U_m(β) from the hopping terms and decomposing them into local two-qubit circuits equivalent to XY-type gates (thus avoiding fermionic SWAPs and reducing gate count); setting phase operators U_p(γ) from the problem Hamiltonian H_p for portfolio optimization; initializing variational angles using a discretized quantum adiabatic ansatz (Eqs. (18)) and refining parameters (γ, β) by classical optimization (BFGS) to minimize the expected energy Ep(γ,β). The study evaluates performance by noiseless simulation (qulacs) and real-device experiments on a trapped-ion QPU (IonQ Aria via Amazon Braket). Error mitigation (debiasing) and post-selection on measured particle number (M) are used when processing experimental results. Metrics include probability distributions over solution energies, expectation value of energy relative to the true minimum normalized by the problem energy range W, and probability of satisfying the fermion-number constraint. Baselines considered are the ladder-driver FQAOA (lad-FQAOA), an XY-QAOA variant, and random constrained sampling.

**Algorithms used:** Fermionic QAOA (FQAOA), QAOA (as baseline / related algorithm), XY-QAOA (baseline), BFGS classical optimizer
**Frameworks:** qulacs, Amazon Braket

**Experimental setup:** Noiseless quantum-circuit simulations were run with the qulacs simulator. Hardware experiments were executed on IonQ Aria (trapped-ion QPU) accessed via Amazon Braket. Experiments used 16 qubits (ND = 16 with N = 8, D = 2) and performed 10,000 measurement shots per probability distribution. Error-mitigation (debiasing) was applied to measurement outcomes and post-selection on the fermion-number constraint (M = 4) was performed.

**Dataset:** Portfolio optimization parameters: asset covariance matrix σ_{l,l'} and mean returns μ_l taken from a prior study (referenced as Ref. [2], using σ from Fig. 2 and μ from Table IV in that work). Problem-size parameters: N = 8 (assets), D = 2 (unary encoding depth), K = 4 (number of holdings), λ = 0.9 (risk capacity).

## Findings
- [supported] The proposed one-dimensional cyclic driver Hamiltonian (H_cyc_hop) reduces the number of required hopping terms / gate operations in the mixing unitary from O(N^2 D) (ladder) to O(ND) (cyclic), thereby decreasing circuit two-qubit gate count.
- [supported] Noiseless simulations show cyc-FQAOA (cyclic driver) has comparable optimization performance to lad-FQAOA (ladder driver) and both outperform the previously studied XY-QAOA for the studied portfolio instance.
- [supported] Experiments on a trapped-ion QPU (IonQ Aria via Amazon Braket) with 16 qubits at p=1 show cyc-FQAOA has a higher probability of conserving the target fermion number (post-selection M=4) and a higher probability mass on low-energy solutions than lad-FQAOA.
- [supported] In the QPU experiments (p=1, with debiasing and post-selection) the expectation value of normalized energy ∆E/W was 0.23 (σ=0.16) for cyc-FQAOA and 0.34 (σ=0.17) for lad-FQAOA; both beat random constrained sampling (0.38 (σ=0.18)).
- [supported] The paper provides explicit gate-count formulas: for general p the single- and two-qubit gate counts are (Q1,Q2) = (388+232p, 96+272p) for cyc-FQAOA and (388+504p, 96+512p) for lad-FQAOA (examples given in text).
- [supported] Empirical result reported: cyc-FQAOA improved the probability of obtaining low-cost solutions by a factor ≈2.5 relative to lad-FQAOA in the reported trapped-ion experiments at p=1.
- [speculative] The FQAOA framework enforces hard constraints via fermion-number conservation and is (theoretically) guaranteed to converge to the quantum adiabatic algorithm (QAA) in the limit of large circuit depth p (statement based on prior theoretical work).
- [speculative] The paper analytically constructs the ground state (initial state) of the cyclic tight-binding driver and states it is unique (non-degenerate) for the model as designed.
- [speculative] The authors suggest the cyclic-driver design will generally reduce noise effects on QPUs because of the smaller circuit depth / fewer gates, but note that post-selection and bit-flip errors will become limiting at larger depths and call for error correction.

**Results summary:** The authors introduce a one-dimensional cyclic tight-binding driver Hamiltonian for Fermionic QAOA (FQAOA) that preserves fermion-number constraints while significantly reducing required quantum-gate operations compared to a prior ladder-driver design. Noiseless simulations show comparable optimization quality between cyclic and ladder drivers (both outperforming an XY-QAOA baseline for the chosen portfolio instance). Trapped-ion QPU experiments (IonQ Aria, 16 qubits) at p=1 demonstrate the cyclic driver yields a higher probability of constraint preservation and a higher probability of low-energy (better) solutions than the ladder driver, with reported normalized expectation energies ∆E/W = 0.23 (cyclic) vs 0.34 (ladder) after debiasing and post-selection. The cyclic ansatz achieves a ~2.5× improvement in finding low-cost solutions experimentally, attributed to a reduced two-qubit gate count and shallower effective circuits.

**Performance claims:**

- Explicit gate-count formulas: cyc-FQAOA single/two-qubit gates (Q1,Q2) = (388 + 232 p, 96 + 272 p); lad-FQAOA (Q1,Q2) = (388 + 504 p, 96 + 512 p). For p=1 this yields cyc = (620 single, 368 two-qubit) and lad = (892 single, 608 two-qubit) (counts implied by the formulas in text).

## Limitations
- [inferred] Experiments are limited to a small problem instance (N=8, D=2, K=4; 16 qubits), so scalability to larger, industry-relevant problem sizes is untested.
- QPU experiments were performed only at shallow circuit depth (p = 1); the paper notes post-selection works well for shallow circuits but performance is expected to degrade at larger depths due to bit-flip errors.
- Constraint (fermion-number) conservation is violated on the real device by noise, requiring post-selection and error-mitigation; the probability of obtaining the correct particle number on QPU was low (PM=4 ≈ 0.17 for cyc-FQAOA).
- There is a trade-off between driver expressivity and circuit cost: the ladder driver mixes more effectively in noiseless simulation but requires substantially more gates and is thus more sensitive to noise in hardware experiments (author-stated trade-off).
- [inferred] The unary (fermionic) encoding requires ND qubits (large qubit overhead) compared to more compact encodings, which may limit practical applicability on near-term devices.
- [inferred] The driver Hamiltonian choices are restricted to tight-binding / specific lattice constructions in this work; alternative drivers and their trade-offs are not explored.
- [inferred] The hopping-integral parameter t is set heuristically by matching energy ranges (t = W / W_hop); this choice may not be optimal across instances or hardware.
- [inferred] Experiments were run on a single hardware platform (IonQ Aria via Amazon Braket); portability and performance on other hardware (e.g., superconducting qubits) and different connectivity/noise characteristics remain unknown.
- [inferred] Error-mitigation was limited to debiasing (sharpening not used); the sufficiency of available mitigation techniques for deeper/ larger circuits is unclear.
- [inferred] Benchmarking was limited (comparison to ladder-FQAOA and XY-QAOA only); broader comparisons to other quantum or classical methods and problem instances are missing.
- [inferred] In some parameter regimes the ground state is degenerate and required selecting a symmetric occupied state; degeneracy handling and its effect on initialization/optimization may be an issue.
## Open questions
- How does cyc-FQAOA scale with problem size (N, D, K) in terms of solution quality and hardware resource requirements?
- How does performance evolve at larger circuit depths p on real devices, given increased constraint-violation from bit-flip and other noise?
- Can practical error-correction or more advanced error-mitigation techniques preserve particle-number conservation sufficiently to enable deeper FQAOA circuits?
- What is the optimal design of driver Hamiltonians that balances mixer efficacy and circuit cost across different hardware backends and noise profiles?
- Is the unary (fermionic) encoding the best mapping for constrained optimization on near-term devices, or do more compact encodings yield better practical performance?
- How sensitive is performance to the heuristic choice of hopping integral t (energy-scale matching), and can better parameter-selection strategies be developed?
- How robust are the reported gains (cyclic driver reducing noise impact) under different noise models, and on other quantum hardware (e.g., superconducting qubits)?
- Can the cyclic-driver FQAOA be effectively applied to other types of constrained combinatorial optimization problems beyond portfolio optimization?
- To what extent do gate-count reductions translate into real advantage as device fidelities, connectivity, and compiler optimizations evolve?
- How do classical baselines (heuristics/solvers) compare in wall-clock time and solution quality when accounting for quantum overheads like post-selection and error-mitigation?
