## Abstract summary
The authors introduce a method using Quantum Signal Processing (QSP) to encode financial derivative payoffs directly into quantum amplitudes, removing the need for costly coherent quantum arithmetic. For common derivative contracts they report large resource reductions (notably ~16x fewer T-gates and ~4x fewer logical qubits) and estimate quantum advantage would require ~4.7k logical qubits and devices capable of ~10^9 T-gates at ~45 MHz. The technique is applied to payoff encoding (e.g., European calls and autocallables) and is argued to be extensible to other quantum state-preparation tasks.
## Methodology
The authors develop and analyze a method that replaces coherent quantum arithmetic in derivative-payoff encoding with Quantum Signal Processing (QSP). The approach encodes payoff functions directly into ancilla amplitudes by: (1) preparing a fixed-point binary register |x> representing a scalar (typically a log-return), (2) constructing a unitary (Usqrt) that, via a uniform superposition of indices and a binary comparator (implemented with a ripple-carry comparator), encodes sqrt(x) into the amplitude of an ancilla qubit, and (3) applying a QSP singular-value transformation driven by repeated applications of the constructed unitary and its inverse, interleaved with single-qubit Z-rotations whose angles (phase-factors) are chosen so the overall QSP polynomial P approximates the desired payoff-transformation f(x) on the domain [0,1]. The paper focuses on target payoff shapes common in finance (e.g. (A e^{x} - B)/C and compositions with sqrt), shows how to handle fixed-point registers with digits left of the binary point and negative inputs by offsetting, and provides explicit constructions for the required unitaries and projectors. Polynomial approximations are generated using an optimization-based Chebyshev expansion / minimax formulation on Chebyshev nodes (discrete optimization over coefficients of even Chebyshev polynomials), and available QSP phase-factor toolkits are referenced for computing the QSP phases. The method is applied to two derivative examples (European call and autocallable): the re-parameterization approach is used to load multivariate normal distributions for log-returns (N(µ,Σ)), logical payoff conditions are computed into ancilla qubits (using CNOT/Toffoli logic for comparisons and barriers), and QSP replaces the explicit exponentiation and payoff arithmetic at the final encoding step. For resource estimation the authors extend an existing Q# implementation from prior work, choose conservative fixed-point representations (n=15, p=5), pick polynomial degree (d=20) to meet a payoff-amplitude tolerance (ϵ_p = 1e-3), and apply Iterative Quantum Amplitude Estimation to convert single-shot amplitude accuracy into end-to-end resource counts. They decompose circuits to Clifford+T to estimate T-count, T-depth and logical qubit requirements and compare against a baseline arithmetic + lookup-table approach. The paper reports detailed resource comparisons (T-depth, T-count, logical qubits) and required logical T-gate clock rates for parity with a classical 1s runtime baseline.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum Signal Processing (QSP) / Quantum Singular Value Transformation (QSVT), Iterative Quantum Amplitude Estimation (IQAE), Chebyshev polynomial approximation (optimization-based minimax over Chebyshev nodes), Re-parameterization method for loading multivariate normal distributions (for log-returns), Comparator / ripple-carry comparator circuits (binary comparison), Clifford+T resource decomposition (for T-count/T-depth estimates)
**Frameworks:** Q#, QSPPACK (referenced), pyqsp (referenced)

- [supported] For representative derivative pricing instances (autocallable example with a = 3 assets, T = 20, target accuracy ϵ = 2×10^-3 and payoff amplitude precision ϵp = 10^-3) the QSP-based implementation substantially reduces resource requirements compared to a prior arithmetic/lookup approach.
- [supported] Using the specific QSP construction based on the Usqrt unitary (Eq. (13)) leads to the largest resource savings compared to both standard arithmetic and a Usin-based QSP.
- [supported] In the constructed benchmark, QSP (Usqrt) reduces T-gate count and logical qubits by factors of ≈16× and ≈4× respectively versus the arithmetic baseline considered.
- [supported] The paper provides explicit circuit constructions and numerical polynomial approximations (e.g., degree d = 20 Chebyshev polynomial for the autocallable payoff) used in the QSP workflow.
- [supported] End-to-end resource estimates (including iterative amplitude estimation) yield an Usqrt-QSP requirement of ≈4.7k logical qubits, total T-count ≈2.4×10^9, and total T-depth ≈4.5×10^7 for the benchmark instance.
- [supported] Based on the above, they estimate a quantum processor would need to execute T-gates at ≈45 MHz to match a classical one-second pricing runtime for the benchmark if Usqrt-QSP is used.
- [speculative] QSP-based methods are argued to be more natural/efficient than computing values via binary quantum arithmetic and then amplitude-encoding, and could be applied to other subroutines (e.g., state preparation) to reduce resources.
- [speculative] Extending QSP to multivariable transformations (M-QSP) could further reduce resources and broaden applicability (e.g., replacing quantum arithmetic in multivariate distribution preparation), but this remains future work.
- [supported] A practical limitation of the present approach is that standard QSP implementations apply (near-)arbitrary polynomial transforms of a single scalar; multivariate function approximation is not addressed in the presented constructions.

**Results summary:** The paper demonstrates that Quantum Signal Processing can be applied to the payoff-encoding component of derivative pricing quantum circuits to avoid expensive coherent quantum arithmetic. Through explicit circuit constructions and numerical polynomial approximations, the authors show large resource reductions for a realistic autocallable pricing benchmark: roughly 16× fewer T-gates and 4× fewer logical qubits compared to a recent arithmetic/lookup-table approach. They introduce a Usqrt-driven QSP workflow (preferred over a Usin-based variant) and provide end-to-end resource estimates (including amplitude estimation) that indicate a threshold for potential quantum advantage at the scale of a few thousand logical qubits and gigascale T-gate counts.

## Limitations
- QSP in its current form can only provide approximations to functions of a single variable.
- QSP transformations and the unitary constructions considered are limited to mappings whose domain and range lie in [0,1], requiring rescaling and/or shifts of inputs (e.g. adding s) to fit this interval.
- The QSP framework only implements polynomial transformations with definite parity (even or odd), which requires workaround extensions when the target function does not have definite parity.
- The paper focuses specifically on the payoff component of derivative pricing; other circuit components (e.g. state loading / N(µ,Σ)) are not replaced by QSP in this work.
- [inferred] Handling negative or large-magnitude inputs requires extra fixed-point arithmetic (e.g. shifting by s and larger registers), introducing overhead.
- [inferred] Polynomial approximation error and the required polynomial degree (and therefore QSP circuit depth) can grow with function complexity, affecting resource savings in some cases.
- [inferred] Implementations based on Usin (sin-based unitary) can incur very large T-depth due to many controlled-Ry rotations and rotation synthesis precision requirements.
- [inferred] Practical application requires reliable numerical methods to compute QSP phase factors for high-degree polynomials; numerical stability and scalability of phase-finding for large-degree transforms may be a concern.
- [inferred] The approach assumes certain payoff functional forms (e.g. (A e^x - B)/C) are amenable to QSP; less-structured or highly multivariate payoffs may not benefit without further development.
- [inferred] End-to-end resource estimates (e.g. logical clock rate, T-count) rely on fault-tolerant assumptions and chosen decomposition/synthesis strategies; practical realizations depend on future hardware and compilation optimizations.
## Open questions
- Can multivariable QSP (M-QSP) be developed and made practical so that it replaces quantum arithmetic for multivariate functions such as the N(µ,Σ) construction?
- How broadly applicable is the QSP amplitude-encoding approach to derivative payoffs beyond the (A e^x - B)/C family and to more complicated path-dependent payoffs?
- What are the best strategies to manage polynomial degree vs. approximation error trade-offs across different payoff types to maximize resource savings?
- How numerically stable and scalable are phase-factor computation tools for high-degree QSP polynomials in realistic financial instances?
- Can the Usqrt-driven QSP approach be integrated with other quantum subroutines (e.g. state preparation, generative loading) to further reduce overall resource requirements?
- What are the practical hardware and compilation requirements (error rates, logical clock rates, qubit connectivity) to realize the resource reductions predicted?
- How do the overheads for shifting/scaling inputs (to meet domain/range constraints) affect total resource advantage in diverse real-world contracts?
- Are there techniques to extend QSP to directly encode multivariate payoffs or combine multiple single-variable QSP transforms efficiently?

**Future work:**
- Research on multivariable QSP (M-QSP) to handle multivariate functions and potentially reduce quantum arithmetic in components like N(µ,Σ).
- Explore application of the Usqrt unitary and QSP techniques to further reduce resources in state preparation (extending ideas from Ref. [20]).
- Investigate broader application domains and other use cases where direct amplitude transformations via QSP can replace coherent quantum arithmetic.
- Develop and improve numerical/algorithmic tools for QSP phase-factor calculation and stable construction of high-degree polynomial approximations for finance-relevant functions.

- #idea:quantum-advantage — The Usqrt-driven QSP construction outperforms a Usin-QSP variant and a standard arithmetic+lookup baseline, achieving ~16x reduction in T-gate count and ~4x reduction in logical qubits for the autocallable example.

- #idea:hybrid-approach — The workflow is hybrid: classical Chebyshev/minimax polynomial optimization and external QSP phase-factor toolkits compute the QSP phases; quantum circuits perform the transformed amplitude encoding.
- #idea:near-term-feasibility — Although QSP reduces resources relative to arithmetic encodings, the required scale (thousands of logical qubits and gigascale T-counts) places practical advantage in the fault-tolerant era rather than NISQ.
- #limitation:qubit-count — The presented benchmark requires on the order of 4.7k logical qubits, indicating significant qubit-count limitations for near-term deployment.
- #limitation:noise — Resource and runtime estimates implicitly assume robust fault-tolerant operation and do not address noise/error-mitigation overheads for noisy hardware; achieving the T-gate throughput would demand substantial error correction.
- #limitation:data-encoding — Standard QSP is formulated for single-scalar polynomial transforms; multivariate payoff/state-preparation (M-QSP) extensions are noted as future work and not solved here.
- #limitation:no-empirical-validation — Results are based on analytical constructions, classical simulations, Q# resource estimation and toolkit computations; no experiments on physical quantum hardware are reported.
