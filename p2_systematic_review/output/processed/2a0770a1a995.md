## Abstract summary
The paper investigates quantum machine learning methods for financial forecasting, presenting two use cases: a Determinantal Point Process (DPP)-enhanced Random Forest for churn prediction that improves precision by ~6% and increases captured withdrawals, and quantum-inspired orthogonal and compound neural network layers for credit risk assessment that match classical performance with far fewer parameters. The authors report classical simulations, small-scale quantum processor experiments (including error mitigation), and argue these quantum ideas can be useful today as quantum-inspired classical methods and hold greater promise as quantum hardware improves.
## Methodology
This study combines classical and quantum-inspired machine learning methods applied to two financial forecasting use cases: customer churn prediction and SME credit-default prediction. For churn prediction, the authors design a DPP-enhanced Random Forest (DPP-RF) that replaces uniform row/column subsampling with Determinantal Point Process (d-DPP) sampling to select diverse subsets for training decision trees. Because full-dataset DPP sampling is costly, they implement a batched sampling pipeline (split training set into batches, sample rows via d-DPP per batch, sample features via d-DPP, train groups of decision trees on small patches, aggregate patches and repeat) and tune hyperparameters by grid search and cross-validation. They benchmark classical SVD-based DPP sampling (dppy library) and implement small-scale quantum DPP circuits (Clifford-loader-based circuits comprised of RBS gates) on IBM quantum processors for reduced-size problems (PCA to d=2,3 and small batch sizes n=4,5,6,8). For credit risk, they design quantum-inspired neural network layers: orthogonal layers (unary data loader + RBS-based parameterized circuit that implements n×n orthogonal matrices) and compound layers (non-unary loaders + RBS circuits whose blocks act as higher-order compound matrices). They introduce an Expectation-per-subspace compound layer that computes per-hamming-weight expectations as outputs. Neural networks are trained classically (JAX) with standard encoders and classification heads; imputation (IterativeImputer with Bayesian ridge), PCA, and hyperparameter searches (ray-tune) are used. Quantum-hardware experiments focus on inference using classically-trained models on IBM devices (ibmq_guadelupe and ibm_hanoi), with error-detection (Hamming-weight postselection) and mitigation/suppression (readout mitigation, Qiskit Runtime Sampler with transpiler optimizations, dynamical decoupling, and M3 measurement mitigation). Metrics include precision at low recall (6%) for churn, ROC-AUC and Gini for credit risk, training time and business KPIs (withdrawals captured).

**Algorithms used:** Determinantal Point Processes (d-DPP), Random Forest, DPP-Enhanced Random Forest (DPP-RF), Classical SVD-based DPP sampling (dppy), Quantum DPP sampling (Clifford-loader based circuits), Clifford loader circuits (with RBS gates), Unary / RY / H (Hadamard) data loaders, RBS-parameterized ansatz (Pyramid, X, Butterfly), Quantum Orthogonal Neural Networks (orthogonal layers), Quantum Compound Neural Networks (compound / expectation-per-subspace layer), PCA (dimensionality reduction), Decision trees (single-tree experiments and ensembles), Classical backpropagation for orthogonal layers, IterativeImputer (Bayesian ridge) for missing data imputation
**Frameworks:** dppy (DPPy) library, JAX, ray-tune, scikit-learn, Qiskit (including Qiskit Runtime Sampler)

**Experimental setup:** Classical experiments run on an Intel Core i5-8350U CPU @1.70GHz with 24 GB RAM. DPP sampling for classical experiments used SVD-based algorithms from the dppy library. Quantum-circuit simulations (noiseless) were used for small-scale experiments. Hardware experiments used IBM quantum processors: ibmq_guadelupe (16 qubits) for small DPP sampling circuits and ibm_hanoi (27 qubits) for orthogonal/compound layer inference. For quantum-hardware-ready DPP experiments the data were PCA-reduced to d = 2 or 3 and batched into n = 4,5,6,8 points per batch. QNN training performed classically; inference circuits were transpiled with optimization level 3 and run with dynamical decoupling and M3 measurement mitigation where indicated.

**Dataset:** Two proprietary Itaú datasets plus public benchmarks. 1) Churn dataset: ~304,000 datapoints with 153 features; target: binary churn indicator (customer withdrawing more than a threshold in the next three months); time-based split into training (≈174,000) and test (≈130,000). 2) SME credit-default dataset: ≈141,500 observations with 32 features (31 numeric, 1 categorical); time-based split into training (≈74,700 covering 12 months) and test (≈66,800 covering subsequent 8 months). Additional reduced/sampled subsets were used for quantum hardware experiments (train ≈1,000; test ≈10,000) and subsamples of 300 points for QPU inference. Public OpenML classification datasets (e.g., madelon, credit-default, bank-marketing, wine, california, etc.) were used for additional benchmarking.

## Findings
- [supported] Replacing uniform subsampling in Random Forests with DPP-based sampling (DPP-RF) improved precision on the bank churn task from 71.6% to 77.5% (measured at low recall, 6%).
- [supported] The DPP-RF increased business-relevant capture metrics: percent of total withdrawals captured increased from 61.42% to 62.77%, and percent of maximum possible withdrawals captured increased from 69.18% to 70.72% (11 test months average).
- [supported] The DPP-RF required substantially more training time on the classical implementation (311 s for baseline RF vs ~54 minutes for DPP-RF on the reported hardware).
- [supported] Classical DPP-RF benchmarking on several OpenML classification datasets shows small AUC improvements on some datasets and comparable performance on others.
- [supported] Quantum DPP circuits (as per Kerenidis & Prakash construction) were implemented on small problem sizes on IBM hardware: hardware sampling matched simulator performance for very small matrices (e.g., up to (6,2) in their reduced experiment) but performance degraded for larger sampled dimensions due to device noise.
- [supported] Orthogonal neural-network layers (quantum-inspired RBS-based constructions) can be trained classically and, on the SME credit dataset, match or slightly exceed the fully-connected baseline while using far fewer trainable parameters (13 vs 64).
- [supported] Expectation-per-subspace compound layers were implemented in classical simulation; they matched classical fully-connected performance on the SME dataset while also using the smaller parameterization, but did not outperform the orthogonal variant.
- [supported] Public-dataset experiments with orthogonal layers (OrthoFNN) showed mixed results versus classical FNNs: sometimes improved ROC-AUC, sometimes slightly worse — results vary by dataset.
- [supported] Classically trained orthogonal model inference executed on IBM 'hanoi' showed: noiseless-simulator subsample Gini ≈ 54.19%, raw hardware Gini ≈ 50.19%, and with compilation + dynamical decoupling + M3 mitigation Gini ≈ 53.68% (close to noiseless).
- [supported] Classically trained compound-layer model (ExpFNN) inference on hardware performed significantly worse under noise (hardware Gini ≈ 40.20% vs noiseless 53.90%), indicating compound layers are more sensitive to current device errors.
- [speculative] Quantum DPP sampling and quantum orthogonal/compound layers have the potential to provide runtime or representational advantages as quantum hardware improves (authors argue potential future benefit).
- [speculative] The quantum DPP circuit construction can achieve gate complexity O(n d) and circuit depth O(d log n) for orthogonal X ∈ R^{n×d} (theoretical claim from prior work cited).
- [supported] Error suppression and mitigation techniques (circuit optimization, dynamical decoupling, M3 measurement mitigation) substantially improved hardware inference results for the orthogonal-layer model, reducing the gap to noiseless simulation to ≈0.5 percentage points Gini on the chosen subsample.
- [speculative] Quantum ideas (orthogonal/compound layers, DPP sampling) can produce practical quantum-inspired classical ML solutions today (e.g., orthogonal layers trained classically) and may scale better on future fault-tolerant quantum hardware.

**Results summary:** This preprint presents two main contributions for financial forecasting: (1) a DPP-enhanced Random Forest (DPP-RF) for churn prediction that uses determinantal point process sampling for more diverse subsampling; in classical experiments DPP-RF improved precision (71.6% → 77.5% at low recall) and modestly increased captured withdrawals while incurring a larger training time; small-scale quantum DPP circuits were executed on IBM hardware and matched simulators for very small instances but degraded with larger dimensions due to noise. (2) Quantum-inspired neural network layers: orthogonal (RBS-based) and compound layers. Orthogonal layers trained classically matched or slightly exceeded baseline fully-connected networks on a SME credit-risk dataset while using far fewer parameters (13 vs 64), and inference of classically trained orthogonal models on IBM hardware reached near-simulator performance after error mitigation. Compound layers explored larger Hilbert subspaces but were more noise-sensitive on current hardware. The authors argue these approaches are promising both as classical quantum-inspired methods today and for future quantum hardware, but they do not claim a demonstrated large-scale quantum advantage.

- Percent of total withdrawals captured (average over 11 test months): baseline 61.42% vs DPP-RF 62.77% (improvement 1.35 pp).
- Percent of maximum possible withdrawals captured (avg over 11 months): baseline 69.18% vs DPP-RF 70.72% (improvement 1.54 pp).

- OpenML ROC-AUC comparisons for DPP-RF vs RF (examples): madelon 0.941 vs 0.916 (DPP-RF better), bank-marketing both 0.881 (equal), california 0.963 vs 0.962 (DPP-RF slightly better).

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The paper demonstrates small-scale quantum circuit implementations that reproduce classical DPP/orthogonal-layer behavior for tiny problem sizes and shows that error mitigation can substantially close the gap to noiseless simulation. However, no large-scale or provable quantum computational advantage in runtime or predictive performance is demonstrated; claims about future advantages are presented as potential/theoretical contingent on improved hardware and algorithms.
## Limitations
- Current quantum hardware noise and errors degrade performance for larger circuits; quantum DPP sampling on hardware falters as matrix dimensions grow (author-stated).
- DPP-RF has substantially longer training time than classical Random Forest due to the DPP sampling bottleneck (author-stated).
- Classical DPP sampling algorithms remain slow or infeasible on very large datasets without recent algorithmic improvements (author-stated).
- Hardware experiments required drastic problem-size reductions (PCA to d=2,3; batches of n=4-8), limiting realism and scalability of the quantum demonstrations (author-stated).
- Compound quantum layers require exponential classical simulation/training time (author-stated).
- Error mitigation and suppression techniques are necessary but add overhead and may not scale; some mitigation methods (e.g., full readout calibration) become intractable as qubit count grows (author-stated).
- No demonstrated large-scale quantum advantage: classical or quantum-inspired solutions currently match or outperform quantum implementations for production-scale problems (author-stated).
- Proprietary datasets and models are not publicly available, limiting reproducibility and external validation (author-stated).
- Business impact evaluation depends on downstream human interventions and profit-per-dollar assumptions, adding uncertainty to real-world benefit estimates (author-stated).
- [inferred] Scalability to enterprise-scale dimensions (n, d) is unproven for both the quantum circuits and the proposed sampling/ensemble strategies.
- [inferred] Hyperparameter search and model design are constrained by batch-size limits for DPP sampling, potentially restricting model optimality.
- [inferred] Compound layers that exploit the full Hilbert space are likely very sensitive to noise on near-term hardware, reducing practical utility until error rates fall.
- [inferred] Some error-mitigation approaches rely on heuristics or classical pre/post-processing that may impose prohibitive overhead for larger problems.

## Open questions
- Can quantum DPP sampling achieve practical, reproducible speedups and accuracy improvements on large-scale, real-world financial datasets compared to best classical algorithms?
- At what scale of qubits, gate fidelity, and circuit depth will quantum orthogonal/compound layers (or full quantum DPP circuits) outperform classical or quantum-inspired solutions in finance?
- How can compound layers be trained efficiently in practice—via classical simulation, hybrid quantum-classical training, or new algorithmic techniques—without exponential cost?
- Which quantum ansatzes or parameterized circuit architectures (butterfly, pyramid, X, others) are most effective and robust for tabular financial data across diverse tasks?
- How well do orthogonal and compound layers generalize across different financial use cases (credit, churn, fraud, pricing), and which tasks benefit most from quantum-inspired structure?
- To what extent can error mitigation and suppression techniques be scaled to close the gap between noisy hardware execution and noiseless simulation for larger circuits?
- What is the end-to-end business ROI when accounting for model improvements, intervention success rates, operational cost of retraining, and latency constraints?
- Can improved classical sampling algorithms or quantum-inspired classical methods capture most of the benefit of DPP-enhanced ensembles without requiring quantum hardware?
- How should quantum ML models be integrated into production risk and decisioning pipelines given current limits on training time, inference latency, and explainability?
- What are the data governance, privacy, and regulatory implications of deploying quantum or quantum-inspired models in financial services?

**Future work:**
- Improve classical DPP sampling methods and further develop quantum DPP algorithms to reduce runtime and make DPP-RF training more practical.
- Test larger compound layers and more expressive quantum architectures as quantum hardware improves, to explore larger portions of the Hilbert space.
- Continue hardware experiments with enhanced error mitigation and suppression techniques (e.g., dynamical decoupling, advanced mitigation like M3) to narrow the gap to noiseless performance.
- Apply the proposed methods to additional financial use cases and public benchmarks to assess generality and robustness; tune architectures per dataset/task.
- Investigate hybrid quantum-classical training approaches for compound layers to mitigate exponential classical simulation costs.
- Explore quantum-inspired classical implementations of the orthogonal/compound ideas to gain near-term practical benefits on GPUs/CPUs.
- Study integration pathways for quantum/quantum-inspired models into production workflows, including latency, retraining cadence, and impact assessment tied to business KPIs.
- Research scalability of error-mitigation strategies and optimization/transpilation pipelines to improve practical performance on NISQ devices.
- Encourage reproducibility by releasing non-sensitive benchmarks, reference implementations, and (where possible) anonymized datasets or synthetic benchmarks.
