## Abstract summary
This paper proposes a blockchain-driven, quantum-resistant identity verification framework for microservice-to-microservice authentication in financial cloud infrastructures. It integrates five components—Quantum Hash Anchoring with Adaptive Commitments (QHA-AC), Federated Quantum Entangled Tokens (FQET), Temporal Blockchain Quantum Graph Signatures (TBQGS), Quantum Zero-Knowledge Identity Tree (QZKIT), and Reinforcement Learning-based Quantum Identity Drift Analysis (RL-QIDA)—and reports high identity spoofing detection (up to ~97%), low authentication latency (~1 ms), strong drift detection (~92–95%), and minimal privacy leakage (<0.5%).
## Methodology
The authors propose and evaluate a multi-component blockchain-driven quantum-resistant identity verification framework for cloud microservices. They design five methods (QHA-AC, FQET, TBQGS, QZKIT, RL-QIDA) and implement an experimental testbed: a hybrid cloud cluster of 10 virtual microservice nodes, where quantum-entropy simulations are executed on IBM Qiskit backends, the blockchain layer runs on a Hyperledger Fabric testbed, and identity components are containerized with Docker. Identity generation uses Quantum Random Walks to produce temporally-evolving quantum hashes (QHA-AC) anchored on-chain; federated entangled tokens (FQET) distribute entangled key pairs across zones and authenticate via fidelity thresholding; temporal identity behavior is modeled as directed acyclic graphs (TBQGS) and encoded by a unitary operator to form compact quantum graph signatures stored/validated via blockchain consensus; privacy-preserving identity proofs are performed by a Quantum Zero-Knowledge Identity Tree (QZKIT) combining Merkle-like roots and a quantum Schnorr-like protocol with Quantum Oblivious Transfer; and a Deep RL agent (RL-QIDA) monitors identity behavior embeddings, computes confidence scores, and updates policy parameters by policy-gradient reinforcement learning to detect drift and trigger trust recalibration. Experiments use three datasets (synthetic FINStack-Micro, MS-FinBench real service logs, and a custom CloudAuthSim-Q with 1000 simulated identity traces) and compare detection accuracy, latency, and drift detection against three baseline methods. Reported metrics include identity spoofing detection accuracy, authentication latency, drift detection accuracy, privacy leakage, and blockchain consensus timestamps.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum Random Walk (QRW) for entropy-driven hash evolution, Quantum Hash Anchoring with Adaptive Commitments (QHA-AC), Federated Quantum Entangled Token generation and fidelity-based verification (FQET), Temporal Blockchain Quantum Graph Signatures (TBQGS) encoding via unitary operator, Quantum Zero-Knowledge Schnorr-like protocol with Quantum Oblivious Transfer (QOT) (QZKIT), Deep Reinforcement Learning (policy-gradient) for RL-QIDA (identity drift detection and trust recalibration)
**Frameworks:** IBM Qiskit, Hyperledger Fabric, Docker

**Experimental setup:** Hybrid cloud cluster of 10 virtual microservice nodes. Quantum entropy simulations executed on IBM Qiskit backends. Blockchain layer implemented on a Hyperledger Fabric testbed. Identity verification components containerized using Docker.

**Dataset:** FINStack-Micro (synthetic financial microservice samples); MS-FinBench (real-time service logs from open-source financial microservices including metadata, authentication traces, and performance metrics); CloudAuthSim-Q (custom simulation dataset of authentication, drift, and entropy fluctuations across simulated service identity traces, 1000 traces).

## Open questions
- How do the proposed mechanisms (QHA-AC, FQET, TBQGS, QZKIT, RL-QIDA) perform on real quantum hardware and real-world quantum communication links subject to realistic noise and decoherence?
- What are the formal security properties and provable guarantees of QZKIT (quantum zero-knowledge), the quantum Schnorr adaptation, and the QHA-AC constructions under standard quantum adversary models?
- What are the practical requirements and architectures for distributing and managing entanglement (FQET) across federated cloud zones—e.g., need for quantum repeaters, trusted nodes, or hybrid classical fallback?
- How scalable is TBQGS blockchain anchoring when the number of services, identity state updates, and graph events grows by orders of magnitude? What are on‑chain storage and consensus throughput limits?
- How robust is RL-QIDA to adversarial drift injection, adversarial training, concept drift types not seen during training, and cross-domain transfer (different financial services)?
- Can the fidelity threshold (θ), trust modulation parameters (α, β), and DRL hyperparameters be adaptively tuned in an online, robust way, and what control-theoretic guarantees exist for such adaptation?
- What end-to-end latency and availability trade-offs arise when combining quantum entanglement distribution, blockchain consensus, and microservice communication in realistic network topologies?
- How can the proposed framework be integrated with existing identity management, service mesh frameworks, regulatory/compliance requirements, and auditing practices in financial institutions?
- What are the energy, cost, and operational implications of deploying quantum-resilient identity systems at production scale, including lifecycle management of quantum keys/tokens?
- Which residual attack surfaces remain (e.g., side-channel, hardware compromise, entanglement manipulation) and how should defenses be extended to mitigate them?
- How should privacy leakage be measured and audited in hybrid classical–quantum identity trees (QZKIT), and how do legal/privacy frameworks apply to quantum-derived identity artifacts?
- What standards, data formats and migration paths are needed to enable interoperability between classical identity infrastructures and the proposed quantum-blockchain hybrid identities?
