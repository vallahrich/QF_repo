## Abstract summary
The preprint proposes a hybrid quantum-classical architecture for multivariate time-series forecasting that integrates a classical encoder, a Parameterized Quantum Circuit (PQC) variational processor, and a classical decoder trained end-to-end with gradient-based optimization. Experiments on electricity load, stock prices, and weather datasets report improved MSE/MAE/R² over LSTM/Transformer/ARIMA baselines, with robustness analysis to NISQ noise and an ablation study showing benefits from the quantum components.
## Methodology
The paper proposes an end-to-end hybrid quantum-classical architecture for multivariate time-series forecasting. A classical encoder (MLP or 1D CNN) first transforms sliding windows of raw time-series into a compact feature vector whose dimension is matched to the quantum processor. Features are encoded into a Parameterized Quantum Circuit (PQC) using angle encoding (alternative amplitude encoding discussed but not used). The PQC comprises repeated layers of parameterized single-qubit rotations and entangling two-qubit gates (e.g., CNOT or CZ); Pauli-Z expectation values on each qubit are measured to form a quantum feature vector. A classical decoder (fully connected layer) maps the quantum outputs to the prediction horizon. Training is performed end-to-end minimizing MSE, with gradients through the quantum circuit obtained via the parameter-shift rule and classical optimizers such as Adam or RMSProp. Experiments use 5-fold cross-validation with 70:15:15 train/validation/test splits, fixed random seeds, and results averaged over multiple runs. Robustness to quantum noise and an ablation (removing quantum components) are evaluated. Implementation uses a PyTorch + PennyLane hybrid stack with support for simulation and backends (Qiskit, Amazon Braket).

**Algorithms used:** Parameterized Quantum Circuit (PQC), Variational/Parametrized Quantum Circuit (VQC), Quantum Neural Network (QNN), Parameter-shift rule (for gradients), Angle encoding (data encoding), Amplitude encoding (discussed), Classical LSTM (baseline), Transformer (baseline), ARIMA (baseline), Adam optimizer, RMSProp optimizer
**Frameworks:** PyTorch, PennyLane, Qiskit (supported backend), Amazon Braket (supported backend)

**Experimental setup:** Hybrid PyTorch + PennyLane implementation. Quantum execution primarily via simulator (PennyLane default qubit simulator), with support for execution on IBM Qiskit or Amazon Braket backends. Experiments run with fixed random seeds and averaged over multiple runs; 5-fold cross-validation with 70:15:15 splits.

**Dataset:** Three benchmark time-series datasets: (1) Electricity load forecasting — hourly regional grid load; (2) Stock price prediction — daily stock prices of a major technology company (financial dataset included as 'Stock Price'); (3) Weather forecasting — hourly temperature and humidity from a weather station. The financial dataset is daily stock prices of a major technology company (source unspecified).

**Results summary:** The preprint reports that a hybrid quantum-classical architecture (classical encoder, PQC variational layer, classical decoder) trained end-to-end outperformed the listed classical baselines (LSTM, Transformer, ARIMA) on three benchmark forecasting tasks according to MSE, MAE and R² as measured in their experiments. An ablation removing the quantum circuit led to worse metrics, and a simulated robustness study showed tolerance to moderate noise but sensitivity to high noise and deep circuits.
