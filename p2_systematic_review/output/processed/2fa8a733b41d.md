## Abstract summary
The authors implement a discrete-time quantum walk in momentum space using an ultracold 87Rb Bose–Einstein condensate, employing microwave pulses as the coin operator and a quantum-resonance ratchet derived from the atom-optics kicked rotor as the conditional momentum shift. They demonstrate hallmark quantum-walk behaviors (ballistic spreading, controllable bias via coin or ratchet, reversibility through unitary evolution) and observe the quantum-to-classical transition by adding phase noise, highlighting advantages such as robustness in momentum space and extendability to higher dimensions and many-body regimes.
## Methodology
The authors implemented a discrete-time quantum walk in momentum space using an ultra-cold 87Rb Bose–Einstein condensate (BEC). Each walk step U_step = T M consists of a microwave (MW) coin operator M that coherently mixes two hyperfine ground states (|F=1,mF=0> and |F=2,mF=0>) and a conditional momentum shift operator T realized as a quantum ratchet based on the atom-optics kicked rotor (AOKR). The coin operator is implemented by resonant MW pulses (e.g. π/2 pulses for balanced coins) with controllable phase and pulse area. The shift operator is implemented by short pulses of an off-resonant one-dimensional standing-wave optical lattice (kicks) applied at quantum resonance (Talbot time) so that the free-evolution factor is the identity; the ratchet effect is produced by preparing the initial external state via a Bragg pulse and by choosing sign/detuning of the lattice light so the two internal states experience oppositely directed ratchet currents. Key experimental parameters reported include kick (ratchet) strengths k (examples: k = 1.45 and k = 1.8), kicking-pulse durations in the Raman–Nath regime (≈384 ns), pulse period equal to the Talbot time (T ≈ Tr ≈ 103 µs), and typical BEC sizes (~70,000 atoms). Momentum distributions (population P(n,j)) are measured after j steps by time-of-flight expansion (~10 ms) and absorption imaging. Numerical modelling used single-particle Floquet-operator evolution with split-operator techniques, averaging over quasimomentum components (Bloch decomposition), and included modeling of a thermal cloud contribution and estimates of spontaneous-emission effects via quantum-jump methods. The authors also performed controlled decoherence experiments by randomizing coin-phase to study the quantum-to-classical transition and demonstrated biased coins and biased ratchets and reversal sequences by applying Hermitian-conjugate operators to recover initial states.

**Experimental setup:** BEC of ~70,000 87Rb atoms prepared in an optical dipole trap and released for the walk. A one-dimensional off-resonant standing-wave optical lattice (λ ≈ 780 nm, ~3.4 GHz red detuned from the referenced transition, formed by two beams incident ~53° from vertical to set spatial period AG) is pulsed to produce AOKR kicks. Pulse timing: kick pulse length ≈ 384 ns (Raman–Nath regime), pulse period set to Talbot time Tr ≈ 103 µs. Bragg pulse used to prepare initial external superposition. Microwave pulses (~6.834 GHz carrier synthesized by mixing a 6.8 GHz oscillator with RF ~34.68 MHz) implement coin rotations (π/2 pulses for Hadamard). Standing-wave nodes, intensity, duration and phase controlled via AOMs driven by AWGs. Imaging: ~10 ms time-of-flight and absorption imaging on the F=2 transition; repump used to pump F=1 atoms prior to imaging. MW amplified to ~30 dBm and delivered via C-band horn. Thermal cloud fraction ~15%.

## Findings
- [supported] Implemented a discrete-time quantum walk (QW) in momentum space using a Bose–Einstein condensate of 87Rb, with two internal hyperfine states as the coin and momentum eigenstates as the walk space.
- [supported] Each walk step is realized by a microwave (MW) coin operator (coin toss) followed by a conditional momentum shift implemented via a quantum-ratchet (atom-optics kicked rotor, AOKR) shift operator, producing entanglement between internal and external degrees of freedom.
- [supported] The experiment reproduces hallmark quantum-walk features: ballistic splitting of the momentum distribution and interference peaks distinct from classical (diffusive/Gaussian) walks.
- [supported] Demonstrated control/steering of the walk direction and speed by (a) implementing a biased coin (unequal MW superposition amplitudes) and (b) implementing a biased ratchet (differential detuning so the two internal states experience different kick strengths).
- [supported] Demonstrated reversibility of the quantum walk by applying the Hermitian-conjugate sequence of operators to recover the initial state (experimentally shown for an 8-step forward + 8-step reversed sequence).
- [supported] Observed and characterized the quantum-to-classical transition by adding phase noise to the coin tosses: signatures of classical behavior appear at modest phase randomness, and fully classical (Gaussian) behavior arises when coin phase is randomized over 2π.
- [supported] Identified an experimental sweet-spot ratchet strength (k ≈ 1.45) that best matches the standard nearest-neighbor quantum walk (q=1); larger k (e.g., k=1.8) yields faster splitting but increased decoherence/dephasing in practice.
- [supported] Current experimental implementation supports discrete walks up to ≈20 steps (limited primarily by momentum-detection window), and detection/step-count can be extended by modest apparatus changes.
- [supported] Quantitative observations: emergence of classical signatures at ≈8% uniform coin-phase randomness, dominant classical behaviour at ≈20% randomness, and fully classical behaviour for coin-phase randomized over full 2π.
- [supported] The experimental platform gives independent access to internal (hyperfine) and external (momentum) degrees of freedom and thus fine-grained controllability (coin and shift), enabling demonstrations such as biased walks and reversal.
- [speculative] The authors propose that momentum-space QWs realized this way are readily extendable to higher-dimensional walks (by multi-dimensional lattices or extra spatial-frequency components) and to many-body quantum walks including interaction effects because the walker is a BEC.
- [speculative] Proposed applications include use as a sensitive atomic interferometer (via reversible recombination of momentum currents), implementations of driven walks, and momentum-space quantum algorithms (e.g., searches of marked momentum states) leveraging coin control.
- [speculative] The claim that this momentum-space realization offers distinct advantages over other implementations (robustness in momentum space, straightforward access to both degrees of freedom, and favorable step counts) is presented as an argument supported by experimental controllability but not as a computational speedup demonstration.
- [supported] Numerical/experimental modeling accounts for thermal fraction (~15% thermal cloud) and estimates spontaneous-emission probability per kick per atom below ~1%, both of which influence observed fidelities and decoherence.

**Results summary:** The authors experimentally realized a discrete-time quantum walk in momentum space using an 87Rb Bose–Einstein condensate. Each step combined a microwave coin toss (internal-state mixing) and a conditional momentum shift implemented by a quantum ratchet (AOKR). They observed characteristic quantum-walk behavior (ballistic splitting and interference), demonstrated controllable steering via biased coins and biased ratchets, and achieved reversal of the walk to recover the initial state. By adding phase noise to the coin operation they mapped the quantum-to-classical transition (signatures appearing at ≈8% coin-phase randomness, dominant classical behavior at ≈20%, and fully classical when randomized over 2π). They identify an optimal ratchet strength (k≈1.45) for nearest-neighbor coupling, report practical limits (detection window and thermal fraction), and estimate negligible spontaneous-emission-induced decoherence at their parameters. The paper discusses extensions and potential applications (higher-dimensional walks, many-body regimes, interferometry, and algorithmic uses) as future or proposed directions.

**Performance claims:**
- Ratchet strength k ≈ 1.45 best matches a standard nearest-neighbor (q=1) quantum walk in momentum space [supported].
- Ratchet strength k = 1.8 yields faster splitting and faster energy growth but shows signs of decoherence/dephasing (saturation of energy) in practice [supported].
- Quantum-to-classical transition: signatures appear at ≈8% uniform randomness in coin phase; walk becomes dominantly classical at ≈20% randomness; fully classical when coin phase randomized over full 2π [supported].
- Demonstrated reversible evolution by performing 8 forward steps and 8 reversed steps, recovering the initial state (reversal fidelity limited by quasimomentum spread and thermal cloud) [supported].
- Current setup allows realization of discrete walks up to ≈20 steps; limitation mainly from imaging/detection momentum window (can be improved) [supported].
- Estimated thermal (non-BEC) fraction ≈15% in experiment, contributing a central amorphous momentum signal that does not follow the ratchet dynamics [supported].
- Estimated spontaneous-emission probability ≲1% per kick per atom under their parameters, so spontaneous emission is negligible for walks up to ~20 steps [supported].
- Each ratchet step shifts average momentum by ≈1 two-photon recoil (nearest-neighbor coupling in momentum space) when using the chosen initial state and resonance conditions [supported].
- Talbot-time quantum resonance used: pulse period chosen as a full Talbot time (Tr ≈ 103 μs in experimental units) to allow MW coin toss between kicks [supported in methods].
## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The paper demonstrates genuine quantum-walk behavior (entanglement between coin and momentum, interference, ballistic spreading and reversibility) experimentally, but it does not demonstrate a computational or provable performance advantage over classical algorithms. Claims that the platform is promising for quantum algorithms and interferometry are proposed as future/speculative applications rather than empirically demonstrated quantum advantage.
## Limitations
- Maximum experimentally demonstrated walk length is ~20 steps (detection and decoherence limit).
- Momentum detection window / imaging system limits observable momentum range and thus limits walk extension.
- Residual thermal (non-BEC) cloud (~15%) produces an amorphous central population that does not participate in the ratchet and reduces fidelity.
- Decoherence and dephasing appear at larger ratchet strengths (e.g., k = 1.8) due to more intense light; energy growth saturates in that regime.
- Reversal fidelity is sensitive to non-ideal quasimomentum (quantum-resonance conditions) and thermal-cloud fraction.
- Current implementation realizes a one-dimensional walk; higher-dimensional walks are not yet implemented.
- Many-body (interaction) effects are not included in the present experiments; the BEC is operated in a regime with negligible nonlinearity.
- Quantum-resonance (Talbot-time) timing and quasimomentum control requirements impose stringent experimental stability constraints.
- The quantum-to-classical transition is readily induced by coin-phase noise, indicating sensitivity to coin-phase stability.
- [inferred] Scalability beyond demonstrated parameters (more steps, larger atom numbers with interactions, higher dimensions) is unproven and likely constrained by decoherence, control precision, and detection.
- [inferred] The work does not demonstrate execution of quantum algorithms or concrete quantum-information tasks on this platform; applicability to algorithmic workloads is untested.
- [inferred] While spontaneous emission was estimated to be negligible at current parameters, increased pulse intensity or longer sequences could make spontaneous emission a limiting decoherence channel.
- [inferred] Practical implementation of biased ratchets or coins for algorithmic steering may require tighter control of detuning/power than demonstrated.
