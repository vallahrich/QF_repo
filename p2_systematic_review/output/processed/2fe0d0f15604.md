## Abstract summary
The authors propose a hybrid quantum-classical classification method for credit sales risk that combines a Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) ansatz with the SWAP-Test to compare quantum-encoded customer records. Classical features are normalized and encoded as multi-qubit states; VQE is trained to produce class pattern states and the SWAP-Test evaluates similarity for classification. The approach is designed to be compact (logarithmic qubit scaling), suitable for NISQ devices, and achieves high accuracy in experiments compared to classical baselines.
## Methodology
The authors propose a hybrid quantum-classical classification pipeline for credit-sales risk based on the Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) to generate class-representative quantum pattern states and the SWAP-Test to compare/score individual normalized sample states against those pattern states. Classical preprocessing first applies min-max normalization to continuous features and then converts each observation into an n-qubit pure state by padding to 2^n amplitudes (adding a zero feature when required) and renormalizing amplitudes (square-root normalization across amplitudes). Entanglement in the prepared sample states is analyzed (using EntDetector) to inform ansatz design. Two ansatz architectures were tested: a standard multi-layer rotation + entangling layer (variant A) and a more compact tailored ansatz (variant B) using fewer Ry rotations and CNOT/CZ entanglers. For each class (and sub-group within a class) the ansatz parameters θ are trained by minimizing a cost derived from measured probability distributions (sum of absolute differences of probabilities) or equivalently minimizing the VQE expectation EVQE = min_θ Σ_j ω_j ⟨0|U†(θ) P_j U(θ)|0⟩. Optimization was performed with classical optimizers (SPSA for the 3-qubit experiments and COBYLA for larger-register experiments). After training, classification of a probe is done by preparing the probe state and performing a SWAP-Test with the class pattern-state; the SWAP-Test first-qubit measurement probability P0 (P0 = 1/2 + 1/2 |⟨ψ|ϕ⟩|^2) is compared to tuned acceptance thresholds (Λ, Ω) to decide membership. The pipeline was evaluated on a small proprietary dataset from a Polish pharmaceutical wholesaler (seven input features representing arrears, dues, delay days, declared payment, and three booleans), using 3 qubits to encode eight amplitudes (padded), and experiments report training accuracies, optimizer runtimes, and comparison with classical classifiers (SVMs, RandomForest, etc.).

**Algorithms used:** Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE), SWAP-Test, PCA (exploratory), Entanglement detection (partial-trace based EntDetector), SPSA optimizer, COBYLA optimizer, Classical classifiers for comparison: SVM, k-NN, Random Forest, Decision Tree, MLP, AdaBoost, GaussianNB, Gaussian Process, QDA
**Frameworks:** Python (general), Scikit-Learn (classical ML), SciPy (optimizers), Seaborn (visualization), EntDetector (entanglement detection)

**Experimental setup:** Experiments were implemented and executed in Python. Training-time measurements were run on a local workstation (Intel Xeon W-2245 3.9 GHz, Windows 11, Python 3.10.7). The authors also mention availability of a 'GPU µ-Lab' at their institute and reference IBM Q Experience as a realistic target for SWAP-Test executions, but the reported runs/timings appear to be local simulations rather than runs on a named QPU.

**Dataset:** Proprietary credit-sales dataset from a Polish pharmaceutical wholesaler (period: 2003). Each observation contains seven decision variables: (x1) customer arrears amount (real), (x2) total current dues (real), (x3) days of delay of the oldest invoice (integer), (x4) declared but not posted payment amount (real), (x5) past cooperation reliability (boolean), (x6) evaluation of customer's cooperating entities (boolean), (x7) promissory note presence (boolean). An auxiliary x8=0 feature was added to reach 8 amplitudes for 3-qubit encoding.

## Findings

- [supported] The authors preprocessed data by classical normalization followed by amplitude encoding (padding 7 features to 8 amplitudes) to produce 3-qubit input states.
- [supported] Entanglement was detected in the normalized sample states (most samples showed all three qubits entangled).

- [supported] The SWAP-Test was used to compare pattern states and test samples; the overlap result maps to the probability formula P0 = 1/2 + 1/2 |⟨ψ|ϕ⟩|^2.
- [speculative] The authors assert the approach is compact and requires only a logarithmic number of qubits (amplitude encoding) and thus is suitable for NISQ devices.

**Results summary:** The paper presents a targeted simulation study applying a VQE ansatz plus SWAP-Test comparator to a credit-sales risk (binary) classification task. Data were classical-normalized and amplitude-encoded into 3-qubit states (7 features padded to 8 amplitudes). Entanglement was present in the encoded samples. In simulations, tuned 3-qubit ansatz circuits achieved up to 100% accuracy on the training set; other VQE configurations yielded high but lower training accuracies. Several classical ML baselines also achieved 100% on the same training data. Reported training times on a CPU were modest for 3-qubit runs (~30–35s with SPSA) but substantially larger for 8-qubit runs (>600s). The authors argue (theoretically/speculatively) that the method is compact (logarithmic qubit scaling) and suitable for NISQ devices, but no empirical demonstration on quantum hardware nor out-of-sample/generalization benchmarks are provided.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The authors claim a potential advantage based on amplitude encoding (logarithmic qubit count) and compact VQE circuits amenable to NISQ devices. However, this is not empirically demonstrated: experiments are simulator-based, classical baselines reached equal training accuracy (including 100%), no out-of-sample/generalization or hardware-noise benchmarks are shown, and no clear runtime or accuracy advantage over classical methods is proven.
## Limitations
- VQE methods suffer from the Barren plateaus problem in general (authors note the problem is known even if not observed in their experiments).
- Manual tuning of acceptance thresholds (Ωg_c) was required to reach best accuracy (authors report tuned-epsilon variants).
- Ansatz must be designed to reproduce entanglement present in the data (authors detect entanglement and emphasize ansatz form matters).
- Learning/optimization time grows substantially with the number of qubits / ansatz complexity (authors report ~30s for 3 qubits vs ~600s for 8 qubits in their setup).
- [inferred] Very small dataset and limited experimental sample size (paper references a small training set — e.g. PCA figure notes 40 probes), raising concerns about statistical validity and overfitting.
- [inferred] Evaluation appears focused on (or reported mainly as) training-set fit; limited evidence of held-out test set / cross-validation and therefore uncertain generalization performance.
- [inferred] No clear demonstration on physical NISQ hardware in the paper (authors state it is possible to run on NISQ devices but report timings from classical hardware/simulations), so real-device noise/latency effects are untested.
- [inferred] Amplitude/state preparation overhead is not accounted for: converting classical vectors to normalized quantum states (amplitude encoding) can itself be costly and may dominate runtime on real devices.
- [inferred] SWAP-Test and related comparisons require repeated measurements (shots) to estimate probabilities — sampling noise and required number of shots are not fully characterized.
- [inferred] Controlled-swap (Fredkin) or multi-qubit controlled operations used by SWAP-Test may be expensive to implement on hardware with limited connectivity/gate sets; hardware-mapping overhead is not addressed.
## Open questions
- How well does the VQE+SWAP-Test approach generalize to new, unseen data (robustness / test-set performance vs. reported training accuracy)?
- How does the method scale (accuracy, optimization time, qubit count, and required circuit depth) for larger datasets, more features, and more classes/groups?
- What is the impact of realistic NISQ noise on classification accuracy, and which error mitigation strategies are effective here?
- How to systematically choose or automate acceptance thresholds (Λg_c, Ωg_c) instead of manual tuning to avoid ad-hoc selection?
- What ansatz architectures are optimal for entangled, real-world financial data? How to balance expressibility, trainability, and circuit depth for NISQ constraints?
- Under what conditions will barren plateaus emerge for this classification setup, and what mitigation strategies (ansatz design, initialization, problem encoding) are effective?
- What is the cost (time and gate count) of state/preparation (amplitude encoding) in practical hardware, and can alternative encodings yield better end-to-end performance?
- How many measurement shots are required in practice for reliable SWAP-Test-based similarity estimates, and how does shot noise affect classification boundaries?
- How does VQE+SWAP-Test classification compare to classical baselines in terms of generalization, resource cost, and robustness on realistic datasets and metrics beyond training accuracy?
- How to efficiently map the required circuits (including controlled-swap operations) to hardware with limited connectivity and native gate sets?

**Future work:**
- Perform experiments on real NISQ hardware (the authors state their low-complexity circuits could be executed on current devices).
- Design and use shallower / data-aware ansatz circuits that preserve required entanglement while minimizing depth for NISQ execution (authors emphasize importance of proper ansatz form and limited depth).

## Contradictions
- contradiction:classical-vs-quantum — The paper asserts superiority over classical classifiers but provides only training-set results on a small proprietary dataset and simulator-based experiments; this limited validation undermines the claim of practical quantum advantage.
- contradiction:scalability — The authors claim logarithmic qubit scaling and NISQ suitability, yet rely on amplitude encoding with padding to 2^n amplitudes and demonstrate only 3-qubit experiments; there is no evidence that the approach scales to realistic, higher-dimensional financial feature sets or to noisy QPUs.
