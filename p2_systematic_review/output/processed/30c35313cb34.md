## Abstract summary
This case-based qualitative study examines how quantum computing (QAOA, QMC, and quantum annealing) is being applied by institutions such as JPMorgan Chase, Goldman Sachs, and BBVA to enhance portfolio optimization, risk management, and asset pricing, demonstrating improvements in computational efficiency and scenario analysis. It also finds that widespread adoption is limited by hardware constraints, high error rates, and regulatory uncertainties, and highlights hybrid quantum-classical models as a practical near-term approach with significant long-term implications for financial risk assessment, high-frequency trading, and cryptography.
## Methodology
The study employs a qualitative, multiple case study approach to explore the application of quantum computing in financial portfolio optimization. It focuses on three leading financial institutions (JPMorgan Chase, Goldman Sachs, and BBVA) selected based on early adoption of quantum computing, availability of publicly accessible data (published research, white papers, case studies, and press releases), and industry influence. The research objectives included exploring the role of quantum computing in portfolio optimization, examining quantum algorithms (e.g., QAOA, quantum annealing, QMC) used in portfolio management, identifying benefits and challenges faced by financial institutions, and assessing future potential and scalability. Data collection relied exclusively on secondary sources: academic journal articles, white papers and industry reports from financial and quantum computing firms, and case studies/press releases from the selected institutions. The approach is interpretative and exploratory, synthesizing findings across cases to identify themes around implementation, computational efficiency, integration challenges, and future prospects. No primary experiments, empirical datasets, or original quantitative simulations were conducted as part of this study.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA), Quantum Monte Carlo (QMC), Quantum Annealing, Harrow-Hassidim-Lloyd (HHL) algorithm, Quantum Interior Point Method (QIPM), Quantum Machine Learning (QML)
