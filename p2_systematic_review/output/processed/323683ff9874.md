## Abstract summary
This chapter surveys quantum machine learning (QML) approaches for time series forecasting, contrasting classical architectures (e.g., LSTM, GRU) with quantum and hybrid quantum-classical models such as variational quantum algorithms, QLSTM, QNNs and quantum reservoir computing. It reviews data encoding strategies, ansatz design, measurement and optimization techniques (e.g., parameter-shift rule), discusses limitations in the NISQ era (noise, barren plateaus, data loading), and outlines future directions for improving forecasting accuracy and scalability.
## Methodology
This study implemented and evaluated an agent-driven system (EdgeInsight) for system-health monitoring on edge-computing nodes. The methodology comprised (a) building a lightweight agent in C that runs in user space on single-board/SoC-based edge nodes, (b) instrumenting the agent to read hardware performance monitoring counters (PMCs) and model-specific registers (MSRs) via inline assembly (RDPMC/RDMSR) to collect CPU, cache, memory, and energy traces, (c) packaging a representative computational workload (YOLOv3, pre-trained on ImageNet) inside a Linux Docker container and executing it on the edge node without GPU/OpenCV acceleration to mirror constrained edge environments, (d) configuring and periodically sampling a chosen set of PMC/MSR events during workload execution, (e) storing timestamped trace samples for post-processing, and (f) validating results by comparing the collected metrics with external profilers (Intel VTune, perf, LIKWID). The post-processing phase computed runtime, FLOPS estimates, cache hit/miss rates, and RAPL-based energy and power for CPU and DRAM. The experiments targeted the feasibility, accuracy and overhead of agent-based tracing for edge health monitoring.

YOLOv3 (pretrained on ImageNet) executed in container without GPU/OpenCV. PMU/MSR accessed via inline assembly (RDPMC/RDMSR). Validation profiling via Intel VTune, perf and LIKWID.

## Limitations

- Agent traces and capabilities are subject to the limitations and variability of the underlying SoC (hardware counters availability, supported events, sensor resolution) (author-stated).
- Accuracy and sampling limitations of hardware performance counters and MSR reads can produce measurement errors if sampling is too frequent or if counters behave differently across platforms (author-stated; cites Zaparanuks et al.).
- RAPL / energy readings update frequency and kernel-mode access constraints limit how power measurements can be used in user-space agents (author-stated).
- Experimental evaluation used a single test platform (Intel Core i3 laptop, no GPU/OpenCV active) which may not fully represent typical SoC-based edge deployments or AI workloads with GPU acceleration (author-stated implicitly via experiment description).
