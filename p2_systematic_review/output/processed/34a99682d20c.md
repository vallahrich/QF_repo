## Abstract summary
The paper proposes QEKLR, a hybrid quantum–classical classifier that encodes classical data with a shallow ZZFeatureMap, builds a fidelity-based quantum kernel, and uses that kernel within a classical logistic regression model to improve classification. The method is NISQ-compatible (≤4 qubits, shallow circuits) and avoids variational barren-plateau issues; experiments on synthetic, Iris, Statlog Heart Disease and Ecoli datasets show strong performance (notably 94.87% accuracy and AUC ~0.98 on Statlog HD), while highlighting scalability and kernel-concentration challenges for larger datasets.
## Methodology
This empirical study proposes QEKLR, a hybrid quantum–classical classification pipeline. Classical datasets are preprocessed via outlier removal using the interquartile range (IQR), dimensionality reduction with PCA (when applicable), and MinMax scaling (scaled to [-1,1]). For multiclass data a binary subset of classes was selected. Preprocessed classical feature vectors (k features, with k <= 4 for quantum encoding) are encoded into quantum states using a shallow ZZFeatureMap (configurable entanglement, full entanglement used; repetitions <= 2). A fidelity-based quantum kernel is computed as the squared inner product of encoded quantum states to produce a kernel matrix (diagonal elements = 1). That kernel matrix is used in a kernelized logistic regression (kernel logistic regression): the decision function is z_i = beta_0 + sum_j beta_j K(x_i, x_j) and probabilities are obtained via the logistic sigmoid. Model parameters are optimized by classical gradient descent minimizing cross-entropy. The approach is compared against classical baselines (RF, LR, CART, KLR) and other QML methods (VQC, QSVC). Evaluation metrics include accuracy, precision, sensitivity/recall, specificity, F1, Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC) and AUC-ROC. Experiments were implemented in Qiskit (software versions reported) and run on simulators (no physical QPU specified).

**Results summary:** The paper introduces QEKLR, a hybrid quantum-classical classifier that encodes classical inputs with a shallow ZZFeatureMap, computes a fidelity-based quantum kernel, and feeds that kernel into classical logistic regression. ## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The paper reports empirical classification improvements on certain small/medium datasets (notably Statlog HD) when using a quantum-enhanced kernel with logistic regression, but does not demonstrate provable computational or generalizable quantum advantage; claims about NISQ suitability and mitigation of PQC training issues are presented as design motivations or theoretical benefits rather than rigorously proven advantages across broader benchmarks.
## Limitations
- NISQ hardware constraints limit qubit counts and scalability (authors note QEKLR is designed for ≤4 qubits and shallow depth to remain NISQ-compatible).
- Barren‑plateau problem affects parameterized quantum circuits (PQCs); authors avoid it by using shallow feature‑map-based quantum kernels rather than trainable variational circuits.
- Limited interpretability of many quantum models; QEKLR provides only moderate interpretability by combining quantum kernel with classical logistic regression.
- Kernel stability / exponential concentration for larger datasets: as dataset size and qubit count increase, kernel values may cluster and lose discriminative power (author‑stated).
- Dataset‑dependent performance: QEKLR achieved strong results on some datasets (synthetic, Iris, Statlog HD) but only comparable performance on Ecoli, indicating variable generalization across domains.
- Use of a shallow ZZFeatureMap with small repetitions (repetition ≤ 2) constrains expressive power—practical for NISQ but limiting for more complex problems.
- [inferred] Experiments appear to be performed in simulation (Qiskit) with no reported runs on actual noisy quantum hardware, so hardware noise impact is untested.
- [inferred] Potential overfitting risk given very high accuracies on small/benchmarked datasets and reduced sample sizes after outlier removal.
- [inferred] Multiclass capability is limited in practice: multiclass datasets were converted to binary by selecting two classes, so true multiclass performance is not evaluated.
- [inferred] Computational cost and scalability of computing the fidelity quantum kernel for medium/large datasets is not reported (potentially high for large Ntrain).
- [inferred] Robustness to realistic noise, calibration error, and limited measurement shots on NISQ devices is not addressed.
- [inferred] Limited discussion of hyperparameter tuning and comparative fairness (e.g., whether classical baselines received equivalent tuning effort) is present.
