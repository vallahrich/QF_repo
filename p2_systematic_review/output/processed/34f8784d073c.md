## Abstract summary
The paper proposes an alternative option pricing approach based on a quantum walk, incorporating superposition and interference to model investors' subjective beliefs about future asset prices. The quantum model reproduces key features of market option prices and helps explain empirical anomalies (e.g., time-to-maturity effects and volatility smiles) while remaining close to classical binomial/Black–Scholes results and being directly implementable on quantum computers.
## Methodology
Теоретическая методика основана на построении дискретного времени квантового блуждания и его отображении на биномиальную модель ценообразования опционов. Формализм включает тензорное гильбертово пространство H = H_C ⊗ H_P, где H_C — двухмерное пространство «монеты» (базисы |↑>, |↓>), а H_P — пространство дискретных положений |i>. Бросок монеты задаётся унитарным оператором R (в качестве примера используется монета Адамара), оператор трансляции T сдвигает компоненту положения вправо или влево в зависимости от состояния монеты, а шаг эволюции U = T · (R ⊗ I). Волновая функция представлена компонентами a_i(n), b_i(n) с рекуррентными соотношениями для следующих итераций; вероятности получения положения вычисляются по правилу Борна. Для применения к финансовым ценам положения интерпретируются как кратные умножения цены акции S → S(1+b) (вверх) или S(1+a) (вниз), а цена европейского колл-опциона оценивается как ожидаемое дисконтированное значение ⟨V⟩ = ⟨(1/(1+r)^N [S_N − K]_+)⟩ при измерении в конце ветвления. Автор описывает способы задать ожидаемую доходность r и волатильность v через параметры a и b (например, p=0.5 и a = r − v/2, b = r + v/2) и указывает необходимость масштабирования внутренней (квантовой) волатильности, чтобы согласовать квадратичный рост дисперсии квантовой модели с линейным масштабированием классической модели (через коэффициент, зависящий от (1 − 1/√2) и времени). Декогеренцию моделируют как неполную интерференцию, вводя случайные фазовые возмущения в оператор монеты R_j (пример: R_j с фазой ς, выбираемой на каждом шаге из нормального распределения mean=0, std=0.5) и усредняя ансамбль симуляций (в статье упоминается среднее по 400 реализациям). Для аналитического описания формулируется дискретно-пространственное уравнение в частных производных с интерференционным членом β_i, приводящим к телеграфному уравнению с вкладом интерференции. Методология расширяется на многомерный случай (портфель из двух активов) использованием R ⊗ R и соответствующего оператора трансляции для совместного сдвига состояний; предложено построение хеджируемого портфеля Π = V − Δ S, вывод формулы для Δ и дисконтированной цены опциона в квантовом формализме, показывающей соответствие арбитражному условию. Набор результатов включает численные симуляции распределений после N шагов (например, N=100), сравнения цен опционов квантовой и классической моделей (разные сроки погашения) и построение подразумеваемой поверхности волатильности. Реальных рыночных данных в работе не использовано — все результаты получены аналитически и численно на моделях и симуляциях.

**Algorithms used:** Discrete-time quantum walk (коин- и трансляционный операторы), Hadamard coin (как частный случай SU(2) монеты), SU(2) coin family (R_{ξ,θ,ψ}), Decoherence via random-phase coin perturbations (ensemble averaging)

## Findings
- [speculative] The paper proposes a quantum-walk-based alternative to classical random-walk/binomial models for option pricing, arguing it better represents investors' subjective beliefs.
- [supported] Quantum walks exhibit interference and lead to qualitatively different probability distributions than classical random walks; in particular variance can grow quadratically with time rather than linearly.
- [supported] Decoherence (e.g. frequent measurement) suppresses interference and causes the quantum-walk dynamics to collapse to the classical random-walk limit.
- [speculative] A balanced initial coin (superposed investor beliefs) produces a symmetric bimodal distribution interpreted as distinct buyer and seller peaks in perceived future prices.
- [speculative] When used to price European call options, the quantum-binomial model yields option prices that are close to classical binomial / Black–Scholes prices for standard parameter choices, especially at short maturities (example: 1 month).
- [speculative] The model offers an explanation for observed time-dependence of implied volatility (short-term options appearing relatively more volatile) via a mismatch between subjective (linear-in-time) perceived volatility and objective (sqrt-time) observed volatility.
- [speculative] Introducing controlled decoherence/interference parameters lets the model interpolate between fully quantum behavior and classical behavior, enabling adjustment to observed market data.
- [speculative] The quantum-walk approach can be naturally extended to multi-asset portfolios and hedged portfolios (appendix derivation), and reproduces the standard replicating-portfolio argument in the model framework.
- [speculative] The model is parsimonious in parameters (author claims no extra ad-hoc parameters are required beyond quantum-walk specification) and can integrate with other quantum-cognition models.
- [speculative] The quantum-walk pricing method can be implemented directly on a quantum computer and may offer computational speed advantages for certain problem classes (citing broader quantum-computing finance literature).

**Results summary:** This theoretical paper develops a quantum-walk (discrete-time quantum random walk) formulation for modeling investors' subjective price beliefs and for pricing European call options. The quantum-walk formalism produces superposition and interference effects that yield non-classical, often bimodal, distributions for imagined future asset prices and a quadratic-in-time growth of variance. By mapping quantum-walk position shifts to multiplicative up/down price moves and applying a discounted-expectation payoff, the author constructs a quantum-binomial option pricing analogue that, under reasonable parameter choices, produces option prices close to classical binomial/Black–Scholes outputs (particularly for short maturities). The model also offers a mechanism—via different time-scaling of subjective (linear) vs. observed (sqrt) volatility—to qualitatively explain some empirical anomalies in implied volatility term structure and short-maturity behavior. Decoherence parameters permit interpolation to the classical limit. The work is theoretical and builds on established quantum-walk physics results; empirical validation is suggested but not performed.

**Performance claims:**
- Variance scaling in the quantum walk: sigma^2 ≅ (1 - 1/√2) * t^2 (quoted from Chandrashekar et al. 2008 and used in the model). (numeric coefficient ≈ 0.2929)
- Corresponding standard-deviation coefficient: sqrt(1 - 1/√2) ≈ 0.5412, used to relate quantum internal volatility to classical volatility scaling.
- Example parameterization: annual risk-free rate r = 0.001 and volatility v = 0.004 gives a = r - v/2 = -0.001 and b = r + v/2 = 0.003 (illustrative numeric example in text).
- Simulation settings referenced: distributions plotted after 100 iterations; ensemble average in decoherence experiment taken over 400 simulations with coin-phase perturbation standard deviation 0.5.
- Option-pricing examples: S0 = 1, annual volatility 20%, risk-free rate 2%, and maturities of 1 month and 6 months are used to compare quantum vs classical prices (figures show 'close' agreement for 1 month and some deviations for other strikes/maturities, but no quantitative error metrics provided).

## Limitations
- Full empirical analysis of market price data is outside the scope of the paper (author-stated).
- The model as presented does not reproduce the square-root-of-time scaling of measured volatility on the implied volatility surface because that effect was not modelled here (author-stated).
- The quantum model produces non-Gaussian, often bimodal and irregular distributions for (subjective) price states, which may seem counter‑intuitive or unattractive to practitioners used to Gaussian assumptions (author-stated).
- Potential methodological objection: importing models from physics into economics can be problematic and may be viewed as conceptually dangerous (author-stated).
- The model assumes measurement/decay (decoherence) mechanisms convert subjective quantum-like beliefs into observed prices; actual market measurement processes (transactions, quoting frequency) and their effective decoherence rates are not empirically specified (author-stated).
- Market microstructure factors (supply/demand, liquidity, transaction frequency, frequently traded options) and behavioural feedbacks complicate the predicted quantum signatures and are not fully incorporated (author-stated).
- [inferred] Calibration challenge: parameters of the quantum walk (coin/operator, initial state, decoherence/noise levels, time-step mapping to real time) are not provided with an estimation/calibration procedure to fit real market data.
- [inferred] Identification problem between subjective (internal) volatility and observed (objective) volatility — how to estimate investors' internal quantum volatility from market prices is unresolved.
- [inferred] Aggregation and heterogeneity: the paper treats symmetric/balanced investor superpositions or simple mixtures; modelling heterogeneous agents and how their individual quantum states aggregate into market prices is not developed.
- [inferred] Model sensitivity: outcomes depend on choice of coin/operator, initial state and decoherence model; robustness to misspecification and noise is not explored.
- [inferred] Practical implementation limits: the paper claims potential quantum-computer implementation but does not address current quantum hardware limitations, resource requirements, or concrete algorithms for real-world option books.
- [inferred] Market frictions (transaction costs, bid-ask spreads, collateral, funding costs) and risk preferences are not modelled and may affect hedging/arbitrage arguments used in constructing the option price.
- [inferred] Empirical performance: it is not established whether the quantum model materially improves pricing, hedging performance, or risk management compared with classical models once calibration and frictions are accounted for.
## Open questions
- How well does the quantum-walk option pricing model fit real market option prices and implied volatility surfaces across strikes and maturities?
- What is the appropriate method to estimate/calibrate the quantum-coin/operator parameters, initial investor-state distributions, and the decoherence/noise parameters from market data?
- What is the empirical magnitude and time-dependence of decoherence in financial markets (i.e., how frequently do 'measurements' occur and how does that vary across assets and maturities)?
- How do heterogeneous investors with different initial quantum states and different degrees of decoherence aggregate to produce observed price dynamics?
- Can the model be extended/calibrated to reproduce the observed square-root-of-time scaling of realized/implied volatility, and how should internal (linear) perceived volatility be mapped to observed volatility metrics?
- To what extent do supply-demand imbalances, liquidity and microstructure effects mask or modify the quantum-interference signatures predicted by the model?
- Does the quantum model provide statistically and economically significant improvements in option pricing, hedging efficacy, or risk measures compared to classical binomial/Black–Scholes approaches?
- How to incorporate realistic market frictions (transaction costs, bid–ask spreads, margining) and investor risk aversion into the quantum-walk framework?
- How can multi-asset correlations, partial entanglement and portfolio hedging be modelled and calibrated in a scalable way?
- What are the concrete quantum-algorithmic implementations for this model on near-term or fault-tolerant quantum hardware, and what are their resource and error‑tolerance requirements?
- How stable are model predictions to different choices of the coin (SU(2) parameters), and can one learn these choices from data?
- How do learning and updating of investor beliefs (dynamic adaptation of the quantum state) interact with market price feedbacks over time?

**Future work:**
- Empirical validation: test and calibrate the quantum walk model on real option and underlying price data, and compare fit/performance to classical models.
- Develop estimation/calibration procedures for coin/operator parameters, initial states and decoherence/noise parameters from market observables (prices, volumes, implied volatilities).
- Extend the model to portfolios of partially correlated assets and to more realistic multi-asset settings (the paper sketches a two-asset extension in the appendix).
- Incorporate and study decoherence mechanisms quantitatively (varying measurement rates) to explore transitions between quantum-like and classical behaviour and to match observed price dynamics.
- Combine the quantum-walk framework with other quantum-cognition or quantum-finance models (and group/entanglement models) to model influences such as investor optimism and agent entanglement.
- Investigate implementation and simulation of the model on quantum computers and assess practical computational advantages and hardware requirements.
- Explore richer coin/operator choices and initial-state constructions (more complex SU(2) parameterisations) and their implications for skew/smile and term-structure of implied volatility.
- Model aggregation of heterogeneous quantum belief states across agents and study resulting market-level dynamics and stability.
- Incorporate market frictions, liquidity effects and behavioural feedback loops into the quantum-walk option pricing framework.
