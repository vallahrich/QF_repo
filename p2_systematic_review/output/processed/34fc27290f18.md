## Abstract summary
This preprint evaluates the Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) for Dynamic Portfolio Optimization (DPO) on real gate-based QPUs, scaling problem instances from 6 up to 112 qubits on the IBM Torino device. The authors benchmark several ansätze and classical optimizers, find that a tailored problem- and hardware-aware ansatz combined with a Differential Evolution optimizer yields the best raw (unmitigated) QPU results, and compare quantum outputs against classical solvers and a quantum-inspired simulated adiabatic evolution baseline.
## Methodology
The authors formulate a Dynamic Portfolio Optimization (DPO) problem and map it to a QUBO/Ising Hamiltonian via: normalization of investment trajectories, binary encoding of discretized investments (N_r bits per asset/time), quadratic approximation of absolute transaction costs, and a soft budget constraint encoded with a quadratic penalty (Lagrange multiplier p=1). They benchmark classical solvers (exhaustive search, Gurobi, DOCPLEX/CPLEX, GEKKO) and a quantum-inspired Simulated Adiabatic Evolution (SAE) implemented with tensor networks (QUIMB). For gate-model quantum experiments they run a Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) on IBM Torino QPU (real hardware). They compare multiple ansatz families (cyclic entangling ansatz, RealAmplitudes (RA), Optimized Real Amplitudes (ORA), and a problem- and hardware-tailored ansatz) and two classical optimizers to drive the VQE: Conjugate Gradient (CG) and gradient-free Differential Evolution (DE). The VQE workflow uses the IBM estimator primitive to evaluate Hamiltonian expectation values during optimization and the sampler primitive to collect final bitstring samples; no quantum error mitigation was applied. Scaling experiments span problem sizes from small (6 qubits) up to XXL (112 qubits), with detailed per-size DE population/generation settings, shot counts, ansatz parameter counts and transpiled circuit depths recorded. Results are compared against classical reference solutions (Gurobi best) using metrics including the optimization cost (objective), Sharpe ratio, and statistics of sampled cost distributions (e.g., % of samples below random-offset baseline).

**Algorithms used:** Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE), Differential Evolution (DE), Conjugate Gradient (CG), Simulated Adiabatic Evolution (SAE) (tensor-network simulation), QUBO to Ising mapping, Exhaustive search, Gurobi (classical optimizer), DOCPLEX / CPLEX (classical optimizer), GEKKO (classical optimizer), Matrix Product State (MPS) tensor-network methods
**Frameworks:** Qiskit (IBM primitives: BackendEstimatorV2, BackendSamplerV2, transpiler, Runtime), QUIMB (tensor networks), SciPy (optimize: conjugate gradient, differential_evolution), Gurobi, DOCPLEX (IBM CPLEX), GEKKO

**Experimental setup:** Gate-model experiments executed on the IBM Torino superconducting QPU (IBM Quantum) between Sep–Dec 2024; classical assistance run on a workstation (Intel Core i9-13900K, 32 GB RAM, NVIDIA RTX A4000). Estimator primitive used with 2.5e3 shots for smallest size and 2.5e4 shots for larger sizes; sampler primitive used with 1e4 shots for smallest and 1e5 for larger sizes. No quantum error mitigation applied. Circuits were transpiled to the IBM Torino coupling map; deliberate ansatz design minimized SWAPs and depth for the 112-qubit case.

**Dataset:** Daily closing prices of seven financial assets across heterogeneous asset classes, covering 1 Jan 2023 to 1 Aug 2023. Daily logarithmic returns computed; rebalancing interval At = 30 days. Assets chosen to represent capital, FX, bond, private, real-estate and commodity categories (tickers provided in Appendix D).

## Findings
- [supported] The authors implemented VQE-based solutions for Dynamic Portfolio Optimization (DPO) on real gate-based QPUs and scaled problem sizes from 6 up to 112 qubits (IBM Torino QPU).
- [supported] Classical benchmarking identified Gurobi as the best classical solver for larger DPO instances (L, XL, XXL), with DOCPLEX matching on some smaller large sizes but underperforming on the XXL instance.
- [supported] A quantum-inspired Simulated Adiabatic Evolution (SAE) tensor-network method produced results close to the best classical solver (Gurobi) and for the XXL case produced a highly biased solution distribution (≈100% of sampled states between the random offset and the Gurobi reference).
- [supported] The choice of ansatz and classical optimizer strongly affects VQE performance: gradient-based Conjugate Gradient (CG) with a cyclic ansatz performed poorly (distributions close to random), while Differential Evolution (DE) combined with problem- and hardware-tailored ansatzes produced substantially better results.
- [supported] A tailored ansatz designed to exploit both the DPO QUBO structure and the IBM Torino connectivity yielded the lowest circuit depth (after transpilation) for the XXL instance and the best empirical VQE distributions among the tested quantum approaches.
- [supported] The authors report raw (unmitigated) QPU experiments — i.e., they did not apply quantum error mitigation — and still obtained notable differences between ansatz/optimizer combinations, indicating some robustness of the chosen VQE implementations to NISQ noise.
- [supported] Different ansatz families produced very different circuit depths after transpilation: for the XXL instance the cyclic ansatz depth was very large (≈2347), Real Amplitudes (RA) was ≈358, ORA ≈100, and the tailored ansatz ≈72.
- [speculative] The paper argues that Differential Evolution is generally more robust to quantum noise and better at addressing barren-plateau-like issues than gradient-based optimizers — supported in their experiments but presented as a broader suggestion for VQE optimization strategy.
- [speculative] The authors state that designing ansatzes tailored to both problem structure and QPU connectivity is a key route to scaling VQE to >100 qubits — their experiments support this instance, but broader generalization remains speculative.
- [speculative] The manuscript claims to have 'reached the 100-qubit utility frontier' on a real, industry-focused problem; while they executed >100-qubit circuits and obtained usable distributions, calling this a crossing of a community-defined 'utility frontier' is interpretative and thus speculative.

**Results summary:** The study implements and benchmarks VQE for Dynamic Portfolio Optimization on IBM's 133-qubit Torino QPU, scaling from 6 to 112 logical qubits. Classical baselines (Gurobi, DOCPLEX, GEKKO, exhaustive) and a quantum-inspired Simulated Adiabatic Evolution (SAE) tensor-network method were used as references. Empirically, gradient-free Differential Evolution (DE) optimizers combined with ansatzes tailored to both the QUBO problem structure and the QPU coupling map produced the best VQE results; the tailored ansatz achieved a much smaller transpiled circuit depth (≈72 for the largest instance) and a probability distribution more biased toward low-cost solutions than other ansatz/optimizer combinations. All quantum experiments reported are raw (no error mitigation). SAE on classical hardware produced distributions close to Gurobi (and near-optimal by their metrics) for the largest instance.

## Limitations
- Quantum noise, decoherence and general NISQ-era hardware limitations constrain performance (author-stated)
- Circuit depth growth (after transpilation) and number of two-qubit gates increase noise and reduce trainability (author-stated)
- Barren-plateau phenomena for deep / highly-parameterized hardware-efficient ansatzes limits VQE trainability as problem size increases (author-stated)
- They did not apply any quantum error-mitigation techniques; results are raw QPU outputs and may be degraded by unmitigated errors (author-stated)
- The Conjugate Gradient optimizer performed poorly / converged to nearly-random solutions for larger instances (author-stated)
- Some ansatz designs (ORA) produced too many parameters and failed to converge within allotted optimizer budgets (author-stated)
- Transpilation and limited QPU connectivity force SWAPs and other extra gates, substantially increasing circuit depth (author-stated)
- QUBO formulation relies on a quadratic approximation of absolute transaction costs (parameter A chosen analytically); this approximation introduces modeling error (author-stated)
- Penalty encoding of budget constraint uses a fixed Lagrange multiplier (p = 1) chosen heuristically; this may not be optimal across instances (author-stated)
- Benchmarks and dataset choices prioritize illustrative value over fully realistic business scenarios, limiting immediate commercial applicability (author-stated)
- Classical solver comparisons were constrained by solver time limits and default settings; some solvers (DOCPLEX/Gurobi) were time-limited for larger sizes (author-stated)
- [inferred] Results may be QPU- and backend-dependent (e.g., IBM Torino specific topology, runtime upgrades during the study), potentially harming reproducibility across devices or time
- [inferred] Discretization of investment amounts via finite N_r bits induces quantization error and limits solution fidelity
- [inferred] Shot counts and sampling budgets were finite and scaled with size; limited sampling may have impacted estimator variance and final solution quality
- [inferred] The study explored only a subset of classical optimizers (CG, DE) and ansatz families; other optimizer/ansatz combinations might perform differently

## Open questions
- How much can standard quantum error-mitigation techniques improve VQE performance on these DPO instances (especially XXL / >100 qubits)?
- How generalizable is the tailored ansatz approach across different QPU architectures and topologies (beyond IBM Torino)?
- What are systematic methods to design problem- and hardware-aware ansatzes that balance expressivity, parameter count and depth?
- Can the approach scale beyond ~112 qubits while maintaining practically useful solutions on real QPUs?
- Is there a regime where gate-based VQE convincingly outperforms classical solvers (i.e., where quantum advantage appears) for realistic DPO instances?
- How sensitive are results to modeling choices and hyperparameters: risk-aversion coefficient, transaction-cost approximation parameter A, Lagrange penalty p, time discretization step At, and N_r resolution bits?
- What is the trade-off between number of parameters and trainability for ORA-like structured ansatzes, and how can parameter explosion be avoided?
- How do sampling shot budgets affect estimator variance and optimization convergence across sizes?
- How robust is the DE optimizer across different problem instances, ansatz sizes, and QPU noise levels; are there better optimizers or hybrid strategies?
- How effective are quantum-inspired tensor-network methods (SAE) at larger scales compared with gate-based QPU runs, in terms of solution quality and computational cost?
- What is the impact of more realistic (non-constant) transaction fees, market impact, and more complex constraints on the QUBO mapping and solver performance?
- How reproducible are the experiments over time as backends evolve (hardware upgrades, runtime improvements) and across different access conditions?
- What is the effect of incorporating readout error mitigation and other calibration-aware techniques on the distribution of sampled solutions?
- How to choose or tune the Lagrange multiplier (penalty) automatically for a given instance to avoid dominance or slack in the objective?
