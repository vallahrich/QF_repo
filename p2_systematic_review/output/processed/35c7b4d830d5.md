## Abstract summary
The paper develops portfolio optimization frameworks that combine a hybrid quantum-classical Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) with SPSA and a futures/options-based Market Sentiment Meter (MSM) to augment mean–variance optimization. Empirical tests on three formulations (continuous weights and a QUBO binary selection) across eight futures assets (2012–2022) indicate that incorporating MSM improves portfolio outcomes and that the VQE-based hybrid method can outperform classical counterparts in some risk regimes.
## Methodology
The study formulates portfolio optimization problems that integrate a market sentiment indicator (the CME Market Sentiment Meter, MSM) into classical mean–variance optimization and into a QUBO amenable to hybrid quantum-classical optimization. Three main optimization formulations were evaluated: (1) continuous-weight mean–variance optimization with box bounds on weights (0.07–0.93) and an equality constraint that weights sum to 1, used as a baseline; (2) same as (1) but with an added MSM penalty term (α·x^T x) where α is the recent-period MSM mean (MSM states mapped to numeric values); (3) a binary QUBO asset-selection formulation (x ∈ {0,1}^n) that maximizes μ^T x − q x^T Σ x − α x^T x subject to selecting B assets (budget constraint). The equality/budget constraints are enforced via penalty terms in the objective. For the QUBO (formulation 3) the authors map the objective to a Hamiltonian and apply the Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) with a two-local parameterized ansatz to approximate the ground state. The classical optimizer used to update circuit parameters is Simultaneous Perturbation Stochastic Approximation (SPSA), chosen for noise robustness. For binary outputs (formulation 3) selected assets are equally weighted post-selection (equal-weight allocation among chosen assets). Experiments run across 342 overlapping small time periods created from most-active futures contract data (2012-01-04 to 2022-06-03) and across multiple risk parameter settings q ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 1, 10} to evaluate performance and sensitivity to risk tolerance. Performance is reported as cumulative portfolio balances/returns and standard risk-return metrics, and compared across the three formulations to assess the impact of including MSM and of using the hybrid VQE approach.

**Algorithms used:** Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE), Simultaneous Perturbation Stochastic Approximation (SPSA), Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) formulation, Mean–variance (Markowitz) optimization

**Dataset:** End-of-day futures data for eight CME/CBOT/NYMEX products (E‑mini S&P 500, CBOT 10‑Year Treasury Note, CME Euro FX (EUR/USD), COMEX Gold, NYMEX WTI Crude Oil, NYMEX Henry Hub Natural Gas, CBOT Corn, CBOT Soybeans) from 2012-01-04 to 2022-06-03. MSM (Market Sentiment Meter) states derived from CME/1QBit methodology using CME EOD data. Daily percent returns were computed; means and covariance matrices computed per analysis period.

## Findings
- [supported] Inclusion of the CME Market Sentiment Meter (MSM) into portfolio optimization objective functions improved backtested portfolio performance for several risk settings (notably q = 0.01 and q = 0.1) relative to the same formulation without MSM.
- [supported] A hybrid quantum-classical workflow using the Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) with SPSA (VQE-SPSA) was implemented to solve a QUBO-style asset selection formulation (binary selection, equal weighting after selection).
- [supported] The VQE-SPSA formulation (Formulation 3) produced higher cumulative returns than the classical continuous QUBO formulation without MSM (Formulation 1) for at least some risk parameters (e.g., q = 0.01).
- [supported] The continuous formulation with MSM (Formulation 2) produced the best overall backtested cumulative return for the experiment (q = 0.01: USD 5,609,935, 560.99% return).
- [supported] The relationship 'higher risk (lower q) leads to higher return' did not hold monotonically in these experiments — observed non-monotonic/paradoxical behavior across q values.
- [supported] A case study in early 2022 (natural gas) demonstrates the MSM-informed strategy responding to sentiment shifts and realizing a large short-term portfolio gain (cited 24% portfolio increase over a 20-day window in the described example).
- [supported] SPSA was chosen as the classical optimizer for VQE due to prior evidence of relative robustness to realistic noise levels (cited literature).
- [speculative] Mapping of MSM discrete states to numeric risk penalties (Complacent: 0, Balanced: 0.1, Anxious: 0.4, Conflicted: 0.5) is an effective and generally applicable way to incorporate market sentiment into mean-variance objectives (methodological choice validated in this experiment but not proven generally).
- [speculative] The two-local hardware-efficient ansatz is appropriate for near-term devices and sufficient to represent solutions for the tested portfolio-size QUBO instances (claimed as rationale for ansatz choice; suitability depends on problem scaling and hardware).
- [speculative] Reformulating continuous-weight portfolio optimization directly for VQE (instead of binary selection + post-weighting) is left as future work and is expected to be nontrivial (proposal/future direction).
- [speculative] The work implies hybrid quantum solutions can be beneficial in real financial portfolio tasks as quantum hardware matures (forward-looking claim).

**Results summary:** The authors evaluated three portfolio optimization formulations on eight futures assets (2012–2022): a continuous classical QUBO-like formulation without MSM (Formulation 1), a continuous classical formulation with MSM (Formulation 2), and a binary-selection QUBO solved with a hybrid VQE-SPSA algorithm including MSM (Formulation 3). Backtests show that adding the MSM sentiment penalty substantially improved performance for low risk-penalty settings (notably q = 0.01 and q = 0.1). The continuous formulation with MSM (Formulation 2) achieved the highest cumulative return in the study (560.99% for q = 0.01). The VQE-based binary-selection formulation with MSM outperformed the comparable classical formulation without MSM in some cases (e.g., q = 0.01), but did not always exceed the MSM-enabled classical continuous formulation. A 2022 case study (natural gas) illustrates how MSM-driven adjustments produced strong short-term gains.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The paper reports that a hybrid VQE-SPSA formulation with MSM outperformed a classical continuous formulation without MSM in some backtests (e.g., q = 0.01). However, the best overall performer was the classical formulation that included MSM, and the study does not demonstrate a general or hardware-level quantum computational advantage (no claim of asymptotic speedup, limited problem size, and no rigorous comparison on equal computational budgets or real quantum hardware vs optimized classical solvers). Therefore any asserted benefit from the quantum approach is experimental/speculative within the presented backtests rather than a demonstrated quantum advantage.
## Limitations
- The numerical experiments did not include transaction costs or market impact (author-stated).
- The hybrid quantum implementation used a binary decision-variable QUBO (asset selection) and assigned equal weights post-selection, rather than optimizing continuous weights inside the quantum routine (author-stated).
- Quantum portfolio optimization with continuous decision variables was not implemented; continuous-variable QUBO reformulation was left for future work (author-stated).
- The study considered only eight futures assets (E-mini S&P 500, 10-yr Treasury, Euro FX, Gold, WTI, Natural Gas, Corn, Soybeans), limiting generalizability (author-stated).
- MSM was incorporated via a single scalar α derived from mapped numerical values for four sentiment states (Complacent: 0, Balanced: 0.1, Anxious: 0.4, Conflicted: 0.5), which is a coarse encoding of market sentiment (author-stated).
- [inferred] The results are based on backtests over historical data (2012–2022) without out-of-sample forward/live trading validation, raising concerns about overfitting and real-world performance.
- [inferred] The portfolio construction assumed a uniform 10% margin across contracts and used most-active contract EOD data and rolling heuristics; these simplifications may misstate real capital requirements and operational complexities.
- [inferred] The formulation 3 (VQE) required selection of a penalty parameter to enforce the budget constraint (1^T x = B) but tuning/robustness of penalty weights is not discussed.
- [inferred] The study does not report whether VQE was run on physical quantum hardware or simulated noise models, nor does it quantify hardware noise effects or wall-clock runtimes, limiting assessment of practical feasibility.
- [inferred] Scalability to larger asset universes (n much larger than 8) is not demonstrated; the QUBO/VQE approach may not scale tractably without further algorithmic or hardware advances.
- [inferred] The choice of ansatz (two-local) and optimizer (SPSA) can materially affect VQE outcomes; sensitivity analysis across ansatz types, circuit depths, and optimizer hyperparameters is not provided.
- [inferred] The MSM mapping to a single mean α ignores potential temporal dynamics or asset-specific sentiment signals (i.e., MSM applied homogeneously across assets), which could limit efficacy.
- [inferred] Transaction execution issues (slippage, liquidity constraints, discrete contract sizes) were not modeled and could materially affect realized returns.
## Open questions
- How will inclusion of realistic transaction costs, bid–ask spreads, slippage and market impact affect the reported performance advantages of MSM-informed hybrid quantum portfolios?
- Can the VQE/QUBO hybrid approach be extended to optimize continuous asset weights directly (rather than post hoc equal-weighting), and if so, how should the Hamiltonian be formulated?
- How does the MSM-enhanced optimization generalize to larger and more diverse asset universes beyond the eight futures considered?
- How sensitive are the results to the numerical encoding of MSM states (the chosen mapping 0, 0.1, 0.4, 0.5), and is there a principled way to calibrate α?
- What is the robustness of the hybrid quantum solutions out-of-sample and in live trading environments (i.e., are gains persistent under realistic market conditions)?
- How does algorithm performance (solution quality, convergence, and runtime) behave on real noisy quantum hardware versus classical simulation of VQE?
- What are the scalability limits of the proposed QUBO/VQE formulation with respect to the number of assets and constraints (cardinality, budget, regulatory limits)?
- How should penalty parameters (for equality constraints) be chosen or adapted to ensure reliable constraint satisfaction without degrading solution quality?
- How does the choice of variational ansatz, circuit depth, and classical optimizer (beyond SPSA) impact solution quality and noise resilience in this financial application?
- Does the observed paradoxical relationship between higher q (risk parameter) and lower portfolio return for some cases hold generally for futures markets, and what mechanisms explain it?
- Can asset-specific or time-varying sentiment signals (rather than a single scalar α) provide further improvements, and how to integrate them into the hybrid quantum framework?
- How would inclusion of additional realistic constraints (transaction limits, cardinality, turnover, leverage, regulatory constraints) affect the viability of the quantum approach?

**Future work:**
- Include transaction costs in the presented formulations and assess their impact on strategy performance (author-stated).
- Perform quantum portfolio optimization with continuous decision variables (author-stated).
- Include additional assets beyond the eight futures considered and assess the impact of expanding MSM coverage on portfolio outcomes (author-stated).
