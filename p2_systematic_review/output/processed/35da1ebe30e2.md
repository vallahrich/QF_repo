## Abstract summary
This review surveys Quantum Machine Learning (QML) methods for complex optimization, systematically comparing algorithms such as QAOA, VQE, QNNs and QSVMs and their application domains (e.g., logistics, finance, drug discovery). It finds QML offers promising improvements in exploring large solution spaces and convergence speed but highlights practical barriers—quantum noise, limited qubit counts, and hardware constraints—and calls for advances in hybrid quantum-classical systems and algorithm design.

Algorithms were compared on efficiency, scalability, convergence capability, and practical feasibility. ## Limitations
- Quantum hardware constraints: limited qubit counts and short coherence times restrict scalability of QML algorithms
- High noise and gate error rates on NISQ devices degrade QML algorithm performance and solution quality
- Significant overhead for quantum error correction (many physical qubits per logical qubit) limiting practical large-scale use
- Sensitivity of variational/hybrid algorithms to parameter initialization and classical optimizer performance (e.g., QAOA and VQE)
- Barren plateau phenomena and vanishing gradients that hinder trainability of Quantum Neural Networks (QNNs)
- Algorithm-specific implementation difficulties (e.g., QPCA and Grover require fault-tolerant hardware or complex controlled unitaries)
- Communication and iteration overhead in hybrid quantum-classical loops (many circuit evaluations required)
