## Abstract summary
The paper studies logarithmic return distributions over long time scales (up to two trading years) and documents frequent non-Gaussian features—notably asymmetry and bimodality—that classical models fail to capture. It proposes and fits a discrete-time quantum walk model, exploiting quantum interference to reproduce bimodal and skewed long-term return shapes, and shows improved descriptive performance versus Gaussian fits across a set of empirical assets.
## Methodology
The study is empirical and modeling-based. The authors collected historical opening-price time series for 31 financial assets from Yahoo Finance (retrieved 2024-10-14), using as long a history as available (up to 30 calendar years). Non-trading days were removed and consecutive trading days treated as successive points. For multiple time scales Δt (1 to 504 trading days) they computed logarithmic return histograms P_{Δt}(g). They quantified bimodality with a bespoke metric BM (Eq. 10) and asymmetry with skewness γ1. To model long-term return distributions they used the discrete-time unitary quantum walk (QW) on the line: a 2×2 SU(2)-type coin U_{η,θ} (coin parameters η, θ), Bloch-sphere initial coin state parameterized by (ϕ, ω), conditional shift operator, and evolution V^n to obtain site probabilities P_j(n)=|a_j(n)|^2+|b_j(n)|^2. For fitting empirical P_{Δt}(g) (primarily Δt=504 trading days) they mapped the quantum-walk position axis to g, smoothed QW distributions by aggregating each three consecutive nonzero positions, set the QW evolution time n=100 for initial fits, and optimized the four QW parameters (η, θ, ϕ, ω) by minimizing mean absolute error (MAE) between empirical and QW-binned histograms. The MAE objective and hyperparameters (relative horizontal shift, small adjustments to number of bins) guided the fit. Optimization used a steepest-ascent hill-climbing local search starting from a broad set of initial guesses and refining until parameter precision 0.0001 radians. To reduce spikiness and better match empirical tails they later averaged 1,000 QW probability distributions with n sampled from a normal distribution N(μ=100, σ^2=152) (rounded to nearest even integer). They compared QW fits against Gaussian fits using MAE and Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistics. Decoherence was explicitly excluded (unitary QW only).

**Algorithms used:** Discrete-time quantum walk (unitary, 1D), Local search / steepest-ascent hill-climbing (parameter optimization)

**Dataset:** Opening-price time series for 31 financial assets obtained from Yahoo Finance (https://finance.yahoo.com/) on 2024-10-14. Time spans up to 30 calendar years where available; non-trading days excluded; analysis focused on returns over Δt up to 504 trading days.

## Findings
- [supported] Return distributions of many assets at large time scales (order of hundreds of trading days, up to 504 trading days = 2 trading years) frequently deviate from Gaussianity and often display pronounced asymmetry or bimodality (empirical analysis of 31 selected assets).
- [disputed] The common assumption that return distributions converge toward a Gaussian distribution for increasing time scales often does not hold for time scales approaching hundreds of trading days (authors' empirical claim challenging the usual literature assumption).
- [supported] A discrete-time unitary quantum walk (DTQW) with tunable coin parameters (η, θ) and initial condition parameters (ϕ, ω) can generate asymmetric and bimodal probability distributions due to interference effects (analytic/numerical DTQW properties and fits).
- [supported] Fitting long-term empirical log-return histograms (∆t = 504 trading days) for selected assets with DTQW probability distributions produces better agreement (lower MAE and often lower KS statistic) than a simple Gaussian fit in most tested cases (see reported MAE and KS results).
- [speculative] The DTQW model can serve as a dynamically-grounded alternative to classical diffusion models (e.g., geometric Brownian motion) for describing long-term asset price evolution; authors present this as a modeling perspective rather than a proof of market microstructure origin.
- [supported] Unitary quantum walks yield deterministic probability distributions (measurement randomness only) while introducing decoherence gradually recovers classical random walk behavior (cited quantum walk literature and used as modeling rationale).
- [supported] In the authors' fitting workflow, aggregating (smoothing) local quantum walk peaks (by grouping three consecutive j positions) and allowing variation in the number of time steps n (sampling n from a normal distribution around n=100) improves empirical fit quality and reduces unrealistic sharp peaks.
- [supported] The authors demonstrate fitted DTQW parameter estimates (η, θ, ϕ, ω) for all 31 assets and observe clustering in the coin parameter space, suggesting some commonality of fitted dynamics across assets in their sample (scatter plots and marginal histograms presented).
- [speculative] The DTQW framework provides a natural way to capture two competing long-term tendencies (rise vs fall) in asset prices, which may explain empirical bimodality; this is a proposed interpretation rather than a proven causal mechanism.
- [speculative] The DTQW-based return distributions can be used to motivate or inform quantum-walk-based option pricing models (authors reference prior QW option-pricing literature and suggest consistency with their empirical observations).
- [supported] Methodological choices used in the paper: the core DTQW fit used n=100 time steps (chosen as a compromise between capturing features and computational cost), and then n was smeared with N(100, 15^2) to obtain smoother fits that better match empirical tails.
- [speculative] Extending the analysis to larger datasets, incorporating decoherence in DTQW, and exploring clustering of fitted parameters by sector are promising future research directions (authors' recommendations).

**Results summary:** The authors analyze long-term (up to 504 trading days) log-return distributions for 31 financial assets and find frequent non-Gaussian features, notably asymmetry and bimodality. They propose and fit a discrete-time unitary quantum walk (with a two-parameter quantum coin and Bloch-sphere initial state) to these empirical distributions. Using MAE and KS statistics, the quantum-walk-based fits generally match the global shape of asymmetric and bimodal long-term return distributions better than simple Gaussian fits; methodological refinements (smoothing of local interference spikes and averaging over a distribution of time-step counts) further improve fit realism. The authors position the DTQW as a parsimonious, dynamics-oriented model capable of capturing long-term return features that Gaussian-based models miss, and suggest possible applications to option-pricing frameworks and further empirical work.

## Limitations
- The analysis restricts itself to unitary (no-decoherence) discrete-time quantum walks; decoherence effects are not modelled (author-stated).
- Quantum-walk fits with a fixed number of time steps (n = 100) tended to overestimate peak heights and abruptly fall to near zero beyond peaks (author-stated).
- Achieving highly accurate fits for long horizons (∆t on the order of trading years) is neither expected nor claimed; fits are approximate and meant to capture main features (author-stated).
- Small and selective data set: only 31 assets were analysed and assets were chosen because they exhibited pronounced asymmetry or bimodality, producing selection bias (author-stated).
- The paper does not perform a detailed statistical analysis of the fitted parameter distribution (e.g., PCA or clustering) because the sample size is too small (author-stated).
- Smoothing/aggregation approximations were applied to the quantum walk probability distributions (aggregating three positions) to reduce spikiness; this is an approximation that can affect details of fits (author-stated).
- [inferred] Limited generalizability because the asset sample was selected for exhibiting the phenomena of interest rather than being representative of markets generally.
- [inferred] Potential overfitting to the selected assets and lack of out-of-sample validation or predictive testing (no assessment of forecasting or option-pricing performance reported).
- [inferred] Use of a local-search (steepest-ascent hill climbing) optimizer may lead to convergence to local minima rather than global optima for the quantum-walk parameters.
- [inferred] The choice to fix n (and later to average over a chosen normal distribution for n) is ad-hoc; results may be sensitive to this modelling choice.
- [inferred] Coarse histogram binning (roughly 20 bins) and the introduced hyperparameters (bin shift, small bin-number variations) may influence the reported fits and measures of bimodality.
- [inferred] The goodness-of-fit evaluation relies primarily on MAE and KS statistics; more sophisticated statistical model-comparison methods or likelihood-based inference are not used.
- [inferred] No systematic comparison to alternative classical/non-Gaussian models (e.g., mixture models, jump-diffusion, stable laws) beyond a Gaussian baseline, limiting assessment of relative performance.
- [inferred] The unitary quantum-walk model is deterministic until measurement and omits market stochasticity and noise that real markets exhibit; this may limit realism.
- [inferred] Interpretability gap: coin and initial-condition parameters are not directly mapped to economic/financial quantities, limiting economic interpretability of fitted parameters.
- [inferred] Time-variability of fitted parameters is not addressed; parameters are fitted statically for the whole sample and may not capture changing dynamics over time.
- [inferred] The analysis is limited to time scales up to two trading years (∆t ≤ 504 trading days); behaviour beyond that horizon is not explored.
- [inferred] Data choices (e.g., using opening prices only, exclusion of non-trading days) may affect results but are not fully investigated.
## Open questions
- How should decoherence be introduced into the quantum walk model for financial time series in a principled and financially meaningful way?
- How do return distributions evolve from short to long time scales when decoherence effects are included (i.e., can decoherence produce the observed transition from short-term fat tails to longer-term shapes)?
- Are the fitted quantum-walk parameters (coin and initial-condition parameters) stable over time, and how do they vary across market regimes?
- Do assets from different economic sectors or industries occupy distinct regions in the quantum-walk parameter space?
- Are there systematic differences in quantum-walk parameters between composite (fund/index) assets and single equities?
- How robust are the fits to modelling choices such as the number of time steps n, the smoothing/aggregation procedure, histogram binning, and optimization algorithm?
- Can the quantum-walk model, when calibrated to empirical return distributions, improve practical tasks such as option pricing or risk estimation compared with established models?
- Which decoherence models (e.g., coin phase noise, link disruption) best capture the emergence of classical-like behaviour in financial return distributions as time scale increases?
- How to statistically validate quantum-walk fits beyond MAE and KS—e.g., through likelihood-based methods, cross-validation, or out-of-sample tests?
- How to interpret quantum coin and initial-condition parameters in economic terms (e.g., mapping to competing market forces, investor behaviours, or regime-switching dynamics)?
- What is the impact of stochasticity introduced by decoherence on parameter estimation and uncertainty quantification for the model?
- How does the quantum-walk approach compare to more sophisticated classical non-Gaussian models (jump-diffusion, mixtures, stable distributions) in terms of fit quality and economic usefulness?

## Key ideas

- #idea:quantum-advantage — Improving fit quality required ensemble averaging over QW evolution times (sampling n from N(100,152)) and smoothing (aggregating positions), indicating that an ensemble of unitary evolutions better matches empirical tails than a single deterministic QW run.

- #limitation:no-empirical-validation — Fitting relies on bespoke preprocessing and hyperparameter choices (bin alignment, horizontal shifts, 3-position aggregation, choice of n and ensemble smoothing) and uses local hill-climbing optimizers, raising concerns about sensitivity, robustness and potential overfitting that are not fully explored.
