## Abstract summary
This preprint studies quantum algorithms to compute derivatives of expected values V(x)=E[F(S,x)] with respect to a parameter x, motivated by sensitivity (Greek) calculations in finance. It proposes two approaches— a naive iteration method that evaluates finite-difference terms individually and a sum-in-QAE method that merges the finite-difference summation into a single quantum amplitude estimation— and analyzes their query and qubit complexities. The paper identifies trade-offs depending on the smoothness of F and available qubits, showing the sum-in-QAE method is advantageous for nonsmooth integrands or when qubit resources are limited, while both achieve near-quadratic scaling in the error tolerance up to logarithmic factors.
## Methodology
This is a theoretical / algorithmic study that derives and analyzes quantum algorithms for computing derivatives of expected values V(x) = E[F(S,x)] with respect to a real parameter x. The approach combines classical numerical differentiation (general-order central-difference formulas) with Quantum Monte Carlo Integration (QMCI) via Quantum Amplitude Estimation (QAE). The authors assume access to two primary oracles: OS that prepares the distribution state sum_s sqrt(p_s)|s>, and OF (or a finite-precision variant OF,ε) that computes F(s,x) into a quantum register. OF,ε is assumed to produce an approximation within additive error ε and to consume O(log_a(1/ε)) qubits. They analyze two algorithmic strategies: (1) the naive iteration method, which constructs the finite-difference combination D_{n,m,h}[F(s,·)](x) by iteratively calling OF at the grid points x+jh and then estimates the expectation via QAE; and (2) the sum-in-QAE method, which incorporates the finite-difference summation coefficients into the QAE state preparation so both sums (over s and over finite-difference indices j) are done within a single QAE call. For the sum-in-QAE method the paper introduces the notion of coefficient-loading oracles Om,n_coef and sign-oracles Om,n_sign that prepare the normalized coefficient superposition and sign flags respectively. The methodology includes rigorous error analysis using central-difference residual bounds (Theorem 1) and smoothness assumptions expressed by Gevrey-class bounds (functions in GA,c,σ), and proves lemmas bounding coefficient norms and residual errors. The paper derives asymptotic query-complexity bounds (numbers of calls to OS and OF,ε) and qubit requirements under different smoothness and precision regimes, and identifies trade-offs between differentiation order n, step h, smoothness parameter σ, finite-precision qubit cost, and overall complexity. No empirical experiments or numerical simulations are reported; the work is analytical proofs and complexity derivations (oracles, constructions of Q1/Q2/Q3, and asymptotic bounds).

**Algorithms used:** Quantum Amplitude Estimation (QAE), Quantum Monte Carlo Integration (QMCI), Central-difference numerical differentiation (general order)

## Findings
- [speculative] Quantum algorithms based on quantum amplitude estimation (QAE) can be extended to compute numerical derivatives (sensitivities) of expected values V(x)=E[F(S,x)] by applying finite-difference formulas to the integrand F and estimating the resulting expectation with QAE.
- [speculative] Two concrete quantum methods are proposed: (i) a naive iteration method that calls the integrand oracle OF for each finite-difference grid point and then runs QAE, and (ii) a sum-in-QAE method that folds (sums) the finite-difference coefficients into the QAE amplitude preparation so the inner finite-difference sum and the expectation sum are done in one QAE.
- [speculative] With appropriate choices of finite-difference order n and step h both methods can estimate the m-th derivative V^{(m)}(x) to error ε with query complexity scaling as O(1/ε) up to multiplicative factors that depend on smoothness parameters, finite-difference parameters, and logarithmic factors.
- [speculative] The two methods have trade-offs: the naive iteration method is preferable when F is smooth and many qubits are available (it permits using low-order difference with small h and gives better query constants), while the sum-in-QAE method is preferable when F is nonsmooth or when quantum memory (qubits) is constrained (it avoids repeated calls to OF across grid points and reduces dependence on n).
- [speculative] For nonsmooth integrands (or when F has discontinuities), using high-order central-difference formulas with larger h together with the sum-in-QAE method yields better complexity and qubit usage than naive per-point evaluation with tiny h.
- [speculative] The qubit cost to implement finite-precision evaluation of the integrand OF,ε typically scales logarithmically in the needed precision (qubits ~ O(log(1/ε̃))), and small finite-difference step sizes h force higher precision (and so more qubits) to avoid catastrophic cancellation.
- [speculative] Under Gevrey-class smoothness assumptions (bounds on derivatives parameterized by A,c,σ), the authors derive explicit bounds/conditions (Theorems and Lemmas) ensuring the residual finite-difference error is ≤ ε and give corresponding query and qubit complexity formulas.
- [speculative] Asymptotically, derivative estimation via these quantum methods achieves the same 1/ε scaling (up to polylog factors) known for QMCI (expected-value estimation), i.e., no worse asymptotic dependence on ε than computing the expectation itself.

**Results summary:** The paper develops and analyzes two quantum algorithms for numerical differentiation of expected values V(x)=E[F(S,x)] using quantum amplitude estimation. One method (naive iteration) computes finite-difference terms by iteratively calling the integrand oracle at grid points then runs QAE; the other (sum-in-QAE) embeds the finite-difference coefficient sum into the QAE amplitude preparation so both sums are performed in one QAE. Under Gevrey-class smoothness assumptions on the target function and with explicit choice of finite-difference order and step size, the authors prove bounds on the residual error and derive query- and qubit-complexity estimates. They show that, with suitable parameter choices, derivative estimation can be performed with query complexity scaling O(1/ε) (up to logarithmic and problem-parameter factors) — comparable to the complexity of estimating the expectation itself — and they analyze trade-offs between query count and required qubits, concluding that sum-in-QAE is advantageous for nonsmooth integrands or limited qubit budgets whereas naive iteration can be better for very smooth integrands when many qubits are available.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** theoretical

The paper provides a theoretical (complexity-theoretic) extension of quantum Monte Carlo/QAE techniques to derivative (sensitivity) estimation and proves that, under smoothness (Gevrey-class) assumptions and appropriate finite-difference choices, derivatives can be estimated with query complexity scaling O(1/ε) (up to log and parameter-dependent factors) — matching the quadratic speedup over classical Monte Carlo's O(1/ε^2). The claim is supported by analytical proofs and complexity bounds, not by empirical experiments.
## Limitations
