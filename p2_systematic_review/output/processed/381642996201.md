## Abstract summary
The paper investigates the use of a quantum variational autoencoder (Quantum VAE) for anomaly detection in high-frequency trading (HFT) data, comparing its performance to a classical VAE. Using hybrid quantum-classical training (Pennylane for quantum components and TensorFlow for classical parts) and standard preprocessing, the authors report that the Quantum VAE achieves higher accuracy, recall, F1 score and precision-recall AUC on HFT anomaly detection tasks, while noting limitations such as training instability and current quantum hardware constraints.
## Methodology
The study compares a classical Variational Autoencoder (VAE) with a Quantum Variational Autoencoder (Quantum VAE / QVAE) for anomaly detection on high-frequency trading (HFT) data. The authors collected HFT data (order book features, prices, trade volumes) from major exchanges (cited as Dow Jones and NASDAQ). Preprocessing included data cleaning (missing-value imputation via interpolation, outlier removal), normalization (Min–Max or z-score), feature engineering (derived features such as price changes, volatility, moving averages) and dimensionality reduction/selection. The classical VAE was implemented in TensorFlow as an encoder–decoder MLP: encoder layers with ReLU/ELU activations producing μ and log σ2, the reparameterization trick z = μ + σ * ε, and a decoder reconstructing x′ with appropriate output activations. Loss combined reconstruction loss (MSE or binary cross-entropy depending on data) and a KL-divergence term weighted by β. Optimization used gradient-based optimizers (Adam or RMSprop). Anomaly detection used reconstruction error to compute an anomaly score with thresholding; evaluation metrics included accuracy, precision, recall, F1 score and precision‑recall AUC; latent-space visualization and PCA were used to inspect representations. The Quantum VAE was implemented using PennyLane. Classical neural-network components were hybridized with parameterized quantum circuits: a quantum encoder encoded classical features into quantum states via parameterized gates and (optionally) entangling layers, and a quantum decoder/readout measured observables to reconstruct classical outputs. Training was hybrid (classical optimizer updating both NN and quantum circuit parameters) minimizing a loss composed of reconstruction error plus quantum-specific regularization/KL terms. Experiments compared model performance (accuracy, F1, PR-AUC), training/validation loss curves, latent-space clustering, PCA projections, and anomaly detection visualizations. The quantum approach was reported to outperform the classical VAE on the described HFT dataset.

**Algorithms used:** Variational Autoencoder (VAE), Quantum Variational Autoencoder (Quantum VAE / QVAE), Principal Component Analysis (PCA)
**Frameworks:** PennyLane, TensorFlow

**Experimental setup:** Quantum VAEs were implemented using PennyLane (hybrid quantum-classical circuits) while classical VAEs were implemented using TensorFlow. **Dataset:** High-frequency trading (HFT) dataset containing order-book features, last price, mid price, bid levels, quantities, traded quantities, and related stock price characteristics collected from major exchanges (cited as Dow Jones and NASDAQ).
