## Abstract summary
This master’s thesis implements the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) for the portfolio optimization problem, performing both ideal (noiseless) and realistic noisy simulations using IBM's Qiskit. Results on small instances (up to five qubits) show that QAOA can approximate optimal portfolios, with noisy simulations performing worse than the ideal case (notably by introducing penalized states and degrading energy estimates), and the work discusses limitations (energy gaps for close returns, barren-plateau/noise issues) and avenues for improvements such as warm-starting, mixer modifications, and error mitigation.
## Methodology
This master’s thesis applied the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) to the portfolio optimization problem. The continuous portfolio optimization problem was discretized into a binary (QUBO) formulation (binary inclusion xi ∈ {0,1}) and mapped to an Ising-type final Hamiltonian Hf; the standard initial Hamiltonian H0 = -Σi Xi and initial state |+>^{⊗n} were used. QAOA parameterized circuits with p alternating layers UHf(γ) and UH0(β) were executed; classical optimization (COBYLA) was used to find the 2p parameters (⃗γ,⃗β). Both noiseless and noisy experiments were performed: noiseless runs used the Qiskit statevector simulator, noisy runs used Qiskit’s Qasm simulator with IBM device-calibrated noise models (Ibmq_manila and Ibmq_lima). Financial data (daily closing prices from Yahoo! Finance via the yfinance package) were used to compute asset returns, expected annual returns and annualized covariance matrices (250 trading days scaling). For evaluation, for each QAOA layer depth p (p = 1..21) twenty independent QAOA optimizations were run; for each p the mean of the top probabilities (probability of the optimal bitstring) and the mean of the smallest energies across runs were computed and plotted. Classical baselines were computed by sampling many random weight allocations (e.g., 90k, 300k, 600k random weight vectors) to identify classical optimum portfolios for comparison. Key penalty and problem parameters were: risk weight b = 1/2 and penalty λ = 2N (N = number of assets).

**Algorithms used:** QAOA, COBYLA (classical optimizer)
**Frameworks:** Qiskit (terra), Qiskit-Finance, yfinance, NumPy, SciPy

**Experimental setup:** Noiseless simulations: Qiskit Statevector simulator. Noisy simulations: Qiskit Qasm simulator using IBM-calibrated noise models (ibmq_manila, ibmq_lima). For each problem instance and for each layer depth p (1..21) twenty independent QAOA optimization runs were performed. Qiskit-Finance used 8192 shots per QAOA simulation when building measurement statistics. Classical baselines: large random sampling of weight vectors (examples: 90,000; 300,000; 600,000 samples) to estimate classical optimal portfolios.

**Dataset:** Daily closing prices for selected Brazilian equities retrieved from Yahoo! Finance via the yfinance package. Multiple datasets / experiments used different small sets of assets (2, 3, 4 and 5 assets) with date ranges e.g. 2016-01-01 to 2023-02-24 for most examples and 2016-01-01 to 2021-09-20 for one 4-asset example.

## Findings
- [supported] QAOA can approximate small-instance portfolio-optimization solutions (2–5 qubits) in noiseless state-vector simulations and often converges to the known optimal bitstring for the tested instances.
- [supported] Introducing realistic IBM noise models (Ibmq_manila, Ibmq_lima) degrades QAOA performance: optimal-state probabilities drop and constraint-violating (penalized) bitstrings acquire non-negligible probability.
- [supported] For one five-qubit test instance the noiseless QAOA final-state probability for the optimal bitstring was roughly ~60%, while the same instance with the Ibmq_manila noise model reduced that optimal probability to roughly ~35%.
- [supported] QAOA performance suffers when the classical problem has small energy gaps (small differences in expected returns): the algorithm tends to mix between near-degenerate solutions and may fail to single out the optimal bitstring.
- [supported] In the presence of noise the expectation value of the final Hamiltonian (energy) is a poor single diagnostic because noise increases probability of heavily penalized constraint-violating states, biasing the energy expectation.
- [supported] As the number of qubits increased (2→3→4→5 in these experiments) convergence became progressively more difficult in these tests; nevertheless the optimal state often remained the most probable outcome even when its probability did not approach 100%.
- [supported] Different hardware noise models produced different degradations (example: Ibmq_lima gave better results than Ibmq_manila in one 4-qubit test, plausibly linked to differing T2 values reported for the devices).
- [supported] Practical use of real IBM cloud devices for the used VQA workflow is constrained by queueing, per-job latency, and daily calibrations; the paper therefore used noisy simulators rather than many runs on real machines.
- [supported] Implementation choices: COBYLA (gradient-free) optimizer was used with maxiter=1000 and rhobeg=1.0; QAOA layers p were varied from 1 to 21; Qiskit used 8192 shots per cost-evaluation in the noiseless study.
- [speculative] QAOA (and other VQAs) remain promising approaches for NISQ-era optimization and may deliver advantages as hardware and error-mitigation improve; but quantum advantage for QAOA is not proven analytically.
- [speculative] Algorithmic modifications (warm-starting, constrained mixers, error mitigation, problem-inspired ansatzes) could mitigate small-gap and noise-induced failures observed in the experiments.
- [speculative] The poorer convergence in the five-qubit test (an 'outlier') might be attributable to the larger state space (more bitstrings), but the precise cause was not determined and requires further study.

**Results summary:** The thesis implements and tests the vanilla QAOA (up to 21 layers) on small portfolio-optimization instances (2–5 qubits) using Qiskit. In noiseless state-vector simulations QAOA often converged to the known optimal bitstrings (examples for 2–4 qubits); performance degrades under realistic IBM noise models (Ibmq_manila and Ibmq_lima): optimal-state probabilities decrease and penalized/constraint-violating bitstrings gain probability, which also skews energy-based diagnostics. A five-qubit noiseless example yielded ~60% optimal probability; with the Ibmq_manila noise model this fell to ~35%. The study also documents that small classical energy gaps (similar expected returns) hamper QAOA's ability to select the correct solution, and notes practical limitations of running many VQA parameter-evaluation loops on public IBM hardware.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The thesis does not claim demonstrated quantum advantage. It reports small-instance QAOA behavior (noisy and noiseless) and notes that analytic proof of QAOA advantage is lacking; the suggestion that QAOA may achieve advantage as hardware and methods improve is presented as a forward-looking, speculative position rather than an empirical claim.
## Limitations
- NISQ-era hardware constraints: limited qubit counts and substantial noise (no quantum error correction) that degrade QAOA performance (author-stated).
- Noise causes degraded performance vs. noiseless simulation; penalized (constraint-violating) states gain non-negligible probability under noise, corrupting expectation-value diagnostics (author-stated).
- Expectation value of the final Hamiltonian (energy) is not a reliable performance metric under realistic noisy conditions because noise elevates penalized states (author-stated).
- QAOA parameter optimization scales poorly: the 2p parameter search is expensive and NP-hard in general (author-stated).
- Barren plateau phenomenon (including noise-induced barren plateaus) can make gradient-based optimization ineffective (author-stated).
- QAOA’s practical quantum advantage is not proven analytically; comparisons with classical algorithms remain unsettled (author-stated).
- Small problem instances can show failure modes: QAOA struggled when expected returns of candidate assets were very close (small energy gaps) (author-stated).
- Five-qubit instance produced anomalous behaviour (did not fully converge) that remains unexplained in this work (author-stated).
- Limited accessibility and practicality of real quantum hardware for extensive VQA experiments (queueing, calibration windows, per-circuit queuing requirements) made use of real machines impractical for the experiment methodology (author-stated).
- Use of binary (0/1) asset selection is a modelling simplification that does not capture continuous allocation granularity used in real portfolios (author-stated).
- [inferred] The penalty-term formulation (hard budget constraint encoded as a large penalty) may create large energy separations that complicate optimization and amplify noise effects.
- [inferred] The chosen classical optimizer (COBYLA) and gradient-free strategy were used because noise precluded reliable gradients—this limits the potential of gradient-based optimizer performance.
- [inferred] Circuit depth growth with QAOA layers increases noise accumulation, further limiting useful depth (p) on current devices.
- [inferred] Simulation resource cost (large numbers of shots / many runs per layer) is high, limiting scalability of exhaustive empirical studies on larger instances.
## Open questions
- What caused the anomalous behaviour in the five-qubit simulation (poor convergence despite a clear most-probable optimal state)? (author-stated)
- Can QAOA (or modified versions) reliably scale to larger portfolio sizes (many qubits) while still producing useful solutions on NISQ hardware? (implied/author-stated)
- Which QAOA modifications (e.g., warm-starting, constrained/mixer Hamiltonians, problem-inspired ansatzes) best mitigate small energy gaps and noise-induced failures? (author-suggested)
- Can error mitigation or improved hardware (longer T1/T2, lower gate/readout errors) render expectation-value diagnostics reliable again, and to what extent will that close the gap with noiseless performance? (implied/author-stated)
- What are the best encodings for portfolio weights (binary vs. multi-qubit discretizations or continuous-relaxation warm-starts) to trade off representational fidelity vs. circuit complexity? (author-suggested)
- How to avoid or mitigate barren plateaus in practice for QAOA applied to portfolio optimization (ansatz design, initialization strategies)? (author-stated / implied)
- What classical optimizer choices and initialization heuristics most effectively navigate the QAOA parameter landscape for finance problems under realistic noise? (implied)
- Is the final-state probability gap (difference between the most probable and second-most probable state) a robust performance metric for noisy QAOA, and what thresholds would indicate usable solutions? (author-proposed as future metric)
- Under what conditions (problem structure, parameter regimes, hardware specs) can QAOA deliver performance competitive with or superior to classical optimization approaches for portfolio problems? (author-stated)

**Future work:**
- Test QAOA modifications such as warm-starting (continuous relaxation) and mixed/constrained Hamiltonian (alternative mixers) to improve convergence.
- Simulate larger instances (e.g., six qubits and beyond) to investigate scaling behaviour and to probe the five-qubit anomaly.
- Adopt and evaluate new performance metrics, e.g., the probability difference between the optimal state and the second-most-probable state in the final distribution.
- Explore non-binary encodings: use additional qubits to encode discretized weight magnitudes (more realistic continuous-like allocations) instead of binary selection.
- Apply error-mitigation techniques and assess their impact on noisy QAOA performance, and monitor how hardware improvements (T1/T2, gate/readout errors) affect outcomes.
- Investigate optimizer choices and initialization strategies (heuristic parameter seeding, meta-learning, natural gradient, etc.) to mitigate barren plateaus and speed convergence.

## Contradictions
- Paper reports that QAOA can approximate optimal portfolios on small instances but also demonstrates that noisy simulations and limited qubit counts (2–5 qubits) substantially degrade performance — this contradicts any strong claim of immediate practical scalability to real-world portfolio sizes.
- While the thesis frames QAOA as a promising approach, empirical results are limited to simulator-based experiments and comparison to classical random-sampling baselines suggests that classical methods remain viable for these small instances, undermining claims of clear quantum superiority for practical portfolio problems.
