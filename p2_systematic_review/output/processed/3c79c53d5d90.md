## Abstract summary
This preprint applies digitized-counterdiabatic quantum computing (DCQC) and a counterdiabatic-enhanced QAOA (DC-QAOA) to the discrete mean-variance (Markowitz) portfolio optimization problem by mapping it to an Ising Hamiltonian. Using variationally computed local approximate counterdiabatic terms and digitized adiabatic evolutions, the authors report substantial improvements in ground-state success probabilities across many random problem instances (up to 14 qubits) and show enhanced performance over standard QAOA, highlighting potential benefits for NISQ-era financial applications.
## Methodology
The authors study discrete mean-variance (Markowitz) portfolio optimization by mapping the integer portfolio allocation problem into a QUBO and then into an Ising Hamiltonian Hp (explicit J_ij and h_i formulas given). They evaluate digitized-counterdiabatic quantum computing (DCQC) and a CD-assisted QAOA (DC-QAOA) using classical numerical simulations. For the adiabatic approach they choose Hi = -Σ_i h_x σx_i, a time-dependent schedule λ(t) (a smooth sin^2-based schedule that vanishes at t=0,T), and add approximate counterdiabatic (CD) terms: a local CD (LCD) ansatz Ã_λ = Σ_i α_i(t) σy_i where α_i(t) is obtained by minimizing the action S = Tr[G_λ^2], and an approximate CD (ACD) term derived from the first-order nested commutator (NC) ansatz. Time evolution is digitized using first-order Trotterization: the total time T is discretized into M steps (Δt = T/M) and the evolution operator approximated as a product of local 1- and 2-body exponentials. Performance is assessed by simulating many random problem instances (mi and ρij randomly generated to mimic market trends), measuring ground-state success probability (overlap with the true ground state) and defined enhancement metrics (Renh and Penh) comparing evolutions with and without CD terms. For DC-QAOA they insert a CD-unitary UD(α)=exp(-i α Σ_i h_i σy_i) from an operator pool (operators produced by NC ansatz) into the QAOA layer sequence Up(γ) and Um(β); parameters (γ, β, α) are optimized with a classical stochastic gradient optimizer (Adagrad). They report results over many instances, sweep problem sizes (number of qubits up to 14), varying granularity g (number of binary slices per asset), Trotter-step choices and circuit depths (QAOA layer p).

**Algorithms used:** Digitized Adiabatic Quantum Computing (DAdQC), Counterdiabatic driving (variational approximate CD), Nested commutator (NC) ansatz for approximate gauge potential, First-order Trotter-Suzuki digitization, QAOA, Digitized-Counterdiabatic QAOA (DC-QAOA), Adagrad optimizer (classical)

**Experimental setup:** Classical numerical simulations of digitized quantum evolutions (first-order Trotterized unitary evolution) and variational QAOA circuit simulations. No specific quantum SDK, simulator name, or hardware backend is reported.

**Dataset:** Synthetic financial data: expected returns mi and covariance matrices ρij randomly generated to mimic real-world market trends. No external/real market dataset or public data source is specified.

## Findings
- [supported] Digitized-counterdiabatic quantum computing (DCQC) can be applied to discrete mean-variance (Markowitz) portfolio optimization by mapping the problem to an Ising Hamiltonian and performing digitized adiabatic evolution.
- [supported] Introducing approximate counterdiabatic (CD) driving terms (local CD and first-order nested-commutator ACD) in digitized adiabatic evolutions substantially increases the ground-state success probability in numerical simulations across many problem instances.
- [supported] The first-order nested-commutator approximate CD (ACD) term gave robust improvements across all tested instances (reported enhancement ratio = 1 for tested setups), i.e., it was always advantageous in the authors' simulations.
- [supported] Local CD (LCD) terms produce the largest improvements when the budget granularity (number of partitions g) is small; as g increases the LCD benefit diminishes.
- [supported] For larger granularity g (more binary variables per asset), the ACD (first-order nested-commutator) approach shows greater improvement than LCD.
- [supported] The success-probability enhancement from including CD terms tends to increase with system size in the presented simulations (tested up to N = 14 qubits).
- [supported] DC-QAOA (QAOA augmented with a CD unitary) outperforms standard QAOA in the authors' simulations on small portfolio instances (they report higher average success probabilities for DC-QAOA across layers p = 1, 3, 5).
- [supported] Practical implementation details used in the study: first-order Trotterization (error O(Δt^2)), all-to-all Pauli-Z couplings in the problem Hamiltonian, simulations with up to 20 Trotter steps for some parameter sets.
- [supported] Variational/minimization methods were used to obtain the approximate local CD coefficients without requiring instantaneous eigenstates.
- [supported] The authors observed strong run-to-run variability for hybrid variational algorithms (QAOA/DC-QAOA) due to nonconvex optimization landscapes and sensitivity to initialization (manifested as large standard deviations in Ps across random starts).
- [speculative] Extending beyond 2-local approximate CD terms (higher-order terms) is expected to yield further performance improvements.
- [speculative] Combining digitized-counterdiabatic algorithms with digital-analog quantum computing encodings could, in principle, help approach quantum advantage for industrial financial use cases in the NISQ era.

**Results summary:** The authors adapted digitized-counterdiabatic quantum methods to a discrete mean-variance portfolio optimization task by encoding it as an Ising Hamiltonian and performing digitized adiabatic evolution. Using numerical simulations across many random instances (up to 1000 instances, system sizes up to 14 qubits), they show that including approximate counterdiabatic driving—either local CD (LCD) or a first-order nested-commutator approximate CD (ACD)—markedly improves the probability of finding the ground state within short finite-time, Trotterized evolutions. ACD showed robust universal benefit in their tests, while LCD was most effective for lower granularity (smaller g). They also evaluated DC-QAOA (QAOA augmented with CD unitaries) and found it consistently outperformed naive QAOA on small instances, though both variational approaches suffer from optimization variability and initialization sensitivity. The study is simulation-based and emphasizes potential NISQ-era applicability while noting that further extensions (higher-order CD terms, digital-analog encodings) are possible avenues to explore.

## Limitations
- Digitization requires a large number of gates which reduces circuit fidelity and makes the algorithms impractical on NISQ devices without error correction.
- Adiabatic evolution requires long runtimes; limited coherence times and device noise prevent relying on truly adiabatic evolution in practice.
- Obtaining the exact adiabatic gauge potential (exact CD term) for many-body systems is difficult; exact CD terms are generally non-local N-body operators and experimentally challenging to implement.
- This study considered only approximate, local (2-local) CD terms; higher-order/non-local CD terms were not implemented.
- The simulation used first-order Trotterization, which introduces an O(Δt^2) trotterization error.
- All-to-all Hamiltonian connectivity requires additional SWAP gates on hardware with only nearest-neighbor interactions, increasing circuit depth and overhead.
- Hybrid QAOA/DC-QAOA approaches face classical optimization challenges: non-convex cost landscapes, local minima, barren plateaus, and sensitivity to parameter initialization leading to large run-to-run variance.
- Local CD (LCD) effectiveness decreases as the granularity (g) increases because the density of states near the ground state grows, increasing transition probabilities.
- [inferred] The experiments and simulations were limited to relatively small system sizes (up to N = 14 qubits); scalability to larger, industrial-scale portfolio instances is not demonstrated.
- [inferred] Market data used in experiments were randomly generated to mimic real-world trends; applicability and performance on real historical market datasets were not fully validated.
- [inferred] The impact of realistic device noise and hardware errors on the claimed enhancements from CD-driving (both in DCQC and DC-QAOA) was not fully characterized.
- [inferred] Resource requirements (gate counts, circuit depth, required connectivity) and precise thresholds for achieving quantum advantage were not quantified.
## Open questions
- How can one obtain and implement exact gauge potentials for many-body problem Hamiltonians, and what are practical ways to realize the resulting non-local CD terms experimentally?
- How much additional performance improvement is achievable by including higher-order (beyond 2-local) CD terms or higher orders in the nested-commutator ansatz?
- Can a digital-analog quantum computing encoding layered on top of digitized-counterdiabatic quantum algorithms enable practical quantum advantage for industrial financial use cases on NISQ devices?
- How do DCQC and DC-QAOA perform on real historical market data and on substantially larger portfolio instances relevant to industry?
- How robust are CD-assisted protocols (both DCQC and DC-QAOA) to realistic noise sources, gate errors, and limited qubit connectivity in current hardware?
- What classical optimization strategies or initialization heuristics can mitigate parameter-sensitivity, local minima, and barren-plateau issues in DC-QAOA for finance problems?
- What are the precise resource trade-offs (total evolution time T, Trotter step count M, circuit depth) required to reach practical success probabilities for portfolio optimization?
- How should CD strategies be adapted as portfolio granularity g increases and the density of low-lying excited states grows?

**Future work:**
- Extend the approach to include higher-order CD terms (beyond 2-local) to seek further enhancement in success probabilities.
- Develop a digital-analog quantum computing encoding on top of the digitized-counterdiabatic quantum algorithm as a route toward quantum advantage for industrial use cases in the NISQ era.
- Investigate and benchmark DCQC/DC-QAOA on larger system sizes and realistic market data to evaluate practical applicability.
