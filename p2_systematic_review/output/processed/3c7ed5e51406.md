## Abstract summary
This review emphasizes the need for sectors to proactively prepare for the impact of quantum computing by employing consultancy and governance solutions to assess risks and identify use cases. It outlines benefits such as increased competitiveness, improved security, and structured data practices, and proposes practical readiness strategies including in-house quantum offices, partnerships with quantum providers, engagement with national/international quantum organizations, and creation of governmental and international standards/certification for quantum readiness.
## Methodology
This is a non‑empirical, conceptual review and policy/strategy paper. The author conducted a narrative literature and policy survey drawing on published articles, industry reports, government and organizational initiatives, and news sources to synthesize the current state, risks, benefits, and readiness strategies for quantum computing across sectors. The paper cites academic papers, industry analyses (e.g. McKinsey, BCG), examples of public‑private partnerships (NEOM–Arqit), national and regional quantum initiatives, and market maps to illustrate use cases and ecosystem actors. From this synthesis the author derives a set of preparatory steps, governance recommendations, and proposed organizational responses (e.g., establishing a quantum computing office, contracting with vendors, creating standards bodies). No empirical methods, experiments, simulations, or quantitative analyses are reported; the approach is qualitative, literature‑based, and prescriptive.
