## Abstract summary
This preprint develops quantum algorithmic techniques to accelerate sampling and mixing for nonreversible Markov chains. The authors construct reversibilizations and apply generalized quantum singular-value and eigenvalue transforms (without always requiring the chain's time-reversal) to build approximate reflections through target coherent states, enabling quantum speedups — in some cases beyond the usual quadratic advantage — for preparing stationary distributions and for Quantum Monte Carlo applications.
## Methodology
This is a theoretical and algorithmic study that develops quantum procedures to accelerate sampling from the stationary distribution of nonreversible Markov chains. The authors (1) formalize oracle access and projected unitary encodings (PUE / SPUE) of Markov-chain-derived matrices (flat and curved discriminants) using Szegedy walk operators and qubitization; (2) show how to obtain approximate projectors and reflections onto coherent states |π⟩ (stationary distributions) by applying generalized quantum singular-value transforms (GQSVT) to curved discriminants and generalized quantum eigenvalue transforms (GQET) to flat discriminants; (3) introduce and analyze two workflows that interleave classical Markov steps (running the chain for an integer number of steps) with quantum polynomial transforms: (a) apply GQSVT to P^{τ_rev} (where τ_rev is a reversibilization time defined via pseudo-spectral gaps) to realize a reflection in cost O(√(τ_rev · τ_mix)); (b) apply GQET to the flat discriminant D^{(t)} after t classically-run steps where the chain becomes “reversible on π-average” (introducing the geometric reversibilization Q and the “most reversible” distribution µ), using composed polynomials (fast-forwarding and leading-eigenvalue-selection polynomials) of degree O(δ^{-1/2} log(1/ε)) to isolate the leading eigenvector; (4) derive complexity bounds and sufficient conditions for quantum speedups (including beyond quadratic speedups in favorable regimes) and prove supporting propositions, lemmas and corollaries relating discriminant spectra, overlaps ⟨π|µ⟩, and mixing/pseudo-spectral gaps; (5) provide mathematical proofs and illustrative numerical experiments (toy nonreversible walks with bottlenecks) to demonstrate the separation between the reversibilization time trev and the classical mixing time. The methodological contributions are analytic (spectral analysis, polynomial approximation constructions) and algorithmic (quantum circuit resource counts given in terms of uses of Szegedy walk operators and polynomial degrees).

**Algorithms used:** Szegedy quantum walk, Qubitization / qubitized walk operator, Generalized Quantum Singular Value Transform (GQSVT), Generalized Quantum Eigenvalue Transform (GQET), Quantum Signal Processing (GQSP), Fast-forwarding polynomial construction, Leading-eigenvalue-selection polynomial, Amplitude amplification / state preparation primitives, Quantum Amplitude Estimation (referenced), Quantum Rejection Sampling (referenced)

## Findings
- [speculative] Quantum algorithms can accelerate mixing and state-preparation for nonreversible Markov chains, extending beyond the known quadratic speedup for reversible chains.
- [speculative] Two algorithmic approaches are proposed: (1) a singular-value-transform (GQSVT) approach that requires access to the chain and its time-reversal, and (2) an eigenvalue-transform (GQET) approach acting on the flat discriminant that does not require simulating the time-reversal.
- [speculative] If one runs the chain classically for a number of steps (τ_rev or t_rev) until it is 'reversible enough', then applies a quantum transform, one can build an approximate reflection about the target coherent state |π⟩ with improved cost.
- [speculative] The first method (GQSVT on P^{τ_rev}) attains a complexity scaling of order O(√(τ_rev · τ)), where τ is the chain mixing time and τ_rev is a reversibilization time defined in the paper (Proposition 1).
- [speculative] The second method (GQET on the flat discriminant after t_rev classical steps) attains complexity Õ(γ(Q)^{−1/2} log(1/ε)), where Q is the introduced geometric reversibilization and γ(Q) its spectral gap (Proposition 3).
- [speculative] They introduce and formalize 'reversibility on π-average' (a condition where ⟨π|D^j|π⟩ is close to 1 relative to the spectral gap of the geometric reversibilization Q^j) and define t_rev as the earliest j satisfying it; if t_rev ≪ mixing time then a quantum speedup follows.
- [speculative] The paper formalizes how projected unitary encodings (PUE/SPUE), Szegedy walk operators, GQSVT and GQET can be composed to construct approximate projectors/reflections onto stationary coherent states for nonreversible chains.
- [speculative] They derive polynomial degree and scaling bounds for the 'fast-forwarding' and 'leading-eigenvalue-selection' polynomials used in the transforms (e.g., polynomial degree O(δ^{−1/2} log(1/ε)) to separate eigenvalues).
- [supported] Numerical examples (toy chains with bottlenecks and quasi-stationary behavior) show instances where the reversibility-on-π-average condition is met long before the classical mixing time, suggesting practical regimes where the quantum methods would provide speedups.
- [speculative] Theoretical claims that in some constructed examples the reflection construction time can be exponentially smaller than the mixing time (paper gives an example in the Appendix).
- [speculative] The work discusses potential application impact across statistics, ML, molecular simulation, stochastic differential equations and finance, claiming the algorithms could decisively impact these areas if implemented.

**Results summary:** This preprint develops quantum-algorithmic techniques to accelerate sampling and state-preparation for nonreversible Markov chains. Two complementary workflows are proposed: (1) apply generalized quantum singular-value transforms (GQSVT) to P^{τ_rev} (requiring time-reversal access) yielding overall cost O(√(τ_rev · τ)), and (2) apply generalized quantum eigenvalue transforms (GQET) to the flat discriminant after t_rev classical steps (no time-reversal) with cost Õ(γ(Q)^{-1/2} log(1/ε)), where Q is a geometric reversibilization and γ(Q) its gap. They introduce the notion of 'reversibility on π-average' and show theoretically that when this condition is met long before classical mixing, a quantum speedup (potentially beyond quadratic) follows. The paper proves supporting propositions and illustrates phenomena with numerical examples.

**Performance claims:**
- O(√(τ_rev · τ)) overall complexity for constructing an approximate reflection using GQSVT on P^{τ_rev} (Proposition 1).
- Õ(γ(Q)^{−1/2} log(1/ε)) uses of the Szegedy operator to prepare 2|µ⟩⟨µ|−I up to spectral-norm error ε when reversibility-on-π-average holds (Proposition 3).
- There exist polynomials (fast-forwarding / leading-eigenvalue-selection) of degree O(δ^{−1/2} log(1/ε)) that separate eigen/singular values to accuracy ε (Proposition 2 and related constructions).
- Existence of a reversibilization time τ_rev defined by γ_∞(P) = γ(P^{τ_rev} (P^⋆)^{τ_rev})/τ_rev that controls the pseudo-spectral-gap-based speedup.
- The scaling factor of the fast-forwarding polynomial used in the GQSVT construction is 1 (Corollary in Methods/Appendix).
- Numerical experiments (examples with bottlenecks) show 1 − ⟨π|D^j|π⟩ ≪ γ(Q^j) for j ≪ τ_mix, i.e., t_rev much smaller than the classical mixing time in those examples (empirical observation in figures).
## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** theoretical

The paper provides theoretical algorithms and proven complexity bounds showing potential quantum speedups (quadratic and in some regimes beyond quadratic, even claimed up-to-exponential in certain constructed examples) for sampling from nonreversible Markov chains. These are formal/theoretical results with numerical simulations on toy examples; there is no experimental demonstration on quantum hardware or empirical benchmarking against large-scale classical implementations, so the advantage remains theoretical/speculative.
## Limitations
- The methods require spectral properties (gaps/overlaps) of discriminant matrices that may be hard to verify in practice (author-stated).
- The construction that uses the time-reversal (R⋆) may be infeasible when the time-reversed chain or the stationary distribution π is unknown or hard to sample from (author-stated).
- No general, easy-to-check criterion is provided for the reversibility-on-π-average condition; determining whether the condition holds may require additional spectral information or experimentation (author-stated).
- Performance depends on reversibilization times (τrev, trev); if these are large (comparable to the mixing time) the quantum speedup can disappear (author-stated).
- There is a fundamental lower bound: processes that mix in the time required to cross the underlying graph (diameter) cannot be accelerated beyond that limit (author-stated).
- [inferred] The paper focuses on finite-state Markov kernels; extension to continuous-state processes or infinite state spaces is not fully developed and may face technical challenges.
- [inferred] Implementation assumes access to oracles that prepare superpositions |x> -> sum_y sqrt{P(x,y)} |x,y>; these oracles may be expensive or unavailable for some practical transition kernels.

- [inferred] The methods rely on being able to compute or approximate certain polynomials (fast-forwarding, eigenvalue-selection) and their signal-processing scalings; numerical stability/precision issues may arise in practice.
## Open questions
- How can one efficiently and generally verify the reversibility-on-π-average condition for a given nonreversible chain?
- How can practitioners estimate the relevant spectral quantities (spectra of flat/curved discriminants, γ(Q), γ∞(P), τrev, trev) in realistic large-scale problems?
- When and how can the time-reversal operator R⋆ be constructed or bypassed in settings where π is unknown or P⋆ is intractable?
- Which classes of nonreversible Markov chains admit trev ≪ mixing time, enabling more-than-quadratic quantum speedups, and can these classes be characterized broadly?
- Can the proposed algorithms be extended and made practical for continuous-state dynamics (SDE discretizations, piecewise-deterministic Markov processes) used in finance and physics?
- How tight are the presented spectral bounds for flat discriminants in concrete models of interest (molecular dynamics, jump processes) and can they be improved?
- What are the best methods to construct Szegedy walk operators efficiently for specific application domains, and what are the trade-offs?
- Can experimental procedures (e.g., phase estimation of qubitized walk operators) be used reliably to obtain the missing spectral information needed to decide algorithmic choices?
- What are the precise quantum resource (qubit count, gate depth, error tolerance) requirements for the algorithms on problem sizes of practical interest?
- How do the proposed quantum speedups translate into end-to-end gains in downstream tasks such as amplitude estimation, importance sampling, or full Monte Carlo pipelines in finance?

**Future work:**

- Extend the theoretical and algorithmic framework to model biochemical networks and piecewise-deterministic Markov processes more realistically.
- Explore applications to financial modeling with nonreversible jump processes and evaluate empirical performance.
