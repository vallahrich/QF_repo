## Abstract summary
The paper introduces protocols to prepare quantum states whose amplitudes encode multivariate functions by linearly combining block-encodings of Fourier and Chebyshev basis functions. The methods avoid arithmetic circuits, QFTs, and multivariate QSP, provide resource scaling (qubit and two-qubit gate counts), and include numerical demonstrations (2D Ricker wavelet, bivariate Student's t, 3D Coulomb electron wavefunctions) and a 24-qubit experimental preparation of bivariate Gaussians on a trapped-ion device. The approach targets improved practicability for applications in finance, physics, and chemistry simulations.
## Methodology
The authors develop and analyze quantum circuits to prepare multi-dimensional quantum states whose amplitudes encode multivariate functions by (i) approximating a target function on [−1,1]^D (or [0,1]^D with a periodic extension) with a truncated Fourier or Chebyshev series of maximal degree d in each dimension, (ii) computing the series coefficients classically (e.g. via FFT-based interpolation), (iii) block-encoding each basis function (Fourier basis via single-qubit phase gates implementing eiπkH where H is the discretization diagonal; Chebyshev basis via qubitized block-encodings and powers to realize Tk(H)), (iv) encoding the coefficients into ancilla registers and combining the basis-function block-encodings with linear combination of unitaries (LCU) A†CBA to form a block-encoding of the approximant fd(H), and (v) applying this block-encoding to a uniform superposition on the main registers and post-selecting ancillas in |0...0⟩ to obtain (after renormalization) amplitudes proportional to fd evaluated on the grid. They analyze resource scaling (qubit counts, two-qubit gate counts) as functions of dimensions D, per-dimension qubits n, and degree d; provide circuit constructions and gate-count estimates for both Fourier and Chebyshev variants; and validate the approach with simulations and an experimental demonstration on a trapped-ion QPU. Classical preprocessing (FFT/interpolation or analytic coefficient evaluation) and postprocessing (kernel density estimation with cross-validation for noisy measurement data) are explicitly incorporated into the pipeline. Success probability, dependence on coefficient norms, and possible amplitude-amplification tradeoffs are discussed.

**Algorithms used:** Linear Combination of Unitaries (LCU), Block-encoding, Qubitization, Fourier series approximation, Chebyshev polynomial approximation, Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) - classical, Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) - classical
**Frameworks:** pytket

**Experimental setup:** Quantinuum H2-1 trapped-ion QPU (32 qubits, all-to-all connectivity). Native gate set: PhasedX(α,β) single-qubit rotations and ZZPhase(θ) two-qubit gates. Typical fidelities reported at experiment time: single-qubit infidelity ~3×10^-5, two-qubit infidelity ~2×10^-3, SPAM errors ~2×10^-3. Circuits compiled with pytket (optimization level 2). Experiments: preparation of 2D Fourier-approximated Gaussian states on 24 qubits (nx=ny=9 main qubits, ax=ay=3 ancilla qubits), runs of 20,000 shots per experiment, no error mitigation applied. Measurement post-selection on ancillas = |0...0⟩, output counts processed with KDE (bandwidth chosen by cross-validation).

## Findings
- [speculative] Introduces a method to prepare quantum states encoding multivariate functions by combining truncated Fourier or Chebyshev series, block-encoding of basis functions, and linear-combination-of-unitaries (LCU) circuits (no arithmetic circuits, QFT, or multivariate QSP required).
- [speculative] Provides analytic resource scalings: two-qubit gate count O(dD + D n log d) for the Fourier-series-based method and O(dD + D d n log n) for the Chebyshev-based method, with qubit count scaling O(D n + D log d) (Fourier) or O(D n + D log d + D log n) (Chebyshev).
- [supported] Demonstrates numerically, via statevector simulation, preparation of several example multivariate amplitude-encoded states (2D Ricker wavelet, bivariate Student’s t-distribution, 3D single-electron Coulomb wavefunctions) matching the intended approximations to machine precision (limited by series truncation error).
- [supported] Presents compilation resource estimates and statevector simulations for 3D Coulomb electron wavefunctions (examples using up to 30 qubits and quantified two-qubit gate counts in Table 2).
- [supported] Reports an experimental demonstration on Quantinuum H2-1 trapped-ion hardware preparing bivariate Gaussian approximations (Fourier degree 3) on a 512×512 grid using 24 qubits with up to 237 two-qubit gates; provides measured fidelities and success probabilities (no error mitigation).
- [supported] Experimental metrics: for the uncorrelated 2D Gaussian experiment, fidelity F = 0.986 and empirical success probability ≈ 0.1299; for the correlated 2D Gaussian experiment, fidelity F = 0.918 and empirical success probability ≈ 0.0877 (20,000 shots each).
- [speculative] Claims the method is simpler and more effective than prior proposals (e.g., Fourier Series Loader, Walsh series loader, black-box/Grover-Rudolph approaches) because it avoids QFT and arithmetic, and can have lower two-qubit-gate counts asymptotically (discussion and comparison in Appendix D).
- [speculative] States that the success probability of the LCU-based preparation equals (sum_x |fd(x)|^2) / (N^2 2^{Dn}) with N = sum of absolute coefficient magnitudes; empirical examples show success probabilities on the order of a few to a few tens of percent for tested functions.
- [supported] Provides open-source code and data (GitHub and Zenodo) to reproduce the protocols and experiments.

**Results summary:** The paper develops and analyzes state-preparation protocols that encode multivariate functions into quantum-state amplitudes by (i) approximating target functions with truncated Fourier or Chebyshev series, (ii) block-encoding the basis functions, and (iii) combining them with LCU. They claim lower practical complexity than several prior approaches and provide code and numerical data for reproducibility.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

It provides methods with potentially lower practical gate/qubit overheads compared to some prior state-preparation proposals and demonstrates experimentally feasible implementations on mid-scale hardware, but these constitute efficiency/engineering improvements and resource-scaling analyses rather than proofs or demonstrations of algorithmic quantum advantage.
## Limitations
- Requires the target function to admit a truncated Fourier or Chebyshev series (Lipschitz for Chebyshev; Lipschitz + periodicity or periodic extension for Fourier).
- Accuracy and gate/resources depend on the approximation degree d; the scaling d(ε) (degree required for target error ε) is problem-dependent and not analyzed in the paper.
- Success probability is generally < 1 (post-selection required); boosting it requires amplitude estimation or amplification at an extra multiplicative gate cost O(p^{-1/2}_{success}).
- Chebyshev construction needs an extra ancilla register of size b = ⌈log2 n⌉ and has larger two-qubit gate cost (prefactor and dependence on n) than the Fourier variant.
- Periodic-extension of non-periodic targets (for the Fourier method) can introduce discontinuities (e.g., in derivatives) that reduce convergence rates (Gibbs-like issues).
- Multivariate error bounds are harder to characterize rigorously than in 1D; convergence guarantees depend strongly on smoothness and analytic properties of f.
- The method is efficient only if d ∈ O(poly(n)); if d grows too fast with required precision or dimension, the approach becomes impractical.
- Classical preprocessing (computing coefficients, e.g. via multi-dimensional FFT) can be costly in high degree or high dimension (cost ∝ ¯d log ¯d with ¯d = ∏_j d_j), and may dominate end-to-end cost.
- The Chebyshev block-encoding relies on ∥H_C∥ = 1; for operators with λ ≠ 1 the mapping would not be exact and requires additional considerations or approximations.
- Noise and circuit depth materially affect fidelity in practice; deeper circuits (e.g., for correlated targets) performed worse in the hardware demonstration.
- [inferred] For high D (many dimensions) the qubit and gate counts (scaling ∼ dD + D n log d or ∼ dD + D d n log n) may become prohibitive, limiting applicability to moderate-dimensional problems.
- [inferred] The approach assumes access to either analytic coefficients or the ability to compute them classically; when function evaluations are expensive, preprocessing may be impractical.
- [inferred] The requirement to post-select on ancillas and to have ancilla registers for coefficients trades circuit depth for additional qubits; in qubit-limited regimes this could be a constraint.
- [inferred] Choice of compiler/decomposition and multi-controlled gate synthesis (e.g., C_bZ implementations) materially influences actual two-qubit costs and is not fully optimized in the paper.
## Open questions
- What is the general/typical scaling d(ε) (degree vs. desired approximation error) for multivariate functions of practical interest and how does that affect end-to-end complexity?
- How to best mitigate and analyze errors introduced by periodic extension (Fourier) and by truncation/interpolation for non-smooth or singular functions?
- Can the Chebyshev approach be adapted effectively when ∥H∥ ≠ 1 (i.e., for operators that require rescaling), and what are the trade-offs of approximate remedies?
- What are optimal constructions/decompositions for the multi-controlled gates (e.g., C_bZ) used in the Chebyshev block-encoding, given different hardware connectivity/constraints?
- How to integrate amplitude amplification or amplitude estimation with minimal overhead in realistic hardware/noisy settings so as to improve success probability efficiently?
- To what extent can low-rank/structured classical approximations (tensor decompositions, cross approximation, MPS/Tensor-Train) reduce preprocessing and quantum resources for high-D problems?
- How expressive and practical are multivariate QSP (M-QSP) variants in comparison to the LCU-based approach; can M-QSP be made numerically stable and sufficiently expressive?
- What is the best way to combine the presented LCU-based preparation with univariate QSP (or other filtering) to implement tasks like hypersurface preparation or other multivariate non-linear transforms?
- How do noise, error accumulation, and realistic device constraints change the practical resource break-even between this method and alternatives (FSL, Walsh, black-box, Grover–Rudolph, adiabatic, MPS)?
- How to perform end-to-end error budgeting (classical preprocessing error, truncation/interpolation error, block-encoding error, circuit noise) for application-specific targets (finance, chemistry, PDEs)?

- Integrate state preparation with downstream quantum algorithm workflows (including error mitigation and amplitude amplification) and evaluate end-to-end performance.
- Apply the method to more realistic quantum-chemistry/physics unit cells (e.g., FCC lattices) and to antisymmetrized many-electron initial states for first-quantized simulations.
- Explore improved periodic-extension or smoothing schemes to reduce Gibbs-like artifacts for Fourier approximations of non-periodic targets.
- Perform systematic benchmarking and comparison (including noise and compilation effects) against other state preparation methods (FSL, Walsh, black-box, Grover–Rudolph, adiabatic, MPS) across problem sizes and dimensions.
