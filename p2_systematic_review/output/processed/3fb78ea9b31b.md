## Abstract summary
The paper introduces Quantum Beetle Antennae Search (QBAS), a quantum-inspired variant of the beetle antennae search meta-heuristic, formulated to solve constrained portfolio optimization. The authors present theoretical properties (stability, convergence, polynomial time/space complexity) and empirical results on real stock data (SSE 50) showing QBAS outperforms swarm algorithms such as PSO and GA in convergence speed and Sharpe-ratio performance.
## Methodology
The authors formulate a quantum-inspired variant of the Beetle Antennae Search (BAS) algorithm, named Quantum Beetle Antennae Search (QBAS), and apply it to constrained portfolio optimization. They convert the constrained Sharpe-ratio portfolio problem into an unconstrained single-objective by adding penalty terms for equality and inequality constraints (weights sum to one and bounds 0<=Ei<=1). QBAS is derived by modeling the BAS update as a quantum particle in a delta potential well: they use the time-independent Schr¨odinger equation for a delta potential, obtain an exponential-form probability density for the quantum displacement, and collapse that distribution (Monte Carlo sampling) to produce a stochastic update rule X_n = X_{n-1} ± (L/2) ln(1/u) with L = 1/(g |C ΔF|) (C = d_n c). The classical BAS, PSO and GA are used as baselines. Empirical evaluation includes (1) optimization benchmark functions (Ackley, Rastrigin, Beale, Levi N.13) run 20 times from different initial conditions, and (2) portfolio selection experiments on stock-return data drawn from the Shanghai Stock Exchange 50 Index (reported date range 21 Mar 2019–18 Apr 2019) with portfolios of sizes 20, 50, 75 and 100. Performance metrics reported are the objective value F(E) (modified Sharpe objective), Sharpe ratio, and the equality constraint residual S(E)=sum(Ei). Convergence plots and comparative tables versus BAS, PSO and GA are provided. The algorithmic loop uses a step-control parameter d_n that decays via d_n = 0.95 d_{n-1} + 0.01 and runs for a fixed number of iterations (reported experiments use K≈100). Several penalty weight parameters (β1..β4) and g (characteristic constant with requirement g>ln(sqrt(2))) are introduced but not fully numerically specified in the paper.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum Beetle Antennae Search (QBAS), Beetle Antennae Search (BAS), Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Genetic Algorithm (GA)

**Dataset:** Stock return data claimed to be taken from the Shanghai Stock Exchange 50 Index (SSE 50 Index). Time period reported: 21 March 2019 to 18 April 2019. Experiments performed on subsets of size 20, 50, 75 and 100 stocks.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The paper presents QBAS as a quantum-inspired algorithm (derivation uses quantum-mechanical formalism) and reports empirical improvements over classical heuristics on specific benchmark and portfolio instances. However, QBAS is not executed on quantum hardware, and no formal complexity separation or hardware-based quantum speedup is demonstrated; thus any claimed 'quantum advantage' is speculative/empirical on tested instances rather than a demonstrated quantum computational advantage.
## Limitations
- QBAS requires three objective-function evaluations per iteration (F(Xr), F(Xl), F(X)), which the authors state makes it computationally expensive and time-consuming for substantially large datasets.
- QBAS is a single-particle meta-heuristic algorithm; the authors note this limits its search capability and makes it prone to getting stuck in local minima (it does not ensure local-minima avoidance).
- The experimental evaluation uses a limited time window and specific stock set (stocks from SSE 50 Index selected between 2019-03-21 and 2019-04-18), implying limited empirical scope and potential lack of generality.
- The formulation and experiments do not incorporate many realistic portfolio constraints (cardinality constraints, lower and upper bounds, class constraints, round-lots constraints, pre-assignment constraints), which the authors acknowledge were not included.
- The algorithm was compared to BAS, PSO and GA only; no comparison was made with other quantum/meta-heuristic methods (e.g., QPSO, quantum annealing-based approaches) beyond those listed.
- [inferred] The presented QBAS is a quantum-inspired formulation but the paper does not implement or demonstrate execution on real quantum hardware (i.e., no hardware-level quantum implementation or resource assessment).
- [inferred] Sensitivity to hyperparameters (e.g., penalty weights β1..β4, scaling factors, dn decay schedule) and robustness across parameter settings is not extensively characterized.
- [inferred] The study focuses on Sharpe-ratio as the objective; other performance considerations such as transaction costs, turnover, liquidity constraints, alternative risk measures, or out-of-sample performance are not evaluated.
- [inferred] The cost/benefit trade-off between QBAS's faster convergence (per iteration) and per-iteration computational overhead is not quantified (no wall-clock time or scalability analysis at large N).

- How does QBAS perform across different markets, longer time horizons, and under more volatile/less-stable market conditions (e.g., emerging markets) beyond the limited dataset used?
