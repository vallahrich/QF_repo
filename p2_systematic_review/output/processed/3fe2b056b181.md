## Abstract summary
The paper formulates a multivariate nonlinear integer programming model for bank credit scorecard optimization and transforms it into a QUBO formulation to be solved with annealing-based methods. It demonstrates use of quantum annealing (and simulated annealing for larger Hamiltonians) to select credit cards and threshold combinations that maximize bank revenue under several scenarios, reports example solutions and analyzes advantages and limitations of the approach.
## Methodology
The authors formulate the credit scorecard selection/thresholding problem as a multivariate nonlinear integer programming problem that maximizes a bank total-income objective. They encode each card-by-threshold choice as a binary variable (100 credit cards × 10 threshold groups = 1000 binary variables) and express the revenue objective in quadratic form (X^T A X or X^T K X). Constraints (each card must pick exactly one threshold; choose exactly k cards in some scenarios) are enforced via penalty terms added to the objective to produce a QUBO/Hamiltonian. The paper describes constructing the Hamiltonian (potential + kinetic components) and a quadratic_to_qubo routine to assemble the QUBO matrix from quadratic coefficients. For optimization they apply a quantum-annealing style algorithm conceptually (Hamiltonian evolution, transverse field, acceptance via Metropolis criterion) for one scenario, and classical metaheuristics for others: simulated annealing for constrained multi-card selection scenarios and classical exhaustive / depth-first search enumeration for small instances and verification. The annealing-based procedures initialize parameters (initial temperature T0, initial transverse-field strength lambda0, MaxSteps), generate candidate states by random perturbation, accept or reject candidates using the Metropolis probability, and reduce the transverse field or temperature according to a cooling schedule until termination. Final outputs are the selected card(s), chosen threshold(s) and the associated maximum bank income for each scenario.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum annealing (QA), Simulated annealing (SA), Depth-first search (DFS) / enumeration

**Experimental setup:** No concrete hardware/simulator configuration is reported.
