## Abstract summary
The paper introduces the basic principles of quantum computing and surveys its applications and industry status, with a focus on optimization. It highlights work at Universidad de Montevideo and the startup Quantum-South on air cargo loading and portfolio optimization, and aims to motivate professionals and students to engage in quantum optimization research.
## Methodology
The authors addressed combinatorial optimization problems (exemplified by Airbus Quantum Computing Challenge Problem #5: Aircraft Load Optimization) by mapping the loading problem to a knapsack-style optimization and formulating an appropriate Hamiltonian for quantum processing. They used a hybrid variational approach (VQE) on IBM cloud-accessible universal quantum gate devices (via Qiskit/IBM Quantum) by encoding the objective and constraints into a quantum-mechanical cost Hamiltonian and running the VQE iterative quantum-classical optimization loop. In parallel they implemented equivalent formulations as QUBO models to target quantum annealing platforms. The implemented solution models container types and aircraft positions (case: A330-200F), enforces weight and volume constraints (and considers ordering effects such as center-of-gravity and structural shear limits), and searches for high-quality load configurations. The team reports implementing the algorithms across multiple platforms (VQE on gate-model devices and QUBO on annealers) and refining functionality and efficiency toward real-world scenarios, but they do not report detailed numerical hyperparameters or extensive quantitative benchmarking in the paper.

**Algorithms used:** VQE, QUBO (formulation for quantum annealing / classical annealers)
**Frameworks:** Qiskit, IBM Quantum (cloud access)

**Experimental setup:** Hybrid VQE experiments executed on IBM cloud-accessible quantum processors (universal gate-model devices) using a proposed Hamiltonian derived from a knapsack formulation of the aircraft loading problem; alternative implementations used QUBO formulations for quantum annealing hardware. ## Limitations
- Main limitation of the Universal Quantum Gate Model is maintaining qubit stability as the number of qubits increases (authors).
- We are in the NISQ era: current universal machines have a low number of qubits and can only address limited applications (authors).
- VQE-based approaches on current universal hardware can solve mostly toy problems for logistics (authors).
- Quantum annealing machines are restricted to problems expressible as QUBO (authors).
- Large, high-capacity quantum computers require special installation conditions (very low temperatures) and are typically accessed via the cloud (authors).
- Quantum Supremacy has been claimed for specialized tasks but practical Quantum Advantage for large real-world optimization problems is still to be demonstrated (authors).
- The Airbus Aircraft Loading problem used in the challenge was a simplification of reality, implying that mapping full real-world complexity remains challenging (authors).
