## Abstract summary
This preprint presents LEP-QNN, a quantum machine learning framework that uses a variational quantum neural network with an integrated quantum dropout mechanism to predict loan eligibility. The authors evaluate different optimizers (Adam, RMSProp, Adagrad, gradient descent), report high predictive performance (around 98% accuracy on a loan dataset), and study robustness under multiple quantum noise models to assess practical resilience in NISQ environments.
## Methodology
The study develops a variational Quantum Neural Network (QNN) framework (LEP-QNN) for loan eligibility prediction. The methodology includes dataset ingestion and preprocessing (normalization using mean and standard deviation, data cleaning and feature engineering), angle (rotation) encoding of classical features into quantum states, and a multilayer parameterized ansatz composed of RY and RX single-qubit rotations followed by CNOT entangling gates arranged in a ring. A quantum-dropout regularization scheme is applied during training by randomly deactivating a fraction of rotation gates at certain circuit depths to mitigate overfitting. A pre-testing/ansatz-selection phase samples and evaluates multiple ansatz candidates using cross-validation on portions of the dataset, choosing a fixed ansatz based on accuracy, convergence speed and loss reduction. Training minimizes an MSE cost using variational parameter updates; multiple classical optimizers are compared (Gradient Descent, Adam, RMSProp, Adagrad) with specified step sizes and learning-rate ranges. Experiments include convergence analysis over 100 iterations, evaluation on a held-out test set without dropout, and robustness testing under a variety of quantum noise models (bitflip, phaseflip, bitphaseflip, depolarizing, phase damping, amplitude damping). Performance is reported with standard classification metrics (precision, recall, F1-score, accuracy) and MSE loss and compared against classical baselines.

**Algorithms used:** Variational Quantum Neural Network (QNN), Angle (rotation) encoding, Parameterized variational ansatz (RY/RX + CNOT ring), Quantum dropout (circuit-level dropout), Classical optimizers: Gradient Descent, Adam, RMSProp, Adagrad
**Frameworks:** PennyLane, Qiskit, IBM Quantum (cloud backends)

**Experimental setup:** High-performance classical workstation (Intel Xeon Platinum 8259CL CPU @ 2.50GHz) with access to quantum/cloud backends (IBM Quantum) and hybrid quantum-classical development using PennyLane and Qiskit. Simulations and training performed with circuit simulators; IBM Quantum backends were available for execution.

**Dataset:** Loan eligibility dataset (Kaggle "loan-eligible-dataset" / Dream Housing loan dataset). Contains applicant demographic and financial features including Gender, Marital Status, Education, Dependents, Income, Loan Amount, Loan Term, Credit History, Property Area and Loan Status (binary).

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

Therefore the claim of quantum advantage remains speculative rather than demonstrated.
