## Abstract summary
The paper investigates whether replacing a single dense layer in a classical fraud-detection neural network with a variational quantum circuit (VQC) improves minority-class sensitivity on the Kaggle credit card fraud dataset. Using data re-uploading to expose all 30 features and a novel correlation-guided 'priority entanglement' scheme, the hybrid model (under matched parameter budgets) yields modest but consistent gains in recall and PR-AUC versus tuned classical baselines, with best performance at moderate entanglement budgets and shallow circuit depth; deeper or overly entangled circuits degraded performance due to trainability limits.
## Methodology
The study performs a controlled, parity-matched comparison between a classical neural-network classifier and a hybrid quantum–classical classifier in which a single dense layer is replaced by a variational quantum circuit (VQC). All preprocessing, optimizer, batch size, epochs, and the outer model trunk and head are kept identical between the classical and hybrid models; the only change is the middle block. The hybrid VQC uses data re-uploading to expose all 30 PCA-derived features to a limited qubit budget (Q in {10,15}) by partitioning the features into C = ceil(30/Q) chunks. A data-driven priority-entanglement scheme is used: the Pearson correlation matrix is computed on the training set only, feature pairs with largest absolute correlation that fit within re-uploading chunks are selected as priority two-qubit couplings (num priority pairs varied), and these priority CNOTs are applied before a trainable strongly-entangling block. The VQC measures Pauli-Z expectation values on each wire to produce Q outputs that feed a shared linear head. Training uses weighted binary cross-entropy with logits (positive class weight w+ = N0/N1) optimized by Adam (lr=0.001), batch size 32, for 10 epochs. Experiments ablate qubit count (10 vs 15), quantum depth (1,3,5 layers), and priority-pair budgets (0,10,30,80,135). To limit simulator cost they downsampled the majority class to 10,000 legitimate transactions while retaining all 492 frauds, and used stratified 50/25/25 train/val/test splits. Repeated randomized trials (100 runs for 10-qubit settings, 20 for 15-qubit settings) are used to report mean±std of recall, precision, PR-AUC and other metrics; classical baselines (logistic regression, XGBoost, SVM, and a classical NN with matched parameter count) are included for comparison.

**Algorithms used:** Variational Quantum Circuit (VQC), Data re-uploading, Correlation-guided priority entanglement (custom CNOT scheduling), Logistic Regression, XGBoost, Support Vector Machine (SVM), Classical Neural Network, Adam optimizer
**Frameworks:** PennyLane (default.qubit simulator), PyTorch (Torch interface, PennyLane TorchLayer), scikit-learn (StandardScaler, classical baselines), XGBoost

**Experimental setup:** Simulator-based experiments on a full-state simulator (PennyLane default.qubit) run on a host with an Intel Xeon Gold 6254 CPU and 64 GB RAM. 10-qubit experiments repeated 100 randomized runs per configuration; 15-qubit experiments repeated 20 runs. Wall-clock times reported per configuration; deeper circuits and larger coupling budgets greatly increase runtime (minutes for 10q, hours for 15q).

**Dataset:** Kaggle Credit Card Fraud dataset (Machine Learning Group, ULB and Worldline, 2019). Original size 284,807 transactions with 492 fraudulent cases (~0.17%). Features are 30 anonymized PCA-like components plus 'Amount' and derived hour-of-day; 'Time' column converted to hour-of-day then dropped.

## Findings
- [supported] Replacing a single dense layer in a classical neural network with a variational quantum circuit (VQC) using data re-uploading and a correlation-guided priority entanglement scheme produced small but reproducible improvements in recall and PR-AUC in simulation under matched parameter budgets.
- [supported] The hybrid QNN achieved its best reported PR-AUC of 0.9229 ± 0.006 (15 qubits, 1 quantum layer, 10 priority pairs) compared to a logistic regression PR-AUC of 0.9150 ± 0.014 on the same downsampled benchmark.
- [supported] With 10 qubits the hybrid’s best PR-AUC was 0.9192 ± 0.008 (1 quantum layer, 80 priority pairs) and the highest observed recall was 0.9235 ± 0.017 (1 quantum layer, 135 priority pairs).
- [supported] Against the best classical recall (0.8832 ± 0.014 for the classical neural network), the hybrid reported an absolute recall increase of approximately +0.0468 (to ~0.9301 ± 0.011 in the 15q/1-layer/10-pairs setting).
- [supported] Data re-uploading was used to expose all 30 input features with limited qubit budgets (Q ∈ {10, 15}), enabling the VQC to process the full feature set in chunks.
- [supported] A moderate number of correlation-priority entanglement pairs (roughly 10–80 pairs depending on config) tended to maximize PR-AUC; excessively large pairing budgets or excessive circuit depth often reduced performance (precision/PR-AUC) and exacerbated a precision–recall trade-off.
- [supported] Increasing quantum circuit depth (more layers) did not reliably improve PR-AUC under the used training budget and often produced higher recall at the cost of sharply reduced precision (e.g., 15q, 5 layers).
- [supported] Experiments were simulation-only, used downsampling of the majority class (retain all 492 frauds, 10,000 legitimate samples), and averaged results over many randomized runs (100 runs for 10-qubit configs, 20 runs for 15-qubit configs).
- [supported] Wall-clock training time scaled substantially with qubit count, entanglement budget, and depth: 10-qubit runs ranged minutes per config, 15-qubit runs required hours on CPU simulation.
- [speculative] The correlation-guided priority entanglement provides an inductive bias that focuses representational capacity on empirically informative feature relationships and thus narrows the effective search space during optimization (analogy to attention mechanisms).
- [speculative] The observed performance degradation at very high entanglement budgets or depth is attributed to over-parameterization and trainability issues (e.g., barren-plateau like effects), suggesting shallow, structured ansatzes are preferable under limited training budgets.
- [speculative] The proposed approach (data re-uploading + priority entanglement) is a promising candidate for near-term, hardware-constrained deployments provided hardware-aware pairing and further noise/latency optimizations are applied.
- [speculative] Recommended future directions include hardware experiments, bootstrapped/significance testing of PR-AUC/recall lifts, adaptive sparsification of entanglers, and evaluation on additional fraud/anomaly datasets.

**Results summary:** In simulation on a downsampled version of the Kaggle credit card fraud dataset (all 492 frauds retained, 10,000 legitimate transactions), swapping a single dense layer for a variational quantum circuit with data re-uploading and a Pearson-correlation-driven priority entanglement scheme produced modest, reproducible improvements in minority-class sensitivity. Best reported gains were an absolute PR-AUC increase (e.g., 0.9229 ± 0.006 for a 15-qubit, 1-layer, 10-pair hybrid vs. 0.9150 ± 0.014 for logistic regression) and an absolute recall increase of roughly +0.047 over the best classical baseline. Performance peaked at moderate entanglement budgets and shallow depth; increasing depth or over-entangling often worsened the precision–recall balance. Experiments were simulation-only and run under strict capacity parity between classical and hybrid models.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** demonstrated

The paper presents empirical simulation results showing modest, reproducible improvements in minority-class sensitivity (recall) and PR-AUC when replacing a single dense layer by a VQC with data re-uploading and correlation-guided entanglement under matched parameter budgets. The advantage is demonstrated in simulation on a downsampled Kaggle fraud benchmark, peaks for shallow/moderate entanglement settings, and is limited by circuit depth, entanglement budget, and simulator runtime.
## Limitations
- Simulation-only study: results are obtained via full-state simulation and do not account for hardware noise, limited connectivity, or realistic runtime constraints.
- Anonymized PCA-like features limit interpretability of which raw attributes drive model behavior.
- Fewer repetitions for 15-qubit experiments (20 runs) than for 10-qubit and classical experiments (100 runs), increasing statistical uncertainty for the 15-qubit results.
- Majority-class downsampling (10,000 legitimate transactions) rather than using the full majority class or synthetic oversampling (e.g., SMOTE) may affect generalizability compared with other sampling strategies.
- Potential trainability issues (barren plateaus, vanishing gradients) for deeper/deeperly entangled circuits under the fixed training budget and hyperparameters.
- High computational cost / long wall-clock runtimes for wider/deeper circuits limited the scope and repetition of some experiments.
- Single-dataset evaluation (Kaggle credit card fraud benchmark) restricts assessment of robustness and generalization to other fraud/anomaly datasets.
- [inferred] The parity-matched experimental design swaps only a single dense layer for the VQC, so conclusions are limited to that architectural substitution and may not generalize to other hybrid architectures or full QNN stacks.
- [inferred] The proposed priority entanglement scheme depends on reordering features so that high-correlation pairs lie in the same re-uploading chunk; this design choice may be dataset-specific and may not transfer well when natural feature locality or hardware connectivity constraints differ.
- [inferred] Fixed optimizer, learning rate, batch size, and number of epochs for all runs could mask configurations where deeper/more entangled circuits become trainable with different optimization schedules or initialization strategies.
## Open questions
- How do the observed recall and PR-AUC gains translate to real quantum hardware under realistic noise, limited qubit connectivity, and gate infidelities?
- Are the gains from the correlation-guided priority entanglement scheme statistically significant and robust (e.g., via paired bootstrap or other hypothesis tests) across repetitions and datasets?
- Does the correlation-guided entanglement generalize to other datasets, feature types (non-PCA), and different imbalance regimes, or is it specific to the Kaggle PCA-like features?
- What is the optimal entanglement budget and depth for a given qubit count before trainability and over-mixing degrade performance, and how does this scale with dataset size and feature structure?
- Can hardware-aware (connectivity-constrained) or learned sparse entanglement patterns retain the benefits of the priority scheme while respecting device topology?
- How do different data-encoding schemes, readout observables, and measurement strategies affect the expressive power and trainability of the VQC in this task?
- Would alternative optimization strategies (e.g., layerwise training, identity-preserving initialization, different optimizers or learning-rate schedules) mitigate barren plateaus and enable deeper, more expressive circuits to improve PR-AUC without collapsing precision?
- How does threshold calibration against real-world business cost curves (chargebacks vs review cost) change the practical utility of the hybrid model’s precision–recall trade-offs?
- How sensitive are the results to the preprocessing choices (e.g., downsampling vs oversampling, scaling, inclusion of Time/Amount features) and to different train/validation/test splits?
- What are the latency and inference-throughput implications of deploying such hybrid models in production authorization pipelines, especially when factoring in quantum runtimes or near-term quantum hardware access overhead?

**Future work:**
- Extend the framework to hardware-efficient entanglement layouts that respect device connectivity.
- Perform bootstrapped / paired significance testing to quantify the statistical significance of PR-AUC and recall lifts against classical baselines.
- Investigate dynamic (adaptive) correlation mapping and learned sparsification of entanglement to avoid over-coupling.
- Evaluate robustness under noisy conditions and on actual quantum hardware.
- Develop hardware-constrained pairing strategies and adaptive sparsification to reduce over-coupling.
- Align decision thresholds with cost-sensitive/business cost curves to calibrate operating points for real-world deployments.
- Replicate experiments on additional fraud and anomaly detection datasets to test generalization beyond the Kaggle benchmark.
- Explore mitigation strategies for trainability issues (e.g., identity-preserving initialization, layerwise growth, locality-based regularization) to enable deeper ansatze.
- Investigate hardware implementations and assess performance/latency trade-offs in production-like environments.

- #idea:hybrid-approach — A pragmatic hybrid architecture (classical trunk/head, single quantum middle block) with data re-uploading enables encoding 30 features into Q={10,15} qubits and retains end-to-end training with classical optimizers.
- #idea:near-term-feasibility — Best empirical performance occurs at moderate entanglement budgets and shallow circuit depth, suggesting some NISQ-era usefulness; deeper/over-entangled circuits suffer trainability degradation.

- #limitation:qubit-count — Experiments restricted to 10 and 15 qubits and rely on data re-uploading (feature chunking); scaling to larger feature sets or higher-fidelity encodings may be constrained by qubit availability and runtime.
- #limitation:data-encoding — Uses PCA-like features and data re-uploading with custom priority-entanglement; the costs/overheads and fidelity of such encodings on hardware are not evaluated.
- #limitation:no-empirical-validation — Majority-class downsampling (to 10,000) plus simulator-only evaluation and limited epoch counts mean real-world deployment performance remains unvalidated.
