## Abstract summary
The authors introduce engineered likelihood functions (ELFs) and Bayesian inference techniques to accelerate expectation-value and amplitude estimation on noisy quantum devices, interpolating between standard sampling (VQE) and quantum phase estimation. Their ancilla-free, tunable ELF circuits mitigate noise-induced 'dead spots', increase Fisher information per sample, and are robust to realistic errors; simulations and a runtime model show reduced measurement counts and practical advantages for near-term hardware.
## Methodology
The paper develops and analyzes a methodology for reducing measurement/runtime costs of expectation-value estimation on noisy quantum devices by engineering quantum-generated likelihood functions (ELFs) and performing adaptive Bayesian inference. The authors propose parameterized quantum circuits (both ancilla-free and ancilla-based) composed of sequences of generalized reflections U(x)=exp(-i x P) and V(y)=A R0(y) A^• to produce likelihoods whose bias is a tunable polynomial (Chebyshev forms are a special case). They adopt an explicit exponential-decay noise model for circuit layers and SPAM, yielding a noisy likelihood P(d|θ; f, x)=1/2[1+(−1)^d f ∆(θ;x)], with f=ar{p} p^L. The inference procedure represents the unknown parameter θ=arccos(Π) with a Gaussian prior N(µ,σ^2) and iteratively: (1) chooses circuit parameters x to maximize an information-gain proxy (Fisher information I(µ;f,x) or slope |∆'(µ;x)|) using heuristic optimizers (gradient ascent and coordinate ascent that exploit trigono-multilinear/quadratic structure and O(L) coefficient evaluation); (2) executes the chosen circuit (simulated by sampling from the analytical noisy likelihood) to obtain outcome d; (3) updates the posterior via Bayes' rule and approximates the posterior by a Gaussian of matching mean and variance; (4) repeats until convergence. To speed up the tuning, they precompute lookup tables of optimal x across a dense grid in Π. For Bayesian updates they use an efficient local sinusoidal fit (r, b) of the bias in the prior-concentrated region and closed-form approximate update formulas for mean and variance. They benchmark ELF against standard sampling and Chebyshev likelihood functions (CLFs), study ancilla-free vs ancilla-based circuits, explore the effect of layer fidelity and circuit depth, and develop a runtime model (including scaling with qubit count and two-qubit gate fidelity) that interpolates between Heisenberg-limited and shot-noise-limited regimes.

**Algorithms used:** Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) (as baseline / context), Quantum Amplitude/Phase Estimation (discussed / related), alpha-VQE (referenced / related), Engineered Likelihood Functions (ELF) via generalized reflections, Chebyshev Likelihood Functions (CLF) (special case), Bayesian parameter estimation (Gaussian prior approximation), Gradient ascent (for parameter tuning), Coordinate ascent (for parameter tuning), Local sinusoidal least-squares ﬁt (r,b) for approximate posterior update

**Experimental setup:** Experiments are numerical simulations (classical) of the adaptive Bayesian inference loop. Quantum circuits are not simulated at the statevector level; instead measurement outcomes are synthesized by sampling from the analytical noisy likelihood P(d|θ;f,x) (Eqs. (23) / (D3)). Typical settings: number of circuit layers L up to 6 in presented results; layer fidelity p varied (e.g. 0.75–0.95) and SPAM factor ar{p} often set to 1 in experiments; fidelity of full process f=ar{p} p^L. For Monte Carlo estimation of performance they run M=300 independent simulation runs to estimate RMSE over time. Parameter tuning uses precomputed lookup tables over a grid S of 4001 Π values and local optimization (Algorithms 1-8) called with the prior mean µ and fidelity f. For Bayesian updates they sample Θ = {µ − σ, µ − 0.8σ, ..., µ + σ} (11 points) for the sinusoidal fit. Time per circuit is modeled as T(L)=2L+1 in units of ansatz A duration.

## Findings
- [supported] Engineered Likelihood Functions (ELFs) and 'enhanced sampling' increase the information gain per measurement relative to standard sampling used in VQE, reducing the number of measurements needed in simulation studies.
- [supported] A simple enhanced sampling circuit with one extra layer (L=1) gives a Fisher information of 9 at Π=0 compared to 1 for standard sampling, implying up to a 9× reduction in required measurements in that case.
- [supported] In the noiseless model the single-sample Fisher information for the Chebyshev-style scheme scales quadratically with the number of layers: I ∝ (2L+1)^2.
- [supported] The paper proposes ancilla-free circuits to generate tunable likelihood functions (ELFs); simulations show ancilla-free ELFs typically outperform ancilla-based variants for the tested scenarios.
- [supported] Chebyshev likelihood functions (CLFs) exhibit 'dead spots' (parameters where Fisher information collapses); ELFs obtained by tuning generalized reflection angles can remove or mitigate these dead spots (supported by analytic derivations and simulation).
- [supported] The authors provide efficient algorithms (gradient and coordinate ascent variants) to tune circuit parameters for maximizing proxies of expected information gain; the cost of computing required coefficients scales O(L) and optimization iterations scale O(L^2) per iteration in practice.
- [supported] Bayesian inference (with a Gaussian approximate posterior and sinusoidal local ﬁt) combined with ELFs converges in simulation; inverse MSE of the amplitude estimator empirically grows roughly linearly in time for fixed L in tested regimes.
- [supported] Numerical experiments (simulations) indicate there is an optimal effective circuit fidelity (for a fixed native gate fidelity) in the range ~0.5–0.7, i.e. pushing to deeper circuits can help up to a point after which accrued noise negates gains.
- [speculative] The ELFs approach provides a continuous interpolation between VQE-style sampling (O(1/ε^2) scaling) and phase/amplitude estimation (O(1/ε) scaling) as a function of available coherence/noise; the manuscript provides a runtime model formalizing this transition.
- [speculative] They present a semi-analytic runtime model for noisy devices (Eqs. (2) and (85)) that predicts runtime scaling that depends on a noise parameter λ and precision ε and that interpolates between Heisenberg-like and shot-noise-like regimes.
- [speculative] For large-scale problems (example parameters n=100 logical qubits, layer depth D=200) and optimistic gate-time assumptions, the model suggests non-trivial runtimes only when two-qubit gate fidelities become very high (they discuss requirements on the order of many nines and note error correction trade-offs).
- [speculative] The paper conjectures that ELFs can strictly improve worst-case inverse-variance rates over CLFs by at most a small multiplicative factor (left as a conjecture, not proven).
- [speculative] The adopted noise model for likelihood functions is an exponential decay per layer (f = p^L · p_SPAM), which is sufficient (but not claimed necessary) to produce the modeled ELF behavior; practical implementations will require likelihood-function calibration.
- [speculative] The authors argue (model + simulation) that incorporating active noise tailoring and calibration can suppress systematic bias due to noise, but claim calibration requirements are left for future work.

**Results summary:** The paper introduces engineered likelihood functions (ELFs) and an ancilla-free enhanced-sampling circuit family to accelerate expectation-value (amplitude) estimation for hybrid quantum algorithms such as VQE. Analytic derivations and numerical simulations demonstrate that ELFs can substantially increase Fisher information per measurement (e.g., a 9× increase for a simple L=1 example) and mitigate 'dead spots' present in Chebyshev likelihood functions. The authors present efficient tuning algorithms (gradient/coordinate ascent) and an approximate Bayesian inference pipeline (Gaussian posterior approximation with sinusoidal local fits) to use ELFs in practice. They model noise as exponential per layer and develop a runtime model that interpolates between shot-noise and Heisenberg-like behaviors as device fidelities improve. Simulations (e.g., L=6, per-layer fidelity p=0.9) show ANCILLA-FREE ELFs outperform CLFs and ancilla-based variants across a range of test priors and demonstrate practical trade-offs between circuit depth and accumulated noise. The work is supported by analytical expressions, algorithmic complexity analyses, and extensive classical simulations; it does not include physical quantum-hardware experiments.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The paper presents algorithmic techniques (ELFs, enhanced sampling) and simulation evidence showing substantial reductions in measurement/runtime compared to standard VQE sampling (including analytic Fisher-information gains). However, these are supported primarily by classical simulations, analytical models, and assumptions about noise (exponential per layer) and calibration; there is no experimental demonstration on physical quantum hardware nor a provable, hardware-agnostic asymptotic advantage. Thus the claim of quantum advantage is presented as theoretical/model-supported and speculative rather than experimentally demonstrated.
## Limitations
- The noise model used (exponential decay / simple depolarizing composition per layer plus SPAM factor) is a simplifying assumption and may be too simplistic to describe real device noise accurately (authors acknowledge it is sufficient but not necessary).
- Validation of the noise/likelihood model and methods at large system scales is missing; the exponential-decay likelihood model has been validated only on small experiments (authors note large-scale validation is future work).
- The Bayesian inference routine approximates true posteriors as Gaussians (moment-matching), which is an approximation and may not hold in all regimes.
- Optimization of circuit parameters relies on local heuristic solvers (gradient / coordinate ascent) and lookup tables over discretized Π; global optimality is not guaranteed and scaling of parameter-tuning overhead is a concern.
- Empirical runtime / performance results are based on classical simulations that sample from the modeled likelihood (pseudo-random synthesis) rather than end-to-end noisy quantum hardware experiments.
- The runtime model and its bounds rest on a number of approximations and assumptions (e.g., variance ≈ estimator variance, worst-case bounds from Chebyshev likelihoods, reparameterizations of fidelity as exponential with λ and α); these assumptions limit generality and may break down in high-noise regimes (authors note breakdown when λ ≥ 1).
- There are specific inference "dead spots" for Chebyshev likelihood functions; while ELFs mitigate these, their effectiveness depends on accurate tunability and calibration of generalized reflection angles.
- The approach requires calibrated nuisance parameters (likelihood-function calibration); miscalibration can introduce systematic bias into estimates (authors point out calibration is necessary and left as future work).
- Analysis and claims about favorable regimes (e.g., optimal circuit fidelities around 0.5–0.7 for fixed gate fidelity) are based on particular device and error assumptions and may not generalize across platforms or ansatz choices. [inferred]
- [inferred] The ancilla-free vs ancilla-based comparisons and claimed superiority of ancilla-free ELFs are derived in the contexts considered and may be sensitive to different ansatzes, observables, or hardware connectivity/constraints.
- [inferred] The lookup-table approach for parameter choices (discretizing Π) and repeated parameter optimization may become costly for large-scale or rapidly changing problems; the scalability of the classical precomputation is not fully addressed.
- [inferred] The model uses optimistic hardware timing / gate-time assumptions for conversion of circuit cost to seconds (e.g., extremely small two-qubit gate time), so runtime conclusions may be optimistic for many current platforms.
- [inferred] The paper assumes layer fidelities multiply (f = ¯p p^L) and uniform per-layer error; real devices have heterogeneous fidelities and non-Markovian / gate-dependent errors that can invalidate this model.
## Open questions
- How well does the proposed exponential-decay likelihood model capture real device noise at scale, and how should it be extended or replaced for more realistic noise?
- What is the required precision for likelihood-function calibration (learning nuisance parameters) to ensure that miscalibration bias is negligible in practice?
- Can we derive analytical upper and lower bounds on estimation performance (beyond the empirical / Chebyshev-based bounds used) for ELFs under realistic noise models?
- How to design eﬃcient, scalable algorithms to choose the number of circuit layers L (circuit depth) dynamically and optimally given device noise and target accuracy?
- How robust are the Gaussian posterior approximations and sinusoidal local fits used for fast Bayesian updates across regimes (high noise, multimodal posteriors, or broad priors)?
- To what extent can measurement grouping / parallelization techniques (used in VQE) be combined with enhanced sampling / ELFs, and what are the trade-offs between extra circuit depth and variance reduction?
- Is the conjecture that ELFs increase the worst-case inverse-variance rate (relative to CLFs) by only a small constant factor true in general, and can it be proven?
- What is the impact of non-ideal, gate-dependent, or correlated noise (non-depolarizing / non-i.i.d.) on the effectiveness of ELFs and the derived runtime model?
- How does the method perform on real near-term quantum hardware (small-scale experiments) and which practical obstacles (calibration, drift, crosstalk) matter most?
- What are the practical costs (classical compute, memory) of the parameter-tuning and lookup-table approach as system size and L grow?
- Under what circumstances (error-correction overhead, logical gate times) does quantum error correction become beneficial for these estimation tasks when traded off against increased runtime?
- How to extend ELF and the Bayesian inference methods to multi-parameter estimation tasks or to combined estimation of multiple Pauli-term expectations simultaneously?

- Quantify the effect of miscalibration and model error more rigorously and develop mitigation strategies (active noise-tailoring, calibration protocols).
## Key ideas
- #idea:quantum-advantage — Engineered Likelihood Functions (ELFs) plus adaptive Bayesian inference can reduce measurement counts and increase Fisher information per sample relative to standard sampling, yielding practical runtime improvements in expectation-value/amplitude estimation under realistic noise models.
- #idea:near-term-feasibility — Ancilla-free, tunable ELF circuits and ancilla-based variants are designed for NISQ devices: they mitigate 'dead spots', are robust to SPAM and layer noise, and provide a tunable interpolation between shot-noise and Heisenberg-like regimes depending on fidelity.
- #idea:hybrid-approach — The method is hybrid: classical optimizers, lookup tables and Bayesian updates drive selection of quantum circuit parameters; classical pre/post-processing (sinusoidal fits, Gaussian posterior approximations) are key to practical performance.
- #limitation:noise — The advantage depends critically on per-layer fidelity f = ar{p} p^L; realistic noise causes decay of likelihood amplitude and can force the regime back to shot-noise scaling, limiting asymptotic Heisenberg gains.
- #limitation:simulation-only — All experiments are numerical simulations where measurement outcomes are sampled from an analytical noisy likelihood model (not statevector or real QPU runs); circuit-level errors beyond the exponential-decay model are not empirically tested.
- #limitation:no-empirical-validation — No real-hardware experiments are reported; empirical validation on physical devices is outstanding and necessary to confirm modeled runtime advantages.
## Contradictions
- This work demonstrates that realistic noise models cause an interpolation from Heisenberg-limited to shot-noise-limited scaling, contradicting more optimistic claims that amplitude/phase estimation will routinely yield Heisenberg scaling on noisy near-term devices; practical advantage requires sufficiently high per-layer fidelity and limited circuit depth.
