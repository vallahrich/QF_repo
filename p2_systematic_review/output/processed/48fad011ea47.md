## Abstract summary
This preprint proposes QADQN, a hybrid quantum-classical Deep Q-Network that integrates variational quantum circuits as a multi-head quantum attention mechanism within a reinforcement learning trading agent. The authors backtest the approach on S&P 500 historical data (including transaction costs), report improved returns and risk-adjusted metrics compared to buy-and-hold and a DDPG baseline, and discuss training details such as prioritized replay initialized with Dual Thrust and use of the Lion optimizer.
## Methodology
The paper proposes a hybrid quantum-classical reinforcement learning agent, QADQN (Quantum Attention Deep Q-Network). Input OHLC market data (transformed to log-returns) are fed into an LSTM (hidden size 64) to extract temporal features. The LSTM outputs pass through a classical pre-net (linear layers reducing dimensions 64 -> 32 -> 8 -> 4) whose outputs are encoded into parameterized quantum circuits (angle-embedding RX gates) implemented with PennyLane. These VQCs (entangling with chain CNOTs and layered rotation gates) are used to produce quantum-derived Queries, Keys and Values for a Quantum Multi-Head Self-Attention mechanism (attention matrix computed as Aij = −(Qi − Kj)^2, normalized by sqrt(dh), softmaxed and multiplied by V). The attention outputs are concatenated and passed through an additional quantum layer to produce Q-values. The RL setup is a Deep Q-Network with a target network, prioritized experience replay (initially pre-populated with Dual Thrust strategy trajectories as imitation learning), a discrete action space {sit, buy, sell}, and an Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) selection policy. Training uses Huber loss on TD-errors, optimization with the Lion optimizer, periodic target-network updates, and discount factor γ = 0.95. Backtesting uses the Backtest library including a commission of 0.2%. Performance is reported against Buy & Hold and a DDPG baseline using return, Sharpe, Sortino, and maximum drawdown metrics.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum Attention Deep Q-Network (QADQN), Variational Quantum Circuit (VQC), Quantum Multi-Head Self-Attention (QMSA), Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), Deep Q-Network (DQN) with Target Network, Prioritized Experience Replay, Dual Thrust trading strategy (for replay pre-population / imitation), Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) action selection, Lion optimizer, Huber loss
**Frameworks:** PennyLane, Backtest (backtesting library), Yahoo Finance API (data source)

**Experimental setup:** Hybrid quantum-classical experiments implemented using PennyLane for VQCs (NISQ-style circuits simulated), Backtest library for backtesting. Configured with 4 qubits per quantum layer, 24-day input window, 200 training episodes, commission 0.2%. Training/evaluation performed on historical S&P 500 OHLC data from Yahoo Finance; no specific QPU or cloud hardware is reported.

**Dataset:** S&P 500 index historical OHLC data fetched from the Yahoo Finance API. Training period: 2016-01-15 to 2020-01-15. Two evaluation periods: overlapping (2019-01-16 to 2024-01-16) and non-overlapping (2020-01-16 to 2024-01-16).

**Results summary:** The paper presents QADQN, a hybrid quantum-classical Deep Q-Network that embeds variational quantum circuits to implement a quantum multi-head self-attention mechanism. The authors trained and backtested the agent on S&P 500 historical OHLC data (including a 0.2% commission) and report that the QADQN outperformed Buy & Hold and a DDPG baseline on both overlapping and non-overlapping test periods. Reported performance metrics include overlapping-period return 78.91% and non-overlapping-period return 71.26%, Sharpe ratios around 0.82/0.77, Sortino ratios of 1.19 and 1.28, and a maximum drawdown of 19.24% in both periods. Methodologically, the approach uses a seed replay buffer populated via the Dual Thrust strategy, prioritized replay, a UCB action selection policy, and the Lion optimizer. While the paper argues for potential quantum advantages (reduced attention overhead, improved explainability), these claims are not empirically substantiated as quantum speedups or hardware demonstrations in the work.
