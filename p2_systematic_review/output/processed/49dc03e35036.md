## Abstract summary
The paper provides the first end-to-end resource estimates and an upper bound on the resources needed for meaningful quantum advantage in pricing financial derivatives, using autocallable options and Target Accrual Redemption Forwards (TARFs) as benchmarks. It identifies limitations of prior state-loading approaches, introduces a new re-parameterization method that combines pre-trained variational Gaussian loaders with fault-tolerant quantum computation to reduce resource costs, and estimates benchmark requirements on the order of 8k logical qubits and tens of millions of T-depth (runtime at the order of a second for advantage).
## Methodology
This work is a theoretical, end-to-end resource and error analysis for quantum algorithms to price path-dependent financial derivatives. The authors map derivative pricing to expectation estimation by discretizing asset price (or log-return) paths onto ndT qubit registers and expressing the price as a sum/integral over paths. They use amplitude-encoding approaches that (1) prepare a superposition of paths according to a stochastic model p(omega), (2) compute payoffs in quantum parallel, (3) load the normalized payoff into an ancilla amplitude, and (4) estimate the expected payoff using quantum amplitude estimation. For amplitude estimation they adopt Iterative Quantum Amplitude Estimation (IQAE) resource bounds. The paper investigates two distinct methods to prepare the path distribution: a Riemann-summation style loading (extending prior work) and a novel re-parameterization method. The re-parameterization method operates in return (log-return) space, prepares many independent standard normal quantum states in parallel, applies an affine transform via Cholesky factor L to obtain the correlated multivariate normals for all timesteps/assets, and then (if needed) computes prices from log-returns. The standard-normal loaders are prepared by variational state preparation: an Ry-CNOT ansatz is trained offline using an energy-based pretraining (quantum harmonic oscillator Hamiltonian whose ground state is Gaussian) followed by L-infinity reﬁnement. The paper also presents a detailed fixed-point quantum arithmetic model (addition, multiplication, square root, exponentials, arcsine via piecewise polynomial approximation) and corresponding gate/T-count and T-depth analyses, and propagates arithmetic, discretization and truncation errors to obtain overall error bounds. Two realistic, computationally-challenging, path-dependent derivative benchmarks are used (a basket autocallable and a TARF) with specific contract parameters; the paper computes resource estimates (logical qubits, T-count, T-depth) for both loading approaches and for payoff circuits, identifies blocking normalization issues for the Riemann method, and shows the re-parameterization method reduces resources to levels with logical-qubit counts in the thousands and T-depths in the 1e7-1e8 range for target accuracy ~2e-3. No empirical quantum hardware experiments or classical dataset benchmarks are run; all results are analytical estimates and numerical simulations of small circuit training for the variational Gaussian preparation.

**Algorithms used:** Iterative Quantum Amplitude Estimation (IQAE), Variational state preparation (VQE-style energy-based pretraining + L-infinity reﬁnement), Riemann-summation path loading (extension of previous work), Re-parameterization method (standard-normal loading + Cholesky aﬃne transform), Grover-Rudolph state preparation (discussed and shown impractical here), Fixed-point quantum arithmetic (addition, multiplication, division via multiplication, square root algorithm), Piecewise polynomial approximation methods for smooth functions (exponential, arcsine) via quantum arithmetic, Controlled-Ry angle encoding / rotation synthesis (Cliﬀord+T / Selinger synthesis references), Comparator and logical circuits (reversible comparators, AND/OR) for payoff logic

- [speculative] Grover–Rudolph state-preparation approaches are argued to be inapplicable in practice for derivative pricing because they require evaluation of classical integrals (i.e., Monte Carlo-style integrals) as part of state preparation, negating the desired quantum advantage.
- [supported] Variationally trained Ry-CNOT ansätze can approximate a truncated standard normal discretized on n qubits: the authors report achieving L_infty errors ≈2 × 10^-6 using n=5 qubits and modest circuit depth (they present classical emulation results showing exponential error decay vs depth).

**Results summary:** This preprint gives the first complete resource estimates (fault-tolerant logical qubits and T-depth) for end-to-end quantum pricing of realistically complex, path-dependent derivatives (an autocallable and a TARF). It introduces a new 're-parameterization' state-loading method that prepares standard Gaussians variationally and aﬃne-transforms them to construct path distributions in return space, thereby avoiding an exponential normalization penalty encountered by naive Riemann-sum loading. Using amplitude estimation (IQAE) and detailed fixed-point arithmetic accounting, the authors estimate that their representative autocallable can be priced to error ~2×10^-3 with ≈8k logical qubits and total T-depth ≈5.4×10^7 (TARF: ≈11.5k qubits and T-depth ≈8.2×10^7). They argue that achieving a one-second runtime would need logical T-gate rates around 10 MHz and substantial error-correction capability, so practical advantage is not achievable with current hardware but the estimates provide concrete targets for algorithmic and engineering improvements.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The paper provides a theoretical/resource-estimate-based pathway to potential quantum advantage for pricing complex, path-dependent derivatives, demonstrating reduced resource requirements via a new re-parameterization loading method. However, the claims are projections based on analytical and classical-emulation calculations (not hardware demonstrations) and show that substantial fault-tolerant hardware and further algorithmic optimizations are still required; hence advantage remains speculative rather than demonstrated.
## Limitations
- High resource requirements for end-to-end useful cases: estimates reaching ~8k logical qubits and T-depths on the order of 5×10^7–1.6×10^8 (authors' summary), making near-term implementation infeasible.
- Hardware gap: to run example workloads in ~1s the paper estimates a need for T-gate execution rates (~10 MHz) far above current projected logical clock rates (~10 kHz).
- Riemann-summation path-loading suffers from a normalization factor (P_max) that can grow exponentially in T, producing impractical error scaling unless mitigated.
- Grover–Rudolph distribution loading is inadequate for derivative-pricing use cases (requires performing the same integrations we seek to avoid), so it does not give a practical quantum advantage.
- Amplitude-estimation depth: canonical and many optimized amplitude-estimation variants require oracle/circuit depth O(1/ε), which produces very deep, demanding circuits and large error-correction overheads.
- Unaccounted training costs: the resource estimates exclude the (potentially significant) classical/quantum costs of training the variational Gaussian loaders and any qGAN-based loaders; the paper assumes pretrained circuits but does not include training runtime or resources.
- Model scope: the quantitative resource estimates and algorithms are worked out for geometric Brownian motion; extensions to other models (e.g., stochastic/local volatility) are not fully analyzed here.
- Error sources and propagation: multiple error contributions (truncation, discretization, arithmetic, gate-synthesis and amplitude-estimation error) interact and must be tightly controlled; this drives large register sizes and depths.
- Significant arithmetic/payout cost: payoff evaluation circuits (autocallable, TARF) themselves require substantial resources (thousands of qubits / thousands of T-depth) and contribute non-negligibly to total cost.
- qGAN approach uncertainty: while qGANs are suggested as an alternative for loading, their training overhead, scaling, and guaranteed accuracy in this domain are unknown.
- [inferred] Pretrained variational loaders may incur substantial overhead when compiled to fault-tolerant gate sets (T-count/T-depth from rotation synthesis), which may negate some of the apparent resource savings unless accounted for.
- [inferred] Classical precomputation steps (e.g., Cholesky decomposition, classical preprocessing to choose windows/truncations, importance-sampling distribution design) may become expensive for large d or long T and are not fully included in the quantum resource accounting.
- [inferred] The estimates are sensitive to many constant-factor choices (discretization window w, register sizes n,p, polynomial approximation degrees); resource feasibility could change dramatically with small changes in these parameters.
- [inferred] Scalability to larger baskets (large d) or longer time discretizations (large T) is uncertain and likely to be much more demanding than the benchmark cases analyzed.
- [inferred] Dependence on specifics of error-correction (choice of code, logical gate synthesis techniques, routing/ancilla strategies) is not exhaustively quantified and could materially affect total physical-qubit and time estimates.
## Open questions
- How much can algorithmic and circuit optimizations (loading, payoff evaluation, arithmetic, rotation synthesis) realistically reduce the T-depth and logical-qubit counts toward practical thresholds?
- What are the concrete trade-offs between circuit depth and total oracle calls offered by the low-depth amplitude-estimation variants (the β trade-off), and in which regimes do they yield a net advantage after accounting for error correction?
- Can a practical, general importance-sampling/auxiliary-distribution h(x) be found for multivariate, path-dependent stochastic processes that eliminates or substantially reduces the exponential normalization factor in Riemann summation approaches?
- What are the real-world costs (classical and quantum) of training qGANs or variational Gaussian loaders to target accuracies, and how should those costs be incorporated into resource estimates?
- How do the methods extend (in resource terms) to more realistic market models beyond geometric Brownian motion (e.g., local volatility, stochastic volatility, jump processes), and what extra costs do those models impose?
- What are the best practices for choosing discretization/truncation parameters (n, p, w) to balance truncation/discretization errors against resource costs for various target accuracies?
- How feasible is the compilation/synthesis of the variational-prepared circuits into fault-tolerant Cliﬀord+T sequences with acceptable T-count/T-depth and ancilla costs (including digitization of parameters)?
- How do choices in error-correction architecture and physical-hardware characteristics (logical cycle time, connectivity, ancilla availability) change the required physical resources and time-to-solution?
- Can new or improved loading techniques be developed that avoid the pitfalls of Grover–Rudolph and Riemann methods while remaining practical to implement?
- To what extent can the training/optimization of ansätze (e.g., better optimizers, imaginary-time methods) improve convergence and reduce the depth/width of variational loaders without incurring prohibitive classical overhead?
- How sensitive are the estimated advantage thresholds to realistic, non-ideal noise/models (fault-tolerant overhead, routing, crosstalk), and can those be bounded more tightly?
- Can the kinds of constant-factor and architectural improvements that drove multiple-order-of-magnitude reductions in Shor’s algorithm resources be replicated for derivative pricing algorithms?
