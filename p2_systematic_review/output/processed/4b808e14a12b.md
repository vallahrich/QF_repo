## Abstract summary
The paper maps stock-price time series in portfolios to braid diagrams and interprets those braids using the Ising-anyons model of topological quantum computation. It shows how braids of 4- and 6-stock portfolios can implement 1-qubit and 2-qubit gates (e.g. Hadamard, Pauli, S, controlled-Z) and argues that larger portfolios or indices could form an n-qubit 'quantum code' whose decoding is left for future work.
## Methodology
The paper develops a mapping methodology that converts multivariate stock price time series into braid diagrams and then interprets those braids using the Ising-anyons model of topological quantum computation. The author selects portfolios of stocks with similar price magnitudes to increase the incidence of pairwise crossings, using daily closing prices for the 2014 year obtained from Yahoo Finance. For each trading day the prices of the portfolio constituents are re-ordered (ascending) so that trajectories across days form winding strands; crossings are identified between adjacent strands. For every crossing the paper computes the absolute price-change deltas before and after the crossing (Δ = |P_after − P_before| for each stock) and classifies the crossing as an overcrossing or undercrossing depending on which stock has the larger delta. Each adjacent-strand crossing is mapped to a braid generator (σ_i for overcrossing, σ_i^{-1} for undercrossing). These braid generators are then interpreted as elementary exchange matrices of Ising anyons (2×2 matrices for 4 anyons = 1 qubit; 4×4 matrices for 6 anyons = 2 qubits; 8×8 for 8 anyons = 3 qubits), using the matrix forms and prescriptions from the Ising-anyons literature (Georgiev et al.). Sequences of these elementary matrices corresponding to the temporal sequence of crossings are multiplied to produce unitary matrices that are identified as quantum gates (examples given: S, H, X, Z, controlled-Z). Gates found in local time windows are concatenated in temporal order to produce a quantum circuit (i.e., a time-ordered product of gate matrices) that the author terms the stock-market quantum code. The methodology emphasizes (i) the requirement that portfolios have an even number of stocks (anyon pairs), (ii) selection of stocks with close price levels to ensure crossings, and (iii) analysis at various scales (daily shown; notes that minute/tick data could be used similarly). The work is largely conceptual / mapping-based and demonstrates examples using specific 4- and 6-stock portfolios and selected date fragments where particular gates appear.

**Dataset:** Daily closing prices from Yahoo Finance for 2014. Example portfolios: 4-stock portfolio (PG, DIS, AXP, UNH) and 6-stock portfolio (DIS, NKE, MCD, AXP, UNH, HD).

## Findings
- [supported] Daily closing price time series for selected stocks can be arranged into braid diagrams that display over- and under-crossings.
- [supported] A simple algorithm (comparing absolute price changes before/after an adjacent crossing) can be used to classify a crossing as an overcrossing or undercrossing and thereby produce a braid word for the portfolio.
- [supported] The paper provides worked examples (Dow Jones components, 2014 daily data) where specific segments of stock-price braids correspond, elementwise, to braid generators and small sequences that match the matrix forms of elementary Ising-anyon exchange matrices.
- [speculative] Under the Ising anyons topological quantum computation model, these braid sequences can be interpreted as 1-qubit and 2-qubit quantum gates (examples: Hadamard, S (phase), Pauli X, and controlled-Z) when portfolios of 4 and 6 stocks are used respectively.
- [speculative] By adding more (paired) stocks one can embed higher-dimensional (n-qubit) gate structures and assemble the found gates into larger quantum circuits representing an n-qubit 'quantum code' of the market (e.g., a market index mapped to an n-qubit circuit).
- [speculative] The sequence of quantum gates arising from market-price braids might encode information about market states or future market behavior — i.e., 'deciphering the quantum code' could be informative for future research.
- [supported] Practical constraints for constructing meaningful braids include selecting stocks with close price levels (to ensure crossings) and using even numbers of stocks (pairs) because the mapping uses paired quasi-particle creation/annihilation).
- [speculative] The Ising anyons model is chosen and argued to be better suited than the Fibonacci anyons model for this application because it requires fewer braid matrices for gate realization and is 'more stable' (asserted but not quantitatively demonstrated for financial data).
- [speculative] Multiple different braid-sequences (i.e., non-unique decompositions) can implement the same quantum gate (the paper gives several example decompositions for Hadamard and Pauli gates).
- [speculative] Stock-market-constructed quantum circuits are arbitrary (not designed to solve a specific computational problem) and thus form a naturally occurring but presently mysterious quantum code.

**Results summary:** The preprint demonstrates a method to convert multivariate stock price time series into braid diagrams and, using a rule for signifying over/under crossings, maps those braid words to the elementary exchange (braid) matrices of the Ising-anyon topological quantum-computation model. Using daily 2014 closing prices of selected Dow Jones component stocks, the author shows worked examples where short braid segments correspond to matrices formally identical to common 1-qubit and 2-qubit gates (Hadamard, S, Pauli X, controlled-Z). The paper proposes that larger portfolios (paired stocks) can be interpreted as n-qubit systems whose concatenated gate sequences form a 'quantum code' of market evolution. The work is primarily conceptual and demonstrative: it provides example mappings but does not provide empirical evaluation of predictive power or computational advantage.

## Limitations
- The mapping from stock price crossings to topological braid matrices is largely descriptive and lacks formal validation (author-stated).
- The realized quantum gates and resulting quantum circuits are arbitrary reflections of market activity and are not designed to solve any predefined computational problem (author-stated).
- Selection constraint: stocks chosen for a portfolio must have similar price magnitudes to produce meaningful crossings; portfolios must contain an even number of stocks because quasi-particles are created/fused in pairs (author-stated).
- Data and experiments are limited to daily closing prices over the 2014 year (author-stated).
- The work focuses on Ising anyons as the computational model; choice motivated by relative simplicity/stability but not compared extensively to other anyon models like Fibonacci anyons (author-stated).
- Interpretation gap: the meaning or predictive content of the stock-market 'quantum code' (output of the constructed circuits) is unknown (author-stated).
- [inferred] No statistical analysis, hypothesis testing, or robustness checks are provided to assess whether observed braid/gate patterns are meaningful beyond chance.

- [inferred] Scalability concerns: constructing and interpreting n-qubit circuits for large indices (many stocks) is noted as difficult but not addressed technically.

## Open questions
- What is the meaning and interpretation of the 'quantum code' produced by sequences of quantum gates derived from stock-price braids?
- Can the quantum-circuit representation of a portfolio be used for forecasting future market states or provide actionable trading signals?
- How robust are the braid-to-gate mappings across different time frames (daily, intraday, tick) and different market regimes?
- Does the choice of anyon model (Ising vs Fibonacci or others) materially affect the gates/circuits obtained and their interpretability or usefulness?
- How does the selection of stocks (which stocks, how many, and price scaling) influence the braids and the resulting quantum gates?
- Are the observed braids/gates statistically significant compared with appropriate null models (randomized/surrogate price series)?
- Can the quantum gates/circuits derived from historical price braids be implemented or simulated on real quantum hardware, and if so, what would that yield?
- Is there a principled method to map specific quantum gates (e.g., Pauli X) to concrete financial phenomena (e.g., trend reversal), beyond anecdotal examples?
- How to scale the approach to n-qubit systems representing full market indices and how to manage the exponential growth in circuit complexity?
- Can one derive a systematic decoding procedure for the quantum code such that it yields interpretable macro-variables or risk indicators?
