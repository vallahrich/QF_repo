## Abstract summary
The authors propose a hybrid quantum-classical algorithm to price European and arithmetic Asian options in the Black–Scholes model by exploiting the equivalence between the pricing PDE and the Schrödinger equation in imaginary time. The method uses a variational imaginary-time evolution with a shallow, few-qubit ansatz circuit to approximate the time-evolved state, and numerical experiments show accurate option pricing on low-qubit circuits, suggesting applicability on near-term noisy quantum devices.
## Methodology
The paper develops a hybrid quantum-classical method to price European and arithmetic Asian options by (i) mapping the Black-Scholes PDE to a heat equation and then to a Schrödinger equation along imaginary time (Wick rotation), (ii) discretising the spatial domain (second-order finite differences) to obtain a sparse matrix Hamiltonian H (decomposed as a sum of Pauli-tensor products), and (iii) approximating the normalised imaginary-time evolution |psi(τ)⟩ = γ(τ) e^{-H τ} |psi(0)⟩ with a parameterised quantum trial state |φ(θ(τ))⟩ (ansatz). The time evolution of the ansatz parameters θ(τ) is obtained from McLachlan's variational principle which yields a linear ODE A(τ) ˙θ(τ) = C(τ). The matrix A and vector C are evaluated as expectation values of (modified) quantum circuits (circuits constructed from the ansatz plus extra gates and an ancilla for measurement). The (classical) update uses a forward Euler integrator: θ_{τ+Δτ} = θ_τ + Δτ A(τ)^{-1} C(τ), where ill-conditioning of A is handled by solving a regularised/least-squares problem with an eigenvalue cutoff. The ansatz is a shallow 4-qubit circuit composed primarily of Ry rotations, controlled-Ry, H and X gates; initial parameters θ(0) are obtained by solving a classical optimisation problem (minimising || |φ(θ)⟩ - |ψ(0)⟩ ||, e.g. with Differential Evolution) and ansatz depth is increased until the initial approximation error is below tolerance. Numerical experiments discretise the PDE on 16 grid points (4 qubits), simulate the measurement of A and C and advance θ for many small time steps (example: nT = 500 for European case), reconstruct prices by scaling the quantum-state outputs back to PDE coordinates, and compare to classical PDE solutions via pointwise and norm errors.

**Algorithms used:** Variational quantum imaginary time evolution (ansatz-based imaginary time), McLachlan's variational principle, Euler time integration (forward Euler), Finite-difference spatial discretisation (second-order), Hamiltonian Pauli decomposition, Least-squares solver for ill-conditioned linear systems (numpy.linalg.lstsq), Classical optimisation for ansatz initialisation (e.g. Differential Evolution)

**Experimental setup:** Numerical experiments simulate the hybrid algorithm classically: 4-qubit ansatz (so state vector of size 16) corresponding to 16 spatial grid points; Hamiltonian built by second-order finite differences and decomposed into Pauli terms; expectation values (A and C) are computed as circuit expectation values but simulated, not executed on a reported QPU. Example parameter values: Black-Scholes with S0=K=100, σ=20%, T=1, r=0; spatial log-grid [xmin,xmax] -> [3.9,5.0] (S in [50,150]) for European; Asian grid [ymin,ymax]=[-0.5,0.4]; time discretisation nT=500 (European) leading to Δτ = σ^2 T / nT. Ill-conditioned A handled via least-squares with cutoff ratio 1e-8.

**Dataset:** Synthetic model-generated data under the Black-Scholes model: European call payoff f(S_T)=max(S_T-K,0) and fixed-strike arithmetic Asian call payoff max((1/T)∫_0^T S_t dt - K, 0). No external market data used. Specific experiment parameters: S0=K=100, σ=0.20, T=1, r=0.

## Findings
- [supported] The Black–Scholes pricing PDE can be transformed via change of variables into a heat equation and then mapped to a Schrödinger equation along imaginary time.
- [supported] A hybrid quantum–classical algorithm (variational imaginary-time evolution) can be used to approximate the Schrödinger dynamics for pricing PDEs using a parameterised ansatz circuit and classical update of parameters via McLachlan's variational principle.
- [supported] The paper implements a shallow ansatz (4 qubits, 25 Ry rotation gates) and demonstrates via classical simulation that it can approximate European and arithmetic Asian call option prices with small errors.
- [supported] In the hybrid workflow A(τ) and C(τ) matrices required by the parameter update ODE can be estimated (simulated in the paper) using quantum circuits, while the parameter update (linear solve / Euler step) is done on a classical computer.
- [supported] For the European example the authors used 4 qubits (2^4 = 16 spatial grid points), 500 time steps (nT = 500), and reported maximum errors on the order of 1e-3 in the imaginary-time wavefunction approximation.
- [supported] For the arithmetic Asian example they used a 4-qubit spatial discretisation and reported maximum errors on the order of a few 1e-3 to 1e-2 in the wavefunction approximation.
- [supported] The algorithm requires an ansatz that can represent the terminal payoff (initial quantum state) accurately; the optimisation to match the payoff (θ0) is crucial and non-convex.
- [supported] Practical implementation issues observed: the linear system for parameter updates can be ill-conditioned and the authors apply a cutoff and least-squares regularisation (cutoff ratio used: 1e-8).
- [speculative] The approach is a promising candidate for near-term (NISQ) quantum hardware because it uses few qubits and shallow circuits.
- [speculative] The authors suggest the method could be extended to higher-dimensional PDEs such as basket option pricing (potential mitigation of the curse of dimensionality), but provide no empirical demonstration of that in the paper.
- [speculative] The paper proposes that time-dependent Hamiltonians (e.g., Asian option PDEs) can be handled by piecewise freezing the Hamiltonian on each time interval.
- [speculative] The main limitation identified is reliance on a suitable ansatz and the associated optimisation; authors note that ansatz-free imaginary-time methods might be investigated in future work.

**Results summary:** The preprint proposes a hybrid quantum–classical algorithm that maps Black–Scholes pricing PDEs to an imaginary-time Schrödinger equation and solves the latter via a variational imaginary-time evolution (McLachlan's principle). The authors design a shallow ansatz quantum circuit (4 qubits, 25 Ry gates), determine initial parameters to match the payoff, and simulate the parameter evolution classically while estimating required expectation values as if on a quantum device. Classical simulations reproduce European and arithmetic Asian call option prices on a 16-point spatial grid with small approximation errors (wavefunction error ≲ 1e-3 for the European example, up to a few 1e-3–1e-2 for the Asian example). The paper highlights practical issues (need for a good ansatz, nonconvex initial optimisation, ill-conditioned linear systems) and positions the method as a promising, but not yet empirically hardware-demonstrated, candidate for NISQ-era use and possible extension to higher-dimensional problems.

## Limitations
- Requirement for an ansatz circuit and solving a (non-convex) optimisation problem to fit the initial payoff (author-stated).
- Error checking only at the payoff level; no rigorous control of error propagation through time — the assumption that initial approximation error does not amplify may fail when |ψ(τ)⟩ varies significantly (author-stated).
- Ill-conditioning of the matrix A(τ) and the need to solve an (often ill-conditioned) linear system on the classical side; requires cut-offs and least-squares regularisation (author-stated).
- Handling time-dependent Hamiltonians by freezing the Hamiltonian on each time step is an approximation that may limit accuracy (author-stated).
- Current demonstration on low-dimensional examples (European and arithmetic Asian options) using only few qubits (4 qubits, 16 grid points) — scalability to larger problems not demonstrated (author-stated / implied).
- Near-term quantum hardware constraints: actual quantum devices are noisy and have few qubits; decoherence/noise will affect implementation (author-stated).
- [inferred] The reported results appear to be simulations rather than runs on noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) hardware, so real-device performance and noise resilience are untested.
- [inferred] The approach requires a Hamiltonian decomposition into sums of Pauli-tensor products (Assumption 3.1(ii)); this may be nontrivial or inefficient for large or dense discretisations.

## Open questions
- How to design efficient, compact ansatz circuits that can accurately represent option value functions for more complex payoffs and over full time evolution?
- Can ansatz-free or alternative imaginary-time evolution algorithms (that bypass the high-dimensional optimisation) be adapted to financial PDEs and scale better?
- How do approximation errors in the ansatz at initialization propagate through imaginary-time evolution? Can one derive rigorous error bounds?
- How to efficiently and stably solve the classical linear systems (A(τ)˙θ = C(τ)) for large numbers of parameters, and how to regularise/condition A(τ) systematically?
- How to handle time-dependent Hamiltonians more accurately than freezing per time interval, and what is the cost/benefit trade-off?
- Can the Hamiltonian decomposition into Pauli strings be done efficiently for higher-resolution discretisations or multi-dimensional PDEs?
- How does the hybrid algorithm perform on real noisy quantum hardware (NISQ devices)? What is the impact of noise and limited qubit counts?
- What is the practical scaling of the method to multi-dimensional problems (e.g., basket options) and can it overcome the classical curse of dimensionality in practice?
- What are the concrete resource requirements (qubits, circuit depth, classical computation time) to reach parity or advantage over classical PDE/Monte Carlo methods?
- Are there systematic ways to choose the ansatz depth/layout jointly with the optimisation method to avoid local minima and improve convergence?
