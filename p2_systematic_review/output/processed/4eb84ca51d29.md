## Abstract summary
This paper presents Q-PORT, a systematic study of quantum portfolio optimization that evaluates encoding strategies and scalability trade-offs for hybrid quantum-classical algorithms (VQE and QAOA). The authors compare multi-qubit-per-asset and multi-asset-per-qubit encodings across circuit depths and show that while adding qubits per asset yields negligible precision gains, encoding multiple assets per qubit can greatly improve resource efficiency with only minor loss in solution quality, enabling more scalable quantum portfolio optimization on NISQ-era devices. This paper evaluates practical variants of Quantum Amplitude Estimation (IAE and MLAE) for pricing European call options using historical Apple data. Experiments on a statevector simulator show both quantum estimators return zero expected payoff due to coarse discretization from limited uncertainty qubits, highlighting encoding resolution as the primary limitation. The authors conclude that improved state preparation and adaptive encoding strategies are needed for accurate quantum financial estimation, while noting QAE's potential utility for binary/threshold detection tasks.
## Methodology
This study (Q-PORT) systematically evaluates the scalability and resource trade-offs of quantum portfolio optimization using hybrid quantum-classical algorithms. The authors collected historical stock price data (Yahoo Finance, 2020-01-01 to 2024-01-01) and computed classical Mean-Variance Optimization (MVO) solutions as baselines. The portfolio optimization problem was cast into a QUBO Hamiltonian and solved using two hybrid variational quantum algorithms (VQE and QAOA). Two encoding strategies were compared: (i) multi-qubit-per-stock (redundant qubit groups per asset, aggregated via majority voting after measurement) and (ii) multi-stock-per-qubit (compressed encoding of several stocks on a single qubit, followed by generation of a candidate portfolio pool and clustering via k-means). For each encoding, the authors varied quantum circuit parameters (Ansatz layers i.e., repetitions from 1–5) and used a layered ansatz (Hadamard initialization, parameterized RZ for cost, RZZ entanglers compatible with the QUBO, and RX mixers). Classical optimization of variational parameters used the COBYLA optimizer with up to 5000 iterations. Experiments ran on statevector simulators (no shot noise) and compared quantum outputs to the classical MVO baseline by ranking how often the classical optimal portfolio appears among quantum-generated candidates. Multiple asset set sizes (3, 4, 12 stocks), a fixed risk tolerance (0.2), and investment budget rules were used to probe scalability and encoding eﬃciency. The study reports trends in ranking/quality of solutions versus circuit depth and encoding choice, concluding that multi-qubit-per-stock yields negligible precision gains while multi-stock-per-qubit significantly improves resource eﬃciency with small precision losses. The work applies two distinct experimental pipelines. For quantum portfolio optimization (Q-PORT) the authors develop and compare two encoding paradigms (multi-qubit-per-stock and multi-stock-per-qubit) to reduce qubit requirements: they generate a pool of candidate portfolios (evaluated by expected return and risk), cluster candidates with k-means to group similar solutions, and solve QUBO-formulated portfolio instances using hybrid variational algorithms (VQE and QAOA) with layered ansatz circuits. Ansatz circuits begin with Hadamards, use parameterized RZ rotations for the cost Hamiltonian, RZZ two-qubit entangling gates reflecting QUBO structure, and RX mixer gates; circuit repetitions (layers) are systematically varied from 1 to 5 to control depth/expressivity. Classical optimizer COBYLA (max 5000 iterations) is used and outputs are compared to a classical MVO benchmark via a ranking metric. For quantum amplitude estimation (QAE) applied to European call option pricing, the pipeline encodes a truncated log-normal terminal-price distribution into 2^n bins using Qiskit's LogNormalDistribution, maps the piecewise-linear payoff max(ST - K,0) to an objective amplitude via controlled rotations (LinearAmplitudeFunction), and performs amplitude estimation with Iterative Amplitude Estimation (IAE) and Maximum Likelihood Amplitude Estimation (MLAE) under bounded Grover depths. Quantum estimates are rescaled to expected payoff and compared against classical benchmarks (Black–Scholes closed form and Monte Carlo with 100k samples). Experiments investigate the effects of discretization (number of uncertainty qubits / binning), objective/ancilla qubits, estimation schedules, and circuit expressivity on the ability to recover tail-driven expectations.

**Algorithms used:** VQE, QAOA, COBYLA (classical optimizer), k-means (clustering, candidate pooling), k-means, Iterative Amplitude Estimation (IAE), Maximum Likelihood Amplitude Estimation (MLAE), Quantum Amplitude Estimation (QAE), Black-Scholes (classical baseline), Monte Carlo (classical baseline)
**Frameworks:** statevector-based quantum simulator (unspeciﬁed package), Qiskit, Qiskit Finance, yfinance

**Experimental setup:** Statevector simulations on Google Colab instances (Intel Xeon E5 CPUs). Noisy hardware was not used; all quantum runs were simulator-based (ideal statevector). Classical baseline: Mean-Variance Optimization (MVO) computed from historical stock returns and covariance.

**Dataset:** Historical stock price data from Yahoo Finance covering Jan 1, 2020 to Jan 1, 2024. Asset groups tested: (i) 3 stocks: Apple, Microsoft, Google; (ii) 4 stocks: Apple, Microsoft, Google, Amazon; (iii) 12 stocks: Apple, Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Tesla, Nvidia, Meta, Netﬂix, JP Morgan, Disney, Visa, Berkshire Hathaway.

**Results summary:** The paper introduces Q-PORT, a systematic benchmarking methodology to study the trade-offs between quantum circuit depth, stock-to-qubit encodings, and scalability for quantum portfolio optimization using VQE and QAOA. In statevector simulations on small problem instances (3, 4, 12 assets) the authors found that allocating multiple qubits per asset yielded little improvement over classical MVO, whereas packing multiple assets per qubit (multi-stock-per-qubit encoding) signiﬁcantly reduced required qubits and improved resource eﬃciency with only modest loss in solution quality. The study also conﬁrmed the expected sensitivity to circuit repetitions and ansatz choice. The authors position multi-stock encodings as a promising approach to reduce QPU resource demands and advance scalable, near-term quantum finance applications, while noting implications for further testing on noisy hardware. Two conference papers evaluated practical aspects of applying near-term quantum algorithms to financial tasks. For portfolio optimization (Q-PORT), simulation experiments comparing multi-qubit-per-stock and multi-stock-per-qubit encodings show that (1) adding redundant qubits per asset generally increases resource demands without improving solution quality and can degrade optimization unless circuits are deeper, and (2) aggressive compression (multiple stocks per qubit) preserves competitive performance, especially for QAOA with more layers, offering a promising scaling path under NISQ constraints. For option pricing using Quantum Amplitude Estimation (IAE and MLAE), experiments on historical AAPL data with 6 uncertainty qubits (64 bins) show that both quantum estimators returned zero expected payoff while classical Black–Scholes and Monte Carlo predicted ~17 USD: the discrepancy is attributed to coarse discretization that hides low-probability, high-payoff tail contributions. Overall, the studies emphasize encoding/state-preparation as the dominant practical limitation and suggest targeted encoding improvements rather than deeper circuits alone.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

Neither paper demonstrates a practical quantum advantage in the studied tasks. Q-PORT reports that compressed encodings plus deeper QAOA layers can preserve solution quality while saving qubits (a promising scaling pathway), and the QAE study reiterates theoretical quadratic speedup of QAE but shows that practical encoding limitations (insufficient qubits/discretization) prevent recovering classical expectations. Thus any advantage remains theoretical/speculative under current resource constraints.
## Limitations

- Circuit depths tested were limited (number of QAOA/VQE layers varied from 1 to 5), so behavior for deeper circuits was not explored.
- Only a single classical optimizer (COBYLA) with a fixed iteration budget (5,000) was used; the impact of optimizer choice and hyperparameter tuning on results was not explored.
- The assessment metric and baseline are tied to classical Mean–Variance Optimization (MVO); other classical baselines or multi-objective formulations were not evaluated.

- Multi-qubit-per-stock encodings were shown to yield negligible precision gains in these experiments, but that conclusion is limited to the datasets, encodings, and circuit conﬁgurations tested.
