## Abstract summary
The paper investigates quantum algorithms for stock price prediction by extracting technical indicators and using PCA and Quantum Annealing for dimensionality reduction and feature selection. It trains a Quantum Support Vector Machine (QSVM) and compares quantum-enhanced pipelines with classical models on datasets for Apple, Visa, Johnson & Johnson, and Honeywell, finding quantum annealing useful for feature selection but no clear QSVM advantage over classical SVM given current hardware and simulation limits.
## Methodology
The study collected historical daily market data (open/high/low/close) for four equities (Apple, Visa, Johnson & Johnson, Honeywell) via the yfinance API for the period 25 Dec 2020 to 25 Dec 2022. From the raw series they computed a set of technical indicators (SMA/EMA moving averages, RSI, MACD, stochastic oscillator, ATR, Aroon up/down) to serve as features. A binary target label was formed per day: Change = 1 if Price_T < Price_T+1 else 0. To reduce dimensionality / select informative inputs they applied two approaches: classical PCA (retaining top 3, 5, and 8 components producing datasets PCA3/PCA5/PCA8) and Quantum Annealing feature selection formulated as a QUBO that produced QA3/QA5/QA8 feature subsets (run on a D-Wave quantum annealer). For prediction they trained a variety of classical supervised classifiers (SVM, Decision Tree, Random Forest, KNN, Logistic Regression, Naive Bayes, Gradient Boosting, XGBoost) and quantum-enhanced classifiers using a Quantum Support Vector Classifier (QSVC) with a ZZ feature map. For QSVC experiments they evaluated four entanglement schemes (linear, circular, full, pairwise). Model performance was assessed primarily with accuracy and F-score, and comparisons were made between classical pipelines and quantum-enhanced pipelines across the reduced-dimension datasets. The authors note resource limitations (simulation and hardware) prevented training QSVC on the 8-feature datasets in some cases.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum Annealing (QUBO-based feature selection), Quantum Support Vector Machine / QSVC (quantum kernel SVM), ZZFeatureMap (quantum feature map with entanglement), Principal Component Analysis (PCA), Classical SVM, Decision Tree, Random Forest, K-Nearest Neighbours (KNN), Logistic Regression, Naive Bayes, Gradient Boosting, XGBoost
**Frameworks:** yfinance (data collection), Qiskit (quantum circuits / ZZFeatureMap / QSVC tooling), scikit-learn (classical ML and SVC integration), D-Wave (quantum annealer / QUBO execution)

**Experimental setup:** Data collected via yfinance for four tickers over 2020-12-25 to 2022-12-25. Feature extraction produced technical indicators; dimensionality reduction via PCA (3/5/8 components) and QA feature selection through QUBO solved on a D-Wave quantum annealer. QSVC experiments used Qiskit ZZFeatureMap with four entanglement schemes; QSVC circuits were executed/simulated on classical hardware (Qiskit simulators). Classical ML models used scikit-learn implementations. Exact QPU model, qubit counts, shots, circuit depth and train/test split/cross-validation details are not reported.

**Dataset:** Daily stock market data (Open/High/Low/Close) for Apple (AAPL), Visa (VISA), Johnson & Johnson (JNJ) and Honeywell (HON) obtained from yfinance covering 25 Dec 2020 to 25 Dec 2022. Derived features: SMA/EMA moving averages, RSI, MACD, stochastic oscillator, ATR, Aroon Up/Down. Labels: daily binary direction (next-day price up/down).

## Findings
- [supported] Feature selection using Quantum Annealing (QA) was effective in extracting relevant features from the financial indicator set and was presented as more effective than PCA in the authors' experiments.
- [supported] Quantum Support Vector Machine (QSVM) did not show a clear accuracy advantage over classical SVM for the stock price binary classification tasks on the datasets used.
- [supported] QSVC models for 8-feature datasets failed to train (simulation/compute limits); dimensionality reduction was necessary to run quantum feature maps in practice.
- [supported] Current quantum hardware / simulation limitations (noise, limited qubits, compute limits) constrained what quantum models could be trained and influenced experimental design.
- [supported] Classical machine learning models often matched or outperformed quantum-enhanced pipelines on the tested stock prediction tasks (observed average accuracies and F-scores).
- [speculative] Quantum Computing in principle can explore exponentially larger solution spaces (superposition/entanglement) and thus may offer computational advantages for some financial tasks.
- [speculative] Quantum Annealing and other quantum optimization algorithms (e.g., QAOA) hold promise for optimization and feature-selection tasks in finance beyond classical methods.
- [speculative] Quantum Neural Networks (QNNs), QLSTM and other quantum-native architectures could potentially improve temporal/sequence modelling performance in finance, though not demonstrated here.
- [supported] Dimensionality reduction (PCA or QA-based feature selection) is practically necessary for near-term quantum machine learning workflows given NISQ-era limitations.

**Results summary:** The authors ran experiments comparing classical ML, PCA-based dimensionality reduction, quantum-annealing feature selection, and QSVM classifiers on binary stock movement prediction for four companies (Apple, Visa, Johnson & Johnson, Honeywell) using two years of daily data and a set of technical indicators. They found that QA-based feature selection could identify relevant features effectively, but QSVM did not provide a clear predictive advantage over classical SVM in these experiments. QSVC models with 8 features failed to train due to compute/simulation limits, underscoring the need for dimensionality reduction in practical quantum ML pipelines. Overall, classical models often matched or outperformed the quantum-enhanced approaches on accuracy and F-score for these datasets.
