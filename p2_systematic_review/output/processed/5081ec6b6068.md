## Abstract summary
This preprint reports a large-scale empirical comparison of quantum, classical, and hybrid quantum-classical machine learning models for Automated Market Maker (AMM) and DeFi trading strategies using one year of daily crypto data. The authors find hybrid quantum-classical models (notably a QASA Sequence variant) outperform pure quantum and classical baselines on average, achieving the highest individual return (13.99%) and best risk-adjusted performance (Sharpe 1.76), while classical ensemble methods remain strong and computationally efficient. The study highlights current limitations of pure quantum models and recommends hybrid approaches for practical DeFi applications.
## Methodology
The study performs a large-scale empirical comparison of classical, pure-quantum, hybrid quantum-classical, and transformer models for AMM/DeFi trading decisions via backtesting on daily cryptocurrency data over the 2024 calendar year. Targets are binary rebalancing/trade signals defined by task-specific threshold rules (AMM rebalance, concentrated-liquidity position, quantum-enhanced price move). Extensive feature engineering is applied: classical pipelines use rich feature sets (50–80 features; 122 features enumerated in the appendix) including price, moving averages, technical indicators, volatility, volume, time, microstructure proxies, lags and interactions; quantum pipelines use compact feature sets (6–8 features) mapped to quantum angles or amplitude encodings. Models are trained using a time-series split (70% train, 15% validation, 15% test) and each model is run 5 independent times to assess robustness. Classical inputs are standardized; quantum features are angle-encoded into [0,2π] and mapped to qubits according to a feature-to-qubit mapping. Evaluations combine model metrics (accuracy, F1, AUC) and financial/backtest metrics (total return, annualized Sharpe, volatility, maximum drawdown, Calmar) and include equity curve comparisons, uncertainty (std across runs), and complexity/efficiency analyses. Hyperparameter choices for several models are reported in the appendix and final results rank models by Sharpe and return while reporting statistical significance tests across runs.

**Algorithms used:** Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, Logistic Regression, Transformer, LSTM, VQE (Variational Quantum Eigensolver) Classifier, QNN (Quantum Neural Network), QSVM (Quantum Support Vector Machine), QASA (Quantum Adaptive Self-Attention) Hybrid, QASA Sequence (quantum-enhanced sequence model), QuantumRWKV / QRWKV (Quantum-enhanced RWKV), Variational Quantum Circuits (VQC), Amplitude encoding, Angle encoding, RWKV

**Dataset:** Daily cryptocurrency price and volume data for three pairs (BTCUSDC, ETHUSDC, USDCUSDT) covering 2024-01-01 to 2024-12-31 (252 trading days). No external data provider or API is specified in the text.
## Experiment details

### Process
Pipeline: (1) Feature engineering per paradigm (classical: full technical and interaction features; quantum: selected compact set mapped to angles/amplitudes). (2) Encoding: classical features standardized; quantum features angle- or amplitude-encoded into qubit parameters. (3) Model instantiation with reported hyperparameters (appendix). (4) Time-series split into train/validation/test; models trained on train, hyperparameters/early stopping via validation, final evaluation on test. (5) Each model repeated for 5 independent runs to estimate variability. (6) Backtesting: model binary outputs generate rebalancing/trading events; portfolio/backtest metrics computed daily and aggregated over the full period. (7) Evaluation: compute accuracy, F1, AUC for model classification; compute total return, annualized Sharpe (√252 scaling), volatility, maximum drawdown, Calmar ratio; compare equity curves and compute uncertainty (std across runs). Report statistical significance tests between model groups (p-values reported for some comparisons).

Baselines include classical ensembles (Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, Logistic Regression) and a Transformer model. Results presented as tables (aggregates and per-model), equity curve plots, risk-return scatterplots, and statistical significance statements (p-values) comparing groups.

## Findings

**Results summary:** In a one-year, multi-asset backtesting study on three crypto pairs using daily data, the authors report that hybrid quantum-classical models (notably QASA Sequence and QASA Hybrid) produced the strongest returns and favorable risk profiles among quantum-based approaches, with QASA Sequence attaining the top individual return (13.99%) and Sharpe (1.76). Classical ensemble methods (Random Forest, Gradient Boosting) remained strong, stable and computationally efficient (returns ≈12–13%, Sharpe ≈1.68). Pure quantum models underperformed (returns ≈3–5%, Sharpe <0.9) and showed higher volatility. Transformer models gave high returns but with elevated volatility. The study includes reproducibility metadata and reports statistical significance for several comparisons, but findings are limited to the dataset, task definitions, feature engineering choices, and backtesting setup used by the authors.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The paper provides dataset-specific empirical evidence that hybrid quantum-classical architectures can outperform pure quantum models and be competitive with classical ensembles on the tested DeFi/AMM backtests; however, pure quantum models did not show advantage, and the observed hybrid gains are limited to the authors' tasks, assets, feature choices, and backtest setup—therefore a general quantum advantage is not demonstrated and remains speculative.
## Limitations

- Model limitations: quantum models are still under development and may have unexploited optimization space (author-stated).
- Computational limitations: quantum model training time is longer, potentially limiting real-time applications (author-stated).
- [inferred] Short time horizon and sample size: only 252 trading days (one year) — limited ability to assess long-term performance and behavior across multiple market cycles.
- [inferred] Narrow asset set and frequency: only three crypto pairs and daily data — limits assessment across asset classes, instruments, and higher-frequency intraday dynamics.

## Open questions

- Can pure quantum models be improved (architectures, encoding, training) to close the gap with hybrid and classical methods?
- What specific aspects of the hybrid architectures (e.g., where quantum layers are inserted, choice of features) drive the observed performance gains?
- How sensitive are the outcomes to design choices such as labeling thresholds (τrebalance, τprice), feature selection, time splits, and hyperparameter settings?
- How would realistic transaction costs, slippage, liquidity constraints, and on-chain costs affect the profitability and risk metrics reported?
- Were the quantum models evaluated on real quantum hardware or simulated environments, and how would hardware noise and limited qubit connectivity impact real-world performance?
- What is the scalability path for quantum-enhanced models to handle higher-dimensional feature sets and longer sequences given current and near-term quantum hardware limits?
- How robust are the models across different market regimes (bull, bear, high/low volatility) and structural breaks?
- Can the reported statistical significance claims be corroborated with larger numbers of runs, bootstrapping, or out-of-sample tests to strengthen inference?
- What are the real-time deployment implications (latency, throughput) for hybrid quantum-classical systems in production trading environments?
- What theoretical guarantees or complexity analyses explain when and why quantum (or hybrid) models should outperform classical ones in financial time-series tasks?
- How do risk management constraints (position sizing, drawdown limits, margin requirements) interact with model signals in realistic execution?
- What is the effect of different quantum feature encodings (angle, amplitude, other embeddings) on model performance and training stability?
- How transferable are model weights or quantum circuits across assets or time periods (transfer learning for finance with quantum layers)?
