## Abstract summary
The paper investigates quantum machine learning (QML) methods that integrate quantum computing with supervised and unsupervised learning, focusing on models such as Quantum SVMs and Quantum Neural Networks and their implementation on NISQ devices. It includes an empirical comparison (using a Kaggle heart disease dataset on IBMQ via Qiskit) showing a QSVM outperforming a classical SVM in accuracy and execution time, and discusses practical limitations (noise, scalability) and application areas like finance, healthcare, and materials science.
## Methodology
The paper conducts an empirical comparison between a quantum-enhanced classifier (Quantum Support Vector Machine, QSVM) and a classical Support Vector Machine (SVM). The authors report implementing both models using Qiskit and running experiments on IBMQ hardware/simulator. The Kaggle heart disease dataset was used as the experimental data source. The study evaluates and compares classification accuracy and execution time of the QSVM versus the classical SVM. The paper, however, does not report critical experimental details such as dataset size or splits, preprocessing steps, QSVM feature map/kernel choice, quantum backend name, number of qubits, circuit depth, shots, or optimizer settings.
