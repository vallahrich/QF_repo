## Abstract summary
The paper introduces VarQFS, a variational quantum algorithm for unconstrained black-box binary optimization that leverages parameterized quantum circuits and a Quantum Natural SPSA optimizer motivated by quantum imaginary time evolution. It applies this approach to the practical problem of feature selection, demonstrating experiments on up to 59 qubits via matrix product state simulation and on 20 qubits on IBM quantum hardware, and reports competitive or improved feature-selection performance compared to classical methods like RFE/RFECV.
## Methodology
The paper introduces a variational quantum optimization framework (VarQFS) to solve unconstrained black-box binary optimization problems, demonstrated on feature selection for a credit-risk data set. The approach maps each candidate binary solution (feature subset) to a computational-basis state of an n-qubit parameterized quantum state |ψ(θ)⟩ prepared by a real-amplitude ansatz. The objective (loss) is the expected black-box score f(x) under the distribution defined by |ψ(θ)⟩, estimated by sampling (shots) from the quantum state and evaluating f(x) classically (here: training a classifier on the selected subset and computing log-loss). Optimization of the circuit parameters is performed using a quantum-natural-gradient-inspired optimizer implemented via a simultaneous-perturbation stochastic approximation (QN-SPSA) that stochastically estimates both the gradient and the quantum Fisher information (QFI) at constant cost. Overlaps needed for the QFI estimator are computed via a compute–uncompute method (preparing U(θ1) followed by U(θ2)†). Experiments are performed both on an MPS (matrix product state) classical simulator (Qiskit Aer MPS) and on real hardware (ibmq_montreal). The UCI German credit data (1,000 examples) is preprocessed with one-hot encoding to expand 20 raw attributes into 59 binary features, split 50/50 into train/test. In the training loop, each measurement outcome maps to a feature subset which is used to train a logistic regression classifier on X_train and compute a log-loss score; that score becomes the black-box f(x) used in the empirical loss estimate. Baselines include classical wrapper methods RFE and RFECV (accuracy and log-loss). Statistical assessment uses 10 independent optimization seeds (for many experiments), bootstrapped 95% confidence intervals (50 bootstraps on 70% subsamples) and paired-sample analyses across 1000 data subsets. Key hyperparameter regimes explored include ansatz depths d∈{0,1,2}, per-iteration shots up to 10,000 (MPS) or 8,000 (QPU), and 5,000 optimizer iterations on the simulator (300 on QPU), with additional SPSA resampling to stabilize gradient/QFI estimates for larger depths.

**Algorithms used:** Variational quantum optimization (VarQFS), Variational Quantum Imaginary Time Evolution (VarQITE) rationale, Quantum Natural Gradient (QNG), QN-SPSA (Quantum Natural SPSA) - SPSA-based QNG approximation, Matrix Product State (MPS) simulation (classical simulator)
**Frameworks:** Qiskit (including Aer MPS simulator), Scikit-learn, LightGBM (tested, not primary), CPLEX (used in analysis for QUBO fitting)

**Experimental setup:** Two backends: (1) classical MPS simulation via Qiskit Aer MPS (exact small-bond simulations for low-depth ansatz) and (2) IBM Quantum superconducting hardware ibmq_montreal (27-qubit device). Parameterized ansatz: Qiskit RealAmplitudes (RY single-qubit rotations + linear CNOT entanglement), depths d=0,1,2. Overlaps for QFI computed with compute–uncompute (doubling effective depth). Loss evaluated by sampling basis states from the circuit, mapping samples to feature subsets, training a classical classifier (logistic regression) on those features, and computing log-loss as f(x). Optimization with QN-SPSA (SPSA gradient & SPSA QFI approximations), with per-iteration sampling (shots) used to estimate the empirical loss.

**Dataset:** German credit (credit risk) data from UCI Machine Learning Repository (1,000 instances). Original 20 attributes with categorical variables encoded via one-hot encoding expanding to 59 binary features. Split into 50% train / 50% test for the main experiments.

## Findings
- [speculative] Introduced VarQFS, a variational quantum algorithm for unconstrained black-box binary optimization (mapping solution space to computational basis and optimizing a parameterized quantum state).
- [speculative] Provided theoretical justification for the approach via a connection to quantum imaginary time evolution and quantum natural gradient, arguing convergence toward the ground state under ideal conditions.
- [supported] Implemented Quantum Natural SPSA (QN-SPSA) as a scalable stochastic approximation to the quantum natural gradient for training the variational circuit.
- [supported] Demonstrated VarQFS on a real-world feature-selection task (German credit risk dataset) using both classical MPS simulation up to 59 qubits and real hardware (ibmq_montreal) for 20 qubits.
- [supported] On a 20-feature (qubit) reduced problem, VarQFS (MPS and QPU experiments) found feature subsets with better training log-loss than classical methods (RFE and RFECV) and comparable or better test log-loss in the presented experiments.
- [supported] On the full 59-feature problem (one-hot encoded to 59 qubits), MPS simulations of VarQFS produced mean best train/test log-loss values that were competitive with or better than RFE/RFECV benchmarks for some ansatz depths.
- [supported] Statistical analyses (paired-score tests over many data subsets) show VarQFS outperformed classical feature-selection baselines on a large fraction of the sampled train/test splits in many of the reported runs (with seed dependence).
- [supported] Demonstrated feasibility of MPS-based simulation for variational quantum training with shot sampling and overlap evaluations for up to 59 qubits (limited-entanglement ansatz), enabling practical study of moderately large instances.
- [supported] Reported wall-clock/back-end timings showing that single-iteration execution on the QPU was substantially slower than MPS simulation (example numbers reported).
- [speculative] Observed that ansatz depth (and hence entanglement) can improve generalization in some settings, but optimization stability depends on QN-SPSA hyperparameters and resampling—further tuning required.
- [speculative] Argued VarQFS's joint consideration of all features (vs. many classical methods that use up to pairwise correlations) is a promising property that may give it an edge on problems lacking exploitable structure.
- [supported] Provided empirical evidence (via QUBO fitting experiments on small n) that the objective landscapes arising from their feature-selection scores can be well-approximated by low-locality (2-local/QUBO) functions for tested problem sizes, suggesting cost-function induced barren plateaus may not be severe in their datasets.

**Results summary:** The authors propose VarQFS, a variational quantum wrapper method for black-box binary optimization and apply it to feature selection. They provide a theoretical link to quantum imaginary time evolution and employ a practical optimizer (QN-SPSA). Empirical demonstrations include MPS simulations up to 59 qubits and hardware runs on a 20-qubit IBM device. Across experiments, VarQFS found feature subsets that produced equal or better log-loss scores than classical baselines (RFE/RFECV) in several cases; statistical analyses report high fractions of training/test splits where VarQFS outperformed classical methods (with seed dependence). They also report runtime comparisons between MPS simulation and QPU execution and discuss practical issues such as ansatz depth, entanglement, and optimizer resampling.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The paper presents empirical evidence that VarQFS can outperform classical feature-selection baselines on the tested datasets and sizes (including small-scale hardware). However, these results are limited in scale, seed-dependent, and rely on MPS simulations and a single QPU example; no provable or general quantum advantage is demonstrated. The claim of potential advantage is therefore speculative and contingent on future scaling, optimizer tuning, and broader benchmarking.
## Limitations
- Need for an adequately expressive ansatz, sufficiently small (adaptive) step sizes, and enough shots for theoretical guarantees to hold (authors note these are difficult to satisfy in practice).
- QN-SPSA introduces approximation errors (gradient and QFI approximations) that grow with the number of ansatz parameters; higher-depth ansatzes required additional resampling to stabilize training (author-stated).
- MPS simulations are efficient only for circuits with limited entanglement; representing more-entangled ansatzes exactly becomes infeasible with MPS (author-stated).
- Hardware resource constraints limited experiments (reduced iterations, no resampling of SPSA gradient/QFI, limited max shots per execution) and affected training quality on the QPU (author-stated).
- Significant seed dependence of training outcomes and convergence; some seeds gave substantially better results than others (author-stated).
- Small dataset size (1,000 data points) and split choices led to relatively large confidence intervals in results (author-stated).
- For the full 59-feature problem, exhaustive (ground-truth) comparison is impossible, so benchmarking relies on classical heuristics rather than optimal solutions (author-stated).
- Simulation cost is high: the 59-qubit MPS experiments are computationally expensive due to necessity of sampling both loss and overlaps for the QFI approximation (author-stated).
- Overlap computations for QFI via compute-uncompute effectively double circuit depth, increasing required bond dimension and simulator cost (author-stated).
- No error mitigation was used on the QPU experiments; noise may have affected optimization and final performance (author-stated).
- The QUBO fit to the black-box scoring function was empirically successful only for tested sizes and is not guaranteed to generalize to arbitrary data/problem instances (author-stated).
- [inferred] The reported competitive/superior performance may be sensitive to problem choice and the specific data set structure; generalization to other financial datasets or broader problem classes is not established.
- [inferred] Long runtimes and queuing for real hardware (QPU iterations much slower than simulator) limit practical deployment and large-scale experiments.
- [inferred] The black-box scoring function (training classifier) can be a classical computational bottleneck when many samples are required during optimization.
- [inferred] The use of low-depth, 1D nearest-neighbor entanglement ansatz was driven by simulator and hardware compatibility; richer connectivity/ansatz choices remain untested on hardware in this work.
## Open questions
- What is the precise role of entanglement in improving VarQFS performance? Is the improvement from entanglement structure or simply from a larger parameter space?
- Which ansatz/entanglement structures are most appropriate for VarQFS and how to choose them systematically (e.g., via preliminary statistical analysis)?
- How do VarQITE error bounds behave in the stochastic QN-SPSA implementation, and can these be used to control and improve variational implementations in practice?
- How does MPS truncation (approximate MPS simulation) affect training landscapes and can truncation-induced state inﬁdelity create barren-plateau-like effects?
- What characteristics of training data determine whether the induced observable is local or global (i.e., when will cost-function-dependent barren plateaus occur)?
- How well does VarQFS generalize beyond the presented credit dataset to other financial datasets and other combinatorial optimization problems?
- What are the trade-offs between using MPS or other tensor-network classical models as probabilistic models for combinatorial optimization versus the described quantum variational approach?
- How to optimally set QN-SPSA hyperparameters (number of resamples, perturbation magnitudes, step sizes) to ensure reliable convergence across seeds and for larger ansatzes?
- How will VarQFS scale and perform on future quantum hardware with more qubits and lower noise, and what practical limits remain?
- What is the impact of applying error mitigation or error-corrected operations on VarQFS training and outcomes?

**Future work:**
- Study and tune QN-SPSA hyperparameters and resampling strategies to improve convergence, especially for higher-depth ansatzes.
- Investigate VarQITE error bounds in the context of a stochastic QN-SPSA implementation to better understand and control approximation errors.
- Compare VarQFS against a broader set of classical state-of-the-art feature selection methods, including probabilistic-model-based approaches that consider all variable correlations jointly.
- Explore use of MPS or other tensor-network models themselves as probabilistic models for combinatorial optimization tasks (e.g., feature selection).
- Perform larger-scale experiments on quantum hardware as it matures, enabling study of more expressive ansatz classes with nontrivial entanglement.
- Investigate different ansatz circuit choices, including quantum/isometric tensor-network inspired ansatzes, and study their appropriateness for VarQFS.
- Study whether preliminary classical statistical analysis can guide choice of entanglement structure in the ansatz.
- Adapt VarQFS to enforce fixed-size feature subsets (e.g., m features) via Hamming-weight-preserving ansatzes or penalty terms in the loss.
- Explore improved training methods such as using cross-validation techniques within the variational optimization loop.
- Analyze the effects of MPS truncation error on training landscapes and barren plateaus, including whether truncation-induced noise can create training difficulties.
- Apply and evaluate VarQFS on other combinatorial optimization instances and generalized objective function types.
