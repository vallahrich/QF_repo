## Abstract summary
The paper proposes a compact QAOA instantiation that combines a Grover-style feasibility-aware mixer, a diagonal cost Hamiltonian, and a pragmatic grid+refine parameter search to solve constrained combinatorial microgrid network expansion problems. Evaluated on an 11-qubit instance derived from a PyPSA model and benchmarked against a Gurobi global optimum in noiseless statevector simulation, the approach elevates the empirical probability of the classical optimum and improves feasible-subspace signal-to-noise ratio, while highlighting trade-offs and avenues for improved parameterization and hardware validation.
## Methodology
The authors construct a compact, application-driven instantiation of QAOA for a constrained microgrid network expansion problem. They start from a PyPSA model of a small distribution network (N=3 buses) and solve the corresponding MILP with Gurobi to obtain the global optimum and to enumerate feasible binary assignments. The classical linear cost is encoded as a diagonal cost Hamiltonian on n=11 qubits using the Pauli-Z mapping, with an explicit identity shift (implementation Hamiltonian H_impl = 15 I + sum_i a_i Z_i where a equals the raw coefficient vector). Feasibility is enforced with a Grover-style oracle that flips the phase of exactly the feasible basis states; the mixer is implemented as repeated Grover iterates (the Grover operator raised to an integer power per QAOA layer). The phase separator is UC(γ) = exp(−i γ H) with continuous γ-parameters, while the mixer parameter β is treated as a discrete integer count of Grover iterations. Optimization is handled via a two-stage "grid + refine" strategy: a coarse grid search over discrete β-vectors followed by continuous refinement of the γ angles using L-BFGS-B while keeping β fixed. A soft penalty on feasible mass is added to the objective (penalty λ=100, target feasible mass τ=0.95) to steer solutions toward parameter regions with high probability on the feasible subspace. Circuits are executed in a noiseless statevector simulator (Qiskit Aer AerSimulator, method="statevector"), transpiled with optimization_level=1; exact expectation values are computed from the statevector and empirical statistics are estimated by drawing Nshots=5000 samples per evaluated parameter set. Results are benchmarked against the Gurobi global optimum (binary vector 11110111011, cost −5), reporting expected cost, feasible mass, and empirical probability of the classical optimum across QAOA depths (p=1..4).

**Algorithms used:** QAOA, Grover amplitude amplification (Grover mixer), L-BFGS-B (classical continuous optimizer), Grid search (coarse discrete search over Grover iteration counts), Gurobi (MILP solver)
**Frameworks:** PyPSA, Qiskit (Aer AerSimulator), Gurobi

**Experimental setup:** Noiseless statevector simulation using Qiskit Aer AerSimulator with method='statevector'; circuits transpiled with Qiskit optimization_level=1. Simulated problem size: 11 qubits (2^11 = 2048 basis states). QAOA depths explored p = 1..4. For sampling-based metrics, Nshots = 5000 samples were drawn from the simulated statevector.
## Experiment details
### Input
Synthetic microgrid instance generated with PyPSA: small distribution network with 3 buses and candidate generators/lines. Decision problem encoded as an 11-binary-variable MILP (variables represent modular generator and line capacity increments, conductor selectors, and load-serve indicators). Search space size 2048 bitstrings. Gurobi used to enumerate feasibles and produce the reference global optimum (11110111011). Cost coefficient vector used: c = [1,2,0,0,6,0,0,1,-5,-5,-5].

### Process
1) Build PyPSA microgrid model and formulate MILP; solve with Gurobi to obtain global optimum and enumerate feasible set F. 2) Map the linear cost to a diagonal cost Hamiltonian via Pauli-Z (implemented as H_impl = 15 I + sum_i a_i Z_i). 3) Construct a Grover oracle that phase-flips exactly states in F and a diffusion operator; define the mixer in each QAOA layer as r discrete Grover iterations UM(β)=G^r. 4) Initialize the quantum state as the uniform superposition H^{⊗n}|0^n⟩. 5) Run QAOA layers alternating UC(γ)=exp(−iγ H_impl) and UM(β). 6) Parameter search: coarse grid sweep over integer β-vectors (Grover counts per layer), then refine γ parameters using L-BFGS-B while keeping β fixed. 7) Use a loss L(β,γ)=E[C]+λ·max(0,τ−m_F) with τ=0.95 and λ=100 to bias toward high feasible mass. 8) Evaluate each parameter set in noiseless statevector simulator to compute exact expectations and draw Nshots=5000 samples for empirical frequencies (feasible mass m_F, p_exact). 9) Compare QAOA outputs to Gurobi reference (expected cost, probability mass on classical optimum).

### Output
Outputs include exact expectation values of the classical cost E[C] computed from the statevector, feasible mass m_F (total probability on feasible states), empirical/sample-based frequencies (Nshots=5000) including the empirical probability p_exact of the Gurobi-optimal bitstring, and rankings of most probable bitstrings. Baseline: Gurobi global optimum and objective value. Reported metrics: p_exact across QAOA layers (e.g., p_exact ≈ 0.0166 for p=1, ≈0.1730 for p=3), feasible mass, and distribution modes; results reported in tables/summary comments.

## Findings
- [supported] A Grover-style feasibility-aware mixer combined with a diagonal cost Hamiltonian can increase the quantum probability mass on the classical global optimum in an 11-qubit microgrid design instance.
- [supported] On the tested 11-qubit instance (2^11 = 2048 search space) the classical optimal bitstring 11110111011 (cost = -5) attains empirical probabilities of ~0.0166 at 1 QAOA layer, (not among top-5) at 2 layers, ~0.1730 at 3 layers, and ~0.1172 at 4 layers (noiseless statevector simulation, Nshots=5000 sampling).
- [supported] The Grover mixer concentrates amplitude into the feasible subspace (higher feasible mass) and thus improves the signal-to-noise ratio between feasible and infeasible states, enabling the phase-separator to better discriminate low-cost feasible solutions.
- [supported] A mixed discrete–continuous optimisation strategy (coarse grid over integer Grover-iteration counts β followed by continuous refinement of γ angles with L-BFGS-B) is an effective pragmatic workflow for this ansatz in simulation.
- [supported] The implementation uses an explicit oracle that marks exactly the classically feasible states and a diagonal cost operator built from single-qubit Z terms with an identity shift (+15) to keep energies nonnegative.
- [supported] Behaviour is non-monotonic with circuit depth: increasing layer count can both improve and degrade the probability of the classical optimum depending on parameter alignment (observed on layers 1–4).
- [speculative] The authors argue feasibility-focused mixing is a promising pathway to scale constrained combinatorial QAOA and to guide future hardware validation, but no hardware or large-scale empirical scaling results are presented.
- [speculative] The paper suggests that smoother mixers, improved parameterisations, and targeted search heuristics could mitigate the discrete/complex optimisation landscape introduced by integer Grover iterations.

**Results summary:** The paper proposes and simulates an 11-qubit QAOA variant that uses a Grover-style feasibility oracle as the mixer and a diagonal cost Hamiltonian to solve a constrained microgrid expansion MILP derived from a PyPSA model. In noiseless statevector simulation with a grid+refine parameter search and a feasibility-mass penalty, the classical global optimum (found by Gurobi) is present at shallow depth and is most strongly amplified at three QAOA layers (empirical probability ≈0.173). The Grover mixer concentrates amplitude in the feasible subspace, improving signal-to-noise for cost discrimination, but performance varies non-monotonically with depth and the study is limited to simulations on a compact 11-qubit instance (no hardware/noise or large-scale scaling claims).

## Quantum advantage claim

## Limitations
- Small problem size (11 qubits / 2048 classical states): results are demonstrated only on a modest instance and may not generalise to larger microgrid models (author-stated).
- Noiseless statevector simulation: experiments ignore hardware noise, decoherence, gate errors and other realistic device imperfections (author-stated).
- Discrete treatment of the Grover-iteration parameter β creates a non‑smooth, mixed discrete–continuous optimisation landscape that complicates optimisation and gradient-based methods (author-stated).
- Non-monotonic dependence on circuit depth and parameters: increasing QAOA depth can both improve and worsen probability mass on the true optimum, indicating sensitivity of the approach to parameter choices (author-stated).
- Grover mixer marks feasibility but not objective quality: the oracle preserves/enhances feasible subspace but cost discrimination is left to phase separators, which can fail to focus amplitude on the true optimum (author-stated/inferred).
- [inferred] Circuit-depth and gate-count overhead of implementing a Grover oracle + diffusion may be large on hardware, limiting near-term applicability.
- [inferred] The approach's sampling requirements and shot-noise behavior on hardware were not characterised; finite-shot effects could reduce observed amplification of the optimum.
- [inferred] Implementation choices (e.g. using raw coefficient vector as Z-weights and adding an identity offset of +15) may influence dynamics and require tuning for other instances.
- [inferred] The feasibility oracle construction assumes ability to mark all feasible states exactly; building such oracles for larger or more complex MILPs may be challenging (ancilla/workspace, classical-quantum coupling).
- [inferred] Dependence on hyperparameters in optimisation workflow (grid ranges for β, threshold τ=0.95, penalty λ=100, shot count) introduces practical tuning burden and possible sensitivity.
## Open questions
- How does the Grover-mixer QAOA scale with problem size (larger numbers of binary variables / realistic microgrid instances)?
- How robust is the proposed method to realistic hardware noise, limited qubit connectivity, and finite-shot sampling?
- Can the discrete Grover-count parametrisation be relaxed or smoothed to enable gradient-based optimisation while preserving feasibility amplification?
- What are efficient, hardware-friendly constructions of the feasibility oracle and diffusion operator for larger MILP-derived constraints?
- How many Grover iterations per layer are optimal in practice, and how does that scale with the feasible fraction f = |F|/2^n?
- How sensitive are results to implementation choices such as the identity offset, cost-to-Z mapping, and penalty parameters (τ, λ)?
- Can alternative mixers achieve similar feasibility focusing while yielding smoother optimisation landscapes (and better trainability)?
- How does Grover-mixer QAOA compare (in run-time, solution quality, resource cost) to classical heuristics and specialised MILP solvers on larger and more diverse problem instances?
- What are the trade-offs between preserving feasible subspace concentration versus exploring diverse feasible candidates for multi-objective or robust planning?
- How to integrate this approach into near-real-time microgrid reconfiguration workflows, considering time constraints and update frequency?

**Future work:**
- Investigations into improved parametrisations (mixed discrete–continuous strategies) to handle integer Grover counts more smoothly.
- Design and testing of smoother mixers that target feasibility while improving trainability and enabling gradient-based optimisation.
- Development of more targeted search heuristics for the discrete β search (beyond coarse grid + refine) to better navigate the mixed optimisation landscape.
- Scaling experiments and hardware validation: running the approach on noisy quantum hardware and larger problem instances to assess practical performance.
- Exploration of parameter-selection sensitivity (e.g. identity offsets, penalty thresholds, shot counts) and development of robust default choices or adaptive schemes.
