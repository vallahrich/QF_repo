## Abstract summary
This paper introduces Q4FuturePOP, a prototype quantum-annealer-based system for Markowitz portfolio optimization that operates on predicted future asset values rather than historical prices and includes an automatic asset-universe reduction module to lower problem complexity. The system comprises a Predicted Dataset Generation module that builds future price series preserving covariance and target expected returns, an Assets Universe Reduction module that identifies promising assets through repeated QUBO solves, and a Quantum Computing Solver module that formulates and solves the QUBO (tested on D-Wave hardware). Preliminary experiments on real datasets show promising results compared to expert portfolios.
## Methodology
The study presents a prototype system (Q4FuturePOP) composed of three modules: Predicted Dataset Generation (PDG), Assets Universe Reduction (AUR), and Quantum Computing Solver (QCS). PDG takes historical prices, an initial price vector and expert-provided expected returns (Erf) and synthesizes a future price dataset ADf that preserves the historical covariance structure and matches the given expected returns by (i) generating standard-normal random matrix X, transforming it with a Cholesky-based linear transform A (AT = Lh Lx^{-1}) to match covariance, and (ii) finding additive biases b per asset via polynomial/Taylor expansions so that the resulting simulated returns match Erf. AUR reduces the asset universe by iteratively calling QCS E times on ADf, recording assets selected in those preliminary runs and producing a reduced dataset ADf' containing only assets that appeared in any preliminary solution. QCS builds a QUBO from a Markowitz-style Hamiltonian H = α HA + β HB + γ HC with HA (expected-return term), HB (covariance / risk term) and HC (budget constraint), maps the QUBO to a quantum annealer and solves it (binary solution S indicates which assets receive nonzero allocations). A results interpreter converts S into portfolio weights W and computes portfolio expected return, risk (sigma) and Sharpe ratio. For validation, the authors used real financial data from Welzia Management (53 assets over 12 years) to create six use cases (time windows of 12–28 months) and ran AUR+QCS six independent times per use case, comparing outputs to expert portfolios provided by Welzia.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum annealing (D-Wave Advantage), QUBO formulation (Markowitz-based Hamiltonian with terms HA, HB, HC), Cholesky covariance transform, Taylor-series-based root finding for bias b, Heuristic automatic universe reduction (iterative selection across runs)

**Experimental setup:** Quantum annealer experiments were executed on a D-Wave Advantage System6.2 (Pegasus topology) with 5610 qubits and 40134 couplers. The experimental validation used a dataset from Welzia Management: daily prices for 53 assets over 2010-01-01 to 2022-12-13. Six use-case instances (12–28 month windows) were constructed; for each instance AUR+QCS were run 6 independent times and average results reported. The QUBO was built from a Hamiltonian H = α HA + β HB + γ HC; α, β, γ and the AUR internal repetition count E are not specified in the paper.

**Dataset:** Daily price series provided by Welzia Management for 53 assets spanning ~12 years (2010-01-01 to 2022-12-13). Additionally, Welzia provided expert-selected historical portfolios used as baselines and a set of predicted expected returns (Erf) used by PDG to synthesize future datasets. Six evaluation use cases were formed as shorter windows (12–28 months) extracted from the full dataset.

### Process
Pipeline: (1) PDG: generate X ~ N(0,1), compute A via Cholesky (AT = Lh Lx^{-1}) to impose target covariance, and compute additive biases b for each asset so that simulated series yield Erf. Reconstruct ADf from initial values V and simulated returns. (2) AUR: iteratively run QCS E times on ADf, collect assets that receive nonzero allocation in any run to form reduced universe A' and reduced dataset ADf'. (3) QCS: construct QUBO from Hamiltonian H = α HA + β HB + γ HC, where HA = sum_i wi eri, HB = -sum_{i,j} Cov_{i,j} x_i x_j, HC = -(sum_i w_i x_i - bd)^2, with binary indicators x_i; map QUBO to the D-Wave Advantage System6.2 and perform quantum annealing; obtain binary solution S. (4) Results interpreter: convert S into weights W and compute portfolio expected return, risk (σ) and Sharpe (er/σ). Experimental execution: for each of six use-case instances, AUR+QCS were executed six independent times and averages reported. Specific annealing parameters (anneal time, chain strength), QUBO embedding details, and the AUR repetition count E are not reported.

### Output
Outputs per run: selected asset set and allocation weights W (proportions of budget), expected portfolio return (er_portfolio), portfolio risk (σ_portfolio), and Sharpe ratio (er/σ). Reported results are averages across six independent runs per use case. Baseline comparison: expert portfolios supplied by Welzia Management with the same metrics (er and σ). Results are presented in tabular form (Table 1) showing er and σ for expert and Q4FuturePOP outputs per use-case.

## Quantum advantage claim

The paper does not demonstrate a clear, quantifiable quantum advantage. ## Limitations
- System is at a prototypical stage and has not been fully validated as an integrated end-to-end system (author-stated).
- PDG (Predicted Dataset Generation) has been tested but its detailed validation and evaluation are deferred to future work (author-stated).
- Preliminary experiments only exercised AUR+QCS modules separately and on a limited set of six use-cases derived from a single provider's dataset (53 assets over 12 years) — limited empirical scope (author-stated).
- Dependence on expert-provided expected returns (Erf) from Welzia Management for generating future scenarios — introduces external dependency and possible bias (author-stated).
- Relies on current quantum annealer hardware (D-Wave Advantage) which has finite qubits, noisy processes and other NISQ-era limitations; motivates the AUR reduction step (author-stated).
- Performance metrics (e.g., SHARPE) used to assess solutions can be misleading in isolation; expert judgment is required to assess practical portfolio quality (author-stated).
- [inferred] The PDG procedure requires finding real roots of polynomial approximations for each asset (Taylor expansion approach); PDG can fail if such roots do not exist.
- [inferred] The PDG construction (mapping standard normal X via Cholesky and bias b) may produce synthetic future price paths that do not capture realistic non-linear dynamics or regime changes in real markets.
- [inferred] Use of binary variables xi in the presented QUBO formulation suggests limited expressiveness for continuous or fine-grained weight allocations, potentially forcing coarse allocations.
- [inferred] The AUR reduction relies on multiple preliminary QCS runs (E runs) which could be computationally expensive, stochastic, and sensitive to solver noise and parameter settings.
- [inferred] The system's sensitivity to the choice of QUBO weighting parameters (α, β, γ) and to annealer/hybrid solver hyperparameters is not yet characterized.
- [inferred] Results were benchmarked against a single set of expert portfolios from one provider; generalization to other markets, asset universes, or expert styles is untested.
- [inferred] Unclear how prediction errors in Erf propagate through PDG and AUR into final portfolio recommendations; robustness to erroneous forecasts is not evaluated.
## Open questions
- How does the fully integrated Q4FuturePOP perform when validated end-to-end on diverse, out-of-sample datasets and in realistic deployment settings?
- How robust are the final portfolio recommendations to errors or biases in the expert-provided expected returns (Erf)?
- How realistic are the synthetic future price paths produced by PDG compared to alternative forecasting or simulation methods?
- What are the effects of the QUBO parameterization (α, β, γ) on solution quality, and how should these be tuned in practice?
- How does the AUR universe reduction compare with other dimensionality-reduction or feature-selection approaches (classical or quantum) in terms of accuracy and computational cost?
- How many preliminary runs E are needed in AUR to reliably identify the promising sub-universe, and what is the trade-off between E, computation time, and solution quality?
- How well does the approach scale to much larger asset universes (hundreds or thousands of assets) and different market conditions?
- How do different quantum or quantum-inspired solvers (other annealers, gate-based hybrid algorithms, digital annealers) compare when used in QCS for this problem?
- What is the real-world operational performance (execution time, cost, repeatability) of Q4FuturePOP compared with state-of-the-art classical portfolio optimizers?
- How should one integrate expert judgment with QC-generated recommendations in a rigorous, repeatable process acceptable to practitioners and regulators?
- How do annealer-specific limitations (connectivity, noise, embedding overhead) and solver stochasticity influence reproducibility and reliability of results?
- What are the failure modes of the PDG polynomial/root-finding approach and how often do they occur in practice?
