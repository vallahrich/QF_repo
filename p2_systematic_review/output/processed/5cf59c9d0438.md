## Abstract summary
The paper proposes a novel QUBO/Hamiltonian formulation for the 0/1 knapsack problem that requires only two slack variables and reduces reliance on heavy penalty-parameter tuning. Experiments on a quantum annealer for small instances show improved success rates and greater robustness compared with existing encodings, highlighting potential for constrained combinatorial optimization in finance.
## Methodology
The authors propose a new QUBO encoding (called QUBO-type-V) for the 0/1 knapsack problem that uses only two binary slack variables to enforce the inequality constraint. They derive bounds on penalty parameters (denoted A and B) to ensure feasible solutions are favored and compare the proposed formulation against several existing QUBO encodings (QUBO-type-I through -IV). For evaluation they use a set of small, publicly available 0/1-knapsack instances (from Ortega [25]). For each instance they (a) construct QUBO matrices for competing formulations, (b) perform grid/parameter scans over the penalty constants (A and B) using simulated annealing to identify suitable penalty pairs and to visualize deviation of the QUBO ground state from the true optimal, and (c) for selected penalty pairs run experiments on a physical quantum annealer by embedding the QUBO (using EmbeddingComposite) onto an Advantage machine with Pegasus topology. They record whether the annealer ground state corresponds to the known optimal solution, compute deviation metrics, and compare success rates across formulations. Classical exact solvers are used to obtain reference optimal solutions for the small instances. The paper emphasizes reduction in qubit count (N+2 for the proposed formulation) and robustness to penalty parameter choice as primary methodological claims.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum Annealing (QA), Simulated Annealing (classical), Exact classical solvers for reference solutions
**Frameworks:** EmbeddingComposite (D-Wave Ocean embedding API / composite used for mapping to hardware)

**Experimental setup:** Physical quantum annealer (Advantage, Pegasus topology) with embeddings performed via EmbeddingComposite. Simulated annealing used as a classical surrogate to explore penalty-parameter landscapes and to compute ground-state approximations for small instances.

**Dataset:** Small 0/1-knapsack instances from a publicly available online repository (Ortega [25]). Instances are small enough to permit exact classical solutions; the paper labels instances A–J and reports per-instance N and W in tables/figures.
