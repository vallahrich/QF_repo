## Abstract summary
The paper presents a Python-implemented Quantum Support Vector Machine (QSVM) tailored for financial portfolio optimization, focusing on quantum circuit optimization, noise mitigation, and hybrid classical–quantum integration. The authors report that the proposed QSVM achieved 89.65% portfolio performance over time, outperforming other quantum approaches (QPCA, QBM, QKC) and demonstrating improved risk–return tradeoff and efficiency frontier metrics.
## Methodology
The study implements a hybrid classical-quantum pipeline in Python for financial portfolio optimization centered on a Quantum Support Vector Machine (QSVM). The methodology begins with data collection from a Kaggle stock market dataset containing historical prices, volumes and financial indicators. Numerical features are preprocessed using Min-Max normalization to scale values to [0,1]. The quantum component focuses on QSVM model development with emphasis on quantum circuit optimization: gate fusion to reduce gate count and depth, qubit mapping optimization to account for connectivity constraints, and circuit compilation to the target hardware native gate set. Noise mitigation and error correction strategies are discussed (e.g., Steane and surface codes) to increase robustness in the NISQ regime. The pipeline integrates classical preprocessing and evaluation with the quantum QSVM kernel/feature mapping, and compares QSVM performance against several quantum baselines (QPCA, QBM, QKC) using portfolio-level metrics (cumulative portfolio return over time, risk–return tradeoff, and efficiency frontier). The implementation is stated to be in Python, but specific quantum frameworks, hardware, simulator names, and low-level parameter settings are not provided.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum Support Vector Machine (QSVM), Quantum Principal Component Analysis (QPCA), Quantum Boltzmann Machine (QBM), Quantum K-Means Clustering (QKC)

**Dataset:** Kaggle Stock Market Dataset (JacksonCrow stock-market-dataset). Contains historical stock prices, trading volumes and financial indicators for publicly listed firms. Exact dataset size not specified in paper.
