## Abstract summary
This preprint proposes a hybrid quantum-classical framework for portfolio optimization that augments a QUBO mean-variance formulation with an entanglement-informed quantum risk matrix and employs counterdiabatic quantum annealing to accelerate convergence. The work presents penalty parameter bounds, theoretical convergence statements, and experiments combining D-Wave annealing runs (standard schedules) with Qiskit gate-model simulations for quantum risk, reporting improved out-of-sample performance on a 10-stock dataset while noting counterdiabatic schedules were simulated rather than executed on hardware.
## Methodology
The study proposes a hybrid quantum-classical portfolio optimization methodology. The core optimization is a QUBO that augments a classical mean-variance objective with a quantum-informed risk term: fQ(x) = x^T Q_MV x + λ x^T E x + α (B - 1^T x)^2. The classical component uses estimated expected returns µ and covariance Σ (annualized from daily log-returns). The quantum risk matrix E is built by starting from the absolute-value classical correlation matrix |C| and adding a scaled pairwise quantum mutual information term computed from two-qubit parameterized circuits (Ry rotations followed by a controlled-Z) simulated with Qiskit Aer statevector simulator; von Neumann entropies of reduced density matrices produce the mutual information S(ρ_i)+S(ρ_j)-S(ρ_ij). Small entries of E are truncated to ε = 1e-4 to ensure numerical stability. Theoretical work derives conservative bounds for penalty parameters α and λ to enforce cardinality constraints and ensure quantum risk contributions are not dominated by classical terms. Counterdiabatic (CD) annealing theory is presented to justify faster convergence; CD schedules and their effects are evaluated via high-fidelity classical simulations/emulations (not executed on the physical QPU). The optimization pipeline is iterative: build QUBO using the latest µ, Σ, E, run quantum annealing (D-Wave Advantage physical runs used standard annealing for baselines; CD-assisted annealing is simulated), repair infeasible solutions to satisfy cardinality, select best candidate(s), and update statistics until stopping criteria. Empirical evaluation used real market data (10 S&P-derived equities), parameter sweeps for penalties (α, λ), ablation studies (remove quantum risk or CD), baselines (MIQP via Gurobi and a greedy heuristic), and standard statistical tests (paired t-tests, bootstrap CIs, Cohen's d) to assess significance.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum annealing (D-Wave Advantage, standard annealing for physical runs), Simulated counterdiabatic annealing (classical emulation), QUBO formulation (mean-variance + quantum risk + cardinality penalty), Classical optimizer SLSQP (mentioned as classical optimizer timing reference), MIQP (Gurobi) baseline, Greedy heuristic baseline, Hybrid quantum-classical iterative sampler (Markov-chain style with repair steps)
**Frameworks:** Qiskit (Aer statevector simulator), D-Wave tooling (minorminer embedding), Gurobi (MIQP baseline), yfinance (data retrieval)

**Experimental setup:** Physical quantum annealing experiments were performed on the D-Wave Advantage system1.1. Embedding was done with minorminer. Typical annealer settings: 5000 anneal reads per problem, annealing time 20 µs per read, chain strength around 1.5 (swept 1.0–2.0 in calibration), average chain length ≈5–5.5, chain break policy majority vote, 10 independent runs per parameter configuration. Quantum risk (entanglement) calculations were performed on Qiskit Aer statevector simulator using parameterized two-qubit circuits (Ry(θ) on each qubit followed by a controlled-Z), with θ sampled uniformly in [0, π].

**Dataset:** Daily adjusted close prices downloaded via yfinance for 10 equities (AAPL, MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, TSLA, BRK-B, JPM, JNJ, V, PG) covering January 1, 2019 to December 31, 2023. Training (in-sample) used Jan 1, 2019–Dec 31, 2021; testing (out-of-sample) used Jan 1, 2022–Dec 31, 2023.

## Findings

- [speculative] The paper derives conservative, computable penalty-parameter bounds (e.g., α > M n^2 and λ > max|Qij| / emin) that guarantee feasibility of the cardinality constraint and dominance of quantum-risk penalties in the QUBO.

- [supported] Reported experimental performance claims indicate the hybrid quantum-classical method achieved higher out-of-sample objective and Sharpe ratio than classical baselines (e.g., Hybrid objective 0.72 ± 0.03 and Sharpe 1.15 vs MIQP 0.69 ± 0.04 and Sharpe 1.08), with reported p-values < 0.01.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The paper reports empirical improvements of its hybrid approach versus classical baselines on a small (n=10) asset universe and provides statistical metrics (p-values, CIs). However, the key quantum acceleration claim via counterdiabatic annealing is only supported by classical simulations/emulations (not executed on the QPU), and the quantum-risk modeling uses two-qubit simulated entanglement surrogates rather than hardware-native multi-qubit effects. Given the limited problem scale and the simulated nature of the CD advantage, the claimed quantum advantage is suggestive but not demonstrated on hardware and thus remains speculative.
## Limitations
- Counterdiabatic (CD) schedules and their reported benefits were implemented only via classical simulations and emulations, not physically deployed on the D-Wave QPU (author-stated).
- Quantum risk modeling is limited to two-qubit entanglement proxies (von Neumann entropies) and does not capture multi-qubit correlations (author-stated).
- Experiments are restricted to a small asset universe (n = 10); scalability beyond this tested size is not demonstrated (author-stated).
- Embedding and hardware overhead (logical-to-physical qubit ratio, chain strength, chain breaks) impose scalability constraints and affect results (author-stated).
- Transaction costs and turnover penalties were excluded from experiments (author-stated).
- Some penalty parameter bounds provided are conservative; practical deployment relies on empirical tuning and calibration (author-stated).
- Truncation of small entries in the quantum risk matrix E (ε) is introduced to stabilize bounds; this is an approximation that may affect fidelity of quantum risk signals (author-stated).
- Reproducibility gaps: data/processing scripts and detailed device logs are not publicly provided in the manuscript (materials said to be available on request or pending) (author-stated).
- [inferred] Statistical claims (significance, effect sizes) are based on a small number of independent runs and a small asset set, which may limit generality and statistical power.
- [inferred] Entanglement surrogates are generated from randomly parameterized two-qubit circuits (random Ry angles); these may not reflect economically meaningful dependency structures.
- [inferred] Majority-vote chain break policy and chosen chain-strength/embedding heuristics on the annealer may bias sampling outcomes and are not exhaustively analyzed.
- [inferred] The Qiskit Aer statevector simulations ignore hardware noise; entanglement-based risk measures may be optimistic compared to noisy QPU measurements.
- [inferred] Theoretical convergence and CD acceleration results rely on strong assumptions (e.g., gap lower bound, bounded commutator norms) that may not hold for larger or noisy practical systems.
- [inferred] Penalty parameter selection (α, λ, γ, ε) is performed by grid sweeps; no principled or automated selection method is provided, raising concerns about sensitivity and manual tuning effort.
- [inferred] Comparisons are limited to a small set of baselines (MIQP via Gurobi and a greedy heuristic); broader benchmarking versus other classical/metaheuristic methods is missing.
- [inferred] Runtime and cost analyses are limited to the small-scale setting; resource trade-offs at practical portfolio sizes are not quantified.
## Open questions
- Can counterdiabatic schedules be physically implemented on current D-Wave hardware or other QPUs, and will physical implementations reproduce the simulated CD benefits?
- How can the quantum risk model be extended from two-qubit entanglement proxies to multi-qubit or higher-dimensional entanglement that might capture richer market dependencies?
- How does the hybrid method scale in practice to larger asset universes (n >> 10) and what are the embedding, runtime, and solution-quality trade-offs at scale?
- How sensitive are results to the truncation parameter ε, the quantum/classical scaling γ, and the penalty parameters α and λ, and how can these be chosen optimally rather than by grid search?
- How robust are entanglement-derived risk factors and the overall optimization pipeline to realistic hardware noise and device errors when entanglement measures are estimated on QPUs instead of noiseless simulators?
- Can embedding overhead be reduced or optimized (e.g., improved embedding algorithms, topology-aware formulations) to make larger problems feasible on current annealers?
- Do the theoretical assumptions required for the CD acceleration bounds (spectral gap, bounded norms, commutator behavior) hold for practical, many-body Hamiltonians used in QUBO encodings of portfolio problems?
- How generalizable are the empirical gains across different market regimes, asset classes, and longer evaluation periods?
- What is the precise economic interpretation and empirical validation of quantum mutual information (entanglement surrogate) as a financial risk factor beyond the reported correlation with realized covariance?
- Can multi-qubit entanglement measures provide incremental predictive or risk-modeling value over classical higher-moment models (skewness, kurtosis) or multivariate dependence measures?
- How should transaction costs, turnover, market microstructure effects, and regulatory constraints be incorporated into the hybrid quantum-classical optimization framework?
- Is it possible to automate or theoretically justify penalty parameter selection to avoid ad hoc or expensive grid searches?
