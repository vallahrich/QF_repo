## Abstract summary
The paper proposes a novel encryption scheme that integrates a modified quadratic chaotic map with the Fibonacci sequence to generate combined pseudo-random sequences for text and image encryption. Experiments show high key sensitivity, near-ideal entropy, and strong resistance to statistical, differential, noise and occlusion attacks, while claiming computational efficiency and improved robustness against brute-force and potential quantum-enabled threats.
## Methodology
The authors propose and evaluate a chaos-based encryption scheme that combines a modified Quadratic Chaotic Map (QCM) with sequences derived from the Fibonacci series. The methodology begins by splitting a user key (example: a 16-bit key) into two parts: one part indexes a starting position in the Fibonacci sequence and the other sets parameters for the QCM. A Fibonacci subsequence is generated from the selected start index; concurrently the modified QCM (X_{n+1} = r + (1 - a X_n^2)) is initialized with given X0 and map parameters to produce a chaotic sequence. These two sequences are element-wise combined (multiplication) and then reduced via modulo operations to fit the target domain (mod 26 for alphabetic text, mod 256 for 8-bit image color channels). The resulting sequence is XORed with plaintext numeric representations (text converted to alphabet indices; images decomposed into RGB layers) to produce ciphertext. The authors analyze the dynamical properties of both the classical and modified QCM (iteration plots, bifurcation diagrams, Lyapunov exponent computations) and empirically evaluate encryption performance and robustness on sample texts and images (USC–SIPI image set: pepper, airplane, plus Lena, Baboon, House, Sailboat). Sensitivity tests (single-bit key changes), statistical analyses (histogram, entropy, pixel correlation), differential attack metrics (NPCR, UACI), similarity/fidelity/difference metrics (SSIM, NCC, GSI, MAE, MSE, PSNR, NAE, AD, MD), and resilience tests (Gaussian noise at multiple power levels and occlusion fractions of 1/4, 1/2, and 3/5) were performed to assess security and robustness. Example parameter values reported include key = "1000110000101100", X0 = 0.02, r = 0, a = 4.

**Algorithms used:** Modified Quadratic Chaotic Map (QCM): X_{n+1} = r + (1 - a X_n^2), Classical Quadratic Chaotic Map (for comparison): X_{n+1} = r - X_n^2, Fibonacci sequence-based key indexing and sequence generation, Sequence combination via element-wise multiplication, Modulo-reduction (mod 26 for text, mod 256 for images), XOR-based encryption (keystream XOR plaintext), Dynamical analyses: bifurcation plotting, Lyapunov exponent computation, Security/quality analyses: histogram, entropy, correlation, NPCR/UACI, SSIM/NCC/GSI, MAE/MSE/PSNR, NAE/AD/MD

**Dataset:** No financial data used. Experiments used text samples (two example plaintexts) and public images from the USC–SIPI image database (pepper, airplane; additional standard test images: Lena, Baboon, House, Sailboat).
## Experiment details
### Input
Images: USC–SIPI image database (pepper, airplane) and common test images (Lena, Baboon, House, Sailboat). Image sizes not specified in the paper. Preprocessing: images decomposed into RGB layers and/or grayscale; pixel values treated as 8-bit (0–255). Chaotic map initial condition example: X0 = 0.02, r = 0, a = 4.

### Process
Pipeline: (1) Accept input (text or image). (2) Split user key into two parts: part A selects starting index in Fibonacci sequence; part B sets/chooses parameters/range for the QCM. (3) Generate Fibonacci subsequence from selected start index. (4) Initialize modified QCM with specified X0 and parameters (r, a) and iterate to produce chaotic sequence (iteration count implied to match data length). (5) Combine Fibonacci and QCM sequences element-wise (multiplication). (6) Apply modulo operation: mod 26 for text keystream values, mod 256 for image keystream values. (7) XOR the modulo-reduced keystream with plaintext numeric representation to create ciphertext. (8) For decryption, repeat keystream generation with the same key/parameters and XOR ciphertext to recover plaintext. Experiments included: single-bit key-flip sensitivity tests (flipping various bits in the 16-bit key), entropy/histogram/correlation/NPCR/UACI/SSIM/etc. evaluations, noise robustness tests (Gaussian noise added with normalized power levels 1e-6, 3e-6, 5e-6, 7e-6), and occlusion tests (masks occluding 1/4, 1/2, 3/5 of the image). Parameters explicitly reported: key example '1000110000101100', X0=0.02, r=0, a=4, Fibonacci start index 140 (for that key), QCM index 44 (for that key). Iteration counts and exact keystream lengths are implicit (matched to plaintext/image size) but not numerically listed.

### Output
Outputs were encrypted texts and encrypted images (ciphertexts) and decrypted outputs under correct/incorrect keys. Quantitative performance metrics reported include: entropy (per channel and grayscale), histograms, adjacent-pixel correlation coefficients (horizontal, vertical, diagonal), NPCR and UACI (differential attack resistance), similarity metrics (SSIM, NCC, GSI), error/difference metrics (MAE, MSE, PSNR), fidelity metrics (NAE, AD, MD), and robustness measures under noise and occlusion (MSE and PSNR reported for noise intensities and occlusion fractions). Results are delivered as numeric tables and visual examples (plain/encrypted layer-wise images and recovered images under occlusion/noise).

## Findings
- [supported] The paper proposes a combined encryption scheme that integrates a modified quadratic chaotic map (QCM) with a Fibonacci-sequence-derived initial condition and demonstrates its application to both text and image encryption.
- [supported] Experimental results show strong key sensitivity: single-bit changes in the 16-bit key produce completely different decrypted outputs for both text and images.
- [supported] Encrypted images exhibit high information entropy (close to the ideal value 8), with reported layer-wise entropies ≈ 7.998–7.999.
- [supported] The scheme yields high resistance to differential attacks with reported NPCR ≈ 99.88–99.92% and UACI ≈ 33.41–33.49% across tested images.
- [supported] Pixel correlation in encrypted images is effectively eliminated (correlation coefficients for adjacent pixels ~0, e.g. horizontal ≈ -0.0111 for Pepper).
- [supported] Dynamical analysis of the proposed QCM (iteration plots, bifurcation diagrams, Lyapunov exponents) shows multiple chaotic parameter intervals and positive maximal Lyapunov exponents for chosen parameters (e.g., MLE = 0.673 for a = 2 and MLE = 2.025 for a = 4), supporting chaotic behavior.
- [supported] The method successfully encrypts/decrypts RGB layers separately and produces visually scrambled outputs that reveal no perceptible original image content.
- [supported] The encryption demonstrates empirical robustness to added Gaussian noise (various small variances) and to substantial occlusion (tested occlusion fractions up to 3/5), with reported MSE/PSNR values for noisy and occluded tests.
- [supported] Pixel-difference and fidelity metrics (MAE, MSE, PSNR, NAE, AD, MD) indicate substantial differences between plain and encrypted images consistent with encryption goals.
- [speculative] The authors claim the proposed QCM-based scheme has lower computational overhead than deep-learning-integrated chaotic methods (e.g., Logistic Map with DNN, Henon Map with ANN), but provide no computational-complexity benchmarks or runtime comparisons.
- [speculative] The paper asserts the scheme defends against brute-force and 'quantum-based decryption attempts' (i.e., is post-quantum resilient) without presenting analysis or experiments involving quantum algorithms or post-quantum cryptanalysis.
- [speculative] The authors suggest the proposed map mitigates periodicity issues present in transforms such as the Arnold Cat Map; while bifurcation and Lyapunov analyses are presented, broader claims about all periodicity-exploitation attack vectors are not empirically evaluated.
- [speculative] Recommendations for future work include optimizing computational efficiency and exploring additional applications; these are forward-looking and not demonstrated in the present work.

**Results summary:** The authors introduce a classical chaos-based encryption approach that combines a modified quadratic chaotic map (QCM) with a Fibonacci-sequence-derived initial condition/key-splitting strategy. They implement and empirically evaluate the scheme on text and multiple standard test images, reporting high key sensitivity (single-bit key flips break decryption), near-ideal entropy, strong resistance to differential attacks (high NPCR/UACI), negligible pixel correlation after encryption, and demonstrable robustness against added Gaussian noise and partial occlusion (recovery tested up to 60% occlusion). Dynamical analyses (iteration plots, bifurcation diagrams, Lyapunov exponents) support chaotic behavior of the QCM for chosen parameters. The paper makes additional claims about lower computational overhead relative to neural approaches and resilience to quantum attacks, but these are not empirically validated.
