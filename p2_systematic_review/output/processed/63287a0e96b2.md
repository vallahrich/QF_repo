## Abstract summary
The paper proposes two hybrid classical–quantum architectures for time-series forecasting: a variational-circuit-based Quantum Neural Network (QNN) and a Dressed/Hybrid Quantum Neural Network (HQNN) that sandwiches variational quantum circuits between classical layers. Both models are trained with classical optimizers on a simulator and evaluated on several benchmark time-series tasks (Mackey–Glass, Lorenz attractor, Box–Jenkins gas furnace, and USD–EUR exchange), showing competitive performance versus traditional MLP, CNN and LSTM baselines with comparable parameter counts. The paper introduces a mathematical model to describe and optimize the process of unloading bulk-carrier raw sugar cargo from a harbor to refinery storage silos, with the main goal of minimizing the vessel's stay at berth. The model formulates the unloading operation via inequalities and intermediate variables and is solved using a hybrid method combining branch-and-bound and tabu search. Computational results demonstrate significant improvements (e.g., reducing observed unloading delays from ~10 to ~5 days) and practical applicability of the proposed scenarios.
## Methodology
The study proposes and evaluates two hybrid classical-quantum neural network architectures for short-term time series forecasting: (i) a Quantum Neural Network (QNN) based on variational quantum circuits (VQCs) and (ii) a Hybrid-Quantum Neural Network (HQNN) composed of Dressed Quantum Circuits (DQC) — a VQC sandwiched between two classical layers — which are then stacked to form a multilayered model. Both approaches use classical optimizers on a host machine to train the circuit parameters. Inputs are standard time-series regressors constructed from prior timesteps. For the QNN the classical features are encoded into a quantum state via parameterized single-qubit rotations (e.g., Ry rotations); a sequence of parameterized layers (single-qubit rotations followed by entangling gates such as CNOTs) is applied and the output is obtained from expectation values of Pauli observables (e.g., ⟨Z⟩). For the HQNN the classical input is first linearly transformed by a classical layer into a dimension compatible with the number of qubits, encoded into the qubits, processed by the VQC, then post-processed by a classical layer to yield the numerical forecast. The authors evaluate both architectures on four short-term forecasting tasks (Mackey–Glass, Lorenz attractor, Box–Jenkins Gas Furnace, and USD/EUR exchange rate), comparing them to classical baselines (MLP, CNN, LSTM). Models are trained with standard loss (mean-squared-error) and classical optimizers (SGD or Adam) and performance is reported via RMSE/MAE/MAPE; experiments are run on the Pennylane lighting.qubit simulator backed by PyTorch. The provided conference proceedings include multiple empirical studies using a range of algorithmic and modeling methodologies. Object-detection/segmentation tasks (endoscopy artifact detection) were addressed by training one-stage real-time detectors (YOLOv4 and YOLACT) in PyTorch with data augmentation (photometric, geometric, geophotometric) and evaluation using mean average precision (mAP) at diﬀerent IoU thresholds (mAP25, mAP50, mAP75). Another study used a Retinanet (ResNet-50 + FPN) detector for white blood cell detection and a siamese VGG16 network with contrastive loss for WBC classiﬁcation; experiments used transfer learning (COCO weights), sliding-window / crop-based preprocessing and standard metrics (accuracy, precision, recall, F1). Multi-agent pathﬁnding research adapted pebble-motion concepts with custom procedures (PushToEmpty, PushAlongPath, PushThroughVFrom) and integrated them into existing solvers (Push-and-Rotate) for large agents; experimental comparisons used CCBS and other MAPF baselines. There is also methodological work on hypervolume computation (WFG/iWFG) with code debugging and optimization and anytime MAPF solvers combining Conﬂict-Based Search (CBS) with Focal Search (FS); empirical evaluation compared variants and search-reuse strategies. Evolutionary/metaheuristic studies include genetic algorithms, memetic algorithms, siamese networks, and hyper-heuristics (ant-colony hyperheuristics) applied to problems such as Boolean-function construction, containership stowage, and multi-objective optimization (MOEA) experiments. Mathematical optimization (mixed integer/binary linear models) and heuristic/tabu/branch-and-bound combinations were used for logistics problems (sugar unloading). Across studies, typical methodology elements are: formal problem modeling (objective + constraints), baseline comparison, algorithmic implementation, hyperparameter tuning, use of synthetic and public benchmarks (EAD2020, BCCD, DTLZ, MovingAI) and reporting standard metrics (mAP, precision/recall/F1, hypervolume, runtime/success rates).

**Algorithms used:** Variational Quantum Circuit (VQC), Quantum Neural Network (QNN), Dressed Quantum Circuit (DQC) / Hybrid-Quantum Neural Network (HQNN), Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), Adam optimizer, Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD), YOLOv4, YOLACT, RetinaNet (ResNet50 + FPN), VGG16 (siamese), Focal loss, A* (low-level), Safe Interval Path Planning (SIPP), Conﬂict-Based Search (CBS), BCBS (bounded-suboptimal CBS), ECBS (enhanced CBS), Anytime BCBS, Anytime ECBS, CCBS, Push and Rotate (PMG), IWFG (incremental WFG hypervolume), Ant Colony Optimization (as hyperheuristic high level), Tabu Search, Branch-and-Bound, Genetic Algorithms, Memetic Algorithms, Focal Search, Feature Pyramid Network (FPN)
**Frameworks:** PennyLane (lighting.qubit simulator), PyTorch, Gurobi, NetBeans/Java, EMO Project (C/MPI), Open MPI, Keras-retinanet (referenced weights repo)

**Experimental setup:** Simulations were performed using the PennyLane built-in 'lighting.qubit' simulator together with PyTorch. All models (QNN, HQNN, MLP, CNN, LSTM) were trained for 100 epochs with classical optimizers. Specific optimizers and learning rates varied by dataset (see parameters). Training/testing splits: Mackey–Glass 1000 samples (train 500 / test 500); Lorenz 1000 samples (train 500 / test 500); Gas Furnace 296 samples (train 200 / test 96); USD/EUR 376 samples (train 300 / test 76).

**Dataset:** Four time-series datasets: (1) Mackey–Glass chaotic time series (τ=17, simulated, 1000 points), (2) Lorenz attractor (σ=10, ρ=28, β=8/3 simulated, 1000 points), (3) Box–Jenkins (Gas Furnace) time series (industrial dataset, 296 points), (4) USD-to-EUR daily exchange rate (public FX data, 376 points).

## Findings
- [supported] The paper proposes two classical–quantum hybrid architectures for time-series forecasting: a Variational Quantum Circuit-based Quantum Neural Network (QNN) and a Dressed/Hybrid Quantum Neural Network (HQNN) that combines quantum layers with classical pre/post-processing.
- [supported] Experiments were performed on four time-series forecasting tasks (Mackey–Glass, USD/EUR exchange, Lorenz attractor, and Box–Jenkins gas furnace) using simulated quantum circuits (Pennylane simulator) and classical baselines (MLP, CNN, LSTM).
- [supported] On the Mackey–Glass univariate forecasting task the QNN achieved the best RMSE among tested methods (QNN RMSE = 0.01360) outperforming the MLP (RMSE = 0.02047), CNN (RMSE = 0.04586) and LSTM (RMSE = 0.01866).
- [supported] On the Box–Jenkins (multivariate) task the HQNN gave competitive results (HQNN RMSE = 0.69741) slightly better than MLP (0.75594) but LSTM had marginally better RMSE (0.64989) in that task.
- [supported] On the USD/EUR exchange forecasting task the QNN reported the lowest RMSE (QNN RMSE = 0.00281) among the tested classical and hybrid models.
- [supported] For the chaotic Lorenz attractor forecasting, the paper reports mixed outcomes: the QNN outperformed the MLP on x(t+1) and y(t+1) predictions but not on z(t+1), indicating task-dependent comparative performance.
- [supported] The hybrid models were trained and evaluated using standard deep-learning tooling (PyTorch, Pennylane simulator) and modest computational resources (single simulated GPU environment) — i.e., the quantum circuits were simulated, not run on quantum hardware.
- [speculative] The authors suggest that hybrid quantum–classical architectures (VQC + classical fine-tuning) may be a promising direction to explore for short-term time-series forecasting and could offer advantages in certain problem classes.
- [speculative] The paper implies that QNN/HQNN may offer a viable alternative to classical deep models in some problems and that more research on architectures and encoding/feature choices could further improve results.
- [supported] For endoscopic artifact detection, YOLOv4 and YOLACT were evaluated: geometric data augmentation improved mAP25/mAP50, and both methods outperformed several EfficientDet baselines on mAP25/mAP50.
- [supported] In that study YOLOv4 with geometric augmentation achieved the highest reported mAP25/mAP50/mAP75 among the compared methods.
- [supported] A Retinanet + Siamese VGG16 two-stage approach achieves strong white blood cell (WBC) detection and classiﬁcation results; overall classification accuracy around 95% was reported on the evaluated dataset.
- [supported] Visualizations (t-SNE) indicate siamese embeddings reduce inter-class similarity for WBCs (but eosinophil vs neutrophil remain challenging).
- [supported] A modification to the Push and Rotate multi-agent pathﬁnding algorithm was proposed to handle large (non-point) agents; the adapted algorithm (P&R-LA) empirically solved more instances under time limits than CCBS on tested roadmaps.
- [supported] The paper identifies and implements routines (PushToEmpty, PushThroughVFrom) to clear edges for large-agent MAPF, but acknowledges the approach is not yet provably complete.
- [supported] Comparative experiments on anytime CBS variants show that re-using high-level CT-tree information (Anytime BCBS) helps versus restarting; conversely, for ECBS-style anytime variants re-starting (naive) can sometimes outperform re-use. Empirical results show Anytime ECBS (naive) often reaches better-quality solutions faster than Anytime BCBS on benchmark maps.
- [supported] A correction to the IWFG (incremental hypervolume) implementation was identiﬁed and ﬁxed (binary search on equal vectors). The corrected IWFG 1.01 yields major runtime improvements when used inside SMS-EMOA compared to previous implementations and outperforms NSGA-III in hypervolume on tested DTLZ instances.
- [supported] For source-code reuse detection, a learned linear combination (Mixmetric) of several similarity measures (LCS, KRG-ST, cosine/trigram, lexical) with thresholds was developed via a genetic algorithm; Mixmetric outperformed the original individual measures and baselines on the SOCO-C benchmark.
- [supported] For constructing Weight-wise Perfectly Balanced (WPB) Boolean functions, experiments indicate that exploring the full Boolean search space (allowing infeasible/unbalanced individuals with a penalty) plus proper crossover/mutation can lead to higher nonlinearity solutions than restricting the search to WPB-only spaces; specific GA variants and settings produced best results (e.g., highest nonlinearity of 112 occurred more often with whole-space search).
- [supported] An ant-colony based perturbative hyper-heuristic (with seven low-level heuristics) was effective at improving stowage plans to satisfy ship stability for the containership stowage problem (MBPP).
- [supported] A hybrid two-phase method (deterministic heuristic for initial solution + perturbative hyperheuristic) solved practical MBPP instances faster than a prior hybrid that used an exact solver (Gurobi) for the initial phase; the heuristic-first method produced similar solution quality with dramatically lower CPU time.
- [supported] A mathematical optimization model and combined Branch-and-Bound + Tabu-Search approach for raw-sugar unloading was proposed and applied to real company data; simulations showed unloading-time reductions (example scenarios reduced unloading from ~10 days to ~5 days under modeled assumptions).
- [speculative] Several papers propose that hybrid hierarchical approaches (heuristic initial solution + metaheuristic refinement) generalize well to real-world logistics problems, but claims of general applicability are stated without broad cross-domain proofs.
- [speculative] The Push & Rotate extension for large agents is suggested as a step toward a complete algorithm; completeness is not proven and authors note remaining gaps—so claims of near-completeness are speculative.

**Results summary:** The authors implemented two hybrid quantum–classical forecasting architectures (a variational quantum-circuit-based QNN and a hybrid Dressed QNN) and evaluated them on four time-series problems (Mackey–Glass, Lorenz, Box–Jenkins, and USD/EUR). Using simulated quantum circuits (Pennylane) and standard classical baselines (MLP, CNN, LSTM), they found that the QNN provided the lowest RMSE on Mackey–Glass (0.01360) and USD/EUR (0.00281) tasks, while the HQNN was competitive on the Box–Jenkins task (RMSE 0.69741). Performance varied by target and dataset: on the Lorenz system the QNN outperformed the MLP on some variables but not all. Overall, the hybrid models achieved competitive results in the tested short-term forecasting tasks using quantum simulations, but no quantum hardware advantage is demonstrated. The collection of conference papers presents a variety of applied AI and optimization advances: (1) object detectors (YOLOv4, YOLACT) with geometric augmentation improve mAP metrics for endoscopic artifact detection and beat several baselines; (2) a Retinanet + Siamese VGG16 pipeline yields ~95% WBC classiﬁcation accuracy and reduces inter-class ambiguity via learned embeddings; (3) multi-agent pathﬁnding for large agents was advanced by adapting Push & Rotate with routines to clear edges, outperforming CCBS empirically though completeness is not yet guaranteed; (4) anytime CBS variants were studied showing search reuse helps BCBS, while naive restarts sometimes work better for ECBS; (5) a coding bug in IWFG hypervolume code was located and ﬁxed, and the corrected IWFG 1.01 significantly speeds up SMS-EMOA and improves many-objective results vs NSGA-III on DTLZ tests; (6) a learned Mixmetric (weighted combination of similarity measures) outperforms prior source-code reuse baselines on SOCO-C; (7) in Boolean function search, allowing infeasible individuals with penalties and using tailored operators led to improved nonlinearity; (8) hybrid hierarchical heuristics (deterministic initial solution + ant-colony perturbative hyperheuristic) are effective for containership stowage (MBPP) and for raw-sugar unloading scheduling, enabling modeled reductions in unloading time from ~10 to ~5 days under the stated assumptions.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** not-applicable

No claim of quantum computational advantage is demonstrated. All quantum circuit elements were executed using a classical simulator (Pennylane simulator) and parameter optimization was performed classically; results therefore indicate hybrid-modelity on simulated quantum circuits but do not show empirical quantum hardware speedup or advantage.
## Limitations
- Experiments were performed using a quantum simulator (Pennylane lighting.qubit) rather than on real quantum hardware, so the results do not reflect noise and constraints of current quantum devices.
- Only a small and limited set of time-series problems was evaluated (two univariate and two multivariate datasets), so conclusions about general applicability remain preliminary.
- The study focused on short-term (one-step-ahead) forecasting; multi-step or longer-horizon forecasts were not explored.
- Only a few quantum-classical hybrid architectures and a limited set of encodings/embeddings were tested (single VQC design and one encoding choice), so conclusions about optimal architectures or encodings are not exhaustive.
- Simulation-based quantum circuits are classically expensive to scale; the presented experiments do not demonstrate scalability to many qubits or high-dimensional inputs.
- [inferred] The dataset sizes used are relatively small compared to many classical deep-learning benchmarks, which may limit the robustness of the statistical conclusions.
- [inferred] The paper does not test robustness to real-world operational issues such as noisy labels, missing data, or nonstationary environments beyond the chosen datasets.
- [inferred] Limited analysis was presented on why certain tasks (e.g., specific variables in the Lorenz system) favored QNNs while others favored classical models — mechanistic explanations are lacking.
- The Incremental WFG hypervolume implementation (IWFG 1.01) as distributed contains a bug (binarySearch) that causes crashes when integrated into MOEAs and when identical objective vectors (indifferent solutions) occur, which prevented broader adoption [stated].
- The IWFG 1.01 method, while fast in practice, still has worst-case exponential complexity and practical performance depends on implementation details; a correct/robust implementation is required to exploit it in frameworks [inferred].
- The 'move-la' procedure and the adapted Push-and-Rotate for large agents (MAPF-LA) remains incomplete: the approach does not cover the third case where interfering agent paths must go through v_to (return by reversal impossible), so the solver is not provably complete [stated].
- The suggested MAPF-LA approach requires choosing an appropriate planning order to be complete and this component is not solved — the current algorithm may fail even when a global solution exists [stated].
- Anytime ECBS and Anytime BCBS behavior can be unpredictable: re-use of previous iterations helps in some settings but may hurt in others; causes (e.g., not updating hfocal heuristics or keeping irrelevant constraints) are not fully understood [stated].
- [inferred] The algorithms that reuse previous search structures (Anytime ECBS) may retain stale heuristic values or irrelevant constraints, which can negatively impact performance and make behavior nondeterministic across instances.
- Experiments with WPB Boolean function search are limited to n = 8 due to computational cost; scaling to practical sizes (e.g., n = 16) was not feasible with current methods and implementations [stated].
- [inferred] The WPB search used the full Walsh-Hadamard transform (costly); without Fast Walsh-Hadamard optimization, larger n cannot be handled efficiently.
- The evolutionary approaches for WPB rely on relatively small datasets and 30 runs; the statistical generalization to larger or more diverse datasets is not established [inferred].
- Artifact detection methods (YOLOv4, YOLACT) perform poorly on small objects (specularities, bubbles), and bubbles are particularly poorly detected because they are small and infrequent in the training set [stated].
- [inferred] Training/testing distribution mismatch: training on single-frame dataset and testing on sequential frames (out-of-sample) may limit generalization; augmentation partly mitigates this but cannot fully replace diverse data.
- The source-code reuse experiments (SOCO-C) include very few positive examples in the test set (322 positives among ~396M pairs); such extreme class imbalance challenges evaluation and may restrict conclusions about recall/precision in practical settings [inferred].
- In several studies, algorithmic improvements are empirically validated but lack formal guarantees (e.g., completeness, optimality) and theoretical analysis of worst-case behavior [inferred].
- Some reported algorithms (e.g., IWFG integration) require non-trivial engineering effort to be incorporated into popular frameworks, limiting reproducibility and adoption [inferred].
- In the containership stowage and raw sugar unloading models, simulations depend on idealized assumptions (no delays, no traffic, continuous work) and results can change significantly when realistic elements (breaks, traffic peaks) are introduced — indicating sensitivity to operational variability [stated/inferred].
- The raw-sugar unloading model and simulations are calibrated for a specific company configuration; generalization to other ports/companies may require re-tuning parameters and assumptions (e.g., shift durations, truck capacities) [inferred].
## Open questions
- How well do the proposed quantum-classical hybrid models perform when run on real quantum hardware that exhibits noise and limited qubit counts?
- How does performance scale with problem dimensionality (number of input features) and with the number of qubits in the VQC?
- Which encoding/embedding schemes (beyond the Pauli rotation used) are most appropriate for diﬀerent kinds of time-series data?
- How do hybrid architectures perform for multi-step forecasting (longer horizons) rather than only one-step-ahead prediction?
- What are the best practices for designing VQC depth and ansatz structure to avoid barren plateaus while capturing long-term dependencies?
- Can the hybrid models be made robust to concept drift and nonstationary time-series beyond the tested datasets?
- Why did QNN outperform classical models on some tasks and not on others — what task/data properties predict where quantum models help?
- What is the computational and energy trade-oﬀ (wall-clock time, classical vs quantum resources) for quantum hybrids vs classical deep-learning baselines at larger scales?
- How can a provably complete algorithm for MAPF with large agents (MAPF-LA) be designed that handles all interfering-agent cases, including the third case where returning moved agents by reversal is impossible [raised explicitly]?
- What is the correct strategy to choose planning order in MAPF-LA so that previously-incomplete routines become complete and robust across topologies and agent sizes [raised explicitly]?
- Why do advanced reuse strategies in Anytime ECBS sometimes perform worse than naive restart strategies? How much does retaining stale hfocal values and irrelevant constraints contribute to this phenomenon and how to mitigate it [raised explicitly]?
- How to design incremental/hybrid high-performance low-level planners (e.g., for ECBS) that allow efficient reuse between iterations without negative side-eﬀects on search quality [raised explicitly]?
- How can WPB Boolean function search scale to larger n (e.g., n = 16)? Which algorithmic and engineering strategies (Fast Walsh-Hadamard, alternative encodings, specialized initialization) are most promising to enable this [raised explicitly and inferred]?
- What are efficient strategies for generating diverse and useful initial populations for WPB search that leverage WPB decomposition to enable search in higher dimensions [raised explicitly]?
- Can the Fast Walsh-Hadamard Transform be integrated into the non-linearity calculation for WPB search to make the approach tractable for n > 8 [inferred]?
- How to reliably detect small and rare object classes (e.g., bubbles, specularities) in endoscopic imagery? Which data-augmentation, architecture, or loss-function strategies can improve detection and segmentation for small objects [stated]?
- How to best combine multiple source-code similarity measures automatically (Mixmetric) for cross-language and low-resource conditions? Which additional features (semantic analysis, program structure) would most improve Mixmetric [raised explicitly]?
- How to interpret and explain the varying behavior of anytime algorithms on different instances—i.e., can we predict when search-reuse will help vs. when restart is better [stated/inferred]?
- How to robustly fix and harden important community code (e.g., IWFG 1.01) and ensure that implementations are resilient to indifferent (identical) solutions and other edge cases [stated]?
- What theoretical analyses can bound the practical performance (or identify worst-case constructs) for algorithms like IWFG and Anytime ECBS when used on many-objective problems [inferred]?
- In practical port/logistics models, how sensitive are optimal truck allocations and shift schedules to realistic uncertainties (traffic peaks, breaks, equipment failures), and how to robustify schedules against such variability [stated/inferred]?

**Future work:**
