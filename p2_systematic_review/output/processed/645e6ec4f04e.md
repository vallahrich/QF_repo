## Abstract summary
This preprint extends digitized counterdiabatic quantum computing to high-dimensional systems (qutrits), deriving qutrit Hamiltonian encodings and approximate counterdiabatic drivings for quadratic optimization problems. The authors benchmark qutrit-based algorithms on multiway number partitioning, max 3-cut, and portfolio optimization (1000 random instances), reporting up to ~90x improvement in solution quality versus qubit encodings and discussing experimental feasibility on trapped-ion and superconducting platforms.
## Methodology
The authors propose and numerically evaluate a high-dimensional digitized counterdiabatic quantum computing (HDCQC) methodology that encodes discrete optimization problems in qutrits (ternary digits) and accelerates adiabatic evolutions using an approximate adiabatic gauge potential (AGP). For each problem (three-part multiway number partitioning, max 3-cut, and Markowitz portfolio optimization) they: map classical discrete variables to trinary variables and then to qutrit operators to derive quadratic (one- and two-body) final Hamiltonians HF and simple local Hi initial Hamiltonians; construct the time-dependent Hamiltonian H(t) = (1-A(t))Hi + A(t)HF + A(t)A_x where A_x is the counterdiabatic term; approximate A_x by the nested-commutator expansion truncated at first order (l=1); digitize the adiabatic evolution into small time steps (Trotter-like product of short local evolutions) using a chosen schedule A(t) (a sin^2-based profile); and simulate unitary evolution for different total times T to compare qutrit (j=3) vs qubit (j=2) encodings. They evaluate performance via an end-of-evolution percentual energy error metric R (based on instantaneous mean energy vs ground energy and the problem energy gap), success probability P_j(T) (probability to land in any degenerate ground state) and the success-probability enhancement P3/2(T)=P3(T)/P2(T). Experiments comprise ensembles (1000 random instances per problem) across three time-regimes (short/medium/long, T={0.1 ω0, 1 ω0, 10 ω0}), plus targeted tests on market data for portfolio optimization; they also analyze instantaneous spectra of the adiabatic Hamiltonian to relate level crossings and gaps to performance differences between encodings.

**Algorithms used:** Digitized Counterdiabatic Quantum Computing (DCQC), Counterdiabatic Driving (Shortcut to Adiabaticity / STA), Adiabatic Quantum Computing (digitized/Trotterized)

**Experimental setup:** Numerical simulations of digitized adiabatic evolutions with an approximate AGP (nested-commutator expansion, order l=1) and Trotter-like time discretization. For each problem the authors ran ensembles of 1000 random instances, compared qutrit (j=3) encodings against qubit (j=2) encodings, and evaluated at total evolution times T in three regimes (short/medium/long: 0.1·ω0, 1·ω0, 10·ω0). For portfolio experiments they also used historical market data (yfinance) for a 6-asset set (AAPL, MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, TSLA, NFLX; 2020–2023) to compute mean returns and covariance matrices; other experiments used synthetic random inputs (random numbers, random 6-node graphs).

**Dataset:** Portfolio experiments used historical market data from yfinance for assets: Apple (AAPL), Microsoft (MSFT), Google/Alphabet (GOOGL), Amazon (AMZN), Tesla (TSLA), Netflix (NFLX) covering 2020–2023 (used to compute expected returns and covariance matrices). Other datasets were synthetic: 6-number sets sampled uniformly on [0,1] for multiway partitioning and random connectivity 6-node graphs for max 3-cut (1000 instances each).

## Findings
- [supported] Digitized counterdiabatic quantum computing (DCQA) extended to qutrits (high-dimensional systems) and applied to quadratic optimization problems.
- [supported] Numerical simulations (up to 1000 random instances per problem) show qutrit-based DCQA often outperforms qubit-based DCQA on three test problems: multiway number partitioning, max 3-cut, and portfolio optimization.
- [supported] For the max 3-cut problem the authors report very large mean success-probability enhancements when using qutrits versus qubits (examples: mean enhancements reported as 35.34, 30.41, 6.99 for three final-time regimes).
- [supported] For multiway number partitioning the reported mean enhancements are modest (examples: 1.31, 1.23, 1.07 for three final-time regimes) and a majority (≈75–85%) of random instances show qutrit improvement for short and medium times.
- [supported] For portfolio optimization the authors report mean qutrit-vs-qubit success-probability enhancements (examples: 3.56 at short time, 1.54 at medium time, 1.12 at long time) across random instances.
- [supported] The paper attributes performance gains to spectral properties: qutrit encodings often produce fewer level crossings and a more favorable low-energy level density during the adiabatic evolution, reducing diabatic transitions.
- [supported] The DCQA used an approximate adiabatic gauge potential (nested-commutator expansion, truncation level l = 1) in all qutrit vs qubit comparisons.
- [supported] The authors tested three evolution-time regimes (short/impulse, medium, long) and report that qutrit advantages are typically largest in short-time (nonadiabatic) regimes.
- [speculative] The authors claim that, without prior knowledge, it is generally better to encode optimization problems using qutrits (and possibly higher-dimensional qudits) instead of qubits.
- [speculative] The authors claim their high-dimensional DCQA is experimentally feasible at least in fully digital form on current platforms (trapped ions and superconducting circuits) because these platforms support qutrit gates and controlled-phase interactions.
- [speculative] The paper asserts that scaling and gate-count/error scaling for the fully digital qutrit implementation behave analogously to the qubit case (worst-case two-body gates scale with number of two-body Hamiltonian terms).
- [speculative] The authors suggest that extending to dimensions beyond qutrits (higher qudits) looks promising for improved codification and algorithm performance.

**Results summary:** This preprint extends digitized counterdiabatic quantum computing to qutrit encodings and evaluates performance on three quadratic optimization tasks (multiway number partitioning, max 3-cut, and Markowitz portfolio optimization) via numerical simulation. Across 1000 random instances per problem and three total-time regimes, qutrit-based DCQA frequently outperformed the qubit counterpart, with the largest gains observed for max 3-cut (mean enhancements up to tens of times and some instances showing ~90× improvement). Gains are attributed to more favorable spectral properties (fewer level crossings and improved low-energy structure) in the qutrit encodings. The authors argue the approach is implementable on current qutrit-capable platforms in a fully digital realization, but experimental realization of these specific algorithms is not reported.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The paper demonstrates via numerical simulation that qutrit-based DCQA can outperform qubit-based DCQA on selected small-instance optimization problems; however, it does not demonstrate practical quantum advantage over classical algorithms or real-device experimental advantage. Broad claims that high-dimensional encodings are generally superior and that the approach is experimentally feasible at scale remain speculative pending hardware demonstrations and larger-instance/benchmark comparisons versus classical methods.
## Limitations
- The counterdiabatic (CD) / adiabatic gauge potential (AGP) is computed using an approximate nested-commutator expansion (truncated, [ = 1) rather than the exact AGP; exact AGP is impractical to calculate (author-stated).
- Digitalization of adiabatic evolution requires long circuit depth which translates into low fidelity on NISQ devices; scalability of total adiabatic time is equivalent to circuit-depth scalability (author-stated).
- Performance strongly depends on spectral properties (gap size and density of level crossings); qutrit advantage is problem-dependent and not guaranteed for all instances (author-stated).
- Implementation discussion is limited to feasibility arguments (trapped ions, superconducting circuits) and does not include an experimental demonstration of the proposed HDCQC protocols (author-stated).
- Worst-case gate-resource scaling: fully digital implementation requires controlled-phase gates for two-body terms and the scaling of errors is dominated by the number of two-body gates (author-stated).
- [inferred] Numerical experiments are performed on small problem instances (e.g., six-node graphs and six-asset portfolios); scalability to larger, practically relevant problem sizes is untested.
- [inferred] The impact of experimental noise, decoherence, and realistic gate fidelities on the reported qutrit advantage is not quantified.
- [inferred] The chosen AGP approximation order ([ = 1) may limit performance; higher-order approximations may change results but were not explored.
- [inferred] Although qutrit encodings can reduce some level crossings, certain problem instances exhibited worse performance with qutrits (P3/2 < 1), indicating unpredictability in when qutrits help.
- [inferred] Resource overheads for encoding classical variables in base-3 (trinary) and the trade-offs versus alternative encodings (e.g., qubit encodings, hybrid encodings) are not fully characterized.
