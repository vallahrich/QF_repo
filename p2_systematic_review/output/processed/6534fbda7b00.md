## Abstract summary
This preprint proposes a hybrid quantum-classical framework (Quantum-Enhanced SAC) that integrates a variational quantum circuit into a Soft Actor-Critic agent to optimize commercial bank funding under CBDC-induced liquidity stress. The authors evaluate barren-plateau mitigation strategies, test the approach in a five-bank simulated network with realistic NISQ noise models and error mitigation (ZNE), and report modest funding-cost reductions versus classical DRL and MPC baselines while noting results are proof-of-concept and contingent on noise-mitigated or fault-tolerant quantum hardware.
## Methodology
The study implements a hybrid quantum-classical Soft Actor-Critic (QSAC) agent to manage simulated CBDC-induced liquidity stress in a custom Gymnasium multi-bank environment. The actor is a purely classical neural network (input: 65-dim state, hidden layers [128,64,32], output: 16-dim action), while the critic is hybrid: classical preprocessing layers [64,32], a 4-qubit, 6-layer Hardware-Efficient Variational Quantum Circuit (VQC) with 28 trainable quantum parameters, and classical readout layers [16,1]. The reward penalizes funding cost and regulatory breaches (R_t = -FundingCost_t - lambda1 * max(0, LCR_target - LCR_t) - lambda2 * TransactionCost, with lambda1=1000, lambda2=0.1). VQC trainability is addressed with identity-block initialization, layerwise (progressive) training, and local cost observables to mitigate barren plateau effects. Training was conducted over 500,000 timesteps in simulation. The authors calibrate a NISQ noise model to IBM’s ibm_nairobi device (single- and two-qubit error rates, T1/T2, readout error) and apply Zero Noise Extrapolation (ZNE) for error mitigation. Extensive ablation studies compare the VQC critic to classical critics matched by parameter count and expressibility, and benchmarks include multiple classical SAC variants, Stochastic and Robust Model Predictive Control (SMPC/RMPC), and a rule-based policy. Evaluation metrics include average funding cost (bps) with confidence intervals, LCR breaches and volatility, systemic risk metrics (network default probability, contagion cascade size, Systemic Risk Index), and statistical significance tests (t-tests, Cohen’s d).

**Algorithms used:** Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), Variational Quantum Circuit (VQC) with Hardware-Efficient Ansatz, Zero Noise Extrapolation (ZNE), Layer-wise (progressive) training, Identity-block initialization, Stochastic Model Predictive Control (SMPC), Robust Model Predictive Control (RMPC)
**Frameworks:** PennyLane, PyTorch, Gymnasium, CasADi, IPOPT, Qiskit (referenced/calibration)

**Experimental setup:** Simulation-only multi-agent environment (custom Gymnasium) of five heterogeneous commercial banks. QSAC trained for 500,000 timesteps on a noise-free simulator and under calibrated NISQ noise (IBM ibm_nairobi). Noise mitigation via Zero Noise Extrapolation applied in noisy experiments.

**Dataset:** Custom simulated multi-bank environment (Gymnasium) representing five heterogeneous banks (B1..B5) with aggregated balance-sheet features, state vector of 65 dimensions per bank, and scenario-based stress inputs (four stress scenarios: A-D). No external market or historical financial dataset used.

### Process
1) Build a 5-bank Gymnasium environment with heterogeneous bank specifications and scenario generators. 2) Instantiate QSAC: classical actor (65->[128,64,32]->16) and hybrid critic (65->[64,32] classical preprocessing -> VQC(4 qubits, 6 layers, 28 quantum params) -> [16,1] readout). 3) Initialize VQC using identity-block initialization and train layers progressively (layer-wise training); use local cost observables. 4) Train agent with SAC loop (policy evaluation/improvement, entropy regularization, temperature adaptation) for 500,000 timesteps in a noise-free simulator. 5) Calibrate NISQ noise model to IBM ibm_nairobi (single/two-qubit error rates, T1/T2, readout error); run noisy experiments and apply Zero Noise Extrapolation (ZNE) mitigation. 6) Perform ablation studies: compare against classical critics matched on parameter count (28 params), expressibility-matched classical (64 params), full classical critic (2,156 params), and against SMPC/RMPC and rule-based baselines. 7) Evaluate across four stress scenarios and compute metrics (funding cost bps, LCR breaches, systemic risk metrics). 8) Report statistical tests (t-tests, confidence intervals) and gradient-variance diagnostics over training.

### Output

Baselines: classical SAC variants, SMPC, RMPC, rule-based policy. Noise-sensitivity outputs: noise-free, ZNE-mitigated, and unmitigated noisy performance.

## Findings

- [supported] The paper reports an 8.1% reduction in average funding cost (95% CI: [6.2%, 9.9%]) for QSAC versus classical DRL baselines, and a 3.2% reduction (95% CI: [1.1%, 5.3%]) versus Model Predictive Control baselines.
- [supported] Ablation studies reported that a small VQC (28 quantum parameters) outperformed a classical neural network with the same parameter count and performed comparably to larger classical networks in this simulated task.
- [supported] Empirical gradient-variance measurements across the 500,000 timestep training run indicate non-vanishing gradients for the 4-qubit, 6-layer Hardware-Efficient Ansatz under the employed initialization and training procedure (identity block initialization + layerwise training).

**Results summary:** The preprint presents a simulation study of a hybrid Quantum-Enhanced Soft Actor-Critic (QSAC) agent for CBDC-induced liquidity management across a five-bank network. Using a 4-qubit, 6-layer variational quantum circuit in the critic, the QSAC agent attains lower average funding costs and eliminates LCR breaches in out-of-sample simulation versus several classical DRL and MPC baselines. Ablations indicate a modest but consistent benefit from the VQC versus classical networks with matched parameters. The authors analyze VQC trainability (empirical gradient variances across a 500k-step run), present a theoretical bound on gradients for the chosen ansatz, and evaluate realistic NISQ noise models with Zero Noise Extrapolation, finding partial mitigation of noise effects. Results are explicitly framed as proof-of-concept and contingent on noise-mitigated or future fault-tolerant hardware and on the stylized nature of the simulated environment.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The authors report a modest, reproducible performance benefit for a small VQC-equipped critic over classical baselines in simulation (including ablations), but the claimed advantage is contingent on noise-free or effectively noise-mitigated execution, limited to a 4-qubit VQC in a stylized environment, and not demonstrated on physical fault-tolerant hardware; therefore any broader quantum advantage remains speculative.
## Limitations
- Proof-of-concept hybrid quantum-classical framework tested exclusively in simulation (no real-hardware or live-market deployment).
- Results contingent on noise-free or noise-mitigated quantum hardware that is not yet commercially available at scale.
- Stylized multi-bank environment: aggregated balance-sheet representation that does not capture the full complexity and heterogeneity of real interbank markets.
- VQC expressibility limited to 4 qubits due to NISQ constraints (small quantum model size).
- Economic estimates are sensitivity-based projections with wide confidence intervals and are not empirically validated.
- Hardware limitations: current quantum noise and decoherence constrain practical performance; fault-tolerant quantum computers would be required for full realization.
- Observed quantum performance advantage is modest and appears contingent on noise-free or effectively noise-mitigated execution (performance degrades with realistic gate error rates).
- Noise model dependence: experiments and mitigation (ZNE) are calibrated to a specific IBM processor (ibm_nairobi), so results depend on noise-model fidelity and calibration.
- [inferred] Scalability of mitigation and training strategies (identity block initialization, layer-wise training, local cost functions) to larger qubit counts and deeper circuits is unproven.
- [inferred] Theoretical barren-plateau analysis and gradient-variance bounds are specific to the 4-qubit, 6-layer HEA; applicability to larger systems or different ansätze is not established.
- [inferred] Potential overfitting or sensitivity to simulation scenario design and hyperparameters — generalization to unseen real-world stress patterns is uncertain.
- [inferred] Limited statistical detail about experimental replication (number of independent seeds / hardware runs) risks uncertainty in robustness and reproducibility until code/data are released.
## Open questions
- Can the QSAC agent maintain its reported performance advantage when executed on real quantum hardware (with realistic noise) rather than noise-mitigated simulators?
- How well do mitigation techniques such as Zero Noise Extrapolation scale to larger circuits, different hardware backends, and production workloads?
- How does the VQC-based critic scale with increasing qubit counts and circuit depth in terms of trainability (barren plateaus), expressibility, and runtime cost?
- To what extent are the reported gains attributable to genuine quantum effects versus architecture choices, classical preprocessing, or hyperparameter tuning?
- How generalizable are results from the five-bank, aggregated simulation to real banking systems with many more institutions and full balance-sheet detail?
- How sensitive are the results to the calibration of the noise model and to the specific stress scenarios and parameterizations used in the simulator?
- What are the computational resource, latency, and operational requirements to integrate a QSAC-like agent into real-time bank liquidity management systems?
- How would regulators and risk managers accept or validate decisions influenced by quantum components — can Q-XAI outputs be made sufficiently interpretable and auditable?
- What are the sample efficiency and convergence properties of QSAC relative to classical DRL and MPC baselines in larger, more realistic environments?
- How robust is the QSAC agent to adversarial market behavior, model misspecification, or unmodeled systemic shocks?
- Can the modest observed cost reductions (e.g., 8.1% vs classical SAC, 3.2% vs MPC) be replicated and economically validated on empirical bank data?
- How would network size, more complex interbank link structures, and heterogeneous behavioral models affect systemic risk metrics and the agent's performance?
