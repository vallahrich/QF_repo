## Abstract summary
The paper introduces the use of intermediate qutrits to decompose Toffoli gates and implement key quantum arithmetic operations (addition/subtraction, multiplication/division, square root, exponential, arcsine) without ancilla or T gates, yielding lower gate counts and reduced circuit depth. It applies these improvements to quantum derivative pricing, showing substantially reduced resource estimates and a higher probability of success despite additional qutrit-related noise.
## Methodology
The authors propose replacing ancilla-based Toffoli decompositions with an intermediate-qutrit decomposition to synthesize quantum arithmetic primitives (addition, subtraction, multiplication/division, square root, exponential, arcsine) used in derivative-pricing algorithms. They (i) adopt fixed-point reversible arithmetic constructions from prior work (carry-lookahead adder, controlled-addition-based multiplication, non-restoring square root, parallel piecewise polynomial approximation for smooth functions) and systematically replace Toffoli gates by a qutrit-based decomposition (three generalized ternary CNOTs, depth 3) so that no T gates or additional ancilla are required; (ii) produce analytical resource-estimation formulas for gate counts and circuit depth expressed in ternary CNOTs and depths for each arithmetic routine; (iii) embed these arithmetic improvements into the derivative-pricing pipeline that uses re-parameterization (VQE-style Gaussian state preparation and affine transforms to obtain log-returns/prices), computes δ(ω)=arcsin(√f(ω)) via piecewise polynomial evaluation and square-root circuits, rotates ˜f into an ancilla amplitude and employs iterative quantum amplitude estimation (IQAE) to estimate expected payoff; (iv) evaluate error effects by modelling gate errors with Kraus/depolarizing channels and amplitude-damping (relaxation) for idle errors, assuming realistic error rates (one-qubit p1≈1e-4, two-qubit p2≈1e-2) and T1 times (qubit T1≈100 μs, qutrit T1≈30 μs), and compute probability-of-success (no-error) as the product of individual gate-no-error probabilities times an idle-error factor; (v) demonstrate correctness of small example circuits (4-bit adder, a 5×3 multiplier) and simulate a multiplier on Google Colab (code provided), and compare resource totals and success probabilities with prior Toffoli/ancilla/T-gate based approaches for a benchmark derivative-pricing instance (3 underlyings, 5 payment dates, 20 barrier dates).

**Algorithms used:** Intermediate-qutrit Toffoli decomposition (ternary intermediate states), Quantum carry-lookahead adder (Draper et al.), Controlled-addition-based fixed-point multiplication (Takahashi et al.), Non-restoring square root quantum circuit (Muñoz Coreas & Thapliyal), Parallel piecewise-polynomial function approximation (for exp / arcsin), Variational Gaussian state preparation (VQE-style) for re-parameterization, Iterative Quantum Amplitude Estimation (IQAE)
**Frameworks:** Google Colab (simulation platform)

**Experimental setup:** Simulations of small circuits (e.g., 4-bit adder; a 5×3 multiplier) were executed on Google Colab (simulator details not specified). Error modelling was analytical/simulated using Kraus-operator based depolarizing noise for gate errors and amplitude-damping for relaxation. Assumed noise parameters: one-qubit error p1≈1e-4, two-qubit / two-qutrit gate error p2≈1e-2; relaxation times T1_qubit≈100 μs, T1_qutrit≈30 μs. Resource estimates and probability-of-success curves were computed for Toffoli counts up to ~30 and for a benchmark derivative-pricing case.

**Results summary:** The paper proposes using intermediate qutrit (ternary) levels to decompose Toffoli gates and synthesize common quantum arithmetic primitives (addition, multiplication, square root, exponential, arcsine) without ancilla and without T gates. ## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The paper presents theoretical/simulation-based resource and noise-model analyses showing reduced gate counts, circuit depth and modeled error rates when using intermediate qutrits for arithmetic primitives; however, there is no experimental demonstration on hardware of end-to-end quantum advantage for derivative pricing. The claimed improvements are therefore speculative and contingent on qutrit-capable hardware with appropriate noise characteristics.
## Limitations
- Toffoli decomposition with intermediate qutrit requires access to higher energy levels, making the design prone to additional errors (author-stated).
- Use of higher-dimensional (qutrit) gates increases susceptibility to relaxation / decoherence (shorter T1) and gate errors; qutrit gates currently have higher noise characteristics (author-stated).
- Validation is limited by availability of qudit hardware and limited experimental data for qutrit gate fidelities and relaxation times (author-stated).
- [inferred] The analysis depends on assumed hardware parameters (e.g., p1=1e-4, p2=1e-2, qubit T1 ≃100 μs, qutrit T1 ≃30 μs); results may change with different, more realistic device models.
- [inferred] The work reports resources in terms of ternary CNOT counts and sets T-cost/T-depth to zero, which may not capture full overheads when integrating into fault-tolerant, surface-code-based architectures that are typically expressed in T-counts/T-depths.
- [inferred] No experimental end-to-end demonstration of the derivative-pricing pipeline on physical qudit hardware; conclusions are based on analytic resource counts and simulations.
- [inferred] Integration with quantum error correction and logical qubit/qudit encodings (and associated overheads) is not addressed.
- [inferred] Practical control, calibration, measurement, and readout overheads for temporarily using qutrit levels (|2⟩) within predominantly qubit systems are not analyzed.
- [inferred] Potential trade-offs between reduced gate count/depth and increased per-gate error for qutrit operations depend on hardware-specific fidelities; generalizability of the observed improvements is unclear.

- How do intermediate-qutrit methods interact with state-preparation techniques used in derivative pricing (e.g., re-parameterization/Gaussian loading) and with amplitude estimation subroutines in a full implementation?
- What is the experimentally observed net effect (in probability of success / error rates) of using qutrit-based decompositions on available qudit-capable hardware?
- What are the implications of the intermediate-qutrit approach for error-thresholds and scalability of quantum finance applications?
