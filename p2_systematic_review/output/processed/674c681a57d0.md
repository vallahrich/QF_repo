## Abstract summary
This technical report updates a prior review on quantum computing methods for derivatives pricing and Value-at-Risk (VaR), focusing on advances since the previous deliverable. It surveys developments in Quantum Accelerated Monte Carlo (QAMC) — including state initialization and amplitude estimation techniques — quantum PDE solvers, and alternate approaches (quantum-inspired financial models and quantum reinforcement learning), with emphasis on NISQ-era implementability and the dominant impact of noise. The authors conclude there are no major breakthroughs since the prior review, but note continued consolidation of theory and growing work to bridge theory and noisy hardware.

## Findings
- [speculative] Quantum Accelerated Monte Carlo (QAMC) promises a quadratic theoretical speedup over Classical Monte Carlo (CMC) for pricing and VaR tasks.
- [supported] Multiple Amplitude Estimation (AE) variants (MLAE, IQAE, QoPrime, RQAE, FasterAE, AdaptQAE, etc.) have been developed since the prior review; these provide different trade-offs in query complexity, depth, and suitability for NISQ hardware and some have been empirically implemented or benchmarked.
- [supported] Noise is the primary limiting factor on current NISQ devices: experiments and hardware studies reported in the literature show noise prevents realizing theoretical speedups in practice.
- [speculative] Accounting for all practical error sources (discretization, state-preparation error, AE algorithm error, readout) can negate the ideal quadratic advantage of QAMC in realistic settings (per theoretical analyses such as Li & Neufeld (2023)).
- [supported] State preparation (initialization of probability distributions and payoffs) remains a central bottleneck; many methods (Grover-Rudolph variants, QSVT-based, low-rank/SVD, tree/divide-and-conquer, MPS, variational, quantum walk based) exist but none satisfy all desired NISQ-era properties (efficiency, robustness, controllable precision, determinism, small ancilla usage).
- [supported] Variational/state-ansatz approaches are promising for NISQ because they can trade precision for lower circuit depth; however, they face practical issues like training difficulties (local minima, barren plateaus), potential uncontrolled relative phases, and scalability limits demonstrated in experiments.
- [speculative] Quantum algorithms solving PDE formulations of pricing can offer polynomial or even exponential speedups theoretically, but those speedups are typically limited to special, structured cases and face practical obstacles (state readout, exponentially small amplitudes, and model restrictions).
- [supported] Empirical and simulation studies (e.g., benchmarking on IBM/Honeywell devices, and empirical noise-threshold studies) quantify how much noise/two-qubit error rates need to improve before AE-based advantages emerge; several works show current hardware is orders of magnitude away from that threshold.
- [supported] There is an active taxonomy of AE approaches: parallel (batch schedules like MLSA/MLAE variants), iterative (IQAE and improvements), and generalized-Grover/QSP/engineered-likelihood approaches — each class has different suitability for NISQ vs. asymptotic guarantees.
- [speculative] Overhead from quantum error correction in a fault-tolerant regime may render the asymptotic quadratic speedup impractical for meaningful financial instance sizes (argument presented as a long-term caveat).
- [supported] The literature since the previous review is largely incremental (improvements, refinements, noise-aware adaptations) rather than containing new disruptive breakthroughs for pricing/VaR quantum algorithms.
- [speculative] Conceptual work exploring 'quantum markets' and quantum-native financial asset models exists (e.g., Bao & Rebentrost), but these are theoretical frameworks and not empirically validated market models.

**Results summary:** This technical review surveys advances since the prior deliverable across three main areas: Quantum Accelerated Monte Carlo (QAMC) and its building blocks (state preparation and Amplitude Estimation), quantum algorithms for solving PDE-based pricing models, and alternative/quantum-native finance proposals. The dominant theme is that theoretical quantum advantages (notably a quadratic speedup for Monte Carlo via amplitude estimation) remain intact on paper, but practical realization is hampered by state-preparation cost, circuit depth, and, above all, noise on NISQ hardware. A broad set of AE variants and state-loading methods have been proposed, many optimized for lower depth or noise robustness; variational circuits and MPS approximations are promising for NISQ, yet they suffer from scalability and training issues. PDE-based quantum approaches may offer larger asymptotic gains but are heavily constrained to special instances and face readout/extraction bottlenecks. Overall, recent work is incremental and focused on bridging theory and NISQ practice rather than demonstrating clear empirical quantum advantage for realistic financial workloads.

**Performance claims:**
- CMC: number of oracle calls N_A = O(1 / ϵ_a^2) for amplitude estimation (classical sampling).
- Quantum Phase Estimation / ideal AE: N_A = O(1 / ϵ_a) (quadratic improvement over CMC) (Brassard et al., 2002).
- MLAE-LIS: N_A = O(1 / ϵ_a^(4/3)) (Suzuki et al., 2020).
- MLAE-EIS: N_A = O(1 / ϵ_a) (Suzuki et al., 2020, exponential schedule asymptotic).
- PLAE (QoPrime family): N_A = O(1 / ϵ_a^(1+β)), depth d = O(1 / ϵ_a^(1−β)) (Giurgica-Tiron et al., 2022) — trade-off parameter β.
- Improved MLAE (Callison & Browne, 2022): N_A = O((1 / ϵ_a) (1/d) log(1/δ)), with d = 2^{q−2} (specifics tied to depth-control parameter q).
- IQAE rigorous bound (Grinko et al., 2021): N_A < 50 / ϵ_a * log(2/δ * log2(π/(4ϵ_a))).
- Modified IQAE (Fukuzawa et al., 2023): N_A < 123 / ϵ_a * log(6/δ) (improved constants).
- FasterAE (Nakaji, 2020): N_A < 4.1×10^3 / ϵ_a * log(4/δ * log2(2π/(3ϵ_a))) (constant-heavy bound).
- AdaptiveAE (Zhao et al., 2022): N_A < (1/ϵ_a) * log(π^2 (T+1) / (3δ)), T = ceiling(log(π/(K ϵ_a)) / log K) (adaptive depth scheduling).
- RQAE (Manzano et al., 2022): N_A < 70π/(6 arcsin(ϵ_a)+1) * log(3.3/δ * log2(π/(2 arcsin(2ϵ_a)))) (real-amplitude variant bound).
- QCoin-style bounds (Abrams & Williams, 1999): N_A = O( (1/a)^(1/(k)) / ϵ_a log(1/δ) ) under particular amplitude regimes and parameters.
## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The report reiterates the theoretical quadratic advantage of AE/QAMC frameworks, but emphasizes that (a) state-preparation cost, depth constraints, and noise in NISQ devices prevent realizing that advantage today and (b) accounting for practical errors and error-correction overhead can eliminate the expected speedup for realistic problem sizes. No demonstrated end-to-end empirical quantum advantage for realistic pricing or VaR workloads is reported; thus the claimed advantage remains theoretical/speculative.
## Limitations
- High levels of noise in current NISQ hardware are the main limiting factor for implementing AE/QAMC algorithms in practice.
- Limited qubit counts and restricted connectivity constrain many initialization and AE methods (auxiliary qubit requirements make some approaches impractical).
- Circuit depth constraints on NISQ devices prevent use of deep Grover/amplification sequences; depth increases error sensitivity.
- Many state-preparation (initialization) methods require O(2^n) gates or large classical preprocessing (e.g., O(n 2^n)), making them infeasible at scale.
- Unitary synthesis approaches are limited to cases where the evolution is unitary; Fokker-Planck / non-unitary dynamics limit applicability.
- Variational state-preparation and qGAN methods suffer from training challenges (local minima, barren plateaus) and often require multiple restarts and heavy classical optimization.
- Approximated-loading methods trade fidelity for fewer gates, but phase errors and uncontrolled relative phases can invalidate downstream algorithms.
- AE algorithms (MLAE and variants) show pathological 'critical points' or exceptional amplitude values that lead to biased estimators and irreducible error floors.
- Noise (both coherent and incoherent) can change the effective likelihoods used in MLAE/IQAE, removing the theoretical quadratic advantage under realistic noise models.
- Rigorous analyses that include discretization, approximation and implementation errors (e.g., Li & Neufeld) indicate the ideal quadratic speedup may not hold when all error sources are considered.
- Error-correction overheads for fault-tolerant operation can negate expected quadratic QAMC speedups for practically sized financial instances.
- Extracting a specific price/amplitude from high-dimensional quantum PDE solutions is hard because target amplitudes become exponentially small, requiring expensive readout.
- Many proposed algorithms assume unrealistic oracles / quantum access to data (e.g., direct quantum access to market prices or payoff matrices) which may not be available in practice.
- Some proposed initialization and AE methods require many controlled operations or ancilla/garbage qubits, increasing resource and noise burden.
- Empirical evidence is limited: few end-to-end, market-calibrated demonstrations exist; several proposals lack calibration/validation with real market data.
- [inferred] There is limited clarity on operational deployment constraints (integration with existing risk/stack pipelines, latency requirements, regulatory acceptance).
- [inferred] The classical overhead (preprocessing, compilation, parameter optimization) required by variational and some parallel state-prep methods may offset quantum gains in practice.
- [inferred] Energy and total-cost-of-ownership analyses are undeveloped, so claimed near-term benefits (e.g., energy efficiency) are speculative without end-to-end studies.
## Open questions
- Can QAMC/AE achieve a true quadratic runtime advantage in practical financial settings once discretization, state-preparation, and implementation errors are fully accounted for?
- What are the concrete hardware thresholds (noise rates per gate, number of qubits, coherence times, connectivity) required to observe quantum advantage for pricing and VaR?
- How can state-preparation methods be designed that simultaneously satisfy efficiency, robustness to noise, controllable precision, determinism (no post-selection), phase correctness, and low ancilla usage?
- What are the best strategies to mitigate coherent noise and other realistic noise sources in AE (e.g., via randomized compiling, engineered likelihood functions, or tailored reflections)?
- How can critical-point/bias phenomena in MLAE-like algorithms be diagnosed and fully mitigated in practice?
- Which AE variant (MLAE, IQAE, RQAE, AdaptQAE, generalized-Grover/QSP approaches) is most suitable for NISQ-era financial workloads under realistic noise and resource constraints?
- How to efficiently and scalably extract required observables or specific amplitudes from quantum PDE solvers in high dimensions without exponential overhead?
- Can variational imaginary-time evolution / variational PDE solvers be made robust and precise enough for derivative pricing on small quantum devices?
- How should quantum financial models (e.g., quantum asset models or quantum market measures) be calibrated to real market data, and do they offer empirical improvements over classical models?
- What are practical methods to estimate multiple parameters or nuisance noise parameters jointly with amplitude estimation (multi-parameter AE) in a robust way?
- To what extent will quantum error correction, when implemented, eliminate or reduce the relative speed advantage for Monte Carlo-style quantum algorithms due to overheads?
- What are realistic end-to-end performance comparisons (including classical pre/post-processing) between quantum approaches and optimized classical methods for industry-scale pricing and VaR?
- How can one certify that variational state-preparation circuits do not introduce harmful relative phases without full tomography?
- What are the limits of MPS-based loading and other low-entanglement approximations as problem size and non-smoothness increase?

- Explore the implications of error-correction overhead on achievable speedups and identify problem regimes where fault-tolerant quantum advantage remains plausible.
