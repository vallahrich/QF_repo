## Abstract summary
This preprint introduces a hybrid quantum–classical Seq2Seq autoencoder (QLSTM) that embeds a depth-1 variational quantum circuit into LSTM gates to learn compact two-dimensional temporal embeddings from quarterly weekly returns. The embeddings are converted into an RBF similarity kernel to drive two portfolio strategies (RBF-DivMom and RBF-Graph); rolling backtests on S&P 500 constituents (2022–2025) show improved latent geometry stability and superior risk-adjusted performance compared with a classical LSTM baseline. The work demonstrates the practical feasibility of shallow VQCs for NISQ-era sequence modeling and interpretable, kernel-based allocation.
## Methodology
The authors propose a hybrid quantum–classical Seq2Seq pipeline for learning compact temporal embeddings from financial time series and using those embeddings for RBF-kernel-based portfolio allocation. Each stock is represented by a single-quarter sequence of weekly returns (≈12–13 observations). A QLSTM encoder maps each sequence into a two-dimensional latent vector; the QLSTM augments each LSTM gate with a depth-1 variational quantum circuit (VQC): the concatenated input and previous hidden state vt = [xt; ht-1] is compressed to a q-dimensional vector, encoded on a q-qubit register, passed through a fixed-depth (depth-1) entangling ansatz, and read out via Pauli-Z expectation values which are projected back before the sigmoid/tanh nonlinearity. A classical autoregressive decoder reconstructs the input sequence using teacher forcing (with probability pTF). The reconstruction loss is mean squared error. Models are trained on a rolling quarterly design: for each quarter, the QLSTM Seq2Seq is trained on the prior 12-month window, then the encoder is frozen and used to embed out-of-sample stocks for the next quarter. Pairwise Euclidean distances in the learned 2D latent space are converted into an RBF kernel K_mn = exp(-||h_m - h_n||^2/(2σ^2)) with σ set to the median pairwise distance. Two downstream allocation strategies consume this kernel: (1) RBF-DivMom — a discrete momentum-based selector regularized by a kernel-similarity penalty λ (grid searched; representative λ=0.15); (2) RBF-Graph — a continuous, graph-regularized optimizer using centrality in the RBF kernel. The pipeline is evaluated via 14 rolling windows from 2022Q2–2025Q2, reporting per-quarter and compounded metrics (CAGR, volatility, Sharpe ratio, max drawdown) and visualizing latent geometry and cluster coherence.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum LSTM (QLSTM) with embedded Variational Quantum Circuits (VQC), Seq2Seq (encoder-decoder) autoregressive reconstruction, Variational Quantum Circuit (depth-1 entangling ansatz, Pauli-Z expectation readout), Radial Basis Function (RBF) kernel (σ = median pairwise distance), RBF-DivMom (discrete momentum selector with RBF similarity penalty, λ), RBF-Graph (continuous graph-regularized optimizer using RBF kernel), Classical LSTM baseline

**Dataset:** Weekly returns of S&P 500 constituents from 2022 to 2025, organized into single-quarter sequences (≈12–13 weekly return observations per stock). Rolling quarterly training/evaluation windows were used (train on prior 12 months, test on the subsequent quarter).

## Limitations
- Authors do not claim quantum advantage; results are presented as improvements from a shallow hybrid augmentation rather than a provable quantum speedup or supremacy.
- Circuit design constrained to depth-1 VQCs for NISQ feasibility, limiting exploration of deeper or more expressive quantum circuits.
- Evaluation uses a single classical baseline (LSTM) rather than stronger contemporary sequence models (e.g., transformers or contrastive/self-supervised methods).
- Encoder projects to a two-dimensional latent manifold — a strong design choice that may limit representational capacity for more complex dynamics.
- Model retraining each quarter (training from scratch per window) may impose practical computational/resource costs for production deployment.
- The RBF kernel uses sigma set to median pairwise distance — a heuristic choice that may be sensitive and not fully validated across regimes.
- Backtests and reported results do not account for trading frictions (transaction costs, slippage, market impact) or detailed portfolio implementation constraints.
- Experiments are limited to S&P 500 weekly returns (2022–2025) — a single dataset and relatively short horizon for generalization claims.
- No ablation studies reported on quantum-layer hyperparameters (number of qubits, encoding scheme, ansatz structure) or how much each component contributes to gains.
