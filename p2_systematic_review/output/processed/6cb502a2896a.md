## Abstract summary
The paper proposes a continuous-variable (CV) quantum algorithm for solving one-dimensional polynomial ordinary differential equations by using the Koopman–von Neumann formalism to map classical dynamics to unitary evolution in an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space. Instead of relying on HHL-based linear-system approaches, the authors compile sequences of Gaussian and non-Gaussian CV gates to approximate the KvN Hamiltonian evolution, argue the method is naturally suited to evolving initial probability distributions (IDP) rather than single initial conditions (IVP), and identify open challenges in Trotter error analysis and complexity characterization.
## Methodology
This is a theoretical / algorithmic study that proposes a continuous-variable (CV) quantum algorithm to solve one-dimensional polynomial ordinary differential equations (ODEs) by mapping the ODE into a unitary Hamiltonian evolution via the Koopman–von Neumann (KvN) formalism. The methodology constructs the KvN Hamiltonian H_F = (1/2)(p F(q) + F(q) p) for polynomial F and then compiles an approximate time-evolution operator using a Trotterisation strategy. The authors decompose the KvN Hamiltonian into monomial generator terms {p, q^k} and provide a recursive commutator-based subroutine (the “Mono” routine) to synthesize arbitrary {p, q^k} evolutions from a native gate set (displacement, squeezing, quadratic, cubic, beam-splitter). A higher-level routine (“Poly”) Trotterises the polynomial Hamiltonian by calling the monomial subroutine per polynomial term. Initial-state preparation is discussed in CV language: approximate position eigenstates are realized as finitely squeezed-and-displaced Gaussian states; the required squeezing is related to desired accuracy via an upper bound on the Lyapunov exponent. Output is obtained by homodyne (q-quadrature) measurement to sample from the evolved distribution. The paper also introduces a conceptual approach to bounding errors in infinite-dimensional CV systems by proposing an energy-constrained circle norm (ECCN) to replace unbounded operator norms in Trotter error estimates and highlights that a full Trotter-error analysis and multi-dimensional extension remain open work. The methodology emphasizes solving the initial distribution problem (IDP) — evolving an initial probability distribution — rather than a single initial-value problem (IVP).

**Algorithms used:** Koopman–von Neumann (KvN) mapping, Trotterisation (Trotter–Suzuki style decomposition), Recursive commutator decomposition (Mono subroutine), Polynomial KvN decomposition (Poly subroutine), Homodyne sampling (q-quadrature measurement)
**Frameworks:** Continuous-variable (CV) quantum computing, Koopman–von Neumann formalism

## Findings
- [supported] The Koopman–von Neumann (KvN) formalism maps (possibly nonlinear) ODE dynamics into a unitary Hamiltonian evolution in an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space, and the expectation value of the position operator follows the ODE trajectory.
- [speculative] A continuous-variable (CV) quantum algorithm can be compiled (as a sequence of Gaussian and non-Gaussian gates) to approximate the KvN evolution for one-dimensional polynomial ODEs without truncating the Hilbert space.
- [speculative] Working directly in a CV (infinite-dimensional) framework avoids the finite-Hilbert-space truncation used in prior qubit-based KvN approaches, but introduces issues related to unbounded operators and Trotter error.
- [speculative] The proposed CV algorithm is intrinsically better suited to solve an initial distribution problem (IDP) — i.e., evolving a probability distribution — than a point initial-value problem (IVP), and can be used to sample the evolved distribution (e.g., to identify attractors or derive Lyapunov exponents).
- [speculative] Position eigenstates (nonphysical) can be approximated by finitely squeezed states; to achieve accuracy ε after evolution time t when the system has Lyapunov exponent upper bound λ, the required squeezing r must satisfy r > λ t + log(ε).
- [speculative] A recursive monomial decomposition (using commutator identities and Trotterisation) can be used to approximate generators of the form {p, q^k} with native CV gates (including cubic gates), forming the building blocks to approximate the full KvN Hamiltonian.
- [speculative] For a one-dimensional quadratic ODE the authors present a Trotter-style sequence whose combined error scales as O((t/M)^{5/2}) (for M overall repetitions), from which they infer the number of cubic gates scales as 2M = O(t ε^{-2/5}) (i.e., M = O(t ε^{-2/5})), up to unspecified prefactors coming from commutator norms.
- [supported] Non-Gaussian gates (e.g., cubic, Kerr) are difficult to implement in practice and are treated as 'expensive' resources in the CV model.
- [supported] Universality conditions for CV quantum computation (Gaussian + at least one non-Gaussian gate) are established in the literature and are referenced as background.
- [supported] There exist known limitations for HHL-based ODE solvers (state preparation, readout, sparsity, condition number) and a referenced lower bound (Ω(e^{δ t})) applies to quantum ODE algorithms that return amplitude-encoded solutions, implying exponential-in-time complexity in some settings.
- [speculative] A full, rigorous Trotter error theory for infinite-dimensional (CV) systems as required to complete a complexity analysis is not yet resolved and is left for future work; the authors propose an energy-constrained operator norm as a possible direction.

**Results summary:** The paper proposes a new approach to solve ordinary differential equations by combining the Koopman–von Neumann mapping (which makes classical ODE dynamics unitary) with continuous-variable quantum computing. Instead of truncating the infinite-dimensional KvN Hilbert space to qubits, the authors design recursive decompositions and Trotter-style sequences of Gaussian and non-Gaussian CV gates to approximate time evolution for one-dimensional polynomial ODEs. They argue the method is better framed as evolving an initial probability distribution (IDP) rather than a point initial condition (IVP), derive a squeezing bound to approximate position eigenstates, and present tentative error and gate-count scalings for quadratic ODEs. The work is primarily theoretical and exploratory: it provides algorithms and scaling relations but stops short of a full complexity proof because Trotter error analysis for unbounded operators in infinite-dimensional spaces remains unresolved.

**Performance claims:**
- For approximating a position eigenstate with a finitely squeezed state so that the final-state accuracy is ε after time t and with a Lyapunov exponent upper bound λ, the squeezing parameter must satisfy r > λ t + log(ε).
- For a one-dimensional quadratic ODE under the proposed Trotter scheme, the overall Trotter error is claimed to scale as O((t/M)^{5/2}) (where M is the number of overall repetitions), implying a required number of cubic gates scaling as 2M = O(t ε^{-2/5}) (i.e., M = O(t ε^{-2/5})) up to unspecified commutator-norm prefactors.
- Non-Gaussian gates (e.g., cubic) are considered 'expensive' and are the primary costly resource in the gate decomposition.
## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The paper suggests a path that might avoid some known limitations of HHL-based ODE solvers by using KvN + CV simulation without finite truncation, but provides no demonstrated runtime or provable complexity advantage. Key obstacles (unbounded operators, unresolved Trotter error bounds, and dependence on non-Gaussian gate counts) remain unquantified, so any claim of advantage is theoretical/speculative at this stage.
## Limitations
- KvN formalism lives in an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space and involves nonphysical position eigenstates with infinite energy (author-stated)
- Trotter error theory for infinite-dimensional (unbounded operator) continuous-variable systems is not characterized; naive norm-based bounds diverge because operators are unbounded (author-stated)
- Non-Gaussian gates required for universality (e.g., cubic, Kerr) are difficult/expensive to implement in practice (author-stated)
- Current algorithm is explicitly developed only for one-dimensional polynomial ODEs; multi-dimensional extension is not provided (author-stated)
- The algorithm is better suited to the Initial Distribution Problem (IDP) than to the Initial Value Problem (IVP); using finite squeezing to approximate position eigenstates introduces bias for nonlinear ODEs (author-stated)
- Choice of number of Trotter steps M at each nested commutator breakdown is nontrivial and unresolved (author-stated)
- No complete complexity analysis is provided; dependency on commutator norms and possible large prefactors remain undetermined (author-stated)
- A bound on required initial squeezing depends on an upper bound on the Lyapunov exponent; this requires prior knowledge of system chaos properties (author-stated)
- Approximation of position eigenstates by finitely squeezed states requires squeezing r > λt + log(ϵ); for chaotic systems this may become impractically large ([inferred])
- [inferred] Resource requirements (amount of squeezing, number of non-Gaussian gates, and photon-number/energy constraints) may be prohibitive for realistic devices
- [inferred] Scalability to high-dimensional ODE systems may be costly due to increased gate counts, Trotter terms, and non-Gaussian resource needs
- [inferred] Using homodyne sampling of the evolved distribution provides samples rather than exact amplitude-encoded solutions, potentially limiting types of extractable information compared to some qubit-based methods
## Open questions
- How to rigorously characterize Trotter error for continuous-variable, infinite-dimensional systems with unbounded operators?
- What are tight, operational error bounds (and their dependence on energy constraints) for the proposed Trotterization and nested commutator decompositions?
- How to choose the number of Trotter steps M at each commutator level in the nested decomposition to balance the different error scalings?
- How large are the prefactors arising from commutator norms in practice and how do they affect gate counts and runtime?
- How to extend the one-dimensional algorithm to multi-dimensional ODEs in a resource-efficient way?
- Can an energy-constrained circle norm (ECCN) be fully developed and evaluated for all gates in the chosen CV gate set, and how would it inform error estimates?
- How does the expected photon-number (energy) of states evolve under the KvN-generated dynamics and under the decomposed gate sequences, and how does that constrain fidelity/error?
- How does the algorithm's complexity compare quantitatively to qubit-based (HHL or KvN-truncated) approaches for various classes of ODEs?
- To what extent can a variationally trained gate sequence reduce Trotterization cost or gate-depth compared to fixed decompositions?
- How significant is the bias when approximating IVP with finitely squeezed states for nonlinear systems, and what are practical methods to control it?
- Can sampling from evolved distributions via CV devices yield practical advantages for tasks like attractor identification or Lyapunov exponent estimation, and how many samples are required?

**Future work:**
- Fully characterize the Trotter error in infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces for continuous-variable quantum simulations
- Develop and compute the energy-constrained circle norm (ECCN) for the gates in the chosen CV model and use it to derive operational error bounds
- Estimate the evolution of the expected photon number (energy) under the KvN Hamiltonian and decomposed gate sequences (e.g., via the Heisenberg picture)
- Optimize the number of Trotter steps M at each nested commutator breakdown; potentially derive automated/analytic choices
- Extend the algorithm from one-dimensional polynomial ODEs to multi-dimensional ODEs (using beam-splitters and multi-mode operations)
- Explore a variational version of the algorithm where sequences of displacement, squeezing, quadratic and cubic gates have tunable execution-time parameters optimized against data
- Perform a full complexity analysis and compare to qubit-based algorithms (including determination of prefactors due to commutator norms)
- Use continuous-variable complexity measures (e.g., stellar rank) to study and classify resource requirements and computational hardness
- Investigate practical sampling applications such as attractor identification and Lyapunov exponent estimation from evolved distributions
- Develop tighter, implementable gate decompositions (with fewer non-Gaussian gates) and evaluate experimental feasibility of the protocol
