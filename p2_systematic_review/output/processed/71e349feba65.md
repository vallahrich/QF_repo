## Abstract summary
The paper investigates applying quantum machine learning (QML) to multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) in industrial project portfolio management (PPM), addressing challenges from high dimensionality, nonlinear interactions, and combinatorial selection. It proposes QML components—quantum-enhanced feature spaces, variational quantum classifiers, and hybrid quantum–classical optimization—to improve project evaluation, robust prioritization under uncertainty, and efficient exploration of large decision spaces, enabling dynamic portfolio balancing and real-time reallocation strategies.
## Methodology
This paper presents a conceptual, theoretical QML-based methodology for multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) in industrial project portfolio management (PPM). The approach is centered on three core components: (1) quantum-enhanced feature mapping, where heterogeneous project criteria (financial metrics, risk indicators, resource constraints, sustainability measures, strategic priorities) are encoded into high-dimensional quantum feature spaces to capture nonlinear interactions and complex correlations; (2) parameterized quantum models—specifically variational quantum classifiers (VQCs)—used to evaluate, score, and rank project alternatives according to multi-criteria utility or preference functions; and (3) hybrid quantum–classical optimization loops that combine quantum evaluations with classical optimizers to explore combinatorial portfolio configurations and perform constrained selection/aggregation. Methodological steps described include transforming multi-criteria inputs into quantum encodings, training variational circuits to produce prioritization or classification outputs, designing composite loss/objective functions that balance conflicting criteria (including scenario-based or robustness terms), and using iterative hybrid optimization to identify near-optimal portfolio allocations and to enable dynamic reallocation under evolving scenarios. The paper frames these components as elements of an end-to-end decision-support architecture for dynamic portfolio balancing, scenario-based planning, and real-time reallocation, but does not report an empirical implementation or numerical experiments.

**Algorithms used:** Variational Quantum Classifier (VQC), Quantum-enhanced feature mapping / feature encoding, Hybrid quantum–classical optimization (variational/hybrid loops)
