## Abstract summary
This review examines how quantum computing can transform fintech by enhancing risk management and financial modeling—improving portfolio optimization, option pricing, Monte Carlo simulations, fraud detection, and market forecasting—while also posing cryptographic and security challenges. It discusses practical implementation considerations and deployment models (on-premises vs Quantum-as-a-Service), and highlights technical and regulatory barriers such as limited qubit counts, error correction needs, data governance, and compliance requirements.
## Methodology
This paper is a narrative/desk-based review and perspective piece that synthesizes existing literature, public documentation, and industry resources on quantum computing applications in finance and industrial management. The authors survey prior academic publications, technical articles, and online material (e.g., IBM, University of Waterloo, ScienceAlert) to identify potential use cases (portfolio optimization, Monte Carlo acceleration, option pricing, fraud detection, cryptography impacts, synthetic data generation), summarize relevant quantum algorithms (citing Shor and Grover), and discuss operational, security, and regulatory considerations for deployment (on‑premises vs Quantum-as-a-Service, QASM and Python-based development). The paper compiles a comparative table of quantum vs classical advantages/limitations for several financial applications, outlines conceptual implementation pathways (proof-of-concept, simulators, pre/post-processing), and offers recommendations rather than reporting original experiments, datasets, or quantitative benchmarks.
