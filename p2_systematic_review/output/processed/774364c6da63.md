## Abstract summary
The authors introduce hqQUBO, a hybrid-querying QUBO formulation and hardware-efficient variational quantum-classical algorithm to tackle multiple sequence alignment (MSA) by reducing quantum resource scaling to O(NL) via a novel hybrid query encoding. They validate the approach with CPU/GPU simulations and experiments on a trapped-ion HYQ-A37 device using up to 16 qubits, showing that modest entanglement, a quenched CVaR optimization scheme, and the hybrid design yield robust convergence and promising performance on realistic noisy hardware.
## Methodology
The authors map the multiple sequence alignment (MSA) problem to a hybrid-querying QUBO (hqQUBO) formulation using a novel Hybrid Query Encoding (HQE) that encodes N sequences of maximal length L into O(NL) qubits (|1> indicates a letter at a position, |0> a gap). The SP (sum-of-pairs) scoring is computed classically via precomputed weight matrices (query step), and soft constraints (penalty parameter p) enforce letter-count conservation. The optimization is performed with a variational quantum-classical hybrid workflow: a hardware-efficient variational ansatz (HEA) is executed on a quantum device (or simulator) to sample candidate MSA states; measurement outcomes are sent to a classical module that evaluates the SP-score (energy) and returns a loss. They use a CVaR-based loss with a two-stage (quenched) schedule (warm-up with r<1, then r=1) to focus optimization on low-energy tails and reduce trapping in local minima. Gradients are obtained either by automatic differentiation + Adam (GPU simulations) or by SPSA for noisy/real-device runs. The pipeline was benchmarked against a QAOA ansatz (noted to require much larger depth) and across different HEA entanglement depths (single-qubit rotations, 1–3 entanglement layers) to study the effect of entanglement. Experiments include noise-free and noise-induced classical simulations (CPU/GPU) and real-device runs on a trapped-ion HYQ-A37 QPU, with performance evaluated by energy distributions, probability of sampling optimal solutions, and per-iteration time scaling.

**Algorithms used:** Hardware-Efficient Ansatz (HEA), Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) / Variational Quantum Algorithm (VQA), QAOA (used as baseline comparison), Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) loss (two-stage/quenched scheme), Simultaneous Perturbation Stochastic Approximation (SPSA), Adam optimizer (automatic differentiation), Hybrid-querying QUBO (hqQUBO) encoding
**Frameworks:** PyTorch v2.1.2, TorchQuantum v0.1.7

**Experimental setup:** Simulations: noise-free and noise-induced models run on CPU (AMD EPYC 9334 32-core) and GPU (NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3060). Quantum hardware: trapped-ion HYQ-A37 (37 physical qubits). Measurements: 2000 shots per circuit execution in experiments/simulations. Variational circuits: HEA with varying entanglement layers (1–3). Optimization: SPSA for hardware/noisy runs; Adam + automatic differentiation used on GPU simulations. CVaR two-stage optimization (warm-up with r<1 then r=1).

### Process
1) Preprocessing: build weight matrices w(i,j) for pairwise similarity (dictionary T) and set penalty p for soft constraints. 2) Encoding: apply Hybrid Query Encoding (HQE) to represent each sequence as L qubits (|1> letter present, |0> gap); total qubits n = O(NL). 3) Formulate objective as hqQUBO and corresponding Hamiltonian H_p whose eigenvalues correspond to SP-score + penalties. 4) Circuit ansatz: prepare parameterized HEA (single-qubit rotations + 1–3 entanglement layers) on simulator or HYQ-A37. 5) Sampling: run circuit (2000 shots) to obtain bitstrings (MSA states). 6) Classical query/scoring: compute SP-score for each sampled state via lookup weight matrices; compute CVaR loss over lowest-r tail. 7) Optimization: estimate gradients (automatic differentiation + Adam in noiseless/GPU sims; SPSA in noisy/hardware runs) and update parameters. 8) CVaR two-stage schedule: warm-up using L(θ)_r with r<1 for ~100 iterations, then switch to L(θ)_r=1 for ~300 iterations (typical experimental schedule), repeat multiple restarts to mitigate initialization variance (e.g., 200–500 repeats). 9) Evaluation: track energy distributions, probability of sampling global optimum, and per-iteration runtime. Baseline comparisons: QAOA ansatz (deep circuit) vs HEA (shallow).

## Findings
- [supported] The authors implemented a hybrid quantum-classical variational algorithm (hqQUBO) to encode multiple sequence alignment (MSA) into quantum states and executed sampling on a quantum device with classical scoring (hybrid-querying encoding).
- [supported] The hybrid-query encoding (HQE) reduces qubit requirements for the MSA formulation to O(NL) (qubits scale linearly with number of sequences N and maximal length L) compared to prior QUBO encodings that can require higher resource overhead.
- [supported] The authors ran simulations (CPU/GPU) and experiments on a trapped-ion device (HYQ-A37) up to 16 qubits and reported successful generation of optimal and near-optimal MSA solutions from the quantum sampler.
- [supported] A hardware-efficient ansatz (HEA) with modest entanglement outperformed single-qubit-only circuits in converging to low-energy (better-scoring) MSA states in their simulations; increasing entanglement beyond a modest level degraded performance (interpreted as overfitting).
- [supported] HEA (shallow-depth hardware-efficient ansatz) achieved comparable convergence to QAOA for the tested 12-qubit example while requiring substantially lower circuit depth (example: HEA 7 layers vs QAOA depth ~4486 in their example), leading to large time savings in simulation.
- [supported] Use of a two-stage CVaR (quenched CVaR) optimization schedule (warm-up with r<1 then r=1) combined with SPSA gradient estimation improved robustness and reduced iterations needed to reach low-energy states in simulations and experiments.
- [supported] Experimental results on HYQ-A37 showed measurable probability mass on optimal MSA states (despite noise), and empirical performance lay between noiseless simulation and an imperfect noise model simulation.
- [supported] The authors provide device-level performance figures for HYQ-A37 as used in the study (37-qubit device; reported single-qubit fidelity ≈99.9%, two-qubit fidelity ≈98.5%, and SPAM error reported below 0.05%).

- [speculative] The authors estimate a qualitative crossover point at around 22 qubits beyond which running their algorithm on the HYQ-A37 would become advantageous over classical simulation, based on observed per-iteration time scaling; this estimate is heuristic and not a rigorous demonstration of quantum advantage.

**Results summary:** The authors present hqQUBO, a hybrid quantum-classical formulation and hardware-efficient variational approach to multiple sequence alignment (MSA). They introduce a Hybrid Query Encoding (HQE) that maps N sequences of maximal length L onto O(NL) qubits, use shallow hardware-efficient ansatz circuits with modest entanglement, and employ a two-stage CVaR optimization with SPSA for robustness. They validated the approach in GPU/CPU simulations and on a 37-qubit trapped-ion device (HYQ-A37) with experiments up to 16 qubits, reporting successful sampling of optimal and near-optimal alignments, better performance of modestly entangling HEA over single-qubit circuits, and practical advantages in circuit depth and per-iteration time versus a QAOA instance. The authors make heuristic estimates about a crossover point (~22 qubits) where execution on their ion-trap hardware might outperform classical noisy simulations, and discuss broader potential impacts for life-science optimization tasks.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The paper presents empirical evidence that their hybrid HEA approach is more time-efficient and shallower than a QAOA instance for small examples and that experiments on an ion-trap device produce optimal/near-optimal MSA samples up to 16 qubits. However, they do not demonstrate a rigorous, scalable quantum advantage over classical algorithms; the claim of a crossover point (~22 qubits) is heuristic and speculative rather than a proven advantage. Broader claims about accelerating real-world MSA workloads (e.g., for AlphaFold pipelines) are prospective and not demonstrated end-to-end.
## Limitations
- Authors: Current quantum devices are in the NISQ era with limited qubit counts and imperfect gates; this constrains width/depth of circuits and overall scalability.
- Authors: Experimental validation was limited to up to 16 qubits (HYQ-A37); larger-scale implementations were not demonstrated experimentally.
- Authors: The study focuses on collections of short sequence alignments as a proof-of-concept rather than full-length, real-world MSA problems used in production pipelines.
- Authors: Noise and device imperfections affect experimental results; experiments show susceptibility to noise and external disturbances.
- Authors: Noise-induced classical simulations performed worse than experiments, likely because the noise model was inadequate as system size increases.
- Authors: Time/crossover cost estimate (predicting advantage around ~22 qubits) is qualitative and does not account for how the number of optimization iterations scales with qubit count.
- Authors: The approach relies on frequent real-time monitoring and irregular calibration on the device, which contributes substantial, linearly-scaling overhead.
- Authors: The algorithm uses shallow, hardware-efficient ansatz and modest entanglement by design; excessive entanglement was observed to degrade performance (overfitting).
- Authors: No full error-correction or comprehensive error-mitigation strategies were applied in experiments.
- [inferred] The scoring approach uses SP-score and a particular weight matrix preprocessing; this may limit applicability to different biological scoring schemes or domain-specific alignment objectives.
- [inferred] Hybrid Query Encoding (HQE) was validated for short alignments; its effectiveness and overhead for very long sequences or very large N were not evaluated.
- [inferred] The approach offloads scoring (query) to classical computation — while reducing qubit need, this hybrid split may introduce classical bottlenecks for very large datasets.
- [inferred] The work demonstrates one hardware platform (HYQ-A37); portability and performance on other architectures (superconducting, neutral atoms) remain untested.
- [inferred] Measurement cost: gradient estimation and optimization use many shots (e.g., 2000 shots), implying potentially large sampling overhead for scaling up.
## Open questions
- How does the proposed hqQUBO + HEA approach scale (in wall-clock time and number of optimization iterations) for larger, realistic MSA instances (longer sequences, larger N)?
- What is the precise crossover point (qubit count and problem size) where the quantum implementation becomes practically advantageous over classical simulations, when accounting for iteration counts and full overheads?
- How can noise models for classical simulations be improved to faithfully predict experimental behavior as system size increases?
- What is the optimal degree and structure of entanglement in hardware-efficient ansatz for MSA to maximize solution quality while avoiding overfitting and barren plateaus?
- How robust is Hybrid Query Encoding (HQE) when applied to diverse real-world alignment tasks (variable-length sequences, different alphabets, complex gap/penalty schemes)?
- How can advanced scoring schemes (e.g., substitution matrices, affine gap penalties) and richer biological constraints be incorporated efficiently into the hqQUBO framework?
- What error-mitigation or partial error-correction techniques are most effective for improving solution fidelity for this problem class on current hardware?
- How sensitive are optimization outcomes to choices of classical optimizers (Adam, SPSA, etc.), parameter initialization, and shot budgets when scaling up?
- How to reduce sampling (shot) overhead for gradient estimation and CVaR evaluation while preserving convergence quality?
- How transferable are the techniques and compilation/optimization strategies demonstrated here to other quantum platforms and to other combinatorial problems beyond MSA?
- How to integrate quantum-generated MSA outputs into downstream workflows (e.g., AlphaFold or other structure prediction pipelines) in a way that improves overall predictive performance?
