## Abstract summary
This empirical study applies non-linear neural network methods (Deep Recurrent Convolutional Neural Networks, Deep Neural Decision Trees, and Quantum Neural Networks) to estimate a Macro Asset Pricing model for listed European football clubs. The authors report high predictive accuracy (over 90% for top methods), find the model residual accounts for roughly 40% of the variance in the price-dividend ratio, and highlight the importance of long-term risks and habit formation, with the Quantum Neural Network giving the best performance.
## Methodology
The authors estimate a Macro Asset Pricing (MAP) model for listed football clubs by decomposing the log price-dividend ratio into four state components (long-run growth xt, long-run volatility ~σ2_t, surplus consumption/habit ~s_t, and a permanent residual e_t). They implement three non-linear estimation methods: a Deep Recurrent Convolutional Neural Network (DRCNN), Deep Neural Decision Trees (DNDT), and a hybrid Quantum Neural Network (QNN) (quantum-inspired neural architecture). Monthly consumption, dividend and share-price data (Eurostat consumption; dividends and prices from STOXX Europe Football index and Bureau van Dijk Amadeus) for the period 2011–2020 are used. Priors for model parameters are set following prior literature and some manual choices (e.g., steady-state surplus consumption s = log 0.06, σ_e = 1). The state processes are specified (AR(1) for xt and random-walk type processes for others, specific volatility dynamics provided in the paper) and then each neural methodology is trained to map observable inputs to the price/dividend decomposition and to estimate posterior parameters. Training uses a 70%/30% train/test split. Performance is evaluated using goodness of fit, accuracy/precision, RMSE and MAPE and via variance decomposition (contributions of each MAP state to Var(pdt)). The DNDT method uses soft-binning with a Gumbel-Softmax (Straight-Through) approximation and a random subspace forest for scalability. The DRCNN is implemented as a stacked recurrent + convolutional architecture trained with stochastic gradient descent minimizing MSE. The QNN is described as a hybrid quantum-inspired neural network: qubit-like hidden units initialized in superposition, rotation gates applied to update amplitudes, and parameter updates derived from gradients (learning rate reported as 0.1). No quantum hardware, QPU, or quantum software frameworks are reported; the QNN is presented as a quantum-inspired algorithmic layer within a classical training loop.

**Algorithms used:** Deep Recurrent Convolutional Neural Network (DRCNN), Deep Neural Decision Trees (DNDT) with soft binning and Gumbel-Softmax, Quantum Neural Network (hybrid quantum-inspired neural network)

**Dataset:** Monthly consumption (Eurostat), dividends and share prices for listed football clubs from STOXX Europe Football index (plus Manchester United via Bureau van Dijk Amadeus). Period: 2011–2020. Sample: 21 listed football clubs (STOXX Europe Football constituents listed in Appendix A). Data harmonized for European consumption.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The paper reports that a QNN variant outperformed the tested classical/deep architectures on this particular MAP estimation dataset (higher accuracy and lower RMSE). However, this is an empirical, task-specific finding rather than a demonstration of a general quantum computational advantage: there is no demonstration of provable speedup, no use of quantum hardware performance metrics, and the QNN appears as a hybrid/quantum-inspired model evaluated on classical data. Therefore claims of a general or hardware-backed 'quantum advantage' remain speculative based on the provided evidence.
## Limitations
- Priors and some parameters set ad hoc or by convenience (e.g., habit steady state s set to log(0.06); residual volatility σe and Ae set to 1) which may influence estimates
- Volatility specification is simplified to a single long-run volatility process; authors note this assumption is restrictive
- Residual component is large (~40% of price/dividend variance) and interpreted as permanent unobserved information—model leaves substantial variation unexplained
- Results are sector- and sample-specific (STOXX Europe Football index plus Manchester United; 2011–2020 monthly data) which may limit generalisability [inferred]
- Relatively small dataset/time-span for deep learning methods (21 listed clubs, 2011–2020), increasing risk of overfitting and estimation uncertainty [inferred]
- Some coefficients (e.g., price-dividend coefficients) tended toward zero due to prior choices, indicating sensitivity to prior specification and potential identification issues
- DRCNN, DNDT and QNN implementations raise scalability and practical deployment concerns: DNDT's Kronecker-product approach has scalability issues (authors used forest workaround); QNN presentation lacks demonstration on real quantum hardware (likely simulated) [inferred]
- [inferred] Interpretability limitations: while DNDT aims at interpretability, deep models (DRCNN, QNN) can be opaque, complicating economic interpretation and inference
- [inferred] No direct comparison with linear baselines (e.g., OLS) reported on the same dataset in this paper for model selection robustness, beyond literature references
## Open questions
- What are the economic sources driving the sizeable residual component in football-club asset pricing?
- Why is the residual contribution to variance lower in this football-club sample than in previous studies of broader sectors?
- How much of the residual is due to measurement error in consumption/dividend data versus genuinely unobserved risk factors?
- How sensitive are the MAP decomposition results to prior choices and to different volatility specifications?
- Can the reported gains from QNN and DNDT be replicated out-of-sample, across longer time spans, other leagues, regions, or other asset classes?
- How can consumption measures and time-series length/quality be improved to reduce identification uncertainty (as suggested by Kleibergen & Zhan)?
- [inferred] Can Quantum Neural Network approaches be implemented effectively on real quantum hardware and scaled to larger financial datasets?
- [inferred] What methods (regularization, explainable-AI, alternative architectures) best preserve interpretability while retaining the predictive gains of deep models in asset-pricing contexts?
- What new factors (beyond long-term growth, long-term volatility, habit and residual) might be constructible and robustly useful for asset pricing in sports or other sector-specific applications?

**Future work:**
- Expand the MAP estimation to data from other football clubs in other regions of the world or to different sports disciplines (explicitly recommended by the authors)
