## Abstract summary
This paper proposes a Hybrid Quantum Gradient–Classical Approach (HQG-CA) that uses parameterized quantum circuits and quantum gradient information to explore solution spaces for nonlinear optimization problems. Simulation results indicate HQG-CA can improve algorithmic speedup, convergence and solution accuracy across domains such as finance, machine learning and logistics, while acknowledging practical challenges like decoherence, error correction and hardware limitations.
## Methodology
The paper proposes a theoretical Hybrid Quantum Gradient - Classical Approach (HQG-CA) for nonlinear optimization. The methodology is a hybrid workflow that combines classical pre- and post-processing with quantum encoding, quantum processing, measurement and classical refinement. Classical inputs (objective function, constraints, initial parameters) are pre-processed and encoded into parameterized quantum circuits which represent candidate solutions as qubit states. The quantum processing phase applies the HQG-CA optimization routine that uses quantum gradient information (including higher-order derivatives) and entanglement-aware cost functions to navigate the quantum state space. The authors define several analytic measures and cost functions to characterise performance and behaviour: a quantum advantage score AS(O,ε,R,α), a solution-accuracy model SA(O,ε,R,μ,θ), a performance/scalability metric T(O), an overall efficiency OE (including fidelity, error rates and entanglement measures), an entanglement-aware quantum cost R(θ) and its second derivatives ∇2R(θ), and domain-specific objectives such as a quantum-inspired portfolio objective and a constrained Lagrangian with penalty terms. The pipeline ends with measurement of quantum states to yield classical parameters that are refined with classical optimisers and constraint enforcement. The paper reports results from simulation experiments comparing HQG-CA to an Individual Quantum Approach (I-QA) and classical baselines across metrics including algorithmic speedup, solution accuracy, scalability and overall efficiency, but it does not provide low-level experimental details, dataset descriptions, hardware, nor code provenance.

**Algorithms used:** HQG-CA (Hybrid Quantum Gradient - Classical Approach), QAOA, VQE, Quantum annealing, Hybrid quantum-classical genetic (HQG) style technique / genetic hybrid
