## Abstract summary
The paper proposes a constraint-preserving QAOA formulation for cardinality-constrained portfolio selection using Dicke-state initialization and an XY-mixer that preserves Hamming weight, together with a Trotterized parameter initialization to mitigate barren plateaus. In a 2025 monthly walk-forward on 10 US equities, the approach outperformed Simulated Annealing and Hierarchical Risk Parity in Sharpe ratio (1.81 vs 1.31 and 0.98) but incurred substantially higher turnover, highlighting a trade-off between selection quality and implementation costs.
## Methodology
The study formulates cardinality-constrained portfolio selection (select exactly K assets out of N) as an Ising/QUBO-style cost and compares a constraint-preserving QAOA ansatz against classical baselines (Simulated Annealing and Hierarchical Risk Parity). The QAOA variant initializes the circuit in an N-qubit Dicke state of Hamming weight K and uses a complete-graph XY-mixer Hamiltonian (XiXj + YiYj) which preserves total excitation number, guaranteeing the evolution remains within the feasible K-subspace and removing the need for penalty terms. The cost Hamiltonian encodes linear Zi terms from annualized expected returns and ZiZj terms from annualized covariances (Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage). To mitigate barren-plateau risk, parameters (γ, β) are initialized using a Trotter-inspired linear adiabatic ramp (γ ramping up, β ramping down). Optimization is performed in simulation (PennyLane default.qubit statevector) using a gradient-based Adam optimizer (qml.AdamOptimizer) with early stopping; for each depth p∈{1..6} the full probability vector is computed, filtered to bitstrings with Hamming weight K and probability ≥1%, rescored by the classical objective, and the best candidate across depths is chosen. Selected discrete subsets are then assigned continuous weights by solving a bounded Sharpe-maximization (SLSQP) with per-asset box constraints (0.05–0.50) and full-investment. A small continuity bonus is applied to previously held assets in both QAOA and SA to reduce turnover. Simulated Annealing operates on a QUBO with an adaptive penalty scale and fixed sampling parameters (neal, num_reads=5000, num_sweeps=1000). Monthly walk-forward backtests are run on 10 U.S. large-cap equities for calendar year 2025 with a 180-trading-day lookback, transaction costs modeled as 5 bps × turnover, and performance reported via total return, annualized volatility, Sharpe ratio, max drawdown, and turnover.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) with XY-mixer, Dicke state initialization, Trotterized (adiabatic-inspired) parameter initialization, Simulated Annealing (neal.SimulatedAnnealingSampler), Hierarchical Risk Parity (HRP), Adam optimizer (qml.AdamOptimizer), SLSQP (Sharpe-max continuous allocation)
**Frameworks:** PennyLane (default.qubit statevector), PyPortfolioOpt (HRP), yfinance, neal (D-Wave's simulated annealer), Ledoit–Wolf covariance (library unspecified, standard implementation used)

**Experimental setup:** Simulated on PennyLane's default.qubit statevector simulator. QAOA circuits initialized in Dicke state and evolved with XY-mixer on complete graph. Depths p = 1..6 tested. Classical simulated annealing run with neal (num_reads=5000, num_sweeps=1000). Monthly walk-forward backtest for calendar year 2025 with 180 trading day lookback windows. Full probability vectors computed (statevector), filtered by Hamming weight K and probability threshold.

**Dataset:** Auto-adjusted daily Close prices from Yahoo Finance via yfinance for 10 US large-cap equities (AAPL, MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, JPM, V, TSLA, UNH, LLY, XOM). Out-of-sample testing: calendar year 2025 with monthly rebalances; model inputs estimated from preceding 180 trading days. Missing values forward-filled; non-trading rows dropped; returns computed from prices; expected returns and covariances annualized (×252), covariance estimated with Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage.

## Findings
- [supported] A constraint-preserving QAOA ansatz using Dicke-state initialization and an XY-mixer strictly preserves the Hamming-weight-K subspace and therefore enforces the cardinality constraint without penalty terms (demonstrated in the N=10, K=5 experiments).
- [supported] In the authors' 2025 monthly walk-forward backtest on a 10-stock universe (N=10, K=5), the QAOA-XY selector produced higher out-of-sample performance than Simulated Annealing (SA) and Hierarchical Risk Parity (HRP) under their pipeline and cost model.
- [supported] Reported 2025 performance metrics: QAOA-XY total return 30.09%, Sharpe Ratio 1.81, volatility 18.55%, max drawdown −8.27%, and average monthly turnover 76.8% (compared to SA Sharpe 1.31 / turnover 21.0% and HRP Sharpe 0.98 / turnover 21.6%).
- [supported] Increasing QAOA circuit depth (p) up to 6 generally improved the optimized cost in their experiments; the best reported final cost was −0.5355 at p=6 (for N=10, K=5).
- [supported] Trotterized (AQC-inspired) parameter initialization provided a warm-start that yielded trainable optimization trajectories in their simulations; gradient norms did not vanish and were reported to increase up to p=6 in the tested regime.
- [supported] The measurement/readout filter (keeping only Hamming-weight-K strings with probability ≥1%) and rescoring by the classical objective produced feasible candidate portfolios used for allocation.
- [supported] Under the tested transaction-cost model (5 bps × monthly turnover), QAOA-XY maintained superior net performance relative to SA and HRP in the provided backtest.
- [speculative] The paper argues that the constraint-preserving QAOA approach is a viable candidate for production FinTech solvers (Direct Indexing use-cases) — claim generalizing beyond the presented small-scale simulation.
- [speculative] The authors suggest that Trotterized initialization mitigates barren plateaus more generally; while this was empirically observed at N=10, p≤6, broader mitigation at larger N or realistic noise is an open question.
- [speculative] The approach will require further work to scale beyond N=10 and to assess performance/noise-sensitivity on real NISQ hardware — the paper lists scaling and noisy-hardware testing as necessary future work.
- [supported] QAOA-XY exhibited substantially higher turnover than the classical baselines in the experiments, implying trade-offs between discrete selection quality and operational costs.
- [supported] Depth-vs-runtime tradeoff observed: p=3 identified as a practical tradeoff between solution quality and runtime, p=6 as higher-quality but more compute-intensive option in this experimental setup.

**Results summary:** The preprint implements a constraint-preserving QAOA for the K-of-N portfolio selection problem using Dicke-state initialization, a complete-graph XY-mixer, and a Trotterized parameter warm-start. In statevector simulations on a 10-stock universe with monthly 2025 walk-forward rebalancing (N=10, K=5), the QAOA-XY pipeline outperformed a Simulated Annealing QUBO solver and a Hierarchical Risk Parity baseline under the authors' Sharpe-max allocation and 5 bps × turnover transaction-cost model. The algorithm produced higher total return and Sharpe Ratio (30.09% and 1.81) but at the expense of much higher monthly turnover (76.8%). Depth scaling to p=6 yielded lower cost and non-vanishing gradients in these simulations; however, the authors note scalability, noise, and turnover-integration as open challenges.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The authors report better out-of-sample performance for their constraint-preserving QAOA pipeline relative to classical heuristics in statevector simulations on a small N=10 universe, but this is not a general demonstration of quantum advantage: experiments were classical simulations (no noisy quantum hardware), on a small problem size, and used classical optimization and full-state access. Thus the observed empirical benefit is limited in scope and does not constitute proof of a general quantum advantage; broader, hardware-based and larger-N validation is necessary.
## Limitations
- Experiment scale limited to N = 10 assets; results may not generalize to larger universes.
- Circuit depth tested only up to p = 6; behavior and trainability at larger depths are untested.
- High realized turnover (76.8% monthly) — operationally costly and may be prohibitive in less liquid markets.
- Transaction-cost model is simplistic (5 bps × turnover) and does not model market impact or realistic slippage.
- All QAOA experiments run on a statevector simulator (PennyLane default.qubit); algorithm performance on noisy quantum hardware was not demonstrated.
- Benchmarking and backtest limited to a single year (2025) and a single 10-stock universe — limited robustness and regime coverage.
- The approach uses a two-stage pipeline (discrete K-of-N selection followed by continuous Sharpe-max weighting); global optimality of the combined procedure is not guaranteed.
- Readout/candidate filtering (keeping only K-hot strings with probability ≥ 1%) may discard useful low-probability solutions and bias outcomes.
- Simulated Annealing baseline relies on penalty scaling and sampler hyperparameters that can affect comparison fairness.
- Continuity bonus (warm-start) is a heuristic; its magnitude and effects on selection dynamics and turnover were not systematically studied.
- [inferred] Preparing Dicke-state initializations and complete-graph XY-mixers on real hardware may be costly and error-prone; practical state-preparation overheads were not evaluated.
- [inferred] The choice of classical optimizer (Adam) and its hyperparameters may materially affect trainability; sensitivity to optimizer choice was not explored.
- [inferred] The Trotterized (adiabatic-inspired) parameter initialization was validated only for this problem size; its effectiveness across problems and larger N remains unproven.
- [inferred] Sampling limitations (finite shots on real hardware) and readout noise were not modeled; reported statevector probabilities may overestimate achievable solution quality on NISQ devices.
## Open questions
- How can turnover and realistic trading frictions (market impact, slippage) be incorporated directly into the discrete selection objective to produce implementable portfolios?
- How does the constraint-preserving QAOA-XY ansatz scale with increasing N and K in terms of circuit depth, optimizer stability, and sampling/readout requirements?
- How robust are the observed outperformance and trainability results across longer horizons, diverse market regimes, and larger or different asset universes?
- How sensitive are results to noise and device-specific error on real quantum hardware (NISQ), and which hardware-aware ansatz modifications are required?
- Does the Trotterized parameter initialization reliably avoid barren plateaus for larger problem sizes and deeper circuits?
- What is the operational trade-off point (circuit depth vs. runtime) in production settings with strict compute/time budgets?
- How much do heuristic choices (continuity bonus magnitude, readout probability threshold, optimizer settings) affect performance and turnover?
- Can a single-stage formulation (joint discrete-selection-and-weighting) outperform the two-stage pipeline used here, and how could it be implemented for QAOA-like algorithms?
- What is the cost/benefit of preparing Dicke states and full XY-mixers on hardware versus approximate/efficient encodings that may introduce leakage out of the K-subspace?

**Future work:**
- Incorporate explicit trading frictions/costs or temporal regularization (multi-period coupling) directly into the selection objective to control turnover.
- Investigate scaling beyond N = 10: study circuit depth, optimizer stability, and sampling/readout policy adjustments required as problem size grows.
- Conduct broader robustness evaluations across longer horizons, different market regimes, and alternative asset universes to characterize when QAOA offers the largest advantage.
- Implement and test the circuit on noisy quantum hardware or under realistic noise models to assess sensitivity to NISQ limitations and guide hardware-aware ansatz design.
## Key ideas
- #idea:quantum-advantage — Constraint-preserving QAOA (Dicke-state initialization + XY-mixer) produced higher out-of-sample Sharpe (1.81) than Simulated Annealing (1.31) and HRP (0.98) on a 10-asset 2025 monthly walk-forward backtest.
- #idea:hybrid-approach — The workflow is hybrid: discrete subset selection via QAOA in the K-excitation subspace, followed by classical continuous Sharpe-maximization (SLSQP) to assign weights, plus classical continuity bonuses to reduce turnover.
- #idea:near-term-feasibility — NISQ-oriented design choices (XY-mixer to preserve feasibility, Trotterized parameter initialization to mitigate barren plateaus, shallow depths p=1..6) are proposed to improve trainability on near-term devices, although evaluation is on an ideal statevector simulator.
- #limitation:simulation-only — All experiments were run on PennyLane default.qubit statevector simulator; no noisy-hardware experiments or noise-aware runs reported.
- #limitation:no-empirical-validation — No runs on real QPU hardware; claims of practical NISQ applicability are not empirically validated on hardware.
- #limitation:qubit-count — Demonstration limited to N=10 assets (10 qubits); scaling to realistic larger universes is not demonstrated and may be challenging.
- #limitation:noise — Ideal (noise-free) simulation ignores real device decoherence/gate errors which could break Dicke initialisation, XY-mixer fidelity, and measurement filtering strategies.
- #limitation:data-encoding — Preparing Dicke initial states and implementing complete-graph XY-mixers impose nontrivial circuit depth/overhead for real hardware, implying hidden data-encoding and state-preparation costs.
## Contradictions
- Internal trade-off: Although the QAOA approach yielded higher Sharpe, it incurred substantially higher turnover, increasing transaction costs and complicating claims of practical outperformance when implementation costs are considered.
- Scalability concern: The reported advantage is demonstrated on a small N=10, ideal-simulator experiment; this contradicts any implicit suggestion that the method's superior performance will straightforwardly scale to larger asset universes or noisy hardware without further evidence.
