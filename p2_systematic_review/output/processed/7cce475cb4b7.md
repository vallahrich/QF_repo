## Abstract summary
The paper proposes a hybrid quantum perceptron that combines a variational quantum circuit with Grover's algorithm (QVPG) and compares its performance to a variational quantum perceptron (QVP) and a classical perceptron. Using simulations on the Iris dataset with IBM's QASM simulator, the authors report that both quantum models outperform the classical perceptron and that incorporating Grover amplitude amplification improves accuracy and training efficiency over the plain variational approach.
## Methodology
The authors implement and compare three classifiers: a classical perceptron (CP), a quantum variational perceptron (QVP) built from a parametrized variational quantum circuit (VQC), and a novel quantum variational perceptron augmented by Grover amplitude amplification (QVP-G). Classical feature vectors are encoded into quantum states using amplitude encoding (φ(x)|0^p>). The model (ansatz) is a parametrized unitary U(θ) decomposed into single-qubit parameterized gates U(θ,φ,λ) and CNOTs; the output probability is obtained by measuring the first qubit P(q0=1|x,θ) = π(x;θ). Training minimizes an empirical loss L(θ)=1/N Σ l(π(xi;θ), yi) using batch stochastic gradient descent. Gradients are evaluated using analytic derivative identities (parameter-shift-like relations) expressed as linear combinations of expectation values of circuits with shifted parameters. For QVP-G the authors append a Grover-style amplitude amplification circuit (Hadamards, CNOTs, controlled-Z, Pauli-X) after the VQC to amplify target-state amplitudes before measurement. Experiments use the Iris dataset as a testbed; input values are rescaled to fit rotation angles for encoding. Evaluation reports training/validation accuracy and loss over up to 100 training iterations, comparing CP, QVP and QVP-G, run on the IBM QASM simulator (IBM Quantum).

**Algorithms used:** Variational Quantum Circuit (VQC), Quantum Variational Perceptron (QVP), Grover's Algorithm / Amplitude Amplification, Classical Perceptron, Parameter-shift derivative identities (analytic gradient evaluation)
**Frameworks:** IBM QASM simulator (IBM Quantum)

**Experimental setup:** Experiments were executed on the IBM QASM simulator (IBM Quantum) capable of ideal and noise-modeled simulation, with up to 32 qubits available.
