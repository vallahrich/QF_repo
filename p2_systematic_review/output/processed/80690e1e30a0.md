## Abstract summary
The paper evaluates the use of quantum computing techniques—specifically continuous-variable quantum neural networks (CV-QNNs), the Crystals-Kyber post-quantum cryptosystem, and quantum-enhanced Monte Carlo methods—for real-time threat detection, secure communication, and risk modeling in financial IT infrastructures. It reports improvements in detection accuracy and latency and demonstrates potential for quantum-safe communications, while noting practical challenges such as high error rates, environmental sensitivity, and integration complexity that require further technological development and strategic planning.
## Methodology
The study used a theoretical, literature-driven methodology. It conducted an extensive literature review of academic journals, industry reports, and standards documents to identify and analyze how quantum algorithms and post-quantum cryptography can be applied to real-time threat mitigation in financial IT infrastructures. The analysis focused on three areas: (1) Quantum-based anomaly detection, where Continuous-Variable Quantum Neural Networks (CV-QNNs) and hybrid quantum-classical encoding schemes were examined for their suitability in detecting transaction anomalies (credit-card fraud) and the operational aspects of encoding classical features into qumodes and stacking CV quantum layers; (2) Quantum-safe cryptography, where the Crystals-Kyber Key Encapsulation Mechanism (KEM) was reviewed in detail (mathematical basis, parameter sets, IND-CCA2 security, and practical integration into TLS handshake flows) including algorithmic specification and parameter tables; and (3) Quantum-enhanced risk modeling, where Quantum Monte Carlo (QMC) approaches leveraging Quantum Amplitude Estimation (QAE) were evaluated for potential quadratic speed-ups in sampling for scenario and risk estimation. Additionally, the study performed a qualitative feasibility and readiness assessment that considered coherence times, error/noise, environmental constraints, fault-tolerance overheads (surface-code estimates), and integration complexity with existing classical systems. References to existing simulation and prototype demonstrations (e.g., QAE implementations and IBM/Qiskit materials) were used to ground claims and estimate prospective performance gains, while acknowledging that most algorithms remain conceptual or simulated and that full production deployment requires further hardware advances and error correction.

**Algorithms used:** Continuous-Variable Quantum Neural Network (CV-QNN), Crystals-Kyber (post-quantum Key Encapsulation Mechanism), Quantum Monte Carlo (QMC) with Quantum Amplitude Estimation (QAE)
**Frameworks:** Qiskit, IBM Quantum
