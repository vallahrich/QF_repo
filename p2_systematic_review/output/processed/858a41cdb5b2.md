## Abstract summary
The paper presents a general-purpose framework for quantum algorithms that efficiently handle arrays by directly embedding information into quantum amplitudes (avoiding square-root encodings). It defines a two-register 'quantum matrix' representation, a toolkit of quantum arithmetic operations on arrays, and a pipeline covering data loading, manipulation, and information extraction, with discussion of efficiency trade-offs and example oracle manipulations.
## Methodology
The authors propose a modular, conceptual framework for quantum algorithms operating on array-structured data using a ‘quantum matrix’ representation with direct embedding of data into amplitudes (avoiding square-root encodings). The overall pipeline is split into three modules: (1) data loading (they discuss splitting loading into a probability distribution p_ij and a function f_ij, and describe pointwise loading via masking and controlled Ry rotations as well as references to more efficient state-preparation techniques such as multiplexor tree and Schmidt-decomposition-based methods), (2) quantum arithmetic (a toolkit of array-level operations implemented as quantum circuits: reversing, generic permutations (pivot-based swaps using multi-controlled X gates), cyclic permutations, additions/sums via Hadamard transforms, reductions via Walsh–Hadamard on a register, multiplication by constants via controlled rotations on an auxiliary qubit, array–array multiplication by composing oracles with swap operations, and deriving scalar products and squaring by combining these primitives), and (3) information extraction (they describe amplitude read-out using the Quantum Coin method for an initial unamplified estimate followed by iterative Grover-style amplitude amplification to “zoom in” on a confidence interval). The paper discusses algorithmic details (masking, control strategies, use of auxiliary qubits, normalization issues), complexity caveats (pointwise generic state preparation requires exponential resources; constant or highly symmetric arrays are substantially cheaper to load), and provides worked examples (constant vertical shift of an oracle, step-wise/iterative approximation of linear shifts). The authors implemented and tested the described circuit primitives using Qiskit, but the work is primarily methodological and conceptual rather than an empirical evaluation on a specific dataset or QPU.

**Algorithms used:** Direct amplitude embedding (direct embedding), Quantum matrix representation, State preparation via controlled Ry rotations (pointwise loading), Multiplexor tree state preparation (cited), Schmidt-decomposition-based state preparation (cited), Walsh–Hadamard transforms for sum/difference and reductions, Permutation circuits with pivot-based swaps using multi-controlled X, Cyclic permutation circuits, Array multiplication via oracles and swap operator, Quantum Coin amplitude estimation, Grover amplitude amplification (iterative ampliﬁcation), Controlled rotations and multi-controlled NOT gates
**Frameworks:** Qiskit

## Findings
- [supported] The authors introduce a modular pipeline for quantum algorithms on arrays consisting of three stages: data loading, quantum arithmetic (array manipulations), and information extraction (read-out).
- [speculative] Direct embedding (storing values directly in amplitudes without taking square roots) simplifies algebraic arithmetic manipulations on quantum-stored arrays compared to encodings that involve square roots or register encodings.
- [supported] The paper defines a "quantum matrix" data layout (two-register state with row and column registers) to represent bi-dimensional arrays in amplitude encoding and provides a toolkit of elementary array operations (reversing, permutations, cyclic shifts, pairwise sums, global reductions, multiplication by constants, array multiplication via oracles, squaring/scalar product).
- [supported] The authors implemented and tested a selected set of the proposed quantum-arithmetic operations using Qiskit.
- [supported] Loading a fully generic n-qubit quantum state (state preparation) has circuit complexity that, in leading order, scales like O(2^{n+1}) CNOTs (citing multiplexor and Schmidt decomposition approaches).
- [supported] Loading a constant array is much cheaper than generic pointwise loading: worst-case quantum circuit for a constant row requires nI X-gates, two Y-rotations and one multi-controlled NOT (as described in Appendix A.2).
- [speculative] For constant arrays, a quantum loading procedure can require exponentially fewer operations than a classical element-wise copy (the quantum gate count does not scale with the length J of the constant array but with the number of row qubits nI).
- [supported] Many array-level arithmetic operations are efficient as global operations (e.g., Hadamard-based pairwise row/column sums and reductions) whereas single-element manipulations (arbitrary swaps) require significantly more control operations and are therefore expensive.
- [supported] The array multiplication method given relies on availability of oracles for the input arrays (Of, Og) and uses auxiliary qubits plus controlled swaps; the asymptotic efficiency of such product operations depends on the cost of those loading oracles.
- [supported] The paper recommends Quantum Coin / amplitude-estimation-style readout (with unamplified sampling followed by Grover-style amplified iterations) to extract amplitudes or amplitude magnitudes, and outlines an iterative amplification ('zoom-in') strategy using Grover operators to refine amplitude estimates.
- [speculative] The framework and toolbox can be extended to higher-dimensional tensors (multi-dimensional arrays), at the expense of additional controlled operations for masking/subset operations.

**Results summary:** This preprint presents a conceptual and technical framework for handling arrays within quantum algorithms via a "quantum matrix" amplitude encoding combined with direct embedding (storing values directly in amplitudes). The authors develop a modular pipeline (data loading, quantum arithmetic, information extraction), give constructions for many array-level operations (reversals, permutations, cyclic shifts, sums, reductions, constant multiplication, array multiplication via oracles, squaring/scalar products), discuss complexity trade-offs (global operations are efficient; single-element manipulations and generic state preparation are costly), and describe read-out strategies based on amplitude estimation / Quantum Coin methods with Grover-style amplification. They also implemented and tested parts of their toolkit in Qiskit and provide example oracle-manipulation routines (constant and piecewise linear shifts). The paper is largely theoretical and methodological, highlighting both strengths and limits (notably the dependence of many operations on loading/oracle cost).

**Performance claims:**
- Generic n-qubit state preparation (leading order) requires O(2^{n+1}) CNOT gates (cited state-of-art via multiplexors and Schmidt decomposition).
- Loading a constant row can require at most nI X-gates, two Y-rotations and one multi-controlled NOT (worst-case, Appendix A.2).
- The direct-embedding constant-loading procedure's gate count does not depend on the length J of the constant array, leading the authors to claim exponentially fewer quantum operations (in J) to load a constant array compared to naive classical copying.
- Reduction (sum of row elements stored in first column) is achievable by applying Hadamard gates to all column qubits: the transformation isolates the row-sum amplitude into the |0>_{column} state.
- Step-wise piecewise approximation of a linear shift becomes inefficient when the desired accuracy scales as 1/N (where N is discretization points).
## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The paper proposes that certain tasks (e.g., loading a constant array) can be much cheaper on a quantum device than classical elementwise copying and that array-level arithmetic can be performed efficiently because of amplitude encoding and global operations. However, these are theoretical/architectural claims rather than empirically demonstrated quantum speedups: the overall efficiency depends critically on the cost of state preparation/oracles and readout, and no end-to-end quantum advantage is demonstrated experimentally in the manuscript.
## Limitations
- Pointwise data-loading procedure (Appendix A.1) is generally not efficient and not optimized.
- End-to-end algorithmic efficiency depends critically on the availability of efficient state-preparation oracles; many manipulations assume such oracles exist.
- The described loading approaches (multiplexors, Schmidt decomposition) still require O(2^{n+1}) CNOTs for generic n-qubit states (i.e., exponential circuit complexity for arbitrary states).
- Manipulating individual array elements (swaps/permutations of single entries) is costly; global/block operations are much more efficient than single-element operations.
- Many operations rely on multi-controlled gates; extensions to higher-dimensional tensors require additional controlled operations which increase cost and complexity.
- The multiplication-by-constant primitive as presented uses a controlled rotation that directly encodes cos(θ/2) = α, hence it initially targets multipliers in [0,1] and relies on normalization workarounds for other ranges.
- Operations such as array multiplication, squaring and scalar product have efficiency tied to the loading strategy; if loading is slow/expensive, these operations inherit that inefficiency.
- The read-out method emphasized (Quantum Coin / QCoin) has limitations: it delivers absolute values of amplitudes, is insensitive to sign, and needs phase-sensitive strategies for complex amplitudes.
- The step-wise approach to approximate linear shifts becomes inefficient if the desired precision scales as 1/N (i.e., many iterations / larger matrices are required for fine-grained approximations).
- The paper focuses on circuit (gate count) complexity but does not address pre-processing complexity needed in some state-preparation schemes (authors state they discuss only circuit complexity).
- [inferred] The proposed toolkit and algorithms have not been demonstrated on noisy hardware, so resilience to noise and error accumulation (especially with many multi-controlled gates) is unclear.
- [inferred] Resource requirements (qubit counts, depth, multi-controlled gate decomposition costs) for practically relevant problem sizes are not fully quantified.
- [inferred] The approach requires encoding amplitudes within bounded ranges (use of norms like ∥f∥∞); this restricts the class/range of functions that can be directly embedded without additional scaling.
- [inferred] Many of the building blocks (multi-controlled x-gates, deep Hadamard/Walsh layers, repeat measurements/estimations) suggest limited near-term (NISQ) applicability due to depth and noise sensitivity.
- [inferred] The methods largely treat real-valued arrays; handling arbitrary complex-valued data will need additional phase-sensitive mechanisms which are not detailed.
## Open questions
- Can efficient, problem-specific state-preparation oracles be found/constructed for the classes of arrays/functions of interest in finance, so that the pipeline becomes end-to-end efficient?
- What are the best read-out / amplitude-estimation techniques tailored to the direct-embedding encoding to recover signs and phases reliably and efficiently?
- How does the overall resource (qubit count, CNOT count, circuit depth) scale for practically relevant problem sizes in financial applications, and what are concrete thresholds for quantum advantage?
- How resilient are the proposed arithmetic operations and ordering manipulations to realistic noise models and gate errors on current/future hardware?
- What are the trade-offs between pre-processing (classical) complexity to set up state-preparation parameters and quantum circuit depth/complexity?
- How to efficiently generalize and optimize the toolkit for higher-dimensional tensors while controlling the explosion in controlled operations?
- Are there alternative embeddings or hybrid encodings that retain the algebraic advantages of direct embedding while easing loading/read-out costs?
- Can the step-wise approximations (e.g., approximated linear shifts) be improved to achieve required precision with better-than-linear overhead in N?
- How does the choice of normalization (e.g., use of ∥f∥∞) affect numerical stability, precision, and interpretability of outputs in downstream financial computations?
- What classes of financial algorithms (pricing, risk metrics, Monte Carlo integrands) can systematically leverage the presented array arithmetic toolkit to obtain provable speedups?

**Future work:**

- Study and optimize extensions to multi-dimensional arrays / tensors, accounting for the cost of additional controlled operations.
- Benchmarking and comparison studies to evaluate hardware performance and to guide practical implementations (the modular framework is intended to help make benchmarking more transparent).
- Investigate combinations of the proposed framework with alternative amplitude-amplification and estimation techniques.
- Explore practical implementations, including pre-processing costs and realistic resource estimates for financial use cases.
