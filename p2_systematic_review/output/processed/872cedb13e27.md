## Abstract summary
This preprint proposes a noise-resilient hybrid quantum-classical qGAN framework that combines WGAN-GP and MMD losses with expressive quantum ansätze (QCNN and EfficientSU2) and realistic depolarizing noise modeling to improve stability and expressivity on NISQ devices. Empirical results on discretized 2D Gaussian and log-normal targets and a European call pricing task (via Quantum Amplitude Estimation) show substantially lower Wasserstein distances, enhanced noise robustness up to ~2% depolarizing noise, and sub-1% pricing errors on IBM hardware.
## Methodology
The study develops a noise-resilient hybrid quantum-classical qGAN framework to learn and load classical probability distributions into quantum states. The generator is a parameterized quantum circuit (either a QCNN or an EfficientSU2 ansatz) whose measurement probabilities approximate a discretized target distribution; a classical discriminator (MLP) is trained adversarially or a kernel-based MMD loss is used. Two loss strategies are used: WGAN-GP (Wasserstein GAN with gradient penalty) to enforce 1-Lipschitz continuity and stabilize adversarial training, and an MMD loss computed with a FidelityQuantumKernel (ZFeatureMap) to improve multimodal coverage. Noise is modeled explicitly as a depolarizing channel applied to CX gates (Np(ρ) = (1−p)ρ + p I/2^n) and parameter-shift rules are used to obtain unbiased gradients in the presence of noise. Training is performed via PyTorch–Qiskit integration (TorchConnector, SamplerQNN) with ADAM optimizers, alternating critic and generator updates (ncritic = 5), checkpointing, and fixed random seeds. Target distributions (2D Gaussian and log-normal financial returns) are discretized on an 8×8 grid (64 bins) mapped to 6 qubits; experiments include noise sweeps across depolarizing rates and validation on IBM’s 20-qubit Boeblingen backend and simulators. Evaluation uses Wasserstein distance, KL divergence, KS tests, state fidelity, t-SNE visualizations, and an application to European call option pricing via Quantum Amplitude Estimation.

**Algorithms used:** qGAN (quantum generative adversarial network), WGAN-GP (Wasserstein GAN with gradient penalty), MMD-GAN (Maximum Mean Discrepancy GAN with quantum kernel), Parameter-shift gradient rule, Quantum Amplitude Estimation (QAE), ADAM optimizer
**Frameworks:** Qiskit (including Qiskit Machine Learning, SamplerQNN, ParamShiftSamplerGradient, SamplerV2), PyTorch (TorchConnector), Qiskit Aer (AerSampler), SciPy (metrics, distributions), scikit-learn (t-SNE), Matplotlib/Seaborn (visualization)

**Experimental setup:** Primary experiments run on local workstations (Intel Core i7-1165G7, 16 GB RAM; some verification on Intel i9 workstation) and Google Colab CPU instances (Intel Xeon). No GPU acceleration used for training. Quantum experiments employed Qiskit simulators (SamplerV2 / Aer with noise injection, shots=1024) and IBM Quantum hardware (20-qubit Boeblingen) via Qiskit Runtime. Depolarizing noise was applied to CX gates with sweep of p in [0.0, 0.001, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.05].

**Dataset:** Synthetic datasets: (1) 2D isotropic Gaussian (µ=0, Σ=I2) discretized on an 8×8 grid; (2) Log-normal asset return distribution for financial experiments generated via the Black–Scholes style formula ST = S0 * exp((r − σ^2/2)T + σ * sqrt(T) * Z) with S0=100, r=0.05, T=1, σ=0.2. The log-normal data were used for European call pricing via QAE.

## Findings
- [supported] Integrating WGAN-GP and MMD losses with expressive quantum ansätze (QCNN and EfficientSU2) improves training stability and expressivity over the baseline RY–CZ qGAN in the reported experiments.
- [supported] The EfficientSU2–WGAN-GP (reps=8) model achieved the lowest reported Wasserstein distance on the noiseless 2D Gaussian benchmark: WS = 0.00271 ± 0.00041 (mean ± std, run=20).
- [supported] MMD loss provided very stable training dynamics while WGAN-GP was more volatile but effective; learning rates lr=1e-4 (WGAN-GP) and lr=1e-3 (MMD) were reported as optimal in the experiments.
- [supported] Noise sweeps (depolarizing channel applied to CX gates) up to p = 0.05 were performed, and the EfficientSU2–WGAN-GP maintained fidelity > 0.88 up to p = 0.02 and WS = 0.0046 ± 0.0004 at p = 0.02 (mean ± std, run=20).
- [supported] The proposed quantum models significantly outperformed the RY–CZ qGAN baseline in Wasserstein distance (reported ~88% reduction in WS error relative to RY–CZ in this work's experiments).
- [supported] Parameter-shift gradients remain useable under the considered depolarizing noise model; the authors report relative gradient bias < 5% for p < 0.05 and stable ADAM optimization under those conditions.
- [supported] In a financial application (European call option, S0=100, K=100, T=1, σ=0.2, r=0.05) the EfficientSU2–WGAN-GP loaded distribution combined with Quantum Amplitude Estimation produced prices close to classical Monte Carlo: simulator 5.44 ± 0.03, IBM Boeblingen hardware 5.47 ± 0.07, vs classical Monte Carlo 5.43 ± 0.02 (values reported in paper).
- [supported] The authors report reduced shot counts in simulation for the QAE pipeline using their qGAN loader, and an empirical log–log error vs shots slope consistent with quadratic scaling for QAE vs classical Monte Carlo in their experiments (figure claim of ~100× fewer shots for same precision in simulation).
- [supported] The authors report an 18% lower Value-at-Risk (VaR) estimation error for EfficientSU2–WGAN-GP vs QCNN on IBM 20-qubit hardware at p ≈ 0.02 (reported in paper).
- [supported] t-SNE and classical statistical tests (Wasserstein, KL, KS) were used to validate the learned distributions; EfficientSU2–WGAN-GP shows higher KS p-values and tighter t-SNE clusters in the reported experiments.
- [speculative] The paper asserts that the framework 'establishes a practical path toward quantum advantage in generative modeling'—this broader claim is presented as an implication of the results rather than an asymptotic proof.
- [speculative] Suggestions for future directions—federated quantum learning, fault-tolerant integration with error-corrected QAE, scaling to higher-dimensional (3D+) modeling with tensor-network ansätze, and quantum–diffusion hybrids—are proposed as potential extensions, not empirically demonstrated here.
- [supported] Checkpointing, TorchConnector + SamplerQNN integration, and param-shift-based differentiation with injected noise models enabled end-to-end differentiable training in the authors' PyTorch–Qiskit implementation.
- [supported] The authors present convergence behavior: EﬀSU2 R8 converged in 480 ± 90 epochs (reported), and deeper EﬀSU2 (reps=8) outperformed shallower versions in their depth sweep under p = 0.01 noise.

**Results summary:** The preprint reports a hybrid quantum-classical qGAN framework that integrates WGAN-GP and MMD losses with more expressive quantum ansätze (QCNN and EfficientSU2). In their 6-qubit, 8×8-grid experiments on 2D Gaussian and log-normal distributions, the EfficientSU2–WGAN-GP (reps=8) variant attained the lowest Wasserstein distances and higher statistical-fidelity metrics versus a RY–CZ baseline, retained reasonable fidelity under depolarizing noise up to p≈0.02, and when combined with Quantum Amplitude Estimation produced option prices on simulator and IBM hardware close to classical Monte Carlo. The authors demonstrate improved training stability, report concrete noise-sweep evaluations, and claim shot-count reductions for QAE in simulation. They propose this direction as a step toward practical quantum-enhanced data loading and finance applications while noting scalability and hardware throughput limitations.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The paper presents empirical small-scale and simulated evidence consistent with known theoretical benefits of Quantum Amplitude Estimation (quadratic scaling in number of samples) and shows shot-reduction in simulation and close-to-classical pricing on hardware. However, the asymptotic quantum advantage is not demonstrated at scale on fault-tolerant hardware; the claim of a practical pathway to quantum advantage is an extrapolation from small-scale/simulated results and is thus classified as speculative.
## Limitations
- Scalability: experiments were limited to n ≤ 20 qubits; higher-dimensional scaling remains untested (author-stated)
- Kernel design: choice between RBF and quantum kernels requires comprehensive hyperparameter sweeps and task-specific tuning (author-stated)
- Hardware throughput: queue delays (~5 hours/model) and limited access to runtime resources restrict large-scale benchmarking (author-stated)
- Ethical considerations: potential for adversarial exploitation of quantum synthetic data in finance requires oversight (author-stated)
- [inferred] Reliance on a simple depolarizing noise model (depolarizing error applied to CX gates) — other realistic noise channels (e.g., amplitude damping, correlated noise) were not explored
- [inferred] Experiments primarily used synthetic 2D Gaussian and log-normal distributions — generalization to real-world, high-dimensional financial datasets is untested
- [inferred] Limited statistical sampling in noisy experiments (fewer runs for noise benchmarks) and finite-shot estimation may underrepresent variance in hardware deployments
- [inferred] Computational cost and training time: models required long training (e.g., ~480±90 epochs), potentially expensive for larger systems
- [inferred] Remaining optimization challenges: although mitigations (QCNN, WGAN-GP) help, issues like barren plateaus and hyperparameter sensitivity may persist for deeper/larger circuits
- [inferred] Dependence on particular ansatz choices (EfficSU2 reps=8 selected) — trade-offs between expressivity and noise-resilience across different hardware/topologies are not fully characterized
## Open questions
- How can the approach be scaled to substantially higher qubit counts and higher-dimensional distributions while preserving noise resilience?
- Which quantum kernel designs and kernel hyperparameters generalize best across different financial tasks and distributional families?
- How do other, more realistic noise models (e.g., amplitude damping, phase damping, correlated/non-Markovian noise) affect training stability and final fidelity compared to the depolarizing model used?
- What are the precise trade-offs between circuit depth/expressivity and noise robustness across QCNN, EfficSU2, and other ansatz families on real hardware?
- To what extent do results on synthetic 2D distributions transfer to real-world financial data (multi-asset portfolios, time series with temporal dependencies)?
- How can hardware throughput and queue delays be mitigated to enable systematic large-scale empirical studies?
- What privacy, governance, and ethical frameworks are needed to oversee deployment of quantum-generated synthetic financial data?
- How can qGAN-based loaders be integrated with fault-tolerant quantum amplitude estimation at scale, and what are the resource requirements?
- Why do QCNN and EfficSU2 show differing advantages in different noise regimes (e.g., QCNN better WS at moderate noise vs EfficSU2 better fidelity at low noise), and can hybrid ansatzes exploit both benefits?
- What is the potential and practical pathway for combining qGANs with quantum diffusion or other generative paradigms to improve diversity and gradient stability?

- #limitation:data-encoding — Uses discretization to an 8x8 grid (64 bins) to make distribution loading tractable; this highlights potential encoding and resolution limits for continuous financial distributions at scale.
- #idea:hybrid-approach — Extensive engineering choices: ParamShiftSampler gradients, SamplerQNN/TorchConnector integration, WGAN-GP hyperparameters (ncritic=5, lambda=10), and multiple seeds/checkpointing to ensure reproducibility of noisy training.
## Contradictions
- #contradiction:scalability — The paper asserts a pathway toward practical quantum advantage via qGAN loaders + QAE, but all experiments are small-scale (≤20 qubits, low-dimensional benchmarks).
