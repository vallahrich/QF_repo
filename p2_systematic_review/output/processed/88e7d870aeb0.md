## Abstract summary
The paper proposes QADQN, a hybrid reinforcement learning framework that embeds a quantum-inspired multi-head attention mechanism into a deep Q-network to identify profitable trading strategies and improve risk management. Using an LSTM pre-net and classically simulated variational quantum circuits for attention, the method is backtested on S&P 500 historical data and reports superior risk-adjusted returns (higher Sortino and Sharpe ratios, reduced drawdowns) compared to Buy & Hold and DDPG baselines. The authors note limitations including classical simulation of quantum components, evaluation on a single asset, and overlapping train-test windows.
## Methodology
The paper proposes QADQN, a hybrid quantum‑inspired Deep Q‑Network for financial trading. Input market states are encoded as an n‑day historical price matrix (log returns + OHLC‑derived indicators) and processed through an LSTM feature extractor and an MLP pre‑net. The resulting embeddings are passed to a multi‑head quantum‑inspired attention module implemented as parameterized rotation (variational) quantum circuits (simulated classically) that produce query/key/value projections. Attention weights are computed from pairwise negative squared differences of Q and K, normalized, and used to form attention‑weighted representations. A Q‑network produces action‑values; actions are selected with an Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) exploration policy. A Dual Thrust heuristic is used to initialize strategy examples in the replay buffer (imitation style). Training uses prioritized (and standard) experience replay, Huber loss targets with a target network, and parameter updates with Adam (learning rate 1e‑4), with auxiliary sign‑based update notation referenced. Key hyperparameters include replay buffer size 10,000, batch size 64, discount factor gamma=0.95, target network updates every 50 episodes, 200 training iterations and episodic interactions defined on 24‑day rolling windows. The quantum components are executed via classical simulation (Qiskit); no quantum hardware was used. Evaluation is via backtesting on S&P 500 OHLC data (Yahoo Finance) with realistic transaction cost (0.2% per trade), comparing QADQN to Buy & Hold and a DDPG baseline using metrics such as cumulative return, Sharpe ratio, Sortino ratio, and maximum drawdown.

**Algorithms used:** Variational Quantum Circuits (quantum‑inspired attention / parameterized rotation circuits), QUBO formulation (motivational / feature mapping), QAOA‑style ansatz (referenced in variational construction), Deep Q‑Network variant (QADQN), LSTM (temporal feature extractor), Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) exploration, Dual Thrust heuristic (strategy initialization / imitation), Prioritized Experience Replay, Huber loss for temporal‑difference targets, Adam optimizer, DDPG (used as baseline)
**Frameworks:** Qiskit (quantum circuit simulation), TensorFlow 2.14, Backtest (Python library)

**Experimental setup:** Implemented in Python 3.10. Quantum circuits simulated classically with Qiskit. Neural networks trained with TensorFlow 2.14 on a workstation with an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU (24 GB VRAM) and an Intel Xeon CPU. Training reported to complete within ~6 GPU‑hours for four years of S&P 500 data. Experiments used 4‑qubit quantum attention modules; no real quantum hardware was used.

**Dataset:** S&P 500 index historical OHLC price data obtained from the Yahoo Finance API. Training period: 2016-01-15 to 2020-01-15. Evaluation windows: 2019-01-16 to 2024-01-16 (partially overlapping) and 2020-01-16 to 2024-01-16 (non‑overlapping).
