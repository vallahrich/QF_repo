## Abstract summary
The paper presents a reproducible hybrid quantum-classical reinforcement learning framework based on PPO for sector rotation in the Taiwan stock market, comparing classical (LSTM, Transformer) and quantum-enhanced (QNN, QRWKV, QASA) policy/value networks with a unified feature pipeline. Empirical backtests show quantum models often achieve higher training rewards but underperform classical models on real investment metrics (cumulative return, Sharpe ratio), highlighting a reward-design misalignment and optimization instability under NISQ constraints; the authors discuss reward shaping, regularization, and validation-based stopping as remedies.
## Methodology
The authors propose a hybrid quantum-classical reinforcement learning framework for sector rotation in the Taiwan stock market. The agent is trained with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in an actor-critic setup where the actor (policy) and critic (value) networks can be implemented using either classical backbones (LSTM, Transformer) or quantum-enhanced backbones (QNN, QRWKV, QASA). Classical inputs are produced by an automated feature-engineering pipeline that computes technical indicators (moving averages SMA(10,20,50), momentum(5,10), historical volatility over rolling windows) from sector-level daily price, volume and capital-share data and stacks sequences of length 10 for temporal models. Quantum components use angle embedding to encode classical projections into variational quantum circuits (VQCs); outputs are expectation values post-measurement that are integrated into downstream classical layers. The environment provides actions as a probability distribution over 48 targets (47 sectors + dummy); the agent selects top-N sectors (N=10). The proxy reward is based on next-step sector leadership: +1.0 if a chosen sector is among the top-N sectors by capital share at t+1 and a small penalty otherwise (paper reports both -0.1 and in one place 0 as the non-success reward). All models are trained on historical Taiwan stock sector data from 2007-04-23 to 2019-12-31 (training) and backtested on 2020-01-01 to 2025-06-13 (testing) using a rolling-window simulation. Shared PPO hyperparameters and training protocol are used across model variants to ensure fair comparison. Evaluation uses financial metrics (cumulative return, annualized return, annualized volatility, Sharpe ratio, maximum drawdown) to compare classical and quantum-enhanced agents.

**Algorithms used:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE), Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), Transformer (self-attention), Variational Quantum Circuit (VQC) / Quantum Neural Network (QNN), QRWKV (quantum-enhanced RWKV variant), QASA (quantum adaptive self-attention)

**Dataset:** Sector-level Taiwan stock market data (daily), comprising 2,646 listed companies aggregated into 47 industry sectors with capital share, prices and volume. Time span: 2007-04-23 to 2025-06-13. Training split: 2007-04-23 to 2019-12-31; testing: 2020-01-01 to 2025-06-13.

- [supported] In the Taiwan sector-rotation experiments (data 2007-04-23 to 2025-06-13; train 2007-04-23 to 2019-12-31; test 2020-01-01 to 2025-06-13), classical LSTM and Transformer produced higher cumulative returns (~125.33% and ~124.29%) and Sharpe ratios (~0.88) than quantum variants.

- [supported] The implemented quantum models used small VQCs (4 qubits in the reported configs) and angle embedding; quantum components were integrated into policy/value networks.
- [supported] The observed discrepancy between training reward and backtested investment performance indicates a reward–performance misalignment: maximizing the proxy reward did not guarantee better real-world investment outcomes.
- [supported] Quantum-enhanced models showed behavior consistent with overfitting to the proxy reward and exhibited optimization instability in the NISQ-constrained setting.
- [speculative] The expressiveness of quantum circuits under NISQ constraints (shallow circuits, noise, barren plateaus) may compound training instability and generalization issues in financial RL.
- [speculative] Improving alignment between rewards and investment objectives (reward shaping), adding model regularization, and using validation-based early stopping could mitigate the reward–performance gap.

**Results summary:** The paper presents a hybrid quantum-classical PPO framework for sector rotation on Taiwan stock market data, comparing classical (LSTM, Transformer) and quantum-enhanced (QNN, QRWKV, QASA) policy/value architectures under a unified feature-processing and training pipeline. Empirically, quantum models achieved higher proxy training rewards but systematically underperformed classical models in backtested financial metrics (cumulative return, annualized return, Sharpe ratio). ## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The paper does not demonstrate a practical quantum advantage on real-world investment metrics; quantum models achieved higher proxy training rewards but worse backtested returns and Sharpe ratios. The authors present theoretical motivations for QML expressiveness but treat any advantage as conditional/speculative, contingent on better reward alignment and mitigation of NISQ trainability issues.
## Limitations
- NISQ-era hardware constraints (noise, shallow circuit depth, decoherence) explicitly limit quantum model trainability and reliability
- Barren plateau phenomena and optimization instability in variational quantum circuits impede stable training
- Proxy reward design (binary top-N capital-share reward) does not directly optimize real investment objectives such as cumulative return, volatility control, or Sharpe ratio
- Quantum-enhanced models achieved higher training rewards but underperformed on real-world investment metrics, indicating susceptibility to overfitting to the proxy reward
- Absence of standardized quantum-compatible financial environments for benchmarking and reproducible comparisons
- Expressiveness of quantum circuits can amplify reward-specific exploitation (learning spurious correlations) rather than learning robust, generalizable trading policies
- [inferred] Evaluation limited to historical backtesting (no live trading or paper trading validation), so real-world deployment robustness is untested
- [inferred] Experimental scope restricted to a single geography and dataset (Taiwan stock market), limiting conclusions about cross-market generality
- [inferred] Small-scale quantum circuits (4 qubits, few layers) used in experiments limit claims about quantum advantage at larger scales
- [inferred] Model selection and early stopping mechanisms were not used as part of the training protocol (although suggested), increasing risk of overfitting
- [inferred] Reward sparsity and binary formulation (1/-0.1) may encourage risky short-term behavior and ignore transaction costs, market impact, and other practical trading constraints
## Open questions
- Can quantum-enhanced models exceed classical models when the reward function is better aligned with long-term investment objectives (e.g., risk-adjusted returns)?
- How can reward functions be designed or shaped to reliably align proxy training signals with practical investment goals (cumulative return, Sharpe, drawdown)?
- What regularization, encoding, or architectural techniques best mitigate overfitting and instability in quantum circuits under NISQ constraints?
- How severe and general is the tendency of quantum attention mechanisms (e.g., QASA) to overemphasize spurious correlations, and how can this be controlled?
- To what extent are observed quantum-classical performance gaps attributable to hardware noise versus model/reward design?
- How do quantum advantages (if any) scale with increased qubit counts and deeper circuits, and at what point do hardware errors outweigh potential expressivity benefits?
- What validation-based early stopping, cross-validation, or out-of-sample selection strategies are most effective for QRL in finance?
- How transferable are the learned policies and findings to other markets, time periods, asset classes, or market regimes?
- Can standardized quantum-compatible financial environments and benchmarks be developed to enable fair comparisons across QML/QRL methods?

**Future work:**
- Reward shaping to better align training objectives with long-term investment performance
- Model regularization to reduce overfitting of quantum-enhanced networks
- Validation-based early stopping as a method to prevent overfitting and improve out-of-sample performance
- Explore whether quantum models can not only match but exceed classical performance when the reward signal better reflects long-term investment goals
- Investigate robust encoding and architectural choices that balance expressiveness and trainability under NISQ constraints

## Contradictions
- The paper reports that quantum models attain higher proxy training rewards but nevertheless deliver worse financial outcomes in backtests (example: LSTM cumulative return 125.33% vs QASA 93.14%, while QASA had higher training reward). This contradicts simple claims that better training reward implies better economic performance.
- Although quantum components are presented as promising, the study uses 4-qubit VQCs and simulator-based experiments, contradicting any implicit claims that the approach is currently scalable or validated on real quantum hardware.
