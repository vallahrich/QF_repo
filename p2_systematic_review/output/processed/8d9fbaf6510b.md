## Abstract summary
The paper introduces a time-series modeling framework built from a quantum walk defined via quantum Bernoulli noise (QBN). It presents the QBN-based walk, highlights its properties (including infinitely many internal degrees of freedom and classically-behaving limits), and proposes an estimation algorithm that uses the QBN-based walk to predict the next value in observed time-series data, illustrated with worked examples.
## Methodology
This theoretical paper constructs a time-series forecasting model by mapping classical time-series data onto the state evolution of a quantum-walk defined in terms of quantum Bernoulli noise (QBN). The methodology begins by recalling the QBN formalism: a probability space of Bernoulli functionals H = L^2(Ω, F, P) with canonical orthonormal basis {Z_σ}, and the associated annihilation and creation operators d_k and d_k^* satisfying anti-commutation relations and the boundedness properties stated in prior work. The QBN-based quantum walk is defined on the Hilbert space ℓ^2(Z, H) (positions × coin space H) with two shift-like operators L_n and R_n (self-adjoint) that govern time evolution; a localized initial state at position zero is assumed. For forecasting, the authors introduce a time-dependent evaluation functional V(θ) (θ in [0,1]) built from observed real-valued time-series D_n = {x_0,...,x_n} and the QBN-walk state amplitudes; minimization of V(θ) over θ yields parameter(s) θ* used to compute the quantum-walk expected position E[X_{n+1}] which is taken as the forecast for X_{n+1}. They present an explicit three-step algorithm: (1) find θ* minimizing V(θ), (2) compute E[X_{n+1}] from the QBN-walk dynamics with θ*, and (3) iterate the procedure to forecast subsequent steps; when multiple minima occur they propose averaging expectations over minimizing θ. The methodology is illustrated by worked symbolic examples for n=0,1,2 where V(θ) is computed, case distinctions analyzed, and closed-form expressions for E[X_{n+1}] are derived. The work is analytical and relies on lemmas about the QBN operators and the structure of ℓ^2(Z, H) rather than empirical experiments.
