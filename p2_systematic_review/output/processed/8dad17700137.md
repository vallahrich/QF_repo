## Abstract summary
The paper investigates solving portfolio optimization via the Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE). It maps the constrained integer quadratic problem to a QUBO and Ising Hamiltonian using binary encoding and penalty terms, empirically evaluates hyperparameters (ansatz, optimizer, penalty λ) on simulators and IBM NISQ devices, and identifies best practices (e.g., COBYLA optimizer, specific ansatz choices and λ in a moderate range) that yield solutions close to classical optima on sufficiently capable hardware without error mitigation.
## Methodology
The authors translate a constrained integer quadratic Portfolio Optimization (PO) problem into a QUBO and then into an Ising Hamiltonian whose ground state corresponds to the optimal portfolio. Integer asset holdings n are binary-encoded (per-asset binary digits di based on nmax_i = floor(B/Pi)) to produce a binary vector b (n = C b). Budget equality constraints are enforced by adding a penalty term lambda*(P''^T b - 1)^2 to the objective, yielding the QUBO. The QUBO is transformed into an Ising Hamiltonian via bi -> (1 + si)/2 and mapped to Pauli-Z operators (H = sum hi Zi + sum Jij Zi Zj + lambda (sum pi Zi - beta)^2). The Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) is used to approximate the ground state: parameterized ansatz circuits (several types/entanglement patterns tested) are executed on simulators and IBM superconducting QPUs; expectation values of Pauli strings are measured and a classical optimizer updates parameters to minimize the estimated energy. Experiments compare different ansatzes, entanglement patterns and classical optimizers, study the effect of the penalty coefficient lambda, and benchmark quantum results against a classical branch-and-bound solution (CPLEX). Both noiseless and noisy (hardware noise model) simulations are used, and runs are performed on a set of IBM real quantum devices to evaluate device-size/topology/noise effects.

**Algorithms used:** Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE), Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) formulation, Ising Hamiltonian mapping, Classical branch-and-bound (CPLEX) for benchmarking
**Frameworks:** Qiskit, IBM QASM simulator

**Experimental setup:** Simulators: IBM QASM simulator (noiseless) and QASM with imported noise model from IBM Cairo. Real hardware: IBM superconducting quantum processors (ibmq_guadalupe, ibmq_toronto, ibm_geneva, ibm_cairo, ibm_auckland, ibmq_montreal, ibmq_mumbai, ibmq_kolkata, ibm_hanoi). Experiments ran both on simulators and on these IBM cloud QPUs. Quantum circuits encoded the Ising Hamiltonian as Pauli strings and used VQE ansatz circuits (various types and entanglement patterns). No error-mitigation techniques were applied in the reported hardware runs.

**Dataset:** Historical daily closing prices for up to N=4 assets (Apple, IBM, Netflix, Tesla) obtained from Yahoo! Finance via the yfinance Python package. Time range: 2011-12-23 to 2022-10-21 (sub-intervals used for examples). Supplementary CSV 'datafinance.csv' provided with the preprint.

## Findings
- [supported] VQE can be used to solve small portfolio optimization instances (binary-encoded integer formulation) on both simulators and real IBM NISQ devices, producing solutions close to the classical optimum for the tested small problem.
- [supported] The choice of classical optimizer and ansatz strongly affects VQE performance: the Cobyla optimizer was the most stable in these experiments; PauliTwo ansatz converged fastest; TwoLocal and RealAmplitude ansatzes with linear or pairwise entanglement provided the best final solutions under noise.
- [supported] Quantum and measurement noise degrades convergence and can shift the final energy (solution quality); appropriate hyperparameter selection (ansatz + optimizer + penalty) mitigates but does not eliminate noise effects.
- [supported] The penalty coefficient λ (used to embed the budget constraint into the QUBO) requires tuning: the authors empirically identified a workable 'Goldilocks' range roughly 1 ≤ λ ≤ 10 for their problem instance; too-small λ leads to constraint violations and too-large λ dominates the objective producing sub-optimal results.
- [supported] The authors used a custom binary encoding (matrix C) to represent integer holdings; this reduces the required number of qubits versus a straightforward encoding and was used to implement the problem with 12 qubits in their experiments.
- [supported] Experiments on multiple real IBM devices showed a correlation between solution quality and hardware characteristics (quantum volume, topology); some devices (ibmq_toronto, ibmq_kolkata, ibm_auckland) produced solutions that matched the classical benchmark for the small tested instance.
- [supported] The experiments were performed without any error-mitigation techniques; nevertheless, with correct hyperparameter choices, VQE on some real devices achieved solutions very close to the exact classical solution for the small instance.
- [supported] Classical continuous Markowitz portfolio optimization has polynomial (≈O(N^3)) cost and integer/binary formulations are NP-hard / combinatorial — the paper cites these standard complexity facts.
- [speculative] The paper claims that VQE (and gate-based quantum computing) has the potential to scale to larger portfolio optimization problems and outperform classical methods as quantum hardware size/quality increases.
- [speculative] The authors argue VQE is a more general / preferable approach for portfolio optimization compared to quantum annealers (statement motivated by generality rather than proven empirical superiority).
- [supported] Ansätze that better match hardware connectivity/topology map more efficiently and incur less circuit depth overhead, improving performance on current NISQ devices (empirical observation in their experiments).
- [speculative] Future improvements in hardware (higher quantum volume, larger qubit counts, better topology) combined with topology-aware ansatz design will enable practical quantum advantage on realistic portfolio optimization instances.

**Results summary:** The study translated a constrained integer quadratic portfolio optimization into a QUBO / Ising Hamiltonian using a compact binary encoding and solved small instances via VQE on simulators (noiseless and noisy) and multiple IBM NISQ devices. Key empirical findings: optimizer and ansatz choice strongly affect outcomes (Cobyla + certain ansatzes worked best), noise degrades but does not preclude finding near-classical solutions, and tuning the penalty parameter λ is critical (empirically ~1–10). On selected real devices (e.g., ibmq_toronto, ibmq_kolkata, ibm_auckland) the VQE solutions for the small test problem matched the classical branch-and-bound result, all experiments performed without error-mitigation.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The paper does not demonstrate a quantum computational advantage for portfolio optimization; it reports parity with classical solutions on small instances and presents empirical evidence that correct hyperparameters and larger/higher-quality hardware improve VQE performance. Claims that quantum computing will solve larger realistic instances more efficiently are forward-looking and speculative, pending larger, less noisy hardware.
## Limitations
- Experiments use very small problem instances (at most N = 4 assets) and thus do not match realistic portfolio sizes (author-stated).
- All experiments were performed on NISQ hardware that is noisy and not fault-tolerant; results are therefore affected by decoherence, gate infidelity, and readout errors (author-stated).
- No error‑mitigation techniques were applied in the reported runs (author-stated), so reported performance reflects raw NISQ behavior rather than mitigated results.
- The QUBO formulation requires choosing a penalty coefficient λ to embed constraints; solution quality is highly sensitive to this choice (author-stated).
- The study uses a particular binary encoding that reduces qubit count but it is not evaluated how different encoding strategies scale or compare (author-stated/inferred).
- Ansatz and optimizer exploration is limited to a subset available in Qiskit (TwoLocal, RealAmplitude, PauliTwo) and a few optimizers (Cobyla, SPSA, NFT); other ansatz families and optimizers were not explored in depth (inferred).
- Mapping between ansatz entanglement structure and hardware topology was highlighted as important, implying sensitivity to device connectivity; this makes results hardware-dependent (author-stated).
- Benchmarks are limited to a classical branch‑and‑bound solver (CPLEX); no head‑to‑head comparison was provided with other classical heuristics at larger scales or with quantum annealers (inferred/author-stated partial).
- Scalability to larger, realistic instances (many assets, larger bit-precision per asset) is unclear — both in terms of required qubits and circuit depth and in terms of noise accumulation (author-stated/inferred).
- Using a penalty method for constraints can alter the Hamiltonian spectrum (if λ is too large), producing suboptimal results — the approach may become harder to tune for other problems or larger scales (author-stated/inferred).
- [inferred] Experiments are restricted to IBM superconducting hardware; cross-vendor variability (trapped ions, photonics, other superconducting devices) was not assessed.
- [inferred] The study does not evaluate or provide automated procedures for selecting hyperparameters (ansatz depth, λ, optimizer settings) for new problems.
- [inferred] Possible effects of alternative constraint encodings (e.g., different penalty schemes, Lagrangian multipliers adaptive schemes, or hard-constraint constructions) were not investigated.

## Key ideas
- #idea:near-term-feasibility — VQE applied to a QUBO/Ising formulation of integer-constrained portfolio optimization can recover solutions close to classical optima on small instances (N up to 4 assets) when run on current IBM superconducting QPUs.
- #idea:hybrid-approach — The study uses a hybrid quantum-classical VQE workflow (parameterized ansatz on QPU + classical optimizers like COBYLA/SPSA) and identifies classical optimizer and ansatz choices as critical hyperparameters.
- #idea:near-term-feasibility — Empirical best practices reported: COBYLA optimizer, particular ansatz/entanglement patterns, and a moderate penalty coefficient lambda (~1–10, hardware runs used lambda=10) yield the most reliable results without applying error mitigation.
- #limitation:qubit-count — Integer-to-binary encoding of asset holdings (n = C b) inflates qubit requirements (experiments used up to 12 qubits) and constrains problem size to very small portfolios.
- #limitation:noise — Hardware noise and device topology materially affect VQE performance; noisy simulations and multiple IBM backends show device-dependent degradation and variance in results.
- #limitation:data-encoding — Binary encoding and penalty-term enforcement produce many Pauli-string terms and increase circuit/measurement overhead, highlighting overheads in encoding classical financial data onto qubits.
## Contradictions
- The paper promotes near-term applicability of VQE for portfolio optimization but only demonstrates results on very small problem sizes (N=4, 12 qubits) with binary encoding that scales poorly—this contrasts optimistic near-term-readiness implications and raises scalability concerns (contradiction:scalability).
