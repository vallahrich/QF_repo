## Abstract summary
The paper evaluates the impact of compressing amplitude-encoding data embeddings using shallow variational quantum circuits (approximate amplitude encoding) on downstream quantum algorithm fidelity in a noiseless setting. It compares several ansätze, finds the RY-CNOT ansatz performs best for real data, demonstrates substantial depth compression with minor fidelity loss for small qubit counts, and observes that certain algorithms (e.g., QFT) are robust to these approximations, suggesting further study including noise effects.
## Methodology
The authors study how compressing amplitude encoding via shallow variational quantum circuits (VQCs) affects the fidelity of downstream quantum algorithms in a noiseless simulation setting. They (1) construct target amplitude-encoded quantum states corresponding to several synthetic classical data distributions (uniform with small noise, periodic/sparse superpositions of sine waves with varying frequency content, and random normal-distributed amplitudes); (2) design and compare four VQC ansaetze (single-qubit gates without entanglement; a Walsh-Hadamard + linear CZ + RX layer; an RY rotation layer with CNOT entangling layer (RY-CNOT); and a hardware-efficient alternating 2-qubit subcircuit ansatz); (3) train those VQCs with varying numbers of layers to approximate the target amplitude states, studying trainability and required depth as a function of data complexity and number of qubits; and (4) benchmark the effect of using the approximated (compressed) input states as inputs to standard quantum algorithms (Grover search / amplitude amplification, amplitude estimation, and the quantum Fourier transform) by comparing final-state fidelities against the ideal algorithm outputs obtained using exact amplitude encoding. The study focuses on state fidelity metrics, reports depth-compression ratios (e.g., 2x with negligible fidelity loss, up to 5x with several-percent infidelity), and highlights that the RY-CNOT ansatz performed best for real-valued data in fidelity vs. depth tradeoff. Standard algorithm implementations from MQT Bench were used for the benchmarking; all experiments were reported in a noiseless setting (statevector-level comparisons).

**Algorithms used:** Approximate Amplitude Encoding (AAE), Variational Quantum Circuits (VQC) training, Grover search (amplitude amplification), Amplitude estimation, Quantum Fourier Transform (QFT)
**Frameworks:** MQT Bench

**Experimental setup:** Noiseless simulation study using VQC training to approximate amplitude-encoded states for small numbers of qubits. Standard algorithm implementations were taken from MQT Bench. Training and benchmarking were performed by varying ansatz type and number of layers; fidelity of approximated input states and final algorithm output state fidelities were compared to ideal amplitude encoding.

**Dataset:** Synthetic datasets: (1) nearly-uniform probability distribution with low-level noise (to avoid trivial uniform superposition); (2) periodic signals represented as sparse sums of sine waves with varying numbers of frequencies (Fourier-domain-sparse); (3) random amplitude vectors sampled from a normal distribution. No real financial dataset is specified in this paper (reference to prior AAE applications to financial markers is cited, but not used here).

## Findings
- [supported] Standard amplitude encoding circuits can be approximated by training shallow variational quantum circuits (VQCs), enabling substantial compression of the embedding layer depth.
- [supported] The RY-CNOT variational ansatz gave the best approximation fidelity vs. depth-compression tradeoff for real-valued data in the authors' experiments.
- [supported] For generic data and a small number of qubits, amplitude encoding circuits can be compressed by at least a factor of 2 in depth with only marginal (<0.01%) loss in final algorithm state fidelity (noiseless simulations).
- [supported] Higher degrees of compression (up to ~5x) are possible with approximation infidelities under ~5% in the noiseless experiments presented.
- [supported] For approximations with high (>95%) fidelity, running downstream algorithms did not increase the input-state infidelity in the noiseless benchmarks.
- [supported] The number of variational layers required to approximate target states grows with the number of qubits but the observed growth is sub-exponential, implying improved depth-compression scaling compared to exact amplitude encoding.
- [supported] Certain algorithms, notably the quantum Fourier transform (QFT), showed robustness to input-state approximation and may reduce some approximation-induced errors (empirical observation in this work).
- [speculative] The authors suggest QFT's observed robustness may be due to it acting like a high-frequency noise filter (analogy to classical signal processing).
- [supported] The study was performed in a noiseless simulation setting; the effect of device noise on VQC training and compressed-embedding algorithm execution remains to be systematically explored.
- [speculative] Compressing embedding layers via variational circuits could enable execution of larger algorithms on NISQ devices by reducing required circuit depth.
- [supported] Prior work (Nakaji et al. 2022) established approximate amplitude encoding (AAE) that can implement data loading with circuit size polynomial in qubit number; this paper builds on and empirically explores related compression approaches.
- [speculative] The authors recommend systematically classifying quantum algorithms by robustness to input-data compression as a useful direction for future work.

**Results summary:** In noiseless simulations the authors trained several VQC ansaetze to approximate amplitude-encoded data and benchmarked standard quantum algorithms on the compressed inputs. The RY-CNOT ansatz performed best for real-valued datasets. They observed that the variationally compressed embedding can reduce depth by at least ~2x with negligible loss (<0.01%) in final-state fidelity for small qubit counts, and up to ~5x compression with ≤~5% infidelity. The number of variational layers needed grows with qubit count but sub-exponentially, suggesting better scaling than exact amplitude encoding. Downstream algorithms (notably QFT) were often robust to these approximations; for input fidelities >95% the algorithms did not amplify infidelity. The study was limited to noiseless settings and highlights the need to study noise effects and to classify algorithms by robustness to compressed embeddings.

**Performance claims:**
- Amplitude encoding circuit compressed by >=2x depth with <0.01% loss in final state fidelity (noiseless simulations, small qubit counts).
- Up to ~5x depth compression achievable with approximation infidelities under ~5% (noiseless simulations).
- For input-state fidelity >95%, subsequent algorithms did not increase infidelity (empirical observation in benchmarks).

- Work focuses on amplitude encoding only; other classical-to-quantum embedding schemes are not evaluated.
- [inferred] Comparison limited to a set of four ansaetze (and emphasis on the RY-CNOT ansatz); generality across a broader space of ansaetze and architectures is not established.
- [inferred] Input data distributions used for training (noisy uniform, periodic sparse sine waves, Gaussian random) may not reflect the full diversity or structure of real-world financial datasets.
- [inferred] Benchmarks are limited to a few standard algorithms (Grover, amplitude estimation, QFT); robustness for other important algorithms (e.g., VQE, QAOA, finance-specific circuits) is not assessed.

- [inferred] Reported compression factors (e.g., up to 5x) and sub-exponential layer growth are empirical for tested sizes; no formal guarantees or proofs of scaling behavior are provided.
- VQC training was assumed/performed under idealized (noise-free) conditions; training behaviour and optimization challenges (e.g., barren plateaus) in realistic settings are not studied.
## Open questions
- How does noise (device error rates, decoherence) affect VQC training and the execution of quantum algorithms when using approximate embeddings?
- Which classes or families of quantum algorithms are robust to approximate/compressed input state encodings, and which are sensitive? (need a systematic classification)
- How do input data complexity, structure, and dataset size influence the required VQC depth and achievable compression fidelity?
- How do other embedding schemes (beyond amplitude encoding) respond to approximation/compression and what are their trade-offs?
- [inferred] How do approximate embeddings impact task-specific performance in real-world applications (e.g., concrete finance workload metrics), beyond state fidelity?
- [inferred] What are the trade-offs between ansatz choice, trainability, circuit depth compression, and final algorithmic performance across increasing qubit counts?
- [inferred] Can the apparent self-correcting / noise-filtering behaviour observed for QFT be formalized, quantified, and generalized to other algorithms or circuit structures?

**Future work:**
- Establish a systematic classification of quantum algorithms with regard to their robustness to input data compression.
- Investigate more quantum algorithm families and methodically consider other embedding schemes beyond amplitude encoding.
- Understand the effect of noise on VQC training and on the execution of algorithms with approximate embeddings (to assess performance on NISQ hardware).
- Extend studies toward execution and training under realistic hardware conditions to gain insight into practical applicability on NISQ devices.
