## Abstract summary
This paper explores the integration of quantum computing into actuarial risk modeling, proposing a hybrid quantum-classical architecture that leverages algorithms such as Quantum Amplitude Estimation and HHL for faster, more accurate estimation of tail risks, optimization and predictive analytics. Experiments on simulated datasets and IBM quantum hardware indicate potential speedups (reported up to ~65% in some tasks) and improved convergence versus classical Monte Carlo methods, while noting current NISQ hardware limitations and the need for hybrid workflows and further research.
## Methodology
The study uses a hybrid quantum-classical methodology to integrate quantum algorithms into actuarial risk modeling. Actuarial data (mortality tables, claim frequencies, market data, catastrophe scenarios) are first preprocessed classically (cleaning, normalization, feature selection) and then encoded for quantum processing using encodings such as amplitude encoding and angle encoding. The quantum pipeline comprises: identification of dominant risk factors (data-driven feature selection with quantum resources), quantum-enhanced Monte Carlo simulation using Quantum Amplitude Estimation (QAE) to estimate tail probabilities and expected values, QAOA (and related variational quantum algorithms) for optimization tasks such as reinsurance structuring and capital allocation, and quantum machine learning models for predictive tasks (mortality trends, lapse rates, claim/fraud prediction). Results from the quantum components are post-processed classically, calibrated against traditional actuarial models for interpretability and regulatory compliance, and evaluated on accuracy (comparison to historical records and classical simulation), computational efficiency (speedup, convergence), and robustness (sensitivity to quantum noise and stability). Experiments were run on a hybrid quantum-classical processor environment, using both quantum simulators and an IBM superconducting-qubit quantum system, and compared against classical Monte Carlo and classical optimization baselines.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum Amplitude Estimation (QAE), Quantum Monte Carlo (quantum-enhanced Monte Carlo), Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA), Variational Quantum Algorithms (VQAs), Quantum Machine Learning (QML), Harrow-Hassidim-Lloyd (HHL) (mentioned), Quantum annealing (mentioned)

**Experimental setup:** Hybrid quantum-classical setup: quantum algorithms executed on a hybrid processor using IBM superconducting-qubit hardware and quantum simulators; classical resources used for preprocessing, post-processing, optimization augmentation and validation.

**Dataset:** Synthetic insurance claim datasets, catastrophe risk scenario datasets, financial market loss distributions, and publicly available financial risk benchmarks.
