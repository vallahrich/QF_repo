## Abstract summary
This conference paper compares quantum algorithms (QAOA, VQE, Exact Minimum Eigen Solver) with classical methods (Mean-Variance Optimization, Genetic Algorithm, Simulated Annealing) for portfolio optimization using three years of daily returns for 20 NSE stocks. Evaluation using MSE, RMSE, and MAE shows quantum approaches (notably QAOA and the Exact Minimum Eigen Solver) achieved lower prediction errors and improved risk-return profiles versus classical methods, and the authors suggest extensions including more assets, alternative data, and hybrid quantum-classical models.
## Methodology
The study collected historical adjusted closing prices for 20 NSE (India) equities from January 1, 2021 to January 1, 2024 via the yfinance library. Daily percentage returns were computed, missing values were excluded, and the return series were normalized and standardized. A portfolio optimization problem was formulated using expected returns (µ) and the covariance matrix (Σ) of asset returns with an objective to maximize µ^T x − q · x^T Σ x. The risk-aversion parameter q was fixed at 0.5 and the budget constraint was set to half the number of assets (budget = 10). The authors applied three quantum-based optimization methods (Exact Minimum Eigen Solver via NumPyMinimumEigensolver, Sampling VQE (hybrid variational method), and QAOA) and three classical methods (Mean–Variance Optimization solved as the quadratic objective with a budget constraint, Genetic Algorithm, and Simulated Annealing). Quantum methods were described as hybrid quantum–classical where applicable (VQE uses a classical optimizer to tune circuit parameters; QAOA iteratively optimizes circuit angles). Performance was assessed by comparing optimal portfolio weights, portfolio returns, and error metrics (MSE, RMSE, MAE) relative to a baseline equal-weight portfolio. The paper does not report low-level quantum circuit parameters, number of qubits, shots, circuit depths, or specific classical/quantum optimizer choices, nor does it report the hardware or simulator used.

**Algorithms used:** Exact Minimum Eigen Solver (NumPyMinimumEigensolver), Sampling VQE, QAOA, Mean-Variance Optimization (MVO), Genetic Algorithm (GA), Simulated Annealing (SA)
**Frameworks:** yfinance, NumPyMinimumEigensolver

**Dataset:** Adjusted closing prices for 20 NSE stocks (ADANIENT.NS, BAJFINANCE.NS, HCLTECH.NS, HDFCBANK.NS, HDFCLIFE.NS, HEROMOTOCO.NS, HINDALCO.NS, HINDUNILVR.NS, ICICIBANK.NS, INDUSINDBK.NS, POWERGRID.NS, RELIANCE.NS, SBIN.NS, SUNPHARMA.NS, TCS.NS, TATACONSUM.NS, TATAMOTORS.NS, TATASTEEL.NS, TECHM.NS, WIPRO.NS) from 2021-01-01 to 2024-01-01 (daily frequency).
## Experiment details
### Input
Source: yfinance; Size: 20 tickers, daily adjusted close from 2021-01-01 to 2024-01-01; Preprocessing: data completeness check, exclusion of missing values, calculation of daily percentage returns, normalization and standardization of returns, computation of expected returns (µ) and covariance matrix (Σ).

### Process
1) Data acquisition via yfinance and preprocessing (exclude missing data, compute daily returns, normalize/standardize). 2) Compute expected returns vector µ and covariance matrix Σ. 3) Set optimization parameters: risk factor q = 0.5, budget constraint = half the number of assets (10). 4) Formulate objective: maximize µ^T x − q · x^T Σ x with sum(x) = budget. 5) Solve using: (a) Exact Minimum Eigen Solver (NumPyMinimumEigensolver) to find optimal allocation, (b) Sampling VQE — hybrid loop where a parameterized quantum circuit is evaluated and a classical optimizer updates parameters to minimize the objective, (c) QAOA — construct problem Hamiltonian, initialize ansatz and iteratively optimize circuit parameters, (d) Classical MVO (quadratic programming), (e) Genetic Algorithm (population-based evolution of weight vectors), (f) Simulated Annealing (stochastic search with cooling schedule). 6) Collect outputs (optimal weights, portfolio returns) and compute evaluation metrics (MSE, RMSE, MAE) relative to an equal-weight benchmark. Note: implementation-level parameters such as qubit counts, circuit depths, shots, optimizer types and specific solver configurations were not reported.

### Output
Reported outputs include optimal portfolio weight vectors from each algorithm, portfolio return comparisons across algorithms, and error metrics (Mean Squared Error, Root Mean Squared Error, Mean Absolute Error) comparing predicted/optimized results to a baseline equal-weight portfolio. Results were presented in tables and figures (weights, returns, and error-metric comparisons).

- [supported] Among the classical algorithms tested, the Genetic Algorithm produced the best performance (lower MSE than MVO and SA) in this experiment.
- [supported] Mean-Variance Optimization (MVO) and Simulated Annealing (SA) produced substantially higher error metrics in this experimental setup and dataset.
- [supported] The study evaluated algorithm performance using MSE, RMSE and MAE against a benchmark (equal-weight portfolio) and compared resulting portfolio weights and returns.

**Results summary:** On the 20-stock NSE dataset (2021–2024) used in this conference study, the authors report that quantum optimization methods (QAOA, Sampling VQE, Exact Minimum Eigen Solver) produced lower prediction errors than the tested classical methods (Mean-Variance Optimization, Genetic Algorithm, Simulated Annealing). In their reported metrics QAOA and Exact MES had the lowest MSE values; the Genetic Algorithm was the best-performing classical method. The paper concludes that, within this experimental setup, quantum methods can yield improved risk-return profiles and forecast accuracy, while also noting hardware/noise limitations and recommending hybrid approaches and expanded future studies.

The claim is therefore supported by the authors' reported results for this dataset and setup; broader, generalizable quantum advantage beyond this experimental configuration is presented as prospective/speculative by the authors.
## Limitations
- Limited asset universe: study used only 20 equities from the NSE (Jan 2021–Jan 2024), restricting generalizability to larger or more diverse portfolios.
- Hardware and implementation constraints: current quantum hardware limitations (noise, decoherence, robustness) limit practical deployment and may affect results.
- Input conditioning and numerical issues: implementation challenges related to input matrix conditioning and handling of negative eigenvalues affect applicability of some quantum routines.
- Focus on simulated/experimental settings: several references and the paper emphasize theoretical or simulated experiments rather than validated live trading deployments.
- Restricted evaluation metrics: primary reported evaluation uses MSE/RMSE/MAE against an equal-weight benchmark, which may not capture full financial performance (e.g., risk-adjusted returns, transaction costs, turnover).
- Fixed problem hyperparameters: risk factor (q = 0.5) and budget (half the number of assets) were set deterministically, which may bias results and limit insight into robustness across parameter choices.
- Comparative maturity mismatch: classical commercial solvers (e.g., CPLEX) and mature classical methods can outperform current hybrid quantum approaches in some settings, indicating uneven maturity.
- [inferred] Use of quantum simulators/emulators rather than large-scale, error-corrected quantum hardware may overstate practical advantages.
- [inferred] Small sample / limited time-span may induce overfitting or fail to capture regime changes; out-of-sample and cross-validation testing are not described in detail.
- [inferred] Potential implementation/tuning differences across algorithms (quantum vs classical) could bias performance comparisons if not equally optimized.
- [inferred] Single-market focus (NSE India) and selective ticker list introduces geographic/sector bias and limits transferability to other markets.
- [inferred] Constraints common in practical portfolio management (transaction costs, market impact, liquidity, regulatory limits) were not modeled.
- [inferred] Lack of statistical significance testing or uncertainty quantification for the reported performance differences.
- [inferred] Inconsistency between stated evaluation measures (Sharpe, Sortino, max drawdown mentioned) and reported metrics (MSE, RMSE, MAE) may indicate incomplete performance characterization.
## Open questions
- Does the observed quantum advantage (lower MSE for QAOA and Exact MES) persist as the number of assets scales to realistic portfolio sizes (hundreds or thousands)?
- How do the quantum algorithms perform on real quantum hardware (with noise and limited qubit counts) compared to simulator results?
- What is the sensitivity of results to the choice of hyperparameters (risk aversion q, budget constraint, circuit depth, optimizer settings)?
- Can hybrid quantum-classical pipelines (classical preprocessing followed by quantum refinement) consistently improve robustness and scalability in production settings?
- How do quantum approaches compare against state-of-the-art classical commercial solvers (CPLEX, Gurobi) and advanced ML/optimization baselines in real-world benchmarks?
- How robust are the optimized portfolios to changing market regimes, correlated shocks, and estimation error in covariance/return inputs?
- What is the impact of incorporating transaction costs, liquidity constraints, and turnover limits on the relative performance of quantum vs classical methods?
- How should quantum circuits and ansatzes be designed/tuned specifically for portfolio optimization to balance expressivity and trainability?
- Can quantum methods efficiently handle negative eigenvalues and other numerical issues that arise in financial covariance matrices?
- What alternative or additional evaluation metrics (risk-adjusted returns, Sharpe/Sortino, VaR/CVaR, maximum drawdown) reveal about comparative performance?
- How transferable are results across different markets, asset classes (bonds, FX, derivatives), and non-equity instruments?
- What are the computational time and resource trade-offs (runtime, gate counts, classical optimizer iterations) for quantum algorithms versus classical heuristics in practice?
- How does noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) hardware error affect solution quality, and what error mitigation strategies are most effective in finance applications?

**Future work:**

- Perform sensitivity testing to verify how optimal portfolios respond to market condition changes and asset correlation shifts.
