## Abstract summary
This review examines hybrid quantum–classical machine learning architectures for scalable pattern recognition in high-dimensional, noisy datasets, emphasizing quantum feature mapping, variational quantum circuits, and integration with classical optimization. It evaluates potential gains in representational capacity and robustness against practical NISQ-era hardware limits, implementation challenges, and application domains where hybrid systems can complement classical ML.
## Methodology
This paper is a conceptual and synthetic review of hybrid quantum–classical machine learning architectures for pattern recognition in high-dimensional and noisy data environments. The methodology consists of surveying and synthesizing recent advances in quantum machine learning literature to (1) analyze theoretical foundations (e.g., quantum feature mapping and the use of high-dimensional Hilbert spaces), (2) identify architectural design principles for integrating quantum subroutines (such as variational quantum circuits) with classical workflows (including classical optimization and neural models), and (3) evaluate emerging application domains and practical challenges (noise, scalability, hardware constraints, interpretability, and cost–benefit trade-offs). The study emphasizes the complementary roles of quantum modules for feature transformation, kernel estimation, and probabilistic sampling, while classical components handle preprocessing, parameter tuning, and orchestration. The review highlights open research questions (efficient circuit design, integration of classical optimization loops, robustness to noisy data) and calls for interdisciplinary collaboration to translate theoretical work into practical applications.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum feature mapping, Variational quantum circuits (VQC), Quantum-enhanced kernel estimation, Quantum probabilistic sampling, Classical neural networks, Support Vector Machines (SVM), Classical optimization frameworks
