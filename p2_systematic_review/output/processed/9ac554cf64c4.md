## Abstract summary
This conference paper reviews the history, fundamental concepts, major technologies (hardware platforms, quantum error correction, and algorithms), and current research status of quantum computing, distinguishing gate-based quantum computers and quantum annealers. It surveys applications in encryption/communication, data computing and scientific simulation, summarizes relevant standardization activities, and discusses development trends including commercialization potential and risks to classical cryptography.
## Methodology
This paper is a non‑empirical, descriptive literature and policy review. The authors synthesize existing literature, technical reports and standards documents, and public information about national and industrial programs to provide an overview of quantum computing. Their approach consists of (1) surveying foundational academic texts and recent research (e.g., textbooks and selected papers by Nielsen & Chuang, Kaye et al., Preskill, Reiher et al.), (2) summarizing country‑level research status and industrial initiatives (United States, China, Europe, and selected Asian countries), (3) categorizing main technology areas (hardware platforms, quantum coding/error correction, and quantum algorithms), (4) reviewing relevant standardization activities (ITU‑T, ISO/IEC JTC1 activities and work items), and (5) discussing applications and development trends (communications/cryptography, data computing, quantum simulation). No original experiments, datasets, or numerical benchmarks are reported; the methodology is an integrative review of secondary sources and standards documentation to identify key technologies, algorithms, and strategic trends.

**Algorithms used:** Shor's algorithm, Grover's algorithm, HHL (Harrow-Hassidim-Lloyd) algorithm, Hamiltonian simulation algorithms, Quantum annealing / adiabatic quantum computing (as a paradigm)

## Limitations

- Decoherence that destroys quantum coherence remains a fundamental problem requiring quantum coding/error correction (author-stated).
- Quantum teleportation technology is still in exploratory experimental research and not yet mature for practical use (author-stated).
- Existing quantum annealers (e.g., D-Wave) are specialized products and do not represent general-purpose quantum computing (author-stated).

## Open questions
- How can decoherence be overcome or mitigated at scale to enable reliable, large-scale quantum computation?
- What are the most practical and effective quantum error correction and quantum coding schemes for real devices?
- How can small- and medium-scale qubit integrations be scaled to the large numbers required for general-purpose quantum advantage?
- How can high-fidelity, high-speed quantum logic gates and long-term quantum storage be realized in practical hardware?
- What classes of algorithms and applications will yield the first clear, practical quantum advantage (e.g., quantum simulation vs. optimization vs. machine learning)?
- How should classical and quantum resources be combined effectively — what hybrid algorithms and programming models will be most useful?
- When and to what extent will quantum computers be able to break currently deployed cryptographic schemes (i.e., what is the realistic timeline and risk level)?
- What are the most viable approaches to quantum-safe cryptography and how should migration strategies be devised for existing systems?
- How should QKD networks and quantum randomness (QRNG) be standardized and secured at the network and systems level?
- What pathways will lead from current prototypes and pilots to commercialized, industrial-scale quantum computing products and services?

**Future work:**
- Research and development of dedicated quantum computers (quantum simulators) and general-purpose quantum computers.
- Advance hardware technologies: integration of small/medium-scale qubits, development of high-fidelity and high-speed quantum logic gates, and realization of long-term quantum storage.
- Develop and refine quantum error correction, quantum error-averse codes, and other quantum coding techniques to improve reliability.
- Create and optimize quantum algorithms (e.g., Shor, Grover, HHL, Hamiltonian simulation) and develop new algorithms for practical applications.
- Explore and build hybrid quantum-classical algorithms and programming approaches to leverage both resources.
- Accelerate work on quantum communication technologies, including quantum key distribution (QKD), quantum secret communication, and quantum teleportation experiments.
- Drive standardization and normative work (ITU-T, ISO/IEC and related WIs) on QKD architectures, key management, security requirements, and quantum-safe algorithm guidelines.
- Promote pilot applications, industrial promotion, and commercialization efforts to move quantum technologies from lab to market.
- Research quantum-safe cryptographic methods and develop security guidelines for transitioning networks and systems.
