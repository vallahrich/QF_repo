## Abstract summary
This master's thesis develops two complementary approaches to portfolio management. The first demonstrates a market-graph-based quadratic-program K‑medoid clustering method for cardinality-constrained index tracking (with warm-starting and multi-frequency rebalancing) that achieves low tracking errors across multiple indices and volatile periods. The second proposes a combined Inverse Reinforcement Learning (parametric T-REX) and Reinforcement Learning (G-learner) framework to infer portfolio managers' reward functions and optimize allocation policies, showing promise in replicating trading styles though facing challenges with real-world data and incomplete RL results; future work includes exploring quantum computing and richer financial inputs.
## Methodology
The thesis comprises two complementary empirical studies. Study 1 formulates a market-graph k-medoids clustering approach for cardinality-constrained index tracking. For each benchmark index a complete weighted undirected market graph is constructed from daily log-returns: pairwise Pearson correlations ρij are transformed to distances dij = sqrt(0.5*(1-ρij)) and then to adjacency/robustness elements δij = 1 - exp(-1/2 * dij). A combined quadratic 0-1 formulation (QP k-medoids) is used to select k exemplars by minimizing f = β z^T Δ 1 − α (1/2) z^T Δ z with constraints z binary and sum(z)=k; α and β set to 1/k and 1/n respectively. The QP is solved on conventional hardware with the Gurobi commercial solver; a warm-start strategy seeds medoids from the previous rebalance period. Portfolios are constructed from selected exemplars using market-cap-weighting (MCW) or equal-weighting (EW). Rebalancing frequencies (annual, semi-annual, quarterly, monthly) are compared; tracking performance is evaluated by annualized tracking error (std dev of daily return differences, scaled by sqrt(252)) and dollar-value portfolio comparison. Study 2 implements an IRL→RL pipeline to infer and optimize fund-manager behaviour. A parametric T-REX (trajectory-based reward extrapolation) model is used in IRL: funds are ranked by realized returns and a parametric reward r_theta(s,a) with three tunable parameters θ={ρ,η,ω} is learned by maximizing a softmax pairwise log-likelihood (binary cross-entropy) over ranked trajectories. Training used AdamW (6000 epochs, adaptive LR) and convergence was monitored. The learned θ populate the quadratic reward used by the G-learner (a model-based continuous-state/action G-learning variant). For RL, states x_t are sector dollar holdings (N=11 sectors), actions u_t are trade changes, and transitions use x_{t+1} = diag(1 + r_t) (x_t + u_t). Reward is quadratic: R_t = −E[(P̂_t − V_t)^2] − ω u_t^T u_t with P̂_t = ρ B_t + (1−ρ) η 1^T x_t; G-learner performs backward induction to compute quadratic value/action-value functions under KL-regularized policy updates (prior policy π^(0) taken as a multivariate Gaussian based on historical trades). Expected sector returns ¯r_t are estimated with ARIMA (residuals used for covariance Σ_r). The pipeline outputs inferred reward parameters, optimized policies per fund, and out-of-sample comparisons versus benchmarks and baselines (e.g., random selector).

**Algorithms used:** Quadratic Programming k-medoids clustering (0-1 QP; QUBO-style reformulation cited), Gurobi solver (commercial MIP/QP solver), K-medoid clustering, Pearson-correlation to distance transform (d_ij = sqrt(0.5*(1-ρ_ij))) and δ_ij = 1 - exp(-1/2 * d_ij), T-REX (parametric trajectory-based reward extrapolation; IRL), G-learner (model-based RL; continuous-state/action G-learning variant), ARIMA (for expected returns estimation), AdamW optimizer (for IRL parameter training)
**Frameworks:** Gurobi (commercial solver), PyTorch (used for implementing T-REX/IRL training), Bloomberg Terminal (data source)

**Experimental setup:** All market-graph QP experiments were executed on conventional/standard hardware using the commercial solver Gurobi; each QP run used a maximum time limit of approximately three hours. Market graphs were built using a fixed three-year look-back window with a one-month moving average smoothing. Warm-starts were applied by initializing medoids with exemplars from the previous rebalance period. IRL training used PyTorch with AdamW for up to 6000 epochs (convergence observed ~3200). No quantum hardware or quantum annealer was used in the conducted experiments (quantum annealer testing was suggested as future work).

**Dataset:** Financial time-series from Bloomberg Terminal: adjusted closing prices and market capitalizations for eight global equity indices and constituents (Jan 2015–Dec 2023). Indices and cleaned constituent counts include SPX (S&P 500, N=466), SPTSX (N=209), NKY (N=224), AS51 (S&P/ASX 200, N≈184/201 after cleaning), OEX (S&P 100, N≈97), UKX (FTSE 100, N≈93), HSI (Hang Seng, N≈61), DAX (N=35). Additionally, proprietary fund-level holdings and monthly trades for four funds (labelled A–D) with SP/TSX benchmark spanning Dec 2020–Dec 2023 were used; fund data were aggregated to eleven GICS sectors. Missing data handling: drop stocks with >25% nulls, remove non-trading dates, forward/backward fill leading/trailing missing prices, compute daily log returns.
## Experiment details
### Input
Source: Bloomberg Terminal for index constituents (adjusted closing prices, market cap) Jan 2015–Dec 2023; confidential fund holdings/trades Dec 2020–Dec 2023 (aggregated monthly). Sizes: indices varied (SPX N=466; others N≈35–224). Preprocessing: remove assets with >25% missing, align/truncate non-trading dates, backward/forward fill small gaps, compute daily log-returns, construct 3-year lookback windows with one-month moving average for market graphs. For IRL/RL: fund holdings and trades converted to monthly basis points, normalized to initial fund size; expected sector returns estimated using ARIMA on training period (first 24 months) and return residuals used to estimate Σ_r. Funds ranked by total realized return for T-REX training. Cardinalities k for index tracking: k=10 for most indices, k=40 for S&P500 as per Table 2.2.

### Process
Index-tracking pipeline: (1) For each rebalance date, build market graph using 3-year lookback of daily log returns (compute ρ_ij → d_ij → δ_ij). (2) Formulate combined QP k-medoids: minimize β z^T Δ 1 − α (1/2) z^T Δ z subject to ∑ z_i = k, z_i ∈ {0,1} with α=1/k, β=1/n. (3) Solve QP with Gurobi on standard hardware with max time limit ~3 hours; use warm-start medoids from previous period when available. (4) Construct portfolio of selected exemplars with either market-cap weights (cluster-weighted per Eq.2.5) or equal weights. (5) Rebalance at different frequencies (annual, semi-annual, quarterly, monthly), compute daily returns, annualized tracking error TE = sqrt(Var(r_index − r_port)) * sqrt(252), and dollar-value portfolios; compare against random-selector baseline (100 Monte Carlo runs selecting k random stocks). IRL→RL pipeline: (1) Rank fund trajectories by total realized return over training period. (2) Parametric T-REX: optimize reward-parameter vector θ={ρ,η,ω} by maximizing pairwise softmax likelihood over ranked trajectories (Eq.3.1) trained with AdamW (6000 epochs). (3) Instantiate quadratic reward (Eq.3.3) and expected return model (ARIMA) and covariance Σ_r. (4) Run G-learner: prior policy π(0) as multivariate Gaussian (from historical trades); perform KL-regularized backward induction (value and action-value updates per Algorithm 1) to compute quadratic value/action-value coefficients and optimal time-dependent policy π*_t(ut|xt). (5) Apply learned policy out-of-sample, using realized monthly returns to step forward holdings; compare performance vs actual fund and benchmark. Parameters explicitly used: k per index, α=1/k, β=1/n, Gurobi time_limit ≈3h, T-REX AdamW epochs=6000, N_sectors=11, training horizon T=24 months for funds.

## Findings
- [supported] A quadratic-programming K-medoid clustering formulation on market graphs can construct small (cardinality-constrained) portfolios that track broad benchmark indices with low tracking error over recent data (2015–2023).
- [supported] Empirically, six of eight studied indices produced annualized tracking errors roughly ≤10% when using the market-graph QP k-medoid approach and classical solvers over 2018–2023.
- [supported] Using a warm-start strategy (initializing medoids from the previous rebalance period) mitigates computational difficulty and enables solving the QP k-medoid problem with a commercial solver (Gurobi) on standard hardware within practical time limits (solver capped at ~3 hours per run).
- [supported] Portfolio weighting method matters: Market-cap-weighted (MCW) vs equal-weighted (EW) allocations produce materially different tracking errors for some indices, though the QP selection method itself is largely robust to the weighting choice.
- [supported] Rebalancing frequency affects tracking performance and should be index-specific: annual rebalancing is often comparable and simpler in stable markets, while semi-annual or monthly rebalancing can perform better during high volatility.
- [supported] Correlation-based clustering (market-graph clusters) exhibits temporal instability, motivating periodic re-clustering/rebalancing (cites Guedes et al., 2023).
- [supported] Prior work (Hong et al., 2021) applied a QUBO k‑medoid market-graph formulation on custom hardware / digital annealing for 1992–1997 data and reported promising results.
- [speculative] Extending the QUBO market-graph formulation to run on quantum annealers (or other quantum hardware) could enable scaling to larger benchmark indices and potentially offer computational advantages—proposed as future work but not demonstrated here.
- [speculative] Constructing market graphs at the sector level is proposed as a practical approach to reduce cardinality and make the problem more tractable on classical hardware.
- [supported] The parametric T-REX inverse reinforcement learning step can infer simple reward-parameterizations from a small set of funds: in the experiment the learned parameters converged to rho=0.82, eta=2.79, omega=0.001 (indicating inferred correlation with benchmark, growth scaling, and trade-regularization magnitude respectively).
- [speculative] Combining IRL (T-REX inferred rewards) with model-based RL (G-learner) is a promising approach to generate tailored allocation recommendations and potentially improve fund policies; however, full RL (G-learner) results were not completed in this work.

**Results summary:** This thesis empirically validates a market-graph QP K‑medoid clustering approach for cardinality-constrained index tracking on classical hardware, demonstrating generally low annualized tracking errors (six of eight indices ≲10% across 2018–2023) and workable performance when using a warm-start strategy and Gurobi (solver time limit ~3 hours). The study shows that weighting (MCW vs EW) and rebalance frequency materially affect outcomes, and that correlation-based clusters are temporally unstable, motivating periodic rebalancing. In a complementary study, a parametric T-REX IRL model successfully inferred compact reward parameters from fund trajectories (rho≈0.82, eta≈2.79, omega≈0.001); the subsequent G-learner RL optimization was planned but not fully reported. The author highlights testing the QUBO formulation on quantum annealers and sector-level graphing as future directions, but provides no experimental quantum results in this work.

## Limitations
- Study constrained to standard (classical) hardware and a commercial QP solver (Gurobi); no experiments on quantum annealers or purpose-built annealing hardware were performed.
- Warm-start approach (using previous-period exemplars) was required to mitigate computational challenges for the NP-hard QP k-medoids problem.
- QUBO formulation was not executed on quantum annealing hardware; scalability to much larger indices was not demonstrated.
- Correlation-based market-graph clusters are unstable over time; clustering instability affects exemplar selection and requires rebalancing.
- Rebalancing-frequency findings are index-specific and no universal optimal rebalancing period was identified.
- Portfolio construction experiments ignored transaction costs, portfolio turnover, and other trading frictions.
- The IRL-RL experiments used a very small fund sample (four funds); fund position/trade data are confidential and limited, restricting generalizability and benchmarking.
- G-learner (RL) implementation and full out-of-sample results were not complete at time of writing.
- The action space in the RL setup did not incorporate cash (cash holdings were removed from parts of the analysis).
- Reward function for RL was implemented in a parametric quadratic form (three tunable parameters) rather than a higher-capacity model; this was chosen because of limited demonstration data.
- Cardinality (k) values for index tracking were adopted from prior work (Hong et al., 2021) rather than optimized within this study.
- Sector-level aggregation (bucketing to 11 sectors) was used to reduce dimensionality for the RL model, limiting granularity of recommendations.
- [inferred] Use of ARIMA to estimate expected sector returns and residuals to estimate covariance may be a restrictive modeling choice for expected returns in a complex market environment.
- [inferred] Reliance on a small number of reward parameters and quadratic reward structure may limit the expressiveness of inferred investor objectives.
- [inferred] Warm-starting the solver using previous exemplars may bias solutions toward past selections and reduce exploration of alternative representative sets.
- [inferred] The study did not model or incorporate unpredictable cash inflows/redemptions into the RL action space, reducing applicability to real-world fund management without further modeling of flows.
## Open questions
- How robust is market-graph clustering in capturing market dynamics under highly volatile or crisis conditions (e.g., 2020 crash) across different indices?
- How consistent is the market-graph k-medoid selection of representative stocks (exemplars) over time given cluster instability?
- What is the optimal rebalancing frequency for each benchmark index in practice, accounting for transaction costs and real-world trading frictions?
- Can QUBO/quantum annealing approaches (quantum hardware) scale this market-graph/QP k-medoid formulation to much larger indices and provide computational or solution-quality advantages?
- How should optimal cardinality (k) be determined for different indices and market regimes rather than adopted from previous studies?
- Can the combined IRL (T-REX) + RL (G-learner) framework reliably infer meaningful reward parameters from limited, real-world fund data?
- Will the G-learner, when fully implemented and tested, consistently outperform professional portfolio managers out-of-sample?
- How can cash inflows, redemptions, and other fund-level dynamics be predicted and integrated into the RL action/state space for practical fund management recommendations?
- How sensitive are the IRL-inferred reward parameters and the resulting RL policy to (i) the choice of ranking metric for demonstrations (e.g., total return vs. Sharpe) and (ii) the size/diversity of the demonstration dataset?
- What alternative methods for building the market graph (e.g., distance metrics, correlation measures) or alternative clustering strategies could improve stability and tracking performance?
- How can funds be meaningfully grouped by inferred θ parameters (trading style) when larger samples are available, and what are the implications for personalized AI recommendations?
- How to balance model complexity (e.g., moving from parametric T-REX to DNN-based IRL) with limited demonstration data to avoid overfitting while capturing richer reward structures?

**Future work:**
- Test the QUBO formulation on a quantum annealer (quantum computing hardware) to assess scalability and performance.
- Experiment with alternative methods for market graph construction and cardinality optimization.
- Construct portfolios using sector-level market graphs to address cardinality constraints and enable more feasible solutions on standard hardware.
- Refine the market graph-based model, including exploring alternative graph construction methods and methods to stabilize clustering over time.
- Complete and test the G-learner implementation and evaluate out-of-sample performance versus fund managers (retraining cadence and horizon analysis).
- Incorporate additional data dimensions into the RL framework such as cash inflows, redemptions, and other fund-level dynamics; develop reliable prediction models for these flows.
- Expand the RL framework to include more financial data and possibly more granular asset universes (beyond sector buckets).
- Use larger sample sizes of funds so the IRL-inferred θ parameters can be used to group portfolio managers by trading style.
- Explore adopting richer IRL models (e.g., non-parametric or DNN-based T-REX variants) if more demonstration data is available.
- Investigate sector-level and multi-scale graph approaches to improve scalability and tracking for large benchmarks.
