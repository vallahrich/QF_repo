## Abstract summary
The paper presents a quantum computing framework and algorithm for asset portfolio optimization, covering qubit state preparation, gate-based problem encoding, and implementation details using Qiskit. Simulation results report substantial gains in optimization speed and accuracy relative to traditional methods (claiming up to 100% optimization accuracy), while acknowledging current quantum hardware limitations and the need for further research.
## Methodology
The authors construct a quantum computing-based approach to portfolio optimization by (1) building a quantum circuit model where each asset is mapped to one qubit, (2) preparing initial superposition states using Hadamard gates, (3) encoding asset characteristics (expected return and risk) into parameterized single-qubit rotations (rx gates) and encoding inter-asset correlations via CNOT entangling gates, (4) executing the parameterized circuit on Qiskit's Aer simulator with multiple measurement shots, and (5) collapsing and classically evaluating measured bitstrings as candidate portfolio allocations against mathematical portfolio objectives (expected return, risk via covariance matrix, budget and weight bounds). The paper describes the portfolio mathematical model (portfolio expected return as weighted sum, risk via covariance matrix and trace, and budget/weight constraints) and reports simulation experiments across varying circuit sizes (5–25 qubits / 10–50 initial assets), with measured outputs including feasible-configuration ratios, optimization time, optimization accuracy and Sharpe ratio. Implementation details mention Qiskit circuit construction (.h, .rx, .cx), use of Aer simulator, and classical evaluation of measurement outcomes; no specific variational optimizer or explicit named quantum algorithm (e.g., QAOA, VQE) is specified.
**Frameworks:** Qiskit

**Experimental setup:** Simulations run on the Qiskit Aer simulator. Experiments used parameterized circuits: initial Hadamard layers, parameterized .rx rotations per asset, CNOTs for entanglement, and final measurement. Multiple shot counts were used per experiment (1024, 2048, 4096, 8192, 16384). Circuit sizes ranged from 5 to 25 qubits. Classical post-processing evaluates measured bitstrings against portfolio objectives.
