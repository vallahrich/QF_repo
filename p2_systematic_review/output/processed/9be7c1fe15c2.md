## Abstract summary
The paper proposes a hybrid quantum-classical framework that applies Quantum Machine Learning (QNNs, QSVMs and quantum-optimized algorithms including quantum annealing) to improve stock price prediction and high-frequency trading (HFT) optimization. Empirical experiments on simulators and IBM quantum processors report large improvements in execution speed and risk-management efficiency, and the work outlines a pipeline for real-time quantum-enhanced feature extraction, prediction and automated trade execution.
## Methodology
The study proposes and empirically evaluates a hybrid quantum-classical pipeline for high-frequency trading (HFT) optimization and stock price prediction. The pipeline combines classical sequence modeling (an embedding layer, multi-head attention, residual connections, layer normalization, and a feed-forward block with GELU activation) to produce a compact classical representation, which is then angle-mapped into a parameterized quantum circuit (a Quantum Neural Network, QNN) conditioned on market microstructure features. Quantum Support Vector Machine (QSVM), Quantum Annealing (QA) and Principal Component Analysis (PCA) are also used in experiments and in ablation comparisons. The quantum outputs are concatenated with the classical representation and passed through a dense layer to produce next-step price predictions and discrete trading signals (BUY/SELL/HOLD). Experiments were run on an IBM Qiskit simulator (on a classical Intel Core i7, 32 GB RAM) and validated on IBM QPUs (Falcon 5 and Eagle 16). The dataset is a Kaggle Stock Market dataset (daily OHLC, adjusted close, volume across multiple companies). Preprocessing included forward-filling missing values, Z-score outlier removal, computation of technical indicators (moving average, RSI), Min-Max scaling of price features and log transform of volume, and an 80/20 train/test split. Evaluation metrics included prediction accuracy, execution-speed/latency improvement and a risk-management efficiency score, with statistical tests (paired t-test, ANOVA) used to assess significance. An ablation study measured contributions of QSVM-only, QNN-only, QA-only and the hybrid QSVM+QNN+QA model.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum Neural Network (QNN), Quantum Support Vector Machine (QSVM), Quantum Annealing (QA), Principal Component Analysis (PCA), Variational Quantum Algorithms (VQA) (referenced), Multi-head Attention (classical), Feed-forward neural network (classical), Angle mapping (quantum feature encoding), Classical baselines: SVM, LSTM, Random Forest (referenced/comparative)
**Frameworks:** Qiskit

**Experimental setup:** Classical experiments and quantum-circuit simulation were performed using the IBM Qiskit simulator on a workstation with an Intel Core i7 CPU and 32 GB RAM. Selected models were validated on IBM quantum processors: Falcon (5 qubits) and Eagle (16 qubits). Experiments included runs on the simulator and on the real QPUs to assess noise/coherence effects.

**Dataset:** Kaggle "Stock Market Dataset" (Jacksoncrow). Historical daily stock market data comprising Open, High, Low, Close, Adjusted Close and Volume for multiple companies over several years. Technical indicators (moving average, RSI) were computed and used as features.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The paper claims substantial practical quantum advantage (large speed and risk/accuracy improvements) and provides small-scale empirical results on simulators and 5–16 qubit devices. While the reported experimental numbers support an advantage within the authors' experimental setup, broader literature and the limitations of current NISQ hardware (noise, small qubit counts, data-loading costs, and lack of proven end-to-end speedups for real-world financial pipelines) mean that a general, practical quantum advantage for HFT remains speculative rather than demonstrated. Additionally, some sweeping statements about quantum parallelism and direct large-scale data acceleration contradict established understanding and are therefore treated as disputed when stated generally.
