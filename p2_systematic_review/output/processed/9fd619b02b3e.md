## Abstract summary
This work provides a systematic exploration of applying quantum computation to insurance and actuarial science, mapping core insurance tasks (stochastic modeling, optimization, and machine learning) to quantum algorithms. It reports numerical and experimental demonstrations—including a quantum excess evaluation, variational reinsurance allocation, and a quantum Lee–Carter mortality solver on a superconducting processor—and discusses timelines, potential products, and challenges from NISQ to fault-tolerant eras.
## Methodology
The authors provide a conceptual framework mapping insurance and actuarial problems to quantum algorithms (stochastic modeling, optimization, quantum machine learning) and then perform both numerical simulations and experimental demonstrations. They (1) design a quantum algorithm to evaluate policy excess/payments for a transformed discretized loss distribution (log-normal, µ=0, σ=1) by encoding the distribution into n qubits, applying a quantum subtractor, a sequence of controlled rotations (CCRY) and measuring an ancilla to estimate the required expectation; (2) formulate a reinsurance type allocation as a quadratic binary optimization x^T V x (variance minimization), map binary variables x_j to (I - Z_j)/2 to produce a Hamiltonian, and solve it with a problem-specific variational quantum algorithm (VQA) ansatz consisting of a constraint-preserving initialization plus repeated reconfigurable beam-splitter (RBS) layers, optimizing parameters with a classical optimizer and applying measurement-based error mitigation; (3) implement a variational quantum singular value decomposition (QSVD) to solve the Lee–Carter mortality model: prepare the vectorized data matrix |D> as a quantum state and optimize two parameterized circuits (one per subsystem) to recover dominant singular vectors, then use those vectors for further analysis. Numerical simulations use the QPanda framework; hardware experiments run on the superconducting processor "Wukong." Performance is evaluated by comparison with theoretical/truncated integrals (excess evaluation), monitored loss function decrease and comparison between noisy quantum outputs and classically computed loss (reinsurance allocation), and by Frobenius norm and Kullback–Leibler divergence metrics for the Lee–Carter QSVD experiment.

**Algorithms used:** Variational Quantum Algorithm (VQA), Variational Quantum Singular Value Decomposition (QSVD), Quantum state preparation (distribution encoding), Quantum subtractor (QFT-based), Controlled rotation (CCRY) sequences, Quantum Monte Carlo / expectation estimation via measurement (probabilistic estimation), Problem-specific ansatz with Reconfigurable Beam Splitter (RBS) gates, Measurement-based error mitigation
**Frameworks:** QPanda

**Experimental setup:** Experiments were executed on a superconducting quantum processor named "Wukong" (Origin Quantum). Reinsurance allocation used 6 physical qubits (IDs reported: q29, q28, q35, q34, q41, q40) with an ansatz of 3 RBS layers; the Lee–Carter QSVD experiment used 4 qubits and 200 optimization steps. Numerical simulations (excess evaluation) used QPanda and varied n (the number of discretization qubits) in experiments reported from n=2 up to n=10. Measurement-based error mitigation was applied in hardware runs.

**Dataset:** Synthetic datasets for non-life excess evaluation (log-normal loss distribution µ=0, σ=1 truncated to [0,10], discretized to N=2^n points) and randomly generated covariance matrices for the reinsurance allocation experiments. Real-world mortality data for the Lee–Carter experiment were obtained from the Human Mortality Database (mortality.org) limited to years 2014–2017 and ages 0–14, assembled into a 4x4 matrix.

## Findings
- [speculative] Quantum computation is a promising approach to address core insurance and actuarial tasks (stochastic modeling, optimization, and machine learning) by mapping these problems to quantum state preparation, stochastic simulation, quantum Monte Carlo, variational algorithms, HHL-like linear-algebra routines, and quantum annealing.
- [supported] The authors developed and numerically validated a quantum excess-evaluation algorithm (policy excess / reinsurer payment modeling) via discrete encoding and circuit-based arithmetic; accuracy improves with the number of qubits and converges toward the truncated integral approximation.
- [supported] An experimental variational quantum algorithm (VQA) implementation for a reinsurance-type allocation problem was executed on the superconducting quantum processor "Wukong" (6 qubits); optimization reduced the measured loss function and the corresponding classically-computed (noiseless) loss moved closer to the target.
- [supported] The authors implemented a quantum singular value decomposition (QSVD) based solver for the Lee–Carter mortality-forecasting model on "Wukong" (4 qubits) with real-world mortality data; optimization reduced the reconstruction (Frobenius) error and Kullback–Leibler divergences between estimated and target singular vectors.
- [supported] The paper reports device specifications and experimental parameters for the platform used: average relaxation time T1 = 14.84 µs, dephasing time T2 = 1.85 µs, single-qubit gate fidelity ≈ 99.7%, two-qubit gate fidelity ≈ 96.3%.
- [speculative] Quantum stochastic modeling can yield exponential space reductions (via superposition encoding of distributions) and quadratic sample-complexity speedups in expectation estimation through amplitude estimation / quantum Monte Carlo (QMC).
- [supported] Practical implementations on NISQ hardware rely on variational hybrid quantum–classical approaches, problem-specific ansatz design (e.g., RBS layers to enforce constraints), and measurement-based error mitigation to handle noise and constraints.
- [speculative] In a fault-tolerant quantum computing (FTQC) era, more powerful quantum machine learning and linear-algebra-based routines (HHL, QSVD, etc.) will enable larger-scale and more realistic insurance applications (mortality forecasting, catastrophe modeling, online personalized risk evaluation).
- [speculative] There are significant engineering and algorithmic challenges to widespread application: efficient state preparation for arbitrary distributions, measurement/output bottlenecks, and hardware noise; suggested remedies include QRAM, advanced encoding, shadow tomography, and error mitigation/correction.
- [speculative] Quantum computing will enable new insurance products and services (online/personalized risk estimates, improved climate and mortality forecasting, quantum NLP for customer service), but also introduces new sources of uncertainty and systemic risk that need modeling.

## Limitations
- Field is in its infancy: lack of a systematic framework for quantum approaches to insurance (author-stated).
- Prior to this work there were few or no experimental demonstrations on quantum hardware for insurance-specific problems (author-stated).
- Limited empirical analysis on real-world insurance datasets in existing literature (author-stated).
- Significant conceptual gap between traditional actuarial models (which demand interpretability and theoretical analysis) and many quantum algorithm paradigms (author-stated).
- Data encoding is challenging in the NISQ era; efficient state preparation for arbitrary distributions can require exponential resources (author-stated).
- Quantum input/output bottleneck: n qubits encode 2^n amplitudes but each shot yields only one n-bit sample, severely limiting sampling efficiency (author-stated).
- Hardware noise and limited circuit depth (NISQ constraints) restrict the size and fidelity of realizable insurance algorithms (author-stated).
- Many quantum algorithms of interest (e.g., QMC, QSVD, HHL-based ML) require fault-tolerant resources to reach full potential; current devices are insufficient (author-stated).
- Need for specialized subroutines and infrastructure (QRAM, advanced encoding strategies, shadow tomography, error mitigation) which are not yet mature or widely available (author-stated).
- Modeling and evaluation of new risk sources introduced by quantum computation (quantum threats) is an open and unresolved challenge that increases uncertainty for insurers (author-stated).
- [inferred] Demonstrations reported are small scale (e.g., 4x4 Lee-Carter, few-qubit VQAs) and may not reflect complexity of production insurance problems.
- [inferred] Use of truncation, discretization, and post-selection/error-exclusion in examples introduces approximation and potential bias that are not fully quantified.
- [inferred] Variational algorithms used experimentally inherit optimization issues (e.g., local minima, barren plateaus) and their scalability/stability for large insurance problems is uncertain.
- [inferred] Reliance on QRAM or similar oracles creates a single-point technological dependency that may be difficult to realize practically.
- [inferred] Regulatory, auditability and explainability requirements in actuarial practice may limit direct adoption of opaque quantum models without additional methodological advances.
