## Abstract summary
The paper investigates the use of discrete-time quantum walks to model the temporal evolution of asset prices and resulting return distributions. It compares quantum-walk-generated returns to classical models (geometric Brownian motion, L´evy stable and power-law distributions), finding that quantum walks can produce asymmetric distributions, enhanced probabilities of extreme events, and richer temporal dynamics, offering greater flexibility to capture complex market behaviors.
## Methodology
This paper develops and analyses a modeling methodology based on the one-dimensional discrete-time quantum walk (DTQW) as an alternative stochastic process to model financial asset-price evolution and return distributions. The DTQW is implemented by iterating the unitary step operator V = T (C ⊗ I) where C is an SU(2) coin U_{ξ,θ,ζ} (often specialized to the U_θ form and to the Hadamard coin θ=π/4) and T is the conditional shift; the walker initial state is prepared at position j=0 with various coin initializations (examples used: (1/√2)(|↑⟩ + i|↓⟩)⊗|0⟩, |↑⟩⊗|0⟩, and (–i/2 |↑⟩ + i√3/2 |↓⟩)⊗|0⟩). Probability distributions P_j(n)=|a_j(n)|^2+|b_j(n)|^2 are obtained by iterative application of the amplitude update rules per time step (explicit update formulas given) and analyzed as functions of the number of steps n, the coin parameters (η ≡ ξ−ζ and θ), and the initial coin state. The DTQW is introduced into an asset-price stochastic differential equation by replacing the Wiener increment with a quantum-walk increment dQ(t) (dS = μS dt + σ S f(t) dQ(t)), and the paper discusses choosing an f(t) factor to control the DTQW ballistic diffusion (variance ∝ n^2). To explore the transition between quantum and classical diffusion regimes the authors introduce decoherence via two concrete numerical mechanisms: (a) stochastic disruption of links between lattice sites with per-link disruption probability p and corresponding modified amplitude update rules (broken-link model), and (b) random coin-phase noise where a coin phase ζ is sampled uniformly on [0,2π) with probability ~p per step. For decoherent scenarios results are estimated by Monte Carlo averaging (figures report averaging over 1000 realizations for decoherence cases). The methodology includes systematic parameter sweeps over η and θ (often restricted to [0,π/2] for symmetric initial conditions), over n (examples n = 20..500; many figures use n = 50 or n = 100), and over decoherence probability (p and ~p). Quantitative diagnostics computed from the resulting distributions include variance (often normalized by n^2 to expose ballistic scaling), skewness (γ1 via cumulants), and Shannon entropy H(n), and comparisons are made against classical benchmark models (geometric Brownian motion / Gaussian, Lévy-stable, and power-law tails). The authors additionally apply histogram aggregation (smoothing) to remove interference-induced spikiness in the discrete P_j(n) when comparing to typical financial return shapes. No empirical financial dataset is used; all distributions and comparisons are produced analytically or by numerical simulation of the DTQW and of reference analytic distributions.

**Algorithms used:** Discrete-time quantum walk (DTQW), SU(2) coin operator U_{ξ,θ,ζ} (including U_θ specialization and Hadamard coin), Decoherence via stochastic broken-link model, Decoherence via random coin-phase noise, Classical comparator models: Geometric Brownian Motion (GBM), Lévy stable distribution (analytical comparator), Power-law / Pareto tail models (analytical comparator)

## Findings
- [supported] Discrete-time quantum walks (DTQW) produce position/return probability distributions whose shape can be tuned by coin parameters (η, θ) and initial coin state |ψ(0)⟩ (simulations shown).
- [supported] The unitary DTQW shows ballistic diffusion: variance of the walker position grows ~ n^2 (quadratically) with number of steps n (well-known property and used in paper).
- [supported] Introducing decoherence (stochastic broken links or random coin phases) interpolates DTQW behavior from quantum (ballistic) to classical (diffusive ~ n^{1/2}) — demonstrated in simulations.
- [supported] DTQW with decoherence can yield return distributions with heavier tails than Gaussian and a higher probability of extreme events for intermediate decoherence levels (simulations with p ~ 0.1–0.3).
- [supported] Shannon entropy of DTQW position distributions depends strongly on coin parameter θ; Hadamard-like coins (θ≈π/4) maximize entropy in unitary case (simulations).
- [speculative] DTQW parameters (η, θ, |ψ(0)⟩, decoherence probability p) can be economically interpreted and mapped to market phenomena such as bias (bull/bear), volatility, and information shocks.
- [speculative] Replacing the Wiener process in geometric Brownian motion (GBM) with a quantum walk term may overcome specific limitations of GBM (asymmetry, fat tails, non-Markovian features).
- [speculative] DTQW offers a richer generative model for return distributions compared to parametric tail fits (Lévy, power law), because the walk dynamics themselves generate distributions rather than imposing a fixed functional form.
- [supported] Coin parameter θ strongly controls delocalization/variance; approximate scaling relation Var[j]/n^2 ≈ 1 − sin θ for U_θ coins is given (Eq. 17) and empirically illustrated.
- [supported] Initial coin state can bias the DTQW distribution (e.g., |↑⟩⊗|0⟩ biases toward positive increments); histogram smoothing can remove fine interference spikes for comparison with financial data.
- [speculative] DTQW can capture non-Markovian or volatility clustering properties because interference terms produce memory-like effects, so it may align better with observed autocorrelation of absolute returns.
- [speculative] The extra degrees of freedom and parameters in DTQW (vs classical models) are justified by improved flexibility in matching empirical return features; practical efficacy on real market data is not demonstrated in this work.
- [supported] Numerical examples illustrate that moderate decoherence produces non-Gaussian, leptokurtic distributions while full decoherence (p≈0.5 or ˜p=1 depending on scheme) recovers classical random walk distributions.
- [speculative] A DTQW-based financial model could be useful for risk assessment and modeling extreme events, subject to future empirical validation with historical market data.
- [supported] The paper provides explicit simulation settings used in examples (e.g., n=100 steps, averaging over 1000 realizations for decoherence models) and demonstrates qualitative effects of parameter variations.

**Results summary:** This preprint explores discrete-time quantum walks (DTQW) as a flexible generative model for financial return distributions. Through analytic discussion and numerical simulations the authors show that DTQW produce tunable, potentially asymmetric, and leptokurtic return-like distributions; coin parameters (η, θ) and initial coin state control skewness and dispersion, while introducing decoherence allows a continuous transition from quantum (ballistic) to classical (diffusive) behaviors. Shannon entropy and variance diagnostics are used to quantify distributional complexity and diffusion scaling. The work is theoretical and simulation-based; no empirical calibration or out-of-sample testing on historical asset returns is presented, and economic interpretations of parameters are proposed but remain to be validated.

**Performance claims:**
- Variance scaling: unitary DTQW variance grows quadratically with step count n; for U_θ coins Var[j]/n^2 ≈ 1 − sin θ (Eq. 17).
- Skewness range illustrated: for symmetric initial coin state and explored (η, θ) region, skewness γ1 takes negative values roughly in the range −1.04 ≤ γ1 ≤ 0 (figure reports −1.04 ≤ γ1 ≤ −0.52 and −0.52 ≤ γ1 ≤ 0 regions).
- Decoherence thresholds: in the broken-link decoherence model, full classical-like behavior is effectively reached by p = 0.5 in the paper's examples; intermediate p (e.g. p = 0.1, 0.3) produces non-Gaussian heavy-tailed distributions.
- Simulation settings: many example distributions use n = 100 time steps and decoherence-averaging over 1000 simulation runs (reported in figure captions).
- Entropy maxima: Shannon entropy of DTQW position distribution is maximized near θ ≈ π/4 (Hadamard coin) in the unitary case; decoherence shifts the θ of maximal entropy toward smaller θ (figures).
- Prototypical example: Fig. 8 compares a Gaussian (µ=0, σ=1), a Lévy stable (α=β=0.5, µ=0, c=1/√2), and a DTQW with decoherence (n=100, |ψ(0)⟩=|↑⟩⊗|0⟩, Hadamard coin, p=0.3 averaged over 1000 sims) showing heavier tails for the DTQW example.
## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The paper argues that DTQW provides a more flexible and richer generative model (asymmetry, heavier tails, tunable diffusion, memory-like interference) compared with simple classical models, but it does not present empirical validation on historical financial data nor demonstrate computational or statistical advantage on real-world tasks. Thus any claim of advantage is theoretical/speculative and contingent on future empirical testing.
## Limitations
- Introduction of additional model parameters (coin angles η, θ, initial coin state, decoherence probability p or ˜p, function f(t), number of steps n) increases model complexity and the dimensionality of calibration (author-stated).
- Paper is theoretical only; no empirical validation or performance comparison on real-world asset return data is provided (author-stated).
- The function f(t) included to control diffusion lacks a quantum-mechanical interpretation and must be chosen ad hoc to match empirical volatility/diffusion (author-stated).
- Unitary discrete-time quantum walk exhibits ballistic diffusion (variance ∝ n^2), so compensation (via f(t) or decoherence) is required to match conventional diffusive scaling seen in some financial data (author-stated).
- Raw quantum-walk-generated return distributions can be bimodal due to interference — a shape rarely observed in short-term financial returns — necessitating smoothing or decoherence to obtain unimodal-like distributions (author-stated).
- Multiple, conceptually different decoherence mechanisms exist and the choice of mechanism (and its parameterization) affects results; mapping specific decoherence choices to realistic market processes is not unique or resolved (author-stated).
- Interpretation and mapping of quantum-walk parameters to economic/financial meaning (coin angles, initial state, decoherence level) requires further work and calibration (author-stated).
- Numerical instabilities are noted near certain parameter limits (e.g., θ → π/2) in skewness calculations (author-stated).

- To which economic scenarios, market regimes, asset classes, or time scales is the quantum-walk modeling approach most applicable and advantageous?
- Can quantum-walk-based models provide tangible improvements in risk measurement (e.g., tail probabilities, VaR/ES) and forecasting compared to classical approaches?
