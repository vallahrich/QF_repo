## Abstract summary
The paper introduces the multi-Split-Steps Quantum Walk (multi-SSQW), a variational quantum simulation framework using parameterized quantum circuits and a classical optimizer to prepare quantum states that encode complex financial probability distributions. It demonstrates the method on normal, log-normal and binomial distributions and a European call option pricing example, reporting flexible modeling, stable convergence, and efficient computation for financial market simulation and quantum state preparation.
## Methodology
The authors introduce a multi-split-step quantum walk (multi-SSQW) approach for (i) preparing quantum states that encode classical probability distributions and (ii) simulating short-term financial price distributions. The framework couples a parameterized quantum circuit (PQC) with a classical variational optimizer. The PQC uses n+1 qubits (one ancilla/coin qubit and n position qubits) where the coin controls shift operations (split-step evolution ˆS+ and ˆS−) on the position register. The target classical distribution p = {p0,...,p2^n−1} is provided as training data. The PQC applies a sequence of multi-SSQW evolution operators ˆW1, ˆW2, ... (each ˆW decomposed as ˆS−ˆCθ2 ˆS+ˆCθ1) repeated for t steps (walk evolution). After measurement of the position register amplitudes, a trained distribution is computed. A classical optimizer (COBYLA) adjusts the coin/operator parameters (θ) to minimize a loss between trained and target distributions; losses used are mean-squared error (MSE) and Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence. Experiments include parameter sweeps over number of walkers (num) and number of steps, multiple repeated simulation runs (statistical evaluation over 100 runs), application to real stock daily-return histograms (binned) and to synthetic distributions (binomial and log-normal) and an option pricing use case where the trained distribution is used to compute expected payoff.

**Algorithms used:** multi-Split-Steps Quantum Walk (multi-SSQW), Split-step Quantum Walk (SSQW), Parameterized Quantum Circuit (PQC) with variational optimization, Classical optimizer: COBYLA
**Frameworks:** IBM Quantum Experience (simulator / platform mentioned)

**Experimental setup:** Quantum experiments were executed via an IBM Quantum Experience simulator (and authors acknowledge access to IBM Quantum Hub at NTU for real device experiments). PQC layout: n+1 qubits (one ancilla/coin qubit + n position qubits). Initial state prepared with U3 gates; coin operations implemented as parameterized single-qubit U3-like rotations; shift operators implemented as controlled increment (ˆS+) and decrement (ˆS−) circuits on the position register. Classical optimizer (COBYLA) runs on host CPU to update parameters based on MSE/KL loss. Statistical experiments repeated (100 runs) for parameter sweeps over number of walkers and steps.

**Dataset:** Daily stock price data from Yahoo Finance (period: 2022-01 to 2022-03). Daily returns computed and binned into 16 groups (histogram) to form target probability distributions. Additionally synthetic target distributions used: binomial distributions (p=0.3, n=31/63/127) and log-normal distribution used for European call option pricing (parameters S0, K, vol, r, T specified in experiments).

## Limitations

- Achieving efficient performance requires careful selection and tuning of parameters (number of walkers, steps, coin operator parameters), which the authors note is challenging (author-stated).
- [inferred] Scalability constraints: representing fine-grained position-space distributions requires many qubits (n+1 qubits for 2^n positions), which may be infeasible on current hardware.

## Open questions

- Which classical/quantum optimization strategies (optimizers, initialization schemes, regularization) improve training reliability and avoid barren-plateau or trapping in poor local minima?
- How can macroeconomic and fundamental data be incorporated into the multi-SSQW model in a principled way?
