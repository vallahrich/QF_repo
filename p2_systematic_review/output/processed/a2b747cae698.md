## Abstract summary
This preprint studies multi-armed bandits (MAB) and stochastic linear bandits (SLB) with heavy-tailed rewards in a quantum setting. The authors introduce a quantum truncated mean estimator (QTME) that yields a quadratic improvement in estimation error versus classical methods, and develop UCB-style algorithms (Heavy-QUCB and Heavy-QLinUCB) achieving improved regret bounds of ~O(T^{(1-v)/(1+v)}). Empirical results corroborate the theoretical improvements.
## Methodology
This work develops quantum algorithms and theoretical analyses for multi-armed bandits (MAB) and stochastic linear bandits (SLB) under heavy-tailed reward distributions (finite (1+v)-th raw moment, v in (0,1]). Methodologically, the authors (1) design quantum mean estimators for heavy-tailed one-dimensional random variables by combining truncation with the Quantum Monte Carlo (QMC) mean estimator (Montanaro 2015): a Quantum Basic Mean Estimator (QBME) for nonnegative variables and a Quantum Truncated Mean Estimator (QTME) for general variables; (2) integrate QTME into UCB-style frameworks to produce Heavy-QUCB for QMAB (uses a phased / doubling trick to schedule oracle queries and adaptively double pulls per arm) and Heavy-QLinUCB for QSLB (uses a weighted least-squares estimator with carefully chosen weights and an implicit doubling strategy to control confidence set growth); (3) provide rigorous probabilistic analyses proving oracle-query complexity and regret bounds (showing quadratic improvement in mean-estimation error versus classical truncation-based estimators and regret bounds of order ~O(T^{(1-v)/(1+v)}) under the quantum oracle model); (4) empirically validate algorithms via classical simulation of the quantum subroutines (amplitude-estimation-based mean estimation) on synthetic heavy-tailed reward instances, comparing against classical robust UCB and LinUCB baselines and reporting average cumulative regret and standard deviation over repeated trials. The methodology therefore combines algorithmic construction (novel quantum estimators, UCB variants), theoretical performance proofs, and numerical simulation experiments that emulate the quantum oracle procedures.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum Truncated Mean Estimator (QTME), Quantum Basic Mean Estimator (QBME), Quantum Monte Carlo Mean Estimator (QME, Montanaro 2015 / amplitude estimation), Heavy-QUCB (quantum UCB for heavy-tailed MAB), Heavy-QLinUCB (quantum linear UCB for heavy-tailed SLB), UCB (Upper Confidence Bound) framework, Linear UCB, Weighted least-squares estimator, Amplitude estimation (classical simulation of quantum amplitude-estimation outputs)

**Experimental setup:** Experiments were classical simulations executed on an AMD Ryzen 7 7300X CPU with 16 GB RAM. Quantum subroutines (amplitude-estimation-based mean estimation) were simulated using the closed-form measurement-output distribution from Brassard et al. (2002) as in prior work (Wan et al., 2022 / Montanaro 2015). No physical quantum hardware or vendor SDKs are reported.
## Experiment details
### Input
Synthetic datasets (no real/financial data). For QMAB: K=5 arms, three reward-mean instances S1,S2,S3 with means in [0.1,1]: S1 means {0.1,0.3,0.5,0.7,0.9}; S2 µ_a = 0.05(a-5)^2 + 0.1; S3 µ_a = -0.05(a-1)^2 + 0.9. Rewards for each arm sampled from Pareto distributions with shape parameter α = 1.05 + v (ensuring 1+v moment exists) and scale λ_i determined by µ_i via mean = α λ_i /(α-1) => λ_i = (α-1) µ_i / α. The upper bound u on the (1+v)-th moment is taken as max over arms of α λ_i^{1+v} /(α-(1+v)). Experiments set total rounds T = 1e6 and v ∈ {0.2, 0.5}. For SLB: T = 1e6, d=2, three θ* instances on the unit circle, finite action set of 50 actions equally spaced on the positive half of the unit circle. No preprocessing was applied to synthetic samples.

### Process
1) Implement classical simulator for quantum amplitude-estimation based mean estimator using known closed-form sampling distribution (Brassard et al.). 2) For each trial (100 repeats): generate synthetic rewards per the Pareto setup for MAB (or SLB generative model). 3) Run Heavy-QUCB or Heavy-QLinUCB: schedule epochs, compute/ update QTME estimates via simulated oracle queries (number of oracle queries per estimator proportional to n log^{3/2}(1/δ)), use doubling trick or weighted least squares as described to update UCB indices / parameter estimates; continue epochs until budget T is exhausted. 4) Also run baseline classical algorithms (robust UCB for heavy-tailed MAB; LinUCB for SLB) on same synthetic instances. 5) Collect cumulative regret trajectories, compute mean and standard deviation across repeats. 6) Vary failure probability δ in experiments (δ ∈ {0.005,0.01,0.002,1/T}) and v values to study sensitivity. Key algorithmic parameters used in the process (as described in paper) include QTME truncation threshold B = (sqrt(u n / log(1/δ)))^{2/(1+v)}, oracle-query budget per QTME call ≈ c n log^{3/2}(1/δ), epoch sample sizes Ns = C u^{1/(2v)} [log(m/δ)]^{1/(2v)} ϵ_s^{-(1+v)/(2v)} for SLB, and constants C and regularization λ in weighted least squares.

### Output
Primary outputs are average cumulative regret curves and standard deviations over 100 independent runs. Baselines reported: classical robust UCB (Bubeck et al. 2013) for MAB and LinUCB for SLB. Reported metrics: cumulative regret (numerical values and plots), comparisons across v values and δ settings. The experiments demonstrate lower regret for Heavy-QUCB/Heavy-QLinUCB versus the classical baselines and behavior consistent with theoretical scaling.

## Findings

- [supported] The paper reports classical simulations (using amplitude-estimation simulation techniques) that compare Heavy-QUCB to a classical robust UCB baseline on synthetic Pareto-distributed rewards (K=5, T=1e6, v in {0.2,0.5}), showing substantially lower average regret for Heavy-QUCB.
- [supported] The simulation methodology and environment are described (classical CPU simulation of the quantum subroutines via known amplitude-estimation distributions), and multiple experimental instances (different mean-gap instances S1–S3) were averaged over 100 trials.
- [speculative] The authors claim the QTME subroutine and the resulting algorithms can be applied beyond bandits wherever quantum mean estimation for heavy-tailed variables is needed.

**Results summary:** This preprint develops a quantum approach to multi-armed and stochastic linear bandits when reward distributions are heavy-tailed (finite (1+v)-th raw moment with v in (0,1]). The core technical contribution is a Quantum Truncated Mean Estimator (QTME) that attains an estimation-error scaling in the number of quantum oracle queries that is quadratically better (in exponent) than classical truncated-mean estimators for heavy-tailed data. Using QTME, the paper designs Heavy-QUCB (for QMAB) and Heavy-QLinUCB (for QSLB) with proven asymptotic regret bounds that improve the classical T-dependence by a factor ~T^{v/(1+v)} (so regret scales like T^{(1-v)/(1+v)} up to polylog factors). Classical simulations (via amplitude-estimation emulation) corroborate lower empirical regret versus a classical robust UCB baseline on synthetic Pareto rewards.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** theoretical

The paper proves asymptotic quantum improvements: a quantum mean estimator with error exponent improved from n^{-v/(1+v)} to n^{-2v/(1+v)}, and consequent bandit-regret bounds that reduce the T-exponent by a factor ~T^{v/(1+v)} (i.e., regret ~T^{(1-v)/(1+v)} rather than classical ~T^{1/(1+v)}). These advantages are theoretical (proved in the quantum oracle model) and supported by classical simulations of the quantum procedures, but not demonstrated on quantum hardware.
## Limitations
- Assumes each reward distribution has a finite (1+v)-th raw moment and that the constants v and u are known a priori (author-stated).
- The quantum reward model assumes access to a quantum reward oracle Oi (and its inverse) that encodes the reward distribution (author-stated); practical construction of such oracles is not addressed.
- The quantum oracle model and theoretical analysis are presented for finite sample spaces Ω in the oracle definition (author-stated).
- The SLB theoretical results require v ∈ [1/3, 1] (author-stated restriction used in Lemma/Theorem statements).
- Experimental evaluation uses classical simulation of the quantum mean estimation (via amplitude-estimation output distribution) rather than implementation on real quantum hardware (author-stated in experiments).
- [inferred] The algorithms rely on accurate knowledge of u to set truncation thresholds (B) and other hyperparameters; sensitivity to mis-specification of u or v is not analyzed.
- [inferred] The finite-sample-space oracle assumption limits direct application to truly continuous reward distributions unless discretization or other encoding schemes are used.
- [inferred] No quantum lower bounds or matching optimality results are provided for the heavy-tailed quantum bandit setting, so tightness of the achieved regret rates is unclear.
- [inferred] The analysis ignores realistic quantum noise, decoherence, and implementation errors; robustness of the algorithms to noisy quantum devices is not studied.
- [inferred] The polynomial (and sometimes high-order) dependence on dimension d and log factors (e.g., d^2 and multi-log terms in SLB regret) may limit practical scalability.
- [inferred] Experiments are limited to synthetic Pareto-distributed rewards and modest K/d; applicability to real-world financial (or other heavy-tailed) data is not demonstrated.
## Open questions
- Are the regret upper bounds proved (˜O(T^{(1-v)/(1+v)}) improvements) information-theoretically tight in the quantum heavy-tailed setting? In particular, what are matching quantum lower bounds?
- Can the requirement that v and u are known in advance be removed or relaxed? How to adaptively estimate v and u (or set truncation thresholds) online in the quantum setting?
- How can the quantum reward oracle model be instantiated for continuous or real-world data (e.g., financial time series)? What are practical methods to construct the required oracles?
- How robust are QTME and the proposed bandit algorithms to quantum noise and imperfect quantum operations on near-term hardware?
- Can the QTME and heavy-tailed quantum bandit methodologies be extended to other bandit variants (contextual bandits, adversarial bandits), reinforcement learning, or Bayesian optimization under heavy tails?
- Can the dependence on dimension d and the various logarithmic factors in the SLB regret be improved (tighter analysis or improved algorithms)?
- How do the proposed quantum methods perform on real-world heavy-tailed datasets (including financial datasets) compared to classical robust estimators when accounting for end-to-end costs of preparing/querying quantum oracles?
- Are there alternative quantum mean-estimation subroutines (beyond the Montanaro-style amplitude estimation + truncation) that yield better constants, lower oracle complexity, or improved robustness for heavy-tailed distributions?
