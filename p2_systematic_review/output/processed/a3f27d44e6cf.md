## Abstract summary
The paper analyzes the scaling of Hessian-entry variances at random initialization in variational quantum algorithms by expressing Hessian entries as constant-size linear combinations of shifted cost evaluations via exact parameter-shift rules. It proves that for global objectives Hessian-entry variance decays exponentially with the number of qubits, while for term-wise k-local averaged objectives implemented by bounded-depth circuits the variance obeys polynomial bounds controlled by backward-lightcone growth; the same scalings govern the finite-shot measurement cost, and numerical experiments validate the theoretical predictions.
## Methodology
The authors analyze entry-wise Hessian variance at random parameter initialization for variational quantum algorithms (VQAs) by combining exact second-order parameter-shift identities with locality and backward-lightcone arguments. Analytically, Hessian entries are written as constant-size linear combinations of a finite set of shifted cost evaluations, reducing Var_rho(H_jk) to a finite covariance–quadratic form. Two regimes are derived: (i) global objectives under a second-moment concentration assumption (or design-like mixing) yield exponential decay of Hessian-entry variance with system size; (ii) term-wise k-local averaged objectives implemented by bounded-depth, hardware-efficient ansatze admit polynomial variance bounds controlled by the backward lightcone growth VG(k+2rL)/n. Numerical validation is performed via statevector simulations: layered hardware-efficient circuits (Ry single-qubit rotations and brickwork nearest-neighbor CNOT entanglers) are initialized i.i.d. uniformly; gradients and Hessian entries are computed via exact parameter-shift formulas; variances are estimated over ensembles of random initializations and fitted to exponential or power-law models. Complementary diagnostics include Hessian eigenspectra, RMS eigenvalue, near-zero eigenvalue fraction, finite-shot shot-noise modeling (Var_total ≈ a + b/N), and matched optimization trajectories using SGD and quantum natural gradient to illustrate finite-shot resolvability effects.

**Algorithms used:** Variational Quantum Algorithms (VQAs), VQE, Parameter-shift rule (first and second order), Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD), Quantum Natural Gradient (QNG)
**Frameworks:** PennyLane

**Experimental setup:** Statevector simulations in PennyLane (exact statevector backend). Layered hardware-efficient ansatz with L layers: each layer uses a fixed nearest-neighbor brickwork CNOT pattern and n single-qubit Ry rotations (generator Y/2). Parameter initialization i.i.d. uniform on [-π,π). System sizes and depths varied; shifted-cost evaluations via exact parameter-shift formulas; Hessians computed from linear combinations of shifted cost evaluations. Finite-shot experiments performed by sampling measurement outcomes with N shots per shifted circuit when modeling sampling noise.

- [supported] Numerical experiments (statevector simulations) corroborate the predicted dichotomy: global costs show exponentially vanishing Hessian variances with n, while local averaged costs show polynomial decay over the tested ranges of n and L.
