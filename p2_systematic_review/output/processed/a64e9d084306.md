## Abstract summary
The paper implements and compares classical Monte Carlo (MC) and quantum Monte Carlo (QMC) approaches for pricing European call options, detailing quantum state preparation, payoff-circuit construction, and the role of amplitude estimation (AE). Results from simulated gate-based quantum circuits indicate QMC can reduce simulation time and improve payoff estimation relative to MC, though AE provides only limited additional speed-up and practical hardware validation and algorithmic optimization remain necessary.
## Methodology
The study compares classical Monte Carlo (MC) simulation and a gate-based Quantum Monte Carlo (QMC) approach (with Amplitude Estimation (AE)) for pricing a European call option under the Black–Scholes–Merton (BSM) log-normal model. For the classical MC experiments the authors sample the terminal asset price ST from the closed-form GBM solution ST = S0 * exp((r - σ^2/2)T + σ sqrt(T) ε) with ε ~ N(0,1), compute payoffs max(ST - K, 0) across repeated simulations, average the payoffs and discount by e^{-r(T-t)} to obtain the option price. They report an MC run with 200,000 simulated paths. For the QMC procedure they describe (and simulate on a classical machine) the three required building blocks: (1) distribution loading of a truncated, discretized log-normal distribution into an n-qubit register (amplitudes √p_i over 2^n grid points with an affine map from indices to [low, high]); (2) construction of a payoff circuit using comparators and controlled Y-rotations to implement a linearized payoff region (using a sin^2-based approximation with a tunable rescaling factor capprox); and (3) estimation of the expectation value via Quantum Amplitude Estimation (AE). The QMC implementation approximates the linear payoff by mapping values to rotations and measures the probability of the ancilla |1⟩ to recover the expected payoff. Example problem parameters used in reported runs: S0 = 4.36, K = 5, r = 3% p.a., σ = 20% p.a., T = 0.5 years. Reported outputs include estimated prices and wall-clock times for MC and the simulated QMC pipeline. No specific quantum software framework, simulator name, or quantum hardware is cited; the quantum circuits are described conceptually and simulated on a conventional computer environment.

**Algorithms used:** Classical Monte Carlo (MC), Quantum Monte Carlo (QMC), Quantum Amplitude Estimation (AE)

**Experimental setup:** Simulations performed on a traditional (classical) computer. Classical MC simulated 200,000 GBM paths. QMC circuits (state preparation, comparator, controlled rotations and AE) were constructed and executed in a simulated quantum hardware environment on the classical computer; no simulator name, backend, or hardware vendor is specified.

**Dataset:** Synthetic financial data: simulated terminal asset prices generated from the Black–Scholes–Merton geometric Brownian motion (log-normal) model. No external market dataset was used.

### Process
Classical MC pipeline: (1) sample ε ~ N(0,1) and compute ST analytically using the GBM formula; (2) compute payoff = max(ST - K, 0); (3) repeat for N = 200,000 samples; (4) average payoffs and discount by e^{-rT} to obtain option price. QMC pipeline (simulated): (1) prepare quantum state |ψ> = Σ_i √p_i |i> representing truncated/discretized log-normal distribution over 2^n grid points using a distribution-loading unitary; (2) use a comparator to mark whether mapped ST >= K and apply controlled Y-rotations to an ancilla to encode a linearized payoff into amplitudes (using sin^2 approximation with rescaling factor capprox); (3) construct operator A such that the probability of ancilla |1> equals the expected payoff a = E[f(X)]; (4) apply Amplitude Estimation to estimate a (and hence the payoff) and discount to obtain price.
