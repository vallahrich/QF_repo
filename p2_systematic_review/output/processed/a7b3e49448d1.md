## Abstract summary
This paper reviews the convergence of quantum computing and finance, surveying quantum algorithms (e.g., Shor, Grover, QAOA, VQE), quantum cryptography, quantum machine learning, and applications in portfolio optimization, risk management, and blockchain. It also discusses hardware and algorithmic limitations (NISQ devices, error correction, scalability), industry adoption barriers, and outlines a roadmap and research directions toward achieving practical quantum advantage in financial services.
## Methodology
This work is presented as a literature review / systematic review of the convergence between quantum computing and finance. The authors surveyed and synthesized existing theoretical work, algorithmic proposals, and industry reports to (1) explain quantum computing fundamentals relevant to finance, (2) summarize contemporary applications (portfolio optimization, Monte Carlo risk modeling, quantum cryptography and blockchain, and quantum machine learning), (3) outline hardware platforms and vendors, and (4) discuss technical, algorithmic and adoption challenges and future prospects. The paper is organized thematically into fundamentals, current trends (with subsections on financial modeling, cryptography/blockchain, and quantum machine learning), challenges/limitations, and future directions. The manuscript cites prior foundational and recent research and industry examples (e.g., IBM, Google, D-Wave, IonQ, Xanadu, and major banks) to support its synthesis. The paper does not report primary experiments, benchmarks, or a reproducible search protocol (no explicit databases searched, keywords, inclusion/exclusion criteria, date ranges, or PRISMA-style flow are provided), so the methodology is a narrative/systematic synthesis based on selected literature and industry sources rather than an empirical or reproducible meta-analysis.
