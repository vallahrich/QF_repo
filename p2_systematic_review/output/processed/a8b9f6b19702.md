## Abstract summary
This survey traces the evolution of portfolio optimization from Markowitz mean-variance theory to modern techniques that incorporate artificial intelligence, machine learning, alternative data, and robust risk frameworks. It concludes that advanced and hybrid approaches can improve risk-adjusted performance but entail greater computational and implementation challenges, and highlights future research directions such as quantum computing applications and cryptocurrency markets.
## Methodology
This work is a structured, narrative literature survey of portfolio optimization research. The author synthesizes seminal theoretical contributions (e.g., Markowitz, CAPM, APT, Black-Litterman) and recent developments in advanced analytics, machine learning, alternative data, robust and multi-objective optimization, and emerging computational technologies (including quantum computing). The paper is organized thematically into sections covering theoretical foundations, traditional methods, advanced analytics, risk analysis frameworks, modern optimization techniques, implementation challenges, empirical evaluations, and conclusions. The methodology consists of reviewing and summarizing prior literature, performing comparative analyses of different approaches (including discussion of empirical case studies reported in the literature), identifying strengths, limitations, research gaps, and suggesting future research directions. No original empirical experiments, datasets, or computational implementations were performed as part of this survey.

**Results summary:** This conference survey documents the progression of portfolio optimization from classical mean-variance and equilibrium-based models toward data-driven frameworks leveraging machine learning, alternative data, robust and multi-objective optimization. The authors report that empirical studies generally show machine learning and advanced optimization approaches can improve risk-adjusted returns relative to traditional methods, while also noting significant implementation challenges such as computational burden and data quality. The paper advocates hybrid approaches that combine established financial theory with modern computational techniques and flags future research opportunities—particularly explainable AI, cryptocurrency markets, and quantum computing—though it provides no empirical evidence that quantum advantage has been realized in finance.
