## Abstract summary
This preprint presents quantum algorithms specialized for evaluating exponential and Gaussian functions that require only a small number of multiplications, making them efficient for both NISQ and fault-tolerant contexts. The methods substantially reduce gate (Toffoli) and qubit counts compared with prior work (e.g., Haener et al.), with example reductions from 15,690 to 912 Toffoli for the exponential and from 19,090 to 704 for the Gaussian, and modest space requirements (as low as ~70 logical qubits) for realistic applications.
## Methodology
This is a theoretical and algorithmic study that develops efficient quantum circuits to evaluate the exponential f(x') = exp(-α x') and Gaussian f(x') = exp(-α x'^2) functions on a quantum computer. The authors (1) transform the fixed-point domain x' to a dimensionless integer grid x and decompose x into binary qubits, enabling exponentiation to be replaced by a sequence of controlled multiplications by precomputed constants Ai = exp(-α Δ 2^i); (2) present a basic algorithm composed of d controlled multiplications (one per domain qubit) and then refine it to reduce space and gate complexity via two key ideas: (a) transform successive Ai in-place so only one n-qubit constant register is needed (reducing space to O(n) for overwriting multipliers), and (b) truncate the number of multiplications m to the smallest index where Ai falls below the representable fixed-point resolution (m = min(d, floor( log2(n / log2(1/A)) ) + 1)), giving m = O(log n) in important regimes; (3) provide specialized, NISQ-oriented circuit realizations using fixed (unsuperposed) multipliers to hard-wire multiplier bits into the circuit (reducing controlled-addition counts by ∼2× on average), exploit truncated overwriting ripple-carry controlled additions (Takahashi adder) and controlled copy cascades for efficiency, and detail a space-saving alternative that uncomputes intermediate results (a simple reversible 'pebbling' style strategy) to trade extra multiplications for reduced qubit usage; (4) analyze resources using two implementation-independent metrics—multiplication count and Toffoli-count—derive closed-form resource formulas for Toffoli cost and qubit counts (including additional small costs for OR gates, Fredkin/SWAP, ancilla) and asymptotic scaling (including Karatsuba/Gidney scaling when large-n multiplication algorithms are used), and (5) benchmark and compare the proposed circuits (gate-saving and space-saving variants) numerically against the state-of-the-art piecewise polynomial method of H¨aner et al. (which uses domain subdivision, Remez-optimized polynomials, and parallel polynomial evaluation) for representative NISQ parameter sets (e.g., d=7,n=21 and d=8,n=32), reporting Toffoli and qubit counts and discussing applicability to applications such as first-quantized quantum chemistry.

**Algorithms used:** Binary decomposition exponentiation via controlled multiplications (fixed-point), Fixed-multiplier controlled multiplication (hard-wired multiplier bits), Overwriting vs non-overwriting multiplication strategies (QFT-style referenced), Takahashi overwriting controlled ripple-carry adder (controlled addition), Space-saving uncompute/pebbling strategy (reversible uncompute of intermediates), Asymptotic Karatsuba-style multiplication (Gidney implementation referenced), Reversible OR / Toffoli-based primitives, Remez polynomial approximation (referenced for comparison/Häner method)

## Findings
- [supported] The paper presents quantum algorithms to evaluate exponential and Gaussian functions that require only a (generally) small number of multiplications, making multiplications the dominant cost metric.
- [supported] For representative NISQ parameter values (d=7, n=21) the paper reports a reduction in Toffoli count for the exponential function from 15,690 (reference H¨aner et al.) to 912, and for the Gaussian function from 19,090 to 704 (based on analytic resource estimates and circuit constructions in the manuscript).
- [supported] The authors give concrete qubit resource estimates and report that some realistic NISQ implementations can be realized with as few as ≈70 logical qubits (space-saving variant).
- [supported] A refined algorithmic analysis shows that the number of required controlled multiplications m often scales as O(min(d, log n)) rather than O(n), with an explicit formula m = min(d, floor(log2(n / log2(1/A))) + 1) (paper Eq. (11)).
- [supported] The paper provides explicit Toffoli-count formulae for their gate-saving and space-saving exponentiation circuits (e.g., Eqs. (20) and (22)) and detailed circuit decompositions using truncated fixed-point arithmetic and Takahashi additions.
- [supported] The authors describe a space-saving algorithm that reduces space from O(m n) to O(sqrt(m) n) registers at the cost of increasing the number of multiplications by less than a factor of two (they present exact counting rules and examples).
- [speculative] The authors argue these methods are practical for NISQ and also applicable in fault-tolerant contexts; the claim is prospective (based on resource estimates) rather than experimentally demonstrated.
- [speculative] The paper suggests that, when combined with the Cartesian-component-separated (CCS) approach, the new exponential/Gaussian evaluator could make CCS highly competitive for first-quantized quantum computational chemistry (QCC); this is a forward-looking claim based on comparative resource estimates but not experimentally validated.
- [speculative] The authors recommend using 'total multiplication count' as a primary, implementation-independent gate complexity metric for algorithms dominated by multiplications; this is a methodological proposal supported by argumentation rather than experimental proof.

**Results summary:** This preprint introduces specialized quantum algorithms to evaluate exp(−α x′) and exp(−α x′2) with much lower resource costs than a general-purpose polynomial/subdomain approach (H¨aner et al.). By decomposing fixed-point inputs into binary-weighted controlled multiplications by precomputed constants and applying two refinements (in-place transformation of constant factors to reduce space and truncation/early termination to reduce multiplications), the authors derive circuits and resource formulas showing dramatic reductions in Toffoli gate counts and qubit requirements for representative NISQ parameters (e.g., d=7, n=21). They present both a gate-saving and a space-saving implementation, exact Toffoli-count formulae, and show asymptotic behavior of the method, arguing applicability to both NISQ and fault-tolerant regimes and potential relevance for first-quantized quantum chemistry.

**Performance claims:**
- Exponential: Toffoli count reduced from 15,690 (H¨aner et al.) to 912 for a representative NISQ case (d=7, n=21, domain 0≤x′<100).
- Gaussian: Toffoli count reduced from 19,090 (H¨aner et al.) to 704 for a representative NISQ case (d=13, n=24, domain 0≤x′<100).
- Space: Some space-saving implementation variants require as few as ≈70 logical qubits (example: exponential, 10^-7 accuracy, domain 0≤x′<100 reported as 71 qubits and 1409 Toffoli gates).
- Parameter formula: number of multiplications m = min(d, floor(log2(n / log2(1/A))) + 1) (paper Eq. (11)), implying m often grows like O(log n) for fixed A.
- Asymptotic: With Karatsuba-like multiplication, exponentiation gate cost scales as O(n^{log2 3} m) and space as O(n m); with the refined method m may be O(log n), yielding improved asymptotic behavior (paper discussion).
- Space-saving trade-off: the alternative algorithm reduces space from O(m n) to O(sqrt(m) n) while increasing multiplication count mss so that m < mss < 2m (exact recipes and table of values provided).
- Comparison reference: the paper cites an estimated Toffoli cost for the inverse-square-root (Coulomb) function from H¨aner et al. as 134,302, to motivate relevance for quantum chemistry comparisons.

## Limitations
- Method depends on the cost of quantum multiplications; multiplications represent the overall computational bottleneck (author-stated).
- Space and gate/resource trade-offs depend strongly on the particular multiplication algorithm chosen (author-stated).
- Asymptotic advantages (e.g., using Karatsuba/Gidney) only appear for very large n (thousands), which is beyond typical NISQ parameters (author-stated).
- Comparisons with the H¨aner et al. method rely on Toffoli-count metrics and specific multiplication implementations; direct apples-to-apples comparisons are sensitive to choices such as multiplication subroutine and assumed domain xmax (author-stated).
- The presented NISQ-oriented implementations assume fixed-point arithmetic and specific choices of d and n (e.g., d=7, n=21); results and resource estimates may not generalize to other precisions or to floating-point representations (author-stated).
- The non-overwriting multiplication strategy used for comparisons increases qubit requirements relative to overwriting (QFT-based) multiplication; choice of multiplication paradigm affects space estimates (author-stated).
- The space-saving alternative reduces qubit count at the cost of additional multiplications (up to nearly doubling); this trade-off may be unfavorable depending on hardware constraints (author-stated).
- The method assumes Ai values can be precomputed and transformed (X⊗p) cheaply; while noted to be inexpensive relative to multiplications, the practicality depends on bit patterns and may vary (author-stated).

## Open questions
- How will future improvements in quantum multiplication algorithms (e.g., better than schoolbook or Karatsuba) change the practical costs and preferred implementation choices for exponentiation on quantum hardware?
- What is the practical performance of the proposed gate-saving and space-saving circuits on real NISQ hardware, considering noise, connectivity, and gate fidelities?
- How do error accumulation and finite-precision effects in fixed-point arithmetic impact the numerical accuracy of the evaluated exponentials/Gaussians in practice?
- How does the choice of domain interval x'_{max} and parameter A impact the optimal selection of d and n and the overall resource trade-offs for realistic applications?
- Can the method be generalized efficiently to complex-valued α (unitary exponentials) or other functional forms beyond exponentials and Gaussians?
- What pebbling or reversible-computation strategies beyond the simple space-saving scheme presented can further reduce qubit requirements without prohibitive gate overhead?
- How do alternative multiplication paradigms (QFT-based overwriting multipliers, different controlled-add schemes) affect both the resource counts and suitability for direct comparison with other methods?
- How to best integrate this exponentiation approach into larger quantum algorithms for quantum computational chemistry (QCC), notably in the CCS approach, and how does it compare to implementing inverse-square-root directly?
- What are the detailed fault-tolerant resource costs (e.g., T-counts, logical qubits under error correction) when this approach is deployed in an error-corrected setting?
- How sensitive are the claimed Toffoli-count savings (e.g., reduction by an order of magnitude vs H¨aner) to variations in implementation details, parameter choices, and hardware constraints?
