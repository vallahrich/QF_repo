## Abstract summary
This review examines the potential implications of quantum computing for financial risk management, covering fundamentals such as qubits, superposition, and entanglement, and surveying quantum algorithms like quantum Monte Carlo methods and quantum annealing. It highlights applications in portfolio optimisation, risk assessment, derivative pricing and credit modelling, while discussing technical, regulatory, and talent challenges and outlining directions for future research.
## Methodology
This article is a theoretical literature review and conceptual survey rather than an empirical study. The authors synthesize and discuss existing literature on quantum computing fundamentals (qubits, superposition, entanglement), current hardware and algorithmic developments, traditional financial risk management approaches, and potential quantum-enabled applications (portfolio optimisation, risk assessment, derivative pricing, credit risk modelling). The paper draws on published reviews, arXiv preprints, and domain papers cited throughout to summarize potential quantum algorithms and their theoretical implications, identify technical and regulatory barriers, and propose future research directions. No systematic search protocol, dataset, experimental implementation, software framework, or reproducible computational experiments are described in the text; the work is conceptual and descriptive.
