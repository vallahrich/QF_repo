## Abstract summary
The paper benchmarks two variational quantum approaches (QAOA and SamplingVQE/VQE-style ansatzes) for QUBO-formulated portfolio optimization across different ansatz architectures, circuit depths, and asset universes using real 2024–2025 market data. It finds that despite algorithmic convergence, many quantum-optimized portfolios fail practical financial criteria, and proposes an Expert Analysis Evaluation framework to have finance professionals assess economic soundness and market feasibility, highlighting the need to integrate expert judgment into quantum-assisted investment pipelines.
## Methodology
The authors formulate a mean–variance portfolio selection problem as a QUBO by encoding the binary selection vector x ∈ {0,1}^n, adding a quadratic penalty to enforce the cardinality constraint 1^T x = B, and mapping the resulting QUBO to a cost Hamiltonian HC via xi = (1 − Zi)/2. They solve the mapped Hamiltonian using two variational quantum approaches: the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) and a SamplingVQE-style variational algorithm. SamplingVQE is tested with multiple ansatz choices (TwoLocal, EfficientSU2, PauliTwoDesign, RealAmplitudes). Experiments use two problem sizes (n = 4 and n = 10 qubits, representing two asset universes) and sweep circuit depths {2,4,6,8,10}. Each configuration is run with five random seeds; measurement shots are fixed at 1024; the classical optimizer used is COBYLA. Convergence is monitored via the expectation value of HC (cost) and the iterative difference ΔC_t = C(θ_t) − C(θ_{t−1}). The most probable bitstrings are collected and interpreted as portfolios; portfolio returns are computed from historical price data and evaluated out-of-sample on the subsequent month. Finally, a human Expert Analysis layer is applied to the top-ranked quantum solutions to assess liquidity, sector diversification, and practical suitability.

**Algorithms used:** QAOA, SamplingVQE (Sampling VQE / VQE variant)
**Frameworks:** Qiskit, Qiskit-Aer, qiskit-finance (Qiskit Finance module), yfinance (for data retrieval)

**Experimental setup:** Simulations run on Qiskit QASM simulator (Qiskit Aer) using 1024 shots per circuit. Ansatze tested: TwoLocal (Rx,Ry,Cz), EfficientSU2, PauliTwoDesign, RealAmplitudes. QAOA uses the standard alternating-operator ansatz. Classical optimizer = COBYLA. Circuit depths tested = {2,4,6,8,10}. Each experiment repeated with five random seeds.

**Dataset:** Historical stock price data from Yahoo Finance (via yfinance) covering Dec 2, 2024 – May 30, 2025 (six months). Two asset universes: 4-asset set {AAPL, GOOG, MSFT, TSLA} and 10-asset set {AAPL, GOOG, MSFT, TSLA, AMZN, NVDA, GS, MS, NKE, KO}. Out-of-sample test period: June 2–20, 2025.

## Findings
- [supported] QUBO formulation (risk-return with budget constraint via quadratic penalty) was implemented for 4- and 10-asset portfolio selection and mapped to Pauli-Z Hamiltonians for variational algorithms.
- [supported] Both QAOA and SamplingVQE (multiple ansatz) were evaluated on noiseless QASM simulations across five depths (2,4,6,8,10), five random seeds, and 1024 shots.
- [supported] RealAmplitudes, TwoLocal, EfficientSU2, and PauliTwoDesign ansatz show smoother and more stable convergence than QAOA in the reported simulations.
- [supported] QAOA exhibited higher variance and instability (especially at low depth) relative to the VQE-style ansatz in these experiments.
- [supported] PauliTwoDesign improved its objective from approximately −0.85 (depth=2) to ≈−1.0 (depth=10) in the 4-asset experiment (reported behavior).
- [supported] RealAmplitudes reached convergence in fewer than 100 classical optimizer evaluations for the 4-asset cases reported.
- [supported] Increasing circuit depth generally required more evaluations to converge and, in some cases, reduced variance across runs for several ansatz architectures (TwoLocal, PauliTwoDesign, RealAmplitudes) in the 10-asset experiments.
- [supported] Convergence of the quantum objective does not guarantee financially viable portfolios: many algorithmically optimal or frequently sampled portfolios included assets (e.g., AAPL or TSLA) that underperformed in a subsequent short-term period.
- [supported] In the 4-asset case the GOOG+MSFT portfolio (identified by some ansatz at depth 2) produced a positive forward one-month return (0.95% in June 2025), while many other generated 4-asset combinations produced negative forward returns.
- [supported] In the 10-asset experiments deeper circuits (depth=10) tended to produce more diversified and financially plausible portfolios, although not universally across all ansatz.
- [supported] The authors implemented an Expert Analysis Evaluation framework: domain experts reviewed top quantum-generated portfolios for liquidity, sector diversification and investor suitability, and this filtering aligned better with short-term forward returns.
- [speculative] Quantum computing 'provides a potential advantage' for the NP-hard combinatorial portfolio selection problem — the paper argues potential scalability benefits but presents only small-instance simulator results, not a proven advantage on larger instances or hardware.
- [speculative] Results on noiseless simulators may not translate directly to near-term quantum hardware because of noise and decoherence (authors state this limitation but did not test hardware).
- [supported] Algorithm outputs are sensitive to historical-data-only modeling; absence of forward-looking/exogenous variables led to portfolios that sometimes fail pragmatic financial criteria.
- [supported] Specific ansatz and depth choices materially affect which portfolios are high-probability under the trained quantum state; different ansatz/depths produced different favored bitstrings.

**Results summary:** The paper formulates mean-variance portfolio selection as a QUBO and evaluates QAOA and SamplingVQE (with RealAmplitudes, TwoLocal, EfficientSU2, PauliTwoDesign) on 4- and 10-asset instances using noiseless QASM simulation across multiple depths and seeds. VQE-style ansatz generally showed smoother and more stable convergence than QAOA, with certain ansatz (RealAmplitudes, PauliTwoDesign) performing notably well in convergence behavior. However, good algorithmic convergence did not always correspond to financially sound portfolios: several high-probability outputs included underperforming or highly volatile assets, and only some generated portfolios produced positive short-term forward returns (evaluated in June 2025). Deeper circuits tended to help produce more diversified portfolios in larger asset sets but could worsen interpretability in small, highly correlated sets. The authors introduce an Expert Analysis Evaluation stage to filter and validate quantum-generated portfolios, arguing that domain expertise is necessary to achieve practical financial viability.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The paper asserts quantum computing 'provides a potential advantage' for NP-hard portfolio optimization and demonstrates scalable candidate generation on small noiseless simulator instances, but it does not empirically demonstrate a computational advantage over classical methods on larger problems or on noisy hardware. Thus any claimed advantage remains speculative in this work.
## Limitations
- Portfolios produced by the quantum algorithms often violate essential financial criteria such as adequate diversification and realistic risk exposure (author-stated).
- Quantum models rely solely on historical price data and do not incorporate forward-looking information or external variables like geopolitical risk or macroeconomic indicators (author-stated).
- Convergence of the quantum cost function does not guarantee financially viable or practically interpretable portfolios (author-stated).
- Experiments were performed on a simulator (QASM); real quantum hardware would introduce noise, decoherence, and other device non-idealities not accounted for in results (author-stated).
- QAOA exhibited instability and high sensitivity to circuit depth and initialization, leading to high variance in outputs (author-stated).
- Deeper circuits require more classical evaluations to converge and may reduce interpretability despite increased expressibility (author-stated).
- The study is limited to very small problem sizes (4 and 10 assets), leaving questions about scalability to realistic portfolio sizes unanswered (author-stated).
- The expert-evaluation stage is applied as post-processing rather than integrated into the optimization; this creates a human-in-the-loop bottleneck and subjectivity (author-stated/inferred).
- Only a single risk-aversion parameter (q = 0.5) and a heuristic penalty (alpha = n/2) were used, limiting exploration of sensitivity to investor profiles and penalty tuning (author-stated).
- Optimization used a single classical optimizer (COBYLA) and fixed shot count (1024), limiting assessment of optimizer- and sampling-related effects (author-stated/inferred).
- Ansatz selection was restricted to a few Qiskit-provided architectures; broader ansatz design space and hyperparameter search were not explored exhaustively (author-stated/inferred).
- The QUBO formulation used binary (0/1) selections with equal unit prices; continuous weighting of assets, transaction costs, liquidity constraints, and market impact were not modeled (author-stated and [inferred]).
- Validation of portfolio viability was limited to a short out-of-sample period (one month of June 2025), constraining robustness assessment across different market regimes (author-stated).
- Sampling variability (probabilistic nature of quantum outputs and finite shots) leads to selection uncertainty; this stochasticity complicates reproducibility and reliability (author-stated/inferred).
- [inferred] The penalty-encoded constraint handling (cardinality only) may not scale or properly enforce more complex real-world constraints (sector caps, turnover limits, regulatory constraints).
- [inferred] The assumption of unit asset prices and full budget usage is unrealistic and omits portfolio weighting design choices and capital allocation realities.
- [inferred] No transaction costs, slippage, or rebalancing dynamics were included, which can materially affect practical portfolio performance.
- [inferred] The mapping to qubits via binary variables precludes direct handling of fractional asset weights without more complex encodings.
- [inferred] The study does not establish when/if the quantum approach provides advantage over classical heuristics/solvers for these instances.
## Open questions

- What are effective methods to encode richer practical constraints (liquidity, sector limits, transaction costs, turnover, regulatory constraints) into QUBO/Hamiltonian form for quantum algorithms?
- How does performance and portfolio quality scale with larger, more realistic asset universes (hundreds or thousands of assets) on simulators and on actual quantum hardware?

- What is the systematic procedure for choosing penalty strengths (alpha) and risk-aversion parameters (q) to balance constraint satisfaction and economic objectives?
- Which ansatz architectures, entanglement patterns, and circuit depths yield the best trade-off between expressibility, trainability, and financial interpretability across different problem instances?
- Can quantum approaches be extended to directly optimize weighted (continuous) portfolios rather than binary selection, and what encodings are efficient for that?
- How can one quantify and compare any computational or solution-quality advantage of quantum methods vs classical optimization heuristics for portfolio optimization?
- How can the stochasticity of quantum outputs and finite-shot sampling be managed to provide stable, reproducible portfolio recommendations?
- How to automate or semi-automate the expert-evaluation step to reduce subjectivity while preserving domain constraints and interpretability?
- How sensitive are generated portfolios to the choice of classical optimizers, initialization seeds, and measurement budgets?
- How can probabilistic forecasting, alternative risk models, and investor behavioral or ethical preferences be formally integrated into the quantum optimization pipeline?

## Key ideas
- #idea:near-term-feasibility — The paper benchmarks QAOA and SamplingVQE on real 2024–2025 market data for small instances (n=4 and n=10) and finds algorithmic convergence but many resulting portfolios fail practical financial criteria (liquidity, sector diversification, investor suitability).
- #idea:hybrid-approach — Uses a hybrid quantum-classical loop (parameterized ansatz + COBYLA classical optimizer) and explicitly proposes an Expert Analysis Evaluation layer (human-in-the-loop) to assess economic soundness of quantum-generated portfolios.
- #limitation:qubit-count — Experiments are limited to 4 and 10 qubits/assets, restricting conclusions about performance on realistic portfolio sizes and broader market universes.
- #limitation:simulation-only — All experiments run on the Qiskit Aer QASM simulator (1024 shots) with no real quantum hardware results reported.
- #limitation:data-encoding — The study uses a direct QUBO mapping (xi = (1-Zi)/2) and assumes unit asset costs/cardinality constraints without exploring more scalable or efficient encoding strategies; implications for larger problems are not addressed.
## Contradictions
- contradiction:scalability — Although variational algorithms (QAOA, SamplingVQE) converge on small instances (n=4, n=10), the generated portfolios frequently lack financial viability; this challenges narratives that NISQ variational methods are ready to scale to practical portfolio optimization tasks.
- contradiction:classical-vs-quantum — The paper provides no classical optimization baselines and reports that many quantum-ranked portfolios are economically unsound, undermining implicit claims that current quantum approaches already outperform or match classical heuristics for portfolio selection.
