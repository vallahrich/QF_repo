## Abstract summary
This comprehensive review surveys classification schemes for topological quantum materials, covering both fully gapped and gapless systems. It emphasizes the role of nonspatial symmetries (time-reversal, particle-hole, chiral) and spatial symmetries (notably reflection), and explains mathematical tools used in classification — homotopy groups, Clifford algebras, K-theory and non-linear sigma models — while discussing bulk-boundary/defect correspondence, model systems, experiments, and the impact of interactions including collapses of noninteracting classifications.
## Methodology
This paper is a theoretical and pedagogical review rather than an experimental study. The authors synthesize and analyze prior literature to present classification schemes for topological quantum matter. Their methodology is a comprehensive, concept-driven literature survey and theoretical synthesis that (1) identifies the relevant symmetry constraints (time-reversal, particle-hole, chiral, and spatial symmetries such as reflection), (2) frames single-particle (Bloch and Bogoliubov–de Gennes) Hamiltonians and defect Hamiltonians within those symmetry classes, and (3) derives topological classifications using multiple complementary mathematical and field-theoretic tools. Primary mathematical methods discussed and employed in the review include homotopy-group analysis of classifying spaces, Clifford algebra extensions, and K-theory (including defect K-theory and the Grothendieck construction). Topological invariants are constructed using differential-geometric quantities: Chern numbers, winding numbers, Chern–Simons invariants, Fu–Kane Z2 invariants and Pfaffian formulas. The authors also use index-theorem reasoning to relate bulk invariants to boundary/defect zero modes (bulk–boundary and bulk–defect correspondences). For robustness and disorder effects they survey non-linear sigma models and Anderson (de-)localization analyses. For interacting systems they review approaches such as Matrix Product State representations and projective symmetry representations in (1+1)D, gauging/orbifolding symmetries, detecting quantum anomalies (perturbative and global), vortex-condensation and symmetry-preserving surface topological order constructions, braiding-statistics after gauging symmetries, group cohomology/supercohomology, and cobordism-based classifications. The paper cross-checks classification results by showing consistency across these methods and illustrates concepts via concrete model Hamiltonians and references to experimental realizations.

## Findings
- [speculative] Topological phases of noninteracting fermions are exhaustively classified by the ten Altland–Zirnbauer symmetry classes (the 'ten-fold way'), determined by presence/absence and algebraic properties of time-reversal, particle-hole and chiral symmetries.
- [speculative] For fully gapped free-fermion systems (and related topological defects) the possible topological phases in each symmetry class follow a periodic table exhibiting Bott periodicity: period 2 for complex classes and period 8 for real classes.
- [speculative] The primary topological invariants distinguishing gapped phases are integer Chern numbers (for even total dimension) and winding numbers (for odd total dimension); Z2 descendants arise via dimensional reduction and symmetry constraints (examples: Fu–Kane invariants).
- [speculative] Bulk-boundary (and bulk-defect) correspondence: nontrivial bulk topology implies robust gapless boundary or defect modes (e.g., chiral/helical Dirac or Majorana surface states, Fermi arcs, flat bands).
- [speculative] Topological classifications can be derived and understood using multiple mathematical frameworks: homotopy groups, K-theory, Clifford algebras, and nonlinear sigma models (NLσMs) including topological terms.
- [speculative] Bogoliubov–de Gennes (BdG) superconductors naturally implement particle-hole constraints, and realize five of the AZ classes; Majorana representations are useful but unpaired Majorana zero modes are rare and topologically protected in special circumstances.
- [supported] Experimentally realized topological materials include quantum spin Hall systems (HgTe/CdTe), 3D topological insulators (Bi2Se3 and related compounds), topological crystalline insulators (SnTe), Dirac semimetals (Na3Bi), and Weyl semimetals (TaAs), with surface states observed by ARPES and transport signatures.
- [speculative] Spatial (crystalline) symmetries such as reflection/rotation extend the ten-fold classification and protect topological crystalline insulators/superconductors (TCIs/TCSs); mirror Chern numbers and related invariants protect surface modes on symmetry-preserving faces.
- [speculative] Gapless topological materials (nodal superconductors, semimetals) are classified similarly to gapped phases but by codimension p = d − d_F, with stability rules depending on whether Fermi surfaces lie at high-symmetry points or are related by symmetries.
- [supported] Examples of topologically protected gapless features and their consequences: (i) Weyl nodes act as sources/sinks of Berry curvature with Fermi-arc surface states (observed in TaAs); (ii) nodal lines and point nodes in superconductors lead to surface flat bands or arc states (theoretically and in some experiments).
- [speculative] Nonlinear sigma models plus topological terms classify Anderson localization/delocalization at boundaries; topological terms (WZW/Z2 terms) protect delocalized boundary modes against disorder in many symmetry classes.
- [speculative] Interactions can modify or 'collapse' noninteracting classifications: notable examples are the collapse Z → Z8 for 1D class BDI Majorana chains and Z → Z16 for 3D class DIII topological superconductors, demonstrated via interacting constructions (vortex condensation, surface topological order, projective symmetry analyses).
- [speculative] Gauging/orbifolding symmetries and examining quantum anomalies or braiding statistics of gauged theories are powerful diagnostics of interacting SPT phases and can detect obstructions to realizing symmetric, gapped, nondegenerate surfaces.
- [speculative] Open theoretical frameworks for interacting SPTs include group cohomology/supercohomology constructions and cobordism approaches; these explain many but not all interacting SPT phases and are active research areas.

**Results summary:** This review provides a comprehensive theoretical framework for classifying topological quantum matter, both gapped and gapless, in noninteracting (mean-field) fermion systems using symmetry (Altland–Zirnbauer) classes. It explains primary topological invariants (Chern numbers, winding numbers), descendants (Z2), and the bulk-boundary/defect correspondence, and surveys mathematical derivations via homotopy, Clifford algebras and K-theory, and field-theoretic NLσMs. Extensions to topological crystalline phases (reflection, rotation symmetries) are given, along with a classification of gapless semimetals and nodal superconductors (by codimension). The review discusses experimental realizations (e.g., HgTe, Bi2Se3, SnTe, Na3Bi, TaAs) and details how interactions can reduce non-interacting classifications (illustrated by Z→Z8 and Z→Z16 collapses), describing methods to probe interacting SPTs such as gauging/orbifolding, anomaly arguments, and braiding-statistics.

**Performance claims:**
- Periodic classification structure: complex classes repeat with period 2 and real classes with period 8 (Bott periodicity).
- Noninteracting classification of 1D BDI Majorana chains reduces from Z to Z8 under interactions (Fidkowski–Kitaev result).
- Noninteracting classification of 3D class DIII topological superconductors reduces from Z to Z16 when interactions are included.
- Chern and winding numbers serve as integer topological invariants for even/odd total dimensions respectively; Z2 invariants arise as descendants.
- Weyl semimetals exhibit Fermi-arc surface states associated with pairs of Weyl nodes of opposite chirality (experimentally observed in TaAs).

## Limitations
- The review focuses primarily on non-interacting or mean-field Hamiltonians; interacting systems are only briefly surveyed and not comprehensively classified.
- The ten-fold (K-theory) classification and many topological invariants rely on assumptions such as translation symmetry and/or spectral gaps; some constructions (e.g. several Z2 invariants) require special gauge choices or global bases that may be obstructed in realistic/disordered systems.
- Weak topological phases (translation-symmetry-dependent) are not fully represented in the main periodic table; the table shows only strong invariants.
- Spatial-symmetry extensions are discussed mainly for reflection and some point groups; a complete classification incorporating all space-group (especially nonsymmorphic) and magnetic space-group symmetries is lacking.
- Some topological invariants and derivations assume large-band (stable) limits; fragile or small-matrix-dimension 'accidental' topologies are not classified.
- Many topics were omitted or only sketched due to space constraints (examples: symmetry-enriched topological phases, fractional topological insulators, Floquet topological insulators, and detailed interacting classifications).
- [inferred] Experimental realization: although many theoretical proposals exist, experimental realization and control of many predicted topological superconductors and interacting SPTs remain challenging and are not guaranteed by the review.
- [inferred] Computational/practical limitation: for complex materials and realistic models, computing the relevant topological invariants (especially in presence of interactions and disorder) can be difficult or infeasible with the methods emphasized in the review.
- [inferred] Sensitivity: many crystalline-symmetry-protected boundary modes rely on spatial symmetries and therefore can be fragile in the presence of symmetry-breaking disorder or lattice imperfections.
## Open questions
- How do interactions change the non-interacting classifications in general? Which non-interacting topological classes collapse when interactions are included and what is the complete interacting classification?
- What is the complete classification of non-interacting Hamiltonians when all (magnetic) space-group symmetries are included, in particular nonsymmorphic symmetries?
- What are the full sets of interacting SPT phases (and symmetry-enriched topological phases) in dimensions d > 1, including fermionic and bosonic cases?
- Can we construct comprehensive effective field theory descriptions that capture correlated (interacting) topological phases and their response functions (electromagnetic, thermal, gravitational)?
- Which realistic materials realize interacting SPT phases, fractional topological insulators, or topological superconductors beyond mean-field descriptions?
- What are the effects of bulk disorder on topological phases beyond perturbative treatments, and under what conditions do crystalline-protected surface states remain delocalized when symmetries are only respected on average?
- How generally applicable are the diagnostics (orbifolding/gauging, quantum anomalies, braiding statistics) for detecting interacting SPT order across different symmetry groups and dimensions?
- Can the methods used to argue collapse (e.g., vortex condensation, surface topological order) be made fully systematic to enumerate interacting phases in higher dimensions?
- How can one reliably detect and manipulate boundary Majorana modes and other exotic boundary excitations in experiment, while accounting for interactions, disorder, and realistic device constraints?

**Future work:**
- Develop a deeper theoretical understanding of fractional topological phases and correlated SPT phases in d > 1.
- Produce realistic material predictions and proposals for experimental realization of interacting SPTs, fractional topological insulators, and topological superconductors.
- Develop effective field theory descriptions for correlated and interacting topological phases, including response theories (electromagnetic, thermal, gravitational).
- Complete the full classification of noninteracting Hamiltonians including all (magnetic) space-group symmetries, with emphasis on nonsymmorphic groups.
- Engineer topological electronic states in heterostructures (e.g., involving iridates or other strong spin-orbit materials) and optimize interfaces to realize proposed phases.
- Improve material quality and synthesis methods to obtain truly insulating bulk samples for focus on surface/edge phenomena.
- Study the robustness and delocalization of crystalline-symmetry-protected surface states under disorder that only respects spatial symmetries on average.
- Investigate omitted topics noted by the authors, such as symmetry-enriched topological phases, fractional topological insulators, and Floquet topological insulators, in greater detail.
- Develop experimental control and manipulation techniques for topological currents and boundary modes (using electric fields, magnetic fields, strain, etc.) for potential device applications.
