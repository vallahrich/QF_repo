## Abstract summary
The paper develops a method to estimate account-level lifetime loss and attrition distributions using multihorizon survival models (incorporating competing risks of default and prepayment) on Freddie Mac mortgage data and classical Monte Carlo simulation. It finds the resulting lifetime default distributions fit a lognormal well, so only tens of simulations are needed for tail-risk estimates, enabling near real-time use, and it explores quantum Monte Carlo (QAE/Grover-based) approaches that could offer further speedups but require more algorithmic and hardware advances to handle the full competing-risks formulation.
## Methodology
The study builds a loan-level forecasting and uncertainty estimation pipeline using publicly available Freddie Mac 30-year fixed-rate conforming mortgage data. First, Age-Period-Cohort (APC) decompositions (estimated with splines and constrained identifiability conditions) produce lifecycle, vintage and environment components for probability of default (PD) and probability of attrition/pay-off (PA). The lifecycle and environment functions from APC are then used as fixed inputs to multihorizon discrete-time survival models: panel logistic regressions are estimated independently for each forecast horizon to capture horizon-dependent score coefficients (multihorizon survival model). Coefficient confidence intervals and prediction intervals are computed in logit space. The environment term is extrapolated stochastically using a calibrated 2nd-order Ornstein–Uhlenbeck mean-reverting process to produce distributions of environment paths. For final uncertainty estimation a classical Monte Carlo simulation samples (logit-)normal prediction distributions for conditional PD and PA at each time step, propagates them through the competing-risks recursion (survival to time t and unconditional probability at t), and accumulates per-step probabilities to produce lifetime PD (LPD) and lifetime PA (LPA) distributions. Resulting LPD samples are fitted and tested (KS, Cramer–von Mises, Anderson–Darling) against candidate parametric families; strong empirical fit to a lognormal distribution is observed, enabling a significant reduction in required Monte Carlo runs for practical use. Separately, the paper develops and analyses a theoretical quantum Monte Carlo approach (no QPU execution): it maps the Monte Carlo task into a quantum operator A = U S C (U = state-loading of per-step distributions, S = summation/register arithmetic, C = comparator/threshold), and proposes Quantum Amplitude Estimation (QAE) / amplitude-amplification-based techniques (with quantum phase-estimation / QFT/Walsh–Hadamard subroutines) to estimate tail quantiles (VaR-style) with quadratic (1/sqrt(M) -> 1/M) sample complexity improvement. The paper also analyses state-preparation (Grover–Rudolph type) and entanglement requirements and provides qubit-count scaling estimates and bottlenecks for the fully conditional competing-risks quantum implementation.

## Findings
- [supported] A multihorizon survival modelling approach (APC + panel logistic per horizon) was developed and applied to Freddie Mac 30-year fixed mortgage data to estimate PD and PA forecasting components.
- [supported] APC decompositions for PD and PA on the dataset produced plausible lifecycle, vintage and environment functions (empirical plots shown).
- [supported] Conditional forecast uncertainties (in log-odds) were modelled as approximately normal, producing logit-normal per-step PD/PA distributions.
- [supported] Monte Carlo propagation of the per-step distributions for selected accounts produced lifetime PD distributions that fit a Lognormal distribution very well (10,000 simulations; goodness-of-fit tests: KS, Cramer-von Mises, Anderson-Darling accepted Lognormal and rejected Normal).
- [supported] Knowing that the lifetime PD distribution is well-approximated by a Lognormal allows dramatically fewer Monte Carlo samples (tens rather than thousands) to estimate tail risk: example M = 50 runs required ~1.9 seconds on a classical machine for a single account forecast.
- [supported] For the four illustrative accounts from the Freddie Mac dataset, classical Monte Carlo (10,000 sims) and statistical tests confirmed the Lognormal fit of lifetime PD.
- [speculative] Quantum Amplitude Estimation (QAE) and related quantum Monte Carlo algorithms could in principle provide a quadratic speedup (O(1/√M) error scaling vs classical O(1/√M) sampling—i.e. fewer quantum samples for same accuracy) for estimating tail risk or VaR-like statistics.
- [speculative] A simplified (unconditional per-period PD) version of the loan-lifetime problem is amenable to near-term quantum acceleration (because per-period distributions become independent and can be summed), whereas the full competing-risks formulation is far more challenging on near-term hardware.
- [speculative] Loading realistic per-period PD distributions (logit-normal) efficiently into quantum registers is non-trivial; efficient loading is known for certain classes (e.g., log-concave) but not for logit-normal in general, implying extra algorithmic work is needed.
- [speculative] The competing-risks formulation requires entangling and re-using registers (because conditional probabilities are used multiple times), which leads to a qubit/register count scaling that is impractical for near-term QPUs: best-case register/qubit count estimated as 4N registers and 4Nm qubits for N time steps and m qubits per distribution.
- [supported] Numerical example: for monthly horizons (N = 60) with m = 8 qubits per distribution, the competing-risks best-case estimate would need ~240 registers and ~1,920 qubits—beyond near-term hardware.
- [speculative] Because practitioners can pre-assume a parametric form (e.g., Lognormal or Beta) for the final lifetime distribution, a hybrid approach—estimating only mean and variance via quantum algorithms—could substantially reduce required quantum resources and still deliver tail estimates.
- [speculative] Near-term quantum computing may be more impactful if it enables fast real-time account-level uncertainty estimation for underwriting, but this depends on algorithmic developments (efficient loading, in-place conditional computation) and hardware progress.
- [supported] Practical caveats: current QPUs face decoherence and error correction challenges that make present-day implementations noisy; algorithmic and hardware improvements are needed to make the proposed quantum approaches viable.

**Results summary:** The authors implemented a multihorizon survival modelling framework (APC + horizon-specific logistic scoring) on Freddie Mac mortgage data to produce per-period logit-normal PD/PA forecasts and used Monte Carlo simulation to propagate uncertainties to lifetime PD. Empirically, lifetime PD distributions for test accounts were well approximated by a Lognormal; goodness-of-fit tests (KS, Cramer-von Mises, Anderson-Darling) accepted the Lognormal and rejected the Normal. Leveraging the lognormal fit reduces required Monte Carlo samples to the tens (example: M=50, runtime ≈1.9s for a single-account forecast), making real-time classical account-level uncertainty estimation feasible. On the quantum side, the authors review/apply known quantum Monte Carlo (QAE) ideas and argue theoretically that a simplified unconditional-per-period approach could see significant quantum speedups; however, the full competing-risks, in-place conditional calculation requires large numbers of qubits (estimated scaling 4Nm) and additional algorithmic advances, making it impractical on near-term hardware.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** theoretical

The paper argues (theoretically) that quantum Monte Carlo techniques (e.g., Quantum Amplitude Estimation) can provide quadratic speedups in sample complexity for estimating tail probabilities, and that a simplified unconditional-per-period formulation could benefit from near-term quantum acceleration. However, no quantum hardware experiments were performed; the full competing-risks model requires large qubit counts and additional algorithmic advances, so advantage remains theoretical/speculative.
## Limitations
- The full competing-risks quantum algorithm requires far more qubits than near-term hardware will provide (author-stated).
- Current quantum hardware limitations (qubit counts, decoherence, and error correction) make full implementations noisy or infeasible (author-stated).
- The competing-risks formulation increases qubit/register requirements substantially (best-case linear in forecast horizon; example: 4 N registers => 4 N m qubits), exceeding near-term capacity (author-stated).
- Efficiently loading realistic credit-risk distributions (logit-normal) into quantum registers is not yet solved; existing efficient methods assume log-concavity (author-stated).
- Entanglement/copying constraints on quantum registers complicate reusing sampled distributions in multiple calculations and drive up register use (author-stated).
- The analysis omitted practical quantum error correction and decoherence effects; current results assume idealised or near-ideal hardware (author-stated).
- Lognormal fit to lifetime PD distributions may break when additional components (balance dynamics, revenue, expenses) are added, undermining the Monte Carlo shortcut (author-stated).
- The lognormal approximation fails when lifetime default probability approaches the bound (LPD near 1), e.g., severely delinquent accounts (author-stated).
- The unconditional-distribution simplification (modelling UPD(t) directly) is suboptimal because it blends default and prepayment impacts and cannot incorporate multihorizon behavioural scoring properly (author-stated).
- [inferred] The paper's quantum Monte Carlo speed-up claims are mostly theoretical/proof-of-principle — no head-to-head empirical quantum vs classical benchmarking on production hardware is available.
- [inferred] Potential engineering overheads (distribution loading, entanglement setup, QAE/bisection iterations, readout, classical-quantum interfacing) may substantially reduce the practical speed-up.
- [inferred] Regulatory, validation, and operational requirements (model governance, explainability) could slow adoption of quantum-based underwriting despite computational feasibility.
- [inferred] The requirement to set threshold x (e.g., 95th percentile) in operator C and perform bisection loops may introduce additional quantum/classical coordination and overhead not fully characterised.
## Open questions
- Can quantum circuits be developed to perform competing-risks calculations in-place with a relatively small number of qubits?
- Can faster and efficient quantum loading algorithms be found for realistic credit-risk distributions such as logit-normal or logit-normal-like shapes?
- How will decoherence and quantum error correction overheads affect the practical performance and noise of these quantum Monte Carlo algorithms in finance?
- If balance dynamics, revenues, and expenses are added to move from lifetime PD to yield distributions, will the resulting distributions still admit compact parametric fits (e.g., lognormal) usable to reduce simulation needs?
- What is the true end-to-end speed-up when accounting for distribution-loading, entanglement, QAE iterations, bisection search, and measurement overheads on real quantum hardware?
- Can quantum algorithms be adapted to keep necessary correlation/entanglement across time steps without exploding register counts?
- What are the best approaches to incorporate macroeconomic scenario-generation uncertainty (e.g., stress-test models linked to macro factors) into quantum Monte Carlo frameworks?
- Is the unconditional-UPD simplification practically acceptable for underwriting use-cases, given the loss of separability between score estimation and lifecycle/environment effects?
- How robust are the lognormal fits across different portfolios, vintages, product types, or stressed regimes (beyond the datasets tested)?

**Future work:**
- Research whether a quantum circuit can be developed to perform competing-risks calculations in-place with a relatively small number of qubits.
- Develop faster quantum loading methods for realistic (e.g., logit-normal) distributions used in credit risk applications.
- Explore additional quantum algorithm development to make the full competing-risks approach feasible on future hardware.
- Investigate extending the model to include balance dynamics, revenue, and expenses to estimate yield distributions and test whether parametric fits (e.g., lognormal) remain valid.
- Study practical impacts of decoherence and quantum error correction on these algorithms and quantify realistic speed-ups on forthcoming hardware.
## Key ideas
- #idea:quantum-advantage — Quantum Amplitude Estimation (QAE) can in principle give quadratic sample-complexity improvements for tail-quantile (VaR-style) estimation compared to classical Monte Carlo (1/sqrt(M) -> 1/M).
- #idea:hybrid-approach — The paper maps the classical competing-risks Monte Carlo into a quantum operator A = U S C (state loading, summation/register arithmetic, comparator) indicating a workflow where classical preprocessing (APC, survival modelling, environment simulation) feeds a quantum estimation subroutine.
- #idea:near-term-feasibility — Empirical finding that lifetime default distributions fit a lognormal well implies only tens of classical Monte Carlo runs are needed for tail estimates (enabling near real-time use), and the authors conclude QAE-based speedups require substantial algorithmic and hardware advances to handle the full competing-risks formulation.
- #limitation:qubit-count — Theoretical scaling and qubit-count estimates show large qubit/register requirements and entanglement/state-preparation bottlenecks for a fully conditional competing-risks quantum implementation.
- #limitation:data-encoding — State-preparation (Grover–Rudolph type) and loading per-step distributions into quantum registers are identified as major overheads that limit practical quantum speedups.
- #limitation:no-empirical-validation — The quantum Monte Carlo / QAE component is analyzed theoretically only; no QPU or quantum-simulator experiments are reported.
## Contradictions
- The paper demonstrates that classical modelling (APC + multihorizon survival + fitted lognormal lifetime PD) can produce accurate tail estimates with only tens of Monte Carlo runs, which undercuts broad claims that quantum Monte Carlo speedups are currently necessary for near real-time tail-risk estimation.
- Theoretical qubit-count scaling, state-preparation and comparator complexity reported in the paper contradict optimistic narratives that QAE-based algorithms can be straightforwardly scaled to full competing-risks, loan-level portfolios without substantial advances in qubit counts and algorithmic state-loading techniques.
