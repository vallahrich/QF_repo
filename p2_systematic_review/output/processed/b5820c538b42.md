## Abstract summary
This preprint introduces Hybrid HHL++, an improved, near-term-friendly variant of the HHL quantum linear systems algorithm. The authors present a spectral scaling algorithm to choose a matrix scaling factor γ that maximizes useful phase-estimation precision and a heuristic circuit compression method (leveraging semiclassical QPE, mid-circuit measurement, resets and conditional logic) to reduce qubit and gate requirements. They validate the approach with experiments on Quantinuum H-series trapped-ion hardware for small-scale portfolio optimization, reporting the largest experimental HHL demonstrations for an application to date.
## Methodology
The authors develop Hybrid HHL++, a near-term hybrid classical–quantum pipeline that adapts the Harrow–Hassidim–Lloyd (HHL) linear-system solver for noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) devices by (1) introducing a spectral-scaling procedure (Algorithms 1 and 2) to choose a matrix-scaling factor γ that maximizes the utility of m-bit quantum phase estimation (QPE) precision for the relevant eigenvalues, and (2) proposing a heuristic compression of the HHL circuit that (a) uses semiclassical QPE (semiclassical inverse QFT with mid-circuit measurement, reset and conditional logic) to obtain high-precision eigenvalue estimates using a single ancilla qubit, (b) reduces the number of ancillary qubits and controlled rotations in the eigenvalue-inversion step by finding a smaller k ≤ m such that distinct m-bit eigenvalue estimates map unambiguously to k-bit control states, and (c) if necessary averages rotation angles where bins share endpoints. For Hamiltonian simulation they compute U = exp(2π i γ A) classically and synthesize gates via Qiskit product-formula decomposition; they then construct HHL circuits (k-bit QPE → multi-controlled rotations using classically precomputed angles → inverse QPE → postselect) and validate solutions using a controlled-SWAP inner-product test against the classical solution. Empirical evaluation was performed on Quantinuum H-series trapped-ion quantum hardware, its noisy emulator, and Qiskit QASM noiseless simulation. Metrics include fidelity between measured and simulated eigenvalue distributions (classical fidelity measure) and the inner product between HHL output and analytical solution estimated via a controlled-SWAP test; statistical uncertainty was estimated by bootstrapping.

**Algorithms used:** HHL, Hybrid HHL (Lee et al.), Hybrid HHL++ (this work), Quantum Phase Estimation (standard QPE), Semiclassical QPE (semiclassical inverse QFT / iterative QPE variant), Spectral scaling algorithms (Algorithms 1 and 2), Eigenvalue inversion (multi-controlled rotations), Controlled-SWAP (swap test), Hamiltonian simulation (classical computation of U = exp(2π i γ A) via product formulas), Bootstrap resampling (for uncertainty estimation)
**Frameworks:** Qiskit, pytket, Quantinuum emulator, Qiskit QASM simulator

**Experimental setup:** Experiments run on Quantinuum H-series trapped-ion devices (H1 referenced) and its noisy emulator, plus noiseless Qiskit QASM simulator. Circuits were transpiled from Qiskit to the device native gate set via pytket. Data were gathered using various shot counts (400, 1000, 2000) depending on experiment. Two problem sizes were demonstrated from financial data: 2-asset portfolios (4×4 augmented linear systems) and 6-asset portfolios (8×8 augmented linear systems).

**Dataset:** S&P 500 historical asset data used to compute covariance matrices Σ, expected returns r and prices p; these inputs were assembled into constrained mean–variance portfolio linear systems via Lagrange multipliers (augmented matrix A of size N = ˜N + 2).

## Findings
- [supported] Hybrid HHL++ introduces two concrete modifications to prior Hybrid HHL: (1) a spectral-scaling algorithm to choose a matrix-scaling factor γ that improves the use of m-bit QPE, and (2) a heuristic circuit-compression procedure that can reduce QPE ancilla qubits and the eigenvalue-inversion control complexity.
- [supported] The spectral-scaling procedure (Algorithms 1 and 2) can be run with semiclassical QPE on hardware to iteratively find an improved γ that concentrates eigenvalue estimates into the available m-bit range and empirically improves eigenvalue distinguishability.
- [supported] Semiclassical QPE (the semiclassical inverse QFT variant) needs only one ancilla qubit regardless of target bit precision and replaces many two-qubit gates with single-qubit operations controlled by classical registers, reducing qubit count and two-qubit gate use compared with textbook QPE (Table 1).
- [supported] Empirical QPE fidelity results (hardware vs noiseless simulation) reported in the paper: standard QPE fidelities (median ± std) for 3-, 4-, 5-bit precision = 98.6%±1.2, 90.4%±1.7, 42.6%±2.4; semiclassical QPE = 98.1%±1.4, 95.0%±1.6, 43.2%±1.0 (Table 2).
- [supported] For small portfolio-optimization instances (2-asset → 4x4 linear system) executed on Quantinuum H-series trapped-ion hardware, HHL circuits constructed via Hybrid HHL++ produced high-quality solutions: median inner product between the HHL-produced quantum solution and the classical (analytical) solution ≈ 0.92±0.04 (Table 3 / Figure 6).
- [supported] Semiclassical QPE experimental circuits required 3 qubits for 3/4/5-bit precision while standard QPE required 5/6/7 qubits respectively; two-qubit (ZZPhase) gate counts listed in Table 1 show smaller counts for the semiclassical implementation at each precision.
- [supported] The authors executed the modified Hybrid HHL pipeline on Quantinuum H-series hardware and report this as, to their knowledge, the largest-to-date experimental execution of HHL for an application (they report two-qubit gate depths up to values stated in the paper: e.g., median ZZPhase depths reported and an abstract claim of up to 291 two-qubit gate depth).
- [supported] Noise-sensitivity experiments (emulator sweeps) show that reducing two-qubit fault probability improves semiclassical QPE fidelity and HHL solution quality (inner product), indicating dominant sensitivity to two-qubit errors (Figure 7).
- [speculative] The heuristic for compressing the HHL circuit (reducing the number k of QPE ancilla qubits below the precision m used to compute rotation angles) is presented as broadly applicable and beneficial; it is heuristic (no formal guarantees) and its efficacy beyond the tested small instances is not demonstrated.
- [speculative] The authors propose Hybrid HHL++ as a viable near-term application-oriented benchmark for small quantum hardware; this is an intended use-case claim rather than a formally validated standard.
- [speculative] Some theoretical bounds referenced (e.g., the ≤0.19 trace-distance guarantee when reducing precision k relative to m, and how many extra qubits reduce error) are invoked from prior analyses and assumed to transfer to the proposed hybrid executions; practical realization depends on device noise and is heuristic in their pipeline.

**Results summary:** The paper proposes Hybrid HHL++, an improved hybrid classical–quantum implementation of the HHL linear-system algorithm, adding (1) an algorithm to optimize the spectral scaling factor γ for phase estimation and (2) a heuristic to compress the HHL circuit (reducing ancilla qubits and controlled-rotation complexity). The authors implement semiclassical QPE (single-ancilla, mid-circuit measurement based) and demonstrate on Quantinuum H-series trapped-ion hardware that the modified pipeline yields high-fidelity eigenvalue estimation and high-quality solutions for small portfolio-optimization instances (2-asset examples): semiclassical QPE achieves fidelities comparable to or better than standard QPE at modest bit precisions, and the HHL-produced states have median inner product ≈0.92 with classical solutions. They report reduced qubit and two-qubit gate requirements versus textbook QPE, sensitivity analyses showing two-qubit-gate errors dominate performance, and claim these circuits represent one of the largest experimental HHL demonstrations to date. The authors explicitly state they do not claim an asymptotic quantum speedup.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** not-applicable

The authors explicitly state Hybrid HHL and Hybrid HHL++ do not provide an asymptotic quantum speedup for the tested settings (they trade algorithmic asymptotics for near-term implementability). The work focuses on making HHL components executable on NISQ hardware and demonstrating benchmarking/feasibility, not demonstrating quantum advantage.
## Limitations
- Hybrid HHL++ intentionally forgoes the asymptotic quantum speedup of original HHL (authors acknowledge loss of speedup).
- Experiments are limited to very small problem sizes (primarily 2-asset portfolios -> 4x4 systems; simulations up to 8x8).
- The approach relies on classical precomputation of the unitary U = e^{2πiγA} (hybrid Hamiltonian simulation), so it does not demonstrate end-to-end quantum Hamiltonian simulation.
- Semiclassical QPE cannot be used inside the eigenvalue inversion step because repeated mid-circuit measurements collapse the ancilla (explicitly stated).
- Eigenvalue inversion still requires post-selection, which reduces success probability and increases sampling cost.
- The circuit compression is presented as a heuristic without formal guarantees on approximation error or resource savings.
- Spectral scaling algorithm depends on having an overapproximation α of λ_max (authors propose methods but rely on this assumption).
- Results are obtained on a single hardware family (Quantinuum H-series trapped-ion); generality across different architectures is unproven.
- Benchmarks and noise analysis concentrate primarily on two-qubit fault probability; other noise channels and full device characterization are not exhaustively explored.
- Practical limits on number of shots and per-shot latency (especially on trapped-ion hardware) constrain experimental statistics and scale of experiments.
- Handling of negative or degenerate eigenvalues requires adaptations (two's complement, interval handling) that are discussed but not exhaustively analyzed.
- The demonstrated end-to-end pipeline assumes classical access to eigenvalues (via QPE sampling or classical eigensolver); reliance on accurate eigenvalue estimates may limit applicability where sampling is noisy or eigenvalues are close.
## Open questions
- How well does Hybrid HHL++ scale to larger linear systems (beyond the demonstrated 4x4 and simulated 8x8), both in practice on NISQ devices and in resource requirements?
- What are rigorous error bounds and success-probability guarantees for the proposed circuit compression heuristic and for controlling on reduced k-bit registers?
- How robust is the spectral scaling algorithm (Algorithm 1/2) across diverse spectra, high condition numbers, near-degenerate eigenvalues, and in the presence of realistic noise?
- What are the trade-offs between the semiclassical QPE approach and standard QPE as devices evolve (e.g., when mid-circuit measurement/reset/conditional logic have different fidelities)?
- How sensitive is the overall algorithm performance to the choice and accuracy of the overapproximation α and to errors in estimating λ_max?
- How does noise beyond the two-qubit fault probability (e.g., SPAM errors, correlated noise, cross-talk, mid-circuit measurement errors) impact the pipeline end-to-end?
- To what extent can the hybrid components (classical precomputation, classical eigensolvers) be removed or reduced as Hamiltonian simulation improves, and what is the roadmap to recover asymptotic speedup?
- How generally applicable is the method to non-portfolio QLSPs, non-Hermitian matrices (after dilation), or different conditioning scenarios?
- Can the post-selection bottleneck be mitigated or avoided (e.g., via amplitude amplification or alternative inversion strategies) within near-term constraints?
- How will differing hardware connectivity/topology and gate sets affect transpilation overheads and the practical benefits of the semiclassical QPE on other platforms (superconducting vs ion trap)?
- What is the minimum device noise threshold (two-qubit and other errors) required for Hybrid HHL++ to produce useful solutions at larger scales?
- How do sampling complexity and shot requirements scale with desired accuracy for portfolio metrics derived from the solution state (risk, returns, controlled-SWAP comparisons)?

**Future work:**
- Simulate and benchmark Hybrid HHL++ for larger system sizes and different noise levels to bound scaling behavior (authors perform preliminary noise/size simulations and propose further study).
- Provide formal analysis or proofs of approximation/error bounds for the circuit compression heuristic and for reduced-precision controlled-rotation mappings.
- Explore improving or automating the selection of an overapproximation α and robustness of the spectral scaling algorithm across diverse matrices/spectra.
- Extend experiments to other quantum hardware platforms and more varied device calibrations to assess portability and architecture dependence.
- Investigate strategies to reduce or avoid post-selection overhead (e.g., amplitude amplification or alternative inversion circuits compatible with hybrid approaches).
- Develop methods to handle degenerate and near-degenerate eigenvalues more systematically within the reduced-precision inversion framework.
- Study the transition from hybrid to fully quantum implementations as Hamiltonian simulation and fault-tolerance improve, with a roadmap for restoring asymptotic speedups.
- Conduct broader application-oriented benchmarking (more assets, other financial QLSPs) and more extensive comparisons with classical solvers and other near-term quantum linear solvers.
