## Abstract summary
This preprint presents an algorithm to efficiently extract a smooth, positive-definite function ψ(x) encoded in quantum memory without suffering the usual exponentially suppressed sub-normalization when measuring single sites. The method combines binary-segmentation probability integral estimation (using QAE), Chebyshev interpolation and differentiation, and a controlled square-root step to recover ψ(x), and under analyticity and positivity assumptions derives a gate complexity roughly Õ((1/a_ψ) Λ^2 n^2 / ε_total × maxψ/minψ). The approach is aimed at extracting solutions of diffusive PDEs such as the heat equation and applications in finance (e.g., option pricing).
## Methodology
This is a theoretical, algorithmic methodology for reconstructing a smooth, positive-definite function ψ(x) encoded in the amplitudes of an n-qubit quantum state without suffering exponential sub-normalization when extracting pointwise values. Assumptions: ψ(x)>0 on [-1,1], analytic with derivative bounds ||d^kψ/dx^k|| ≤ Λ^{k+1}, and the solution is available as a (possibly non-unitary) state-preparation operator U_ψ with a sub-normalization factor a_ψ (⟨0|_a U_ψ |0⟩_a = a_ψ |ψ⟩). The pipeline has four main components: (1) Estimate cumulative squared-amplitude integrals Ψ(x)=∫_{-1}^x ψ^2(x') dx' using a shift-based binary segmentation protocol that performs up to n segmented Quantum Amplitude Estimation (QAE) calls instead of estimating 2^n point probabilities, thereby absorbing the 1/2^n factor into integration; (2) Interpolate the estimated Ψ at a small number M of nodes using Chebyshev polynomials (solve Va=f for Chebyshev coefficients) with M = O(Λ + log(1/ϵ_cheb)), leveraging optimal conditioning of the Chebyshev-Vandermonde matrix and performing perturbation analysis for node discretization; (3) Differentiate the Chebyshev interpolant to recover an approximation of the integrand ψ^2, with error propagation bounds showing ψ^2 error scales as O(M^2 ϵ_Ψ) under small node perturbations; (4) Take the (positive) square root of that approximant to obtain ψ(x), with controlled error scaling O(ϵ_ψ2 / min_x ψ(x)). The paper provides lemmas bounding complexity and error propagation (including effects of non-unitary preparation via factor 1/a_ψ), and derives an overall gate-complexity scaling of roughly Õ((1/a_ψ) Λ^2 n^2 / ϵ_total × max_x ψ(x)/min_x ψ(x)). The methodology is analytic and focuses on resource/error tradeoffs and stability rather than empirical evaluation.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum Amplitude Estimation (QAE), Shift-based (binary segmentation) probability estimation, Chebyshev interpolation (Chebyshev-Vandermonde linear system), Polynomial differentiation and analytic error propagation, Quantum state preparation oracle (U_ψ) as input

The approach estimates cumulative squared-amplitude integrals using a binary-shifted amplitude-estimation routine, reconstructs the integral via Chebyshev interpolation with M = O(Λ + log(1/ε)) nodes, differentiates the interpolant to recover ψ^2, and takes a controlled square root to obtain ψ. ## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** theoretical

The paper claims a theoretical (analytical) improvement over naive per-site amplitude estimation by avoiding the exponential sub-normalization factor 2^{-n} when extracting local amplitude values. The claimed improvement is derived under specific assumptions (ψ positive, analytic with bounded derivatives Λ, and non-negligible min_x ψ(x)), leading to polynomial-in-n resource scaling for estimation. The claim is not supported by experimental data or independent lower-bound proofs in this preprint, so it remains a theoretical result contingent on the stated assumptions.
## Limitations
- Method requires the target function ψ(x) to be strictly positive (positive-definite) on the interval [−1,1].
- Method requires ψ(x) to be analytic with derivatives bounded by a finite Λ (assumption of high smoothness / analyticity).
- Requires min_x ψ(x) not very small (estimates and square-root step assume ε ≤ min_x ψ(x)).
- Complexity and error bounds depend on the condition ϵΨ ≤ n · max_x ψ^2(x) (constraint on per-sample precision).
- Algorithmic cost scales with 1/a_ψ (sub-normalization factor) — if state preparation has small amplitude a_ψ the cost increases proportionally.
- Chebyshev interpolation requires solving a (perturbed) Vandermonde linear system and assumes ∥Vpert^{-1}∥ = O(1); stability relies on being able to make the node perturbation small (needs exponentially many grid points N = 2^n).
- Cost and error bounds include multiplicative factors dependent on max_x ψ(x) / min_x ψ(x) (sensitivity to dynamic range of ψ).
- Analysis is presented for a 1D setting (explicit 1D abstraction); multidimensional generalization is not provided.
- Reliance on Quantum Amplitude Estimation (QAE) and multiple QAE routines (up to m ≤ n), so practical quantum resource requirements (depth, coherence) may be large.
- Some bounds use big-O / tilde-O notation and hidden constants; practical gate counts, constants and implementation-level noise effects are not quantified.
- [inferred] The method is not applicable (as presented) to functions with zeros, sign changes, discontinuities, shocks, or non-analytic features (authors note these cases as future extensions).
- [inferred] The requirement of known or bounded Λ may be restrictive in practice; estimating Λ or guaranteeing it for realistic financial payoff solutions can be non-trivial.
- [inferred] The approach may be sensitive to hardware noise and finite-sampling/statistical noise beyond the modelled amplitude-estimation errors, as noise resilience is not analyzed.
- [inferred] If the discretization grid size N is not sufficiently large relative to M, the perturbation bounds (e.g., ∥V−Vpert∥) and the assumption ∥Vpert^{-1}∥=O(1) may fail, harming stability.
- [inferred] Practical state preparation (constructing U_ψ or achieving the stated ancilla-subnormalization model) and its cost/feasibility are not addressed.
## Open questions
- How can the method be extended to functions that have zero crossings, sign changes, shocks, singularities or other non-analytic behaviour?
- Can the positive-definite requirement be relaxed (e.g., for functions that change sign) while retaining the exponential improvement?
- How to handle cases where min_x ψ(x) is extremely small or vanishes—can the square-root step and error amplification be controlled in those regimes?
- Can the dependence on the sub-normalization factor a_ψ be reduced or removed (i.e., what are practical state-preparation strategies with favorable a_ψ)?
- How robust is the method to realistic quantum hardware noise, finite sampling noise, and imperfect QAE implementations?
- How do the hidden constants in the tilde-O / big-O bounds affect practical gate counts for problems of realistic financial relevance?
- How to generalize the interpolation / differentiation pipeline to multi-dimensional functions (multi-asset models) while keeping costs tractable?
- What are practical methods to estimate or bound Λ (the derivative-growth parameter) for solution functions arising in financial PDEs?
- How sensitive is the Vandermonde/Chebyshev linear system conditioning in finite-precision classical computation, and what are numerically stable solvers in practice?
- How to construct U_ψ (or the required state-preparation routine) efficiently from quantum solvers of differential equations so that the overall pipeline is end-to-end efficient?

**Future work:**
- Extend the analysis and method to problems where there are zero crossings present, shock or other singularities and in more general setups.

- #idea:hybrid-approach — Hybrid pipeline: quantum subroutines (binary-shifted QAE) produce integral estimates, then classical Chebyshev interpolation, polynomial differentiation and controlled square-root complete the reconstruction of ψ(x).

- #limitation:noise — The analysis presents asymptotic gate complexity and error propagation but does not address noisy/NISQ-device error mitigation or fault-tolerance requirements implicit in amplitude estimation and controlled operations.
- #limitation:no-empirical-validation — All results are analytical lemmas and theorems with no simulation or hardware experiments to validate constants, pre-factors, or robustness beyond the asymptotic bounds.
