## Abstract summary
The paper proposes QPO, a quantum circuit design for discrete portfolio optimization that integrates VQE/QAOA-style optimization with a switchable multi-control SWAP-test to directly compute return values on a quantum device. The design encodes portfolios and expected returns into 2n+2 qubits (for N assets with n=log2N), aims to reduce complexity to O(log N), and is validated on 97 cryptocurrency assets showing close agreement with classical solutions (average Hamming distance ~0.092).
## Methodology
The authors propose QPO, a hybrid quantum-classical circuit design specialized for discrete portfolio optimization. They convert the mean-variance objective with a budget constraint into a QUBO / Ising Hamiltonian and implement a parameterized quantum circuit that can operate in two modes via a control qubit: (1) a VQE-style optimizer (or QAOA for comparison) that searches for the binary portfolio minimizing the Hamiltonian, and (2) an overlap-estimation mode using a multi-control SWAP-test (MCSWAP-TEST) to estimate the return term µ^T x directly on the quantum hardware (or simulator). Encoding of portfolio candidate states |x̂⟩ uses amplitude encoding while the fixed mean vector |µ̂⟩ is prepared via quantum compiling; the circuit size is 2n+2 qubits where n = log2(N) (N = number of assets). Classical pre-processing computes µ and σ from asset price time series; the quantum routine either optimizes the Ising Hamiltonian via VQE/QAOA or measures the overlap ⟨µ̂|x̂⟩ via MCSWAP-TEST. Evaluation compares predicted portfolios to classical linear-programming baselines using average normalized Hamming distance and overlap mean-squared-error across many random subsets of assets. The authors simulate the circuits classically and report optimization quality, overlap estimation MSE, and execution times for varying asset counts.

**Algorithms used:** QPO (proposed circuit), Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE), Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA), Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) / Ising mapping, Multi-control SWAP-test (MCSWAP-TEST), Amplitude encoding, Quantum compiling (state preparation)
**Frameworks:** Qiskit, qo|op (qo—op package), Python

**Experimental setup:** Local classical simulation on Windows 11, Intel Core i7-13700 (13th gen, 24 cores), 32 GB RAM. Software: Python 3.10.9 and Qiskit 0.45.1. Code and results available on GitHub.

**Dataset:** Daily cryptocurrency price data (open, close, high, low) from Binance via the public 'klines' API. 97 distinct assets, time range 2023-04-30 to 2023-06-30. Returns computed per-day as (close - open) / open and averaged over the two-month window to produce per-asset mean returns; covariances computed from the daily returns over the period.

## Findings
- [supported] The authors propose QPO, a quantum-circuit design that integrates a VQE/QAOA-style optimizer with a switchable Multi-Control SWAP-TEST (MCSWAP-TEST) subcircuit to either (a) run a variational optimization or (b) estimate the portfolio return µ^T x on the quantum device.
- [supported] QPO uses 2n+2 qubits (n = log2 N) in their design: n qubits to encode the candidate portfolio |x̂>, n qubits to encode the normalized mean return |μ̂>, plus one ancillary qubit for MCSWAP and one control qubit |k>.
- [supported] The MCSWAP-TEST measurement outcome can be used to estimate the overlap ⟨μ̂|x̂⟩, and the paper gives the theoretical relation and simulated verification (estimator computed as sqrt(P(0) - P(1))).
- [supported] The authors simulated QPO on a dataset of 97 cryptocurrencies (30 Apr 2023–30 Jun 2023) and report empirical results comparing to classical linear-programming solutions.
- [supported] In their experiments (subsets of 2–16 assets, 100 random trials per k), QAOA portfolios achieved approximately zero average Hamming distance to the classical target portfolio, while VQE performance improved (Hamming distance decreased) as number of assets increased.
- [supported] The reported average Hamming distance between the QPO (VQE/QAOA) solutions and classical solutions (over their experiments) is 0.092 (as reported in the abstract/summary).
- [supported] The MCSWAP-TEST subcircuit achieved mean-squared-error (MSE) for the estimated overlap under an acceptance threshold of 1e-2 in their simulations.
- [supported] In their simulation environment, VQE in the QPO circuit ran faster than QAOA for small numbers of assets, and the MCSWAP-TEST consumed significantly less compute time than the variational optimization subcircuits.
- [speculative] The paper suggests that QPO (and hybrid quantum-classical approaches) can offer logarithmic complexity in the number of assets (O(log N)) with respect to certain steps (encoding the solution in n = log2 N qubits) compared with polynomial (e.g., O(N) or O(N^2)) classical methods.
- [speculative] The authors note that careful choice/development of state encoders and Hamiltonian simulation techniques is essential for practical QPO implementations and for reducing quantum resource requirements.
- [speculative] The authors position QPO as an extended/alternative design to standard VQE/QAOA by adding the on-device overlap estimation capability, claiming this reduces post-processing overhead when return values are required.
- [supported] The authors make available code and experimental artifacts (GitHub link) and show that, in simulation, their augmented circuit is functionally capable of both optimization and overlap estimation.

**Results summary:** The paper introduces QPO, a hybrid quantum-classical circuit design that augments VQE/QAOA-style portfolio optimization with an on-circuit Multi-Control SWAP-TEST to estimate portfolio returns (µ^T x) without full classical post-processing. The authors simulated QPO on a 97-asset cryptocurrency dataset (Apr–Jun 2023). Empirical simulation results (subsets of 2–16 assets, 100 trials each) show that QAOA solutions closely match classical linear-programming targets (near-zero average Hamming distance), VQE performance improves with asset count, and the MCSWAP-TEST estimates overlaps with MSE below 1e-2. They report an overall average Hamming distance of 0.092 between quantum and classical portfolios and claim that, conceptually, the approach can yield favorable scaling in qubit count (n = log2 N) for large N, though practical encoder and Hamiltonian-simulation considerations remain.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The paper asserts that hybrid quantum-classical methods and the QPO design can achieve logarithmic scaling in the number of assets (n = log2 N qubits) versus polynomial classical costs. However, the work demonstrates this only in classical simulation for small problems and does not account for practical costs (state preparation, Hamiltonian simulation, noise, readout overhead). Therefore the claimed advantage remains speculative: conceptually plausible under idealized assumptions, but not empirically demonstrated on hardware and subject to well-known caveats about data-loading and resource overheads.
## Limitations
- Experiments were simulated on a classical computer (no runs on real quantum hardware).
- Evaluation used a limited dataset and timeframe (97 cryptocurrency assets, 30 April 2023 to 30 June 2023) and small subproblem sizes (detailed evaluations for 2–16 assets).
- The work focuses on a single-time-step (single-period) portfolio optimization formulation, not multi-period/dynamic optimization.
- QPO requires efficient encoder construction and Hamiltonian simulation; current implementation depends on encoding choices that are resource-intensive (authors state encoder and Hamiltonian simulation must be developed to reduce quantum resources).
- Amplitude encoding has exponential circuit depth and is not suitable for large numbers of qubits; quantum compiling has upfront classical resource costs (explicit trade-offs in encoding methods).
- The circuit uses additional qubits (2n+2 including ancilla and control), introducing overhead from the MCSWAP-TEST and control qubits.
- The constrained optimization is handled via a penalty term (Lagrange multiplier λ); the performance is sensitive to the choice of λ (authors note λ is a key parameter).
- The MCSWAP-TEST overlap estimation requires measurement repetitions and has an acceptance/MSE threshold (authors report an acceptance threshold of 1e-2), which constrains accuracy.
- The authors note that applying quantum computing at scale "comes at the cost of needing complete certainty of the outcome," indicating limitations in robustness or certainty requirements for large combinatorial problems.
- [inferred] No noise or NISQ-era device error analysis was performed; implications of realistic device noise on QPO performance are unexplored.
- [inferred] Although qubit count scales logarithmically with asset number, circuit depth, shot complexity, and practical scalability (for large N) are not characterized.
- [inferred] The budget model in experiments is simplified (budget = number of assets chosen) and does not reflect more realistic heterogeneous cost/weight constraints.
- [inferred] Encoder choice may need per-asset or per-asset-class tuning because assets do not behave uniformly; this heterogeneity is not resolved in the current approach.
- [inferred] Comparison and selection criteria between VQE/QAOA and the QPO design (when to prefer which) remain under-explored despite observed differences in performance.
## Open questions
- How can encoders and Hamiltonian simulation be designed or optimized to scale QPO to much larger asset universes while keeping circuit depth and resource needs practical?
- How does QPO perform on real quantum hardware (NISQ devices) under realistic noise models and device constraints?
- What are the shot/measurement requirements (and scaling) for MCSWAP-TEST overlap estimation to achieve target MSEs as N increases?
- How should the penalty coefficient λ for the QUBO/Ising encoding be chosen or tuned automatically to balance constraint satisfaction and optimization quality?
- Can QPO be extended to handle richer and more realistic portfolio constraints (heterogeneous asset prices/costs, transaction costs, liquidity, turnover, cardinality beyond simple budget-as-count)?
- How can QPO be adapted for multi-period or dynamic portfolio optimization instead of single-time-step outputs?
- Which encoding strategies (amplitude, divide-and-conquer, quantum compiling, or hybrid approaches) are best for particular asset classes or data distributions?
- Under what conditions and problem sizes is VQE/QPO preferable to QAOA or other quantum-classical hybrid algorithms (and vice versa)?
- How sensitive is the approach to choice of ansatz/parameterized circuit for U_x(θ), and what are practical low-depth ansätze that preserve solution quality?
- How generalizable are the results obtained on cryptocurrency data to traditional asset classes (equities, bonds, ETFs) with different statistical properties?

**Future work:**
- Explore the vast potential of encoder designs and Hamiltonian simulation techniques to improve resource efficiency.
- Develop encoder and Hamiltonian simulation implementations that reduce quantum computation resources.
- Conduct experiments on larger asset universes to evaluate scalability and performance.
## Key ideas
- #idea:quantum-advantage — QPO encodes discrete portfolio and expected returns into a 2n+2 qubit circuit (n = log2 N) and claims O(log N) complexity, mapping the mean-variance objective with budget constraint to a QUBO/Ising Hamiltonian optimized via VQE/QAOA.
- #idea:hybrid-approach — The design is hybrid: classical preprocessing computes μ and Σ and a classical optimizer updates quantum circuit parameters; a control qubit switches between VQE/QAOA optimization and a multi-control SWAP-test (MCSWAP-TEST) overlap-estimation mode.
- #idea:near-term-feasibility — Targeted at NISQ-era variational algorithms and tested on small instances (2–16 assets, 4–10 total qubits) with classical simulation, reporting metrics (Hamming distance, overlap MSE, execution time).
- #limitation:qubit-count — Although claiming logarithmic qubit scaling, experiments only use up to 10 qubits (16 assets); real portfolios would require many more qubits and the small-scale tests do not demonstrate feasibility at realistic sizes.
- #limitation:data-encoding — Uses amplitude encoding and quantum compiling for |x̂> and |μ̂>; the paper does not quantify the cost of state preparation, which may hide exponential classical overhead and undermines the O(log N) complexity claim.
- #limitation:simulation-only — All results are from classical (noise-free) simulation on a local machine (Qiskit simulator); no noisy hardware experiments or error-mitigation strategies are reported.
- #limitation:noise — No noise modelling or runs on real QPUs are provided, so near-term applicability claims lack validation under realistic noisy conditions.
## Contradictions
- Claimed O(log N) qubit complexity is not substantiated by experiments: authors validate only up to 16 assets (4 qubits per vector) and do not account for the cost of amplitude encoding/state preparation, which contradicts the scalability claim.
- Empirical results show close agreement with classical linear-programming baselines (average Hamming distance ~0.092) but do not demonstrate superiority or speedup versus classical solvers; this contradicts any implicit claim of quantum performance advantage.
- Stated near-term NISQ feasibility via VQE/QAOA is not supported by hardware experiments or noise analysis; absence of noisy-QPU validation contradicts claims about practical NISQ applicability.
