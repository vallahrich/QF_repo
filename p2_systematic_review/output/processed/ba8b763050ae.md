## Abstract summary
The paper explores the application of quantum machine learning (QML) methods to credit card fraud detection using a European cardholder transaction dataset. It implements and compares classical ML models, a conventional neural network, and a hybrid quantum-classical neural network (using PennyLane), reporting that the hybrid quantum models train more effectively and reach comparable or improved prediction performance. The study highlights potential speed and efficiency benefits of QML but notes practical constraints from noisy, limited quantum hardware.
## Methodology
The study implements a comparative experimental pipeline that evaluates classical machine‑learning models, a conventional (three‑layer) neural network, and a hybrid quantum‑classical neural network for credit‑card fraud detection. The authors use the publicly available Kaggle European cardholders dataset (September 2013), apply data cleaning and preprocessing (missing value handling, min–max scaling, one‑hot encoding where applicable), and reduce dimensionality with PCA (V1..V28 kept alongside Time and Amount). Visualization with t‑SNE is used for exploratory analysis. Classical classifiers (Decision Tree, K‑Nearest Neighbors, Logistic Regression, Support Vector Machine, Random Forest, XGBoost) are trained and evaluated on the dataset. A classical neural network (three layers) is implemented and trained. The hybrid model is constructed by inserting a PennyLane QNode as a trainable Keras layer between classical layers (i.e., a variational quantum circuit sandwiched between classical layers). PennyLane is used to enable differentiable hybrid training; the QNode is wrapped with the KerasLayer (qnn package) so it can be trained like a conventional layer. Due to long quantum computation times the hybrid quantum model was trained on a 10% subset of the data in early experiments. Models are compared using accuracy, F1 score and detection rates. The paper reports that the hybrid quantum approach attains faster convergence in accuracy (for equal data/epochs) and slightly higher peak accuracy than the classical baseline, although wall‑clock training time was higher for the hybrid model.

**Algorithms used:** Decision Tree, K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), Logistic Regression, Support Vector Machine (SVM), Random Forest, XGBoost (gradient boosting), Principal Component Analysis (PCA), t-SNE (visualization), Classical Neural Network (three-layer), Hybrid Quantum Neural Network (variational QNN layer via PennyLane)
**Frameworks:** PennyLane, Keras, scikit-learn, XGBoost (library)

**Experimental setup:** PennyLane was used to build the QNode and convert it into a KerasLayer (qnn package) so the variational quantum circuit could be trained together with classical layers. Classical models and the classical neural network were trained on the (preprocessed) full dataset; the hybrid quantum neural network was trained in early experiments on a 10% subset of the dataset due to long computation times. No specific QPU, simulator backend name, or shot/decoherence settings are reported in the paper.

**Dataset:** Kaggle 'Credit Card Fraud Detection' dataset (European cardholders, transactions recorded in September 2013): 284,807 transactions with 492 frauds (0.172% fraud rate). Features: PCA‑transformed components V1..V28, plus Time and Amount.
