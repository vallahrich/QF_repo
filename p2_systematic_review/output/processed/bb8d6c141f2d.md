## Abstract summary
The paper introduces QNNformer, a hybrid model that integrates quantum neural networks and a quantum multi-attention mechanism to improve long-term time series forecasting by exploiting quantum parallelism, superposition, and entanglement. It incorporates quantum optimization techniques (Grover diffusion and QAOA) to better capture nonlinear and long-range dependencies, and reports state-of-the-art results on multiple real-world datasets, with average improvements over baselines in MSE and MAE.
## Methodology
The authors propose QNNformer, a hybrid classical-quantum architecture for long-term multivariate time series forecasting that integrates a vanilla Transformer encoder with a quantum neural network (QNN) layer and a quantum multi-head attention mechanism. Each multivariate series is treated channel-wise (split to univariate channels), instance-normalized, and partitioned into patches (length P, stride S). Patches are linearly projected and position-encoded, then processed by a Transformer encoder (multi-head attention, FFN, residual connections) to produce latent representations z. These latents are embedded into quantum states via angle (Rx) embedding. Per attention head, variational quantum circuits (QNNs) with entangling layers generate query, key and value quantum states. Grover diffusion is used for amplitude amplification over marked/high-similarity subspaces and QAOA (Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm) cost-and-mixer layers are applied to optimize attention-related Hamiltonians (Ising-style cost with Zi Zj terms and sparsity priors). Quantum attention similarities are computed as squared inner products |⟨Q|K⟩|^2, normalized with softmax to obtain attention weights, and combined with quantum-derived value states, then mapped back to classical space and passed through linear heads to predict T future timesteps. Training optimizes a combined loss (equal-weight MSE + MAE). Experiments evaluate accuracy (MSE, MAE), robustness and runtime against multiple state-of-the-art classical baselines across several public time-series benchmarks.

**Algorithms used:** Grover diffusion (amplitude amplification), QAOA (Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm), Variational Quantum Circuits (VQC) / Quantum Neural Networks, Angle encoding (Rx gate embedding), Classical Transformer (multi-head attention, feed-forward), Softmax attention (classical normalization)
**Frameworks:** Qiskit (Aer simulator), PyTorch

**Experimental setup:** Quantum circuits were simulated with Qiskit Aer. Classical components and training were implemented in PyTorch. Quantum modules were executed on the simulator on a machine equipped with a single RTX 4090 GPU. Experiments used batch size 8 for runtime comparisons and varied lookback lengths L ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720, 1440, 2880}. Quantum circuit sizes used 4–6 qubits in reported experiments.
