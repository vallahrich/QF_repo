## Abstract summary
This review chapter surveys quantum optimization algorithms—principally Quantum Annealing, QAOA, and VQE—and their applications to logistics (supply chain, vehicle routing, warehouse optimization), finance (portfolio optimization, risk management, forecasting), and material science (quantum chemistry and materials design). It summarizes case studies and implementations, and discusses key barriers such as quantum noise, decoherence, limited qubit counts and scalability, while outlining hybrid quantum–classical approaches and future research directions.

## Limitations
- Quantum noise and decoherence limit the accuracy and reliability of computations
- Limited number of qubits in current devices restricts problem size that can be represented
- Short qubit coherence times constrain the duration of usable quantum computations
- Quantum error correction is resource-intensive and current devices often lack enough qubits to implement it effectively
- Scalability of quantum algorithms (e.g., VQE, QAOA, quantum annealing) to large, real-world problems remains limited
- VQE and other quantum chemistry algorithms currently effective only for small-to-medium molecular systems due to hardware constraints
- Algorithm efficiency on noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) devices is suboptimal; many algorithms are not yet tailored to noisy/imperfection-prone hardware
- Integration challenges when combining quantum solutions with existing classical systems, including software/middleware development and workflows
- Limitations in scaling quantum annealers to very large logistics networks and complex, global supply chains
