## Abstract summary
The paper presents a QAOA-based method for financial portfolio optimization by reformulating the selection problem as a knapsack problem. It maps expected returns (via Markowitz analysis) into the knapsack formulation, incorporates domain-specific investment constraints, and evaluates the approach on both simulated and real quantum devices to demonstrate practical applicability.
## Methodology
The study reformulates the financial portfolio selection problem as a knapsack problem and applies the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) to find near-optimal asset selections. Expected returns are computed using Markowitz mean-variance analysis and mapped to knapsack profit coefficients; domain-specific investment constraints are encoded as feasibility constraints in the knapsack formulation. The QAOA circuit incorporates a feasibility oracle and a specialised mixer structure (components that the authors note are consistent with prior constructions), which enforce or bias toward feasible portfolio states. The overall pipeline includes classical preprocessing (Markowitz return estimation and constraint encoding), construction of the problem Hamiltonian with penalty terms for infeasible solutions, implementation of the QAOA ansatz with the feasibility oracle and mixer, and empirical evaluation of the resulting circuits on both simulators and real quantum hardware. The authors acknowledge a bachelor thesis as a design reference for the feasibility oracle and mixer.
