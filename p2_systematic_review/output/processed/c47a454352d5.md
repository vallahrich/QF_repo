## Abstract summary
The paper proposes using split-step quantum walks (SSQW) to construct parameterized quantum circuits that load classical probability distributions into quantum states by treating an ancilla qubit as the coin and optimizing coin parameters with a classical variational optimizer (COBYLA) using MSE. The authors demonstrate Qiskit implementations that fit normal, log-normal and real stock return distributions and show an example application to European call option pricing, reporting efficient convergence on a simulator.
## Methodology
The authors propose a data-loading method for quantum circuits based on Split-Step Quantum Walks (SSQW). Their approach represents classical target probability distributions as the position-space probability amplitudes of a discrete-time split-step quantum walk driven by parameterized coin operators. A parameterized quantum circuit (PQC) is constructed with one ancilla qubit for the coin (coin Hilbert space) and N qubits for the position space (2^N basis states). The SSQW evolution operator W is realized as W = S- C(θ2) S+ C(θ1) and is applied t times to an initial state |0>^{(N+1)}. After evolution, the position-space amplitudes are measured and compared to the target distribution using a mean-squared-error (MSE) loss. A classical variational loop (COBYLA optimizer) updates the coin parameters to minimize MSE. The workflow is repeated until convergence. The authors demonstrate the method on synthetic distributions (normal, log-normal) and real stock daily-return distributions, and show an application to European call option pricing by using the trained distribution to compute expected payoff.

**Algorithms used:** Split-step Quantum Walk (SSQW), Parameterized Quantum Circuit (PQC), Variational classical optimization (COBYLA), Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss
**Frameworks:** Qiskit, NumPy

**Experimental setup:** 5-qubit parameterized quantum circuit: 1 coin (ancilla) qubit + 4 position qubits (16 position states). Initial state |0>^{5}. Apply SSQW evolution operator W for t = 7 steps. Measure position-space amplitudes to obtain trained distribution. Use classical COBYLA optimizer to minimize MSE between trained and target distributions. Typical run: 800 optimization iterations. Implementation executed via Qiskit on a quantum simulator accessible through IBM Q Experience. Reported wall-clock training time ~20 seconds.

**Dataset:** Synthetic datasets: 100,000 samples drawn with NumPy from target distributions (normal and log-normal), truncated to the interval (0, 15). Real financial datasets: daily returns for stocks (Apple, Microsoft, JP Morgan) collected from Yahoo Finance over specific two-month and three-month periods (Jan 2–Mar 2, 2022 and Mar 2–Jun 2, 2022), truncated to (0, 15) for this work. Also parameterized log-normal distribution used for European call option pricing (S0=2, K=2, σ=0.4, r=0.05, T=40).

## Findings
- [supported] The authors propose a split-step quantum walk (SSQW) based parameterized quantum circuit (PQC) method to load classical probability distributions into quantum states by optimizing coin parameters with a classical optimizer.
- [supported] The authors implemented the SSQW-based loading method in Qiskit and released a practical example as open-source software (preprint claim).
- [supported] In numerical simulations with a 5-qubit circuit (1 coin qubit + 4 position qubits), applying a 7-step SSQW and optimizing coin parameters with COBYLA and an MSE loss, the method converged to target distributions in their experiments (they report ~800 iterations).
- [supported] The SSQW-based method successfully fit normal, log-normal, and empirical daily-return distributions (Apple, Microsoft, JP Morgan) in simulation; the authors report the fitted distributions closely resemble the targets and claim high accuracy.
- [supported] The authors observed that initial coin-parameter choices significantly affect convergence and fit quality; exploiting symmetry in the normal distribution reduced the number of free coin parameters and improved performance.
- [supported] The authors used the trained SSQW-produced distribution to compute a European call option expected payoff; for parameters S0=2, K=2, sigma=0.4, r=0.05, T=40 they report analytical expected payoff 5.5342 (target distribution) and 6.9951 (trained distribution), attributing the difference to skewness in the trained distribution.

**Results summary:** This preprint introduces a method for loading classical probability distributions into quantum states using split-step quantum walks (SSQW) as parameterized quantum circuits, optimizing coin parameters with a classical COBYLA optimizer minimizing mean-squared error. The authors implemented the approach in Qiskit and present simulations using a 5-qubit circuit (1 coin + 4 position qubits), 7 SSQW steps and ~800 optimization iterations. They demonstrate fitting normal, log-normal, and empirical daily-return distributions (several stocks) in simulation, report runtimes on the order of tens of seconds, and apply the trained distribution to compute a European call option expected payoff (noting a difference between analytic and trained-distribution payoffs attributed to skewness). The work positions SSQW-loading as a potentially useful primitive for quantum finance workflows but does not demonstrate a true quantum computational advantage.
