## Abstract summary
This review proposes a hybrid quantum–classical framework for financial risk management and portfolio optimization, arguing that combining classical preprocessing with quantum algorithms (QAOA, VQE, QML, and quantum Monte Carlo) can yield improved optimization, forecasting, and risk simulation in the NISQ era. The paper surveys algorithmic components, industry pilot projects, and practical implementation layers, and concludes that hybrid approaches offer a realistic pathway for financial institutions to gain quantum advantages while addressing ethical, economic, and infrastructural challenges.
## Methodology
The paper follows a qualitative, descriptive and analytical research design that synthesizes existing literature, industry reports, and theoretical models to propose a conceptual Hybrid Quantum–Classical Framework for financial risk management and portfolio optimization. No original empirical simulations or experiments were performed. Data sources included peer-reviewed journals, white papers from quantum technology providers, and financial research studies. The purpose was to integrate recent academic and industrial developments, identify inefficiencies in classical optimization for large-scale portfolios, and design a practical hybrid computational structure that combines classical preprocessing and interpretation with quantum optimization cores. The methodological flow comprises literature integration, problem identification, framework design (a five-layer hybrid architecture: classical pre-processing, quantum encoding, quantum processing, hybrid integration, and decision layer), validation logic via comparative discussion and case-based evidence from published studies, and implication analysis for managerial decision-making. The paper emphasizes conceptual framework construction and secondary-data-based argumentation rather than empirical validation.

**Algorithms used:** QAOA, VQE, Quantum Machine Learning (QML), Quantum Monte Carlo (QMC)
**Frameworks:** Hybrid Quantum–Classical Framework (conceptual), IBM Quantum Experience, Amazon Braket, Google Cirq

## Findings

- [supported] NISQ devices currently have limitations (limited qubit counts, short coherence times, and noise) that constrain large-scale pure-quantum computations.
- [speculative] QAOA is well suited for portfolio optimization and constrained financial optimization problems when used inside a hybrid loop.

- [speculative] Quantum Monte Carlo (QMC) approaches could accelerate Monte Carlo-style risk simulations (e.g., VaR, derivative pricing) and produce more accurate probability distributions for loss estimation.
- [supported] Cloud-based quantum services (IBM Quantum Experience, Google Cirq, Amazon Braket) democratize access and allow financial firms to prototype quantum-classical workflows without large capital expenditure.

**Results summary:** The review argues that quantum computing offers theoretical advantages for complex financial tasks (optimization, risk simulation, pattern detection) and that a hybrid quantum–classical framework is the most practical near-term path given current NISQ limitations. It documents industry interest and pilot projects, highlights candidate algorithms (QAOA, VQE, QML, QMC), and outlines a five-layer hybrid architecture (classical preprocessing, quantum encoding, quantum processing, hybrid integration, decision layer).
