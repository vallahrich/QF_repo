## Abstract summary
The authors use variational and diffusion quantum Monte Carlo (VMC and DMC) to compute quasiparticle energy bands and the quasiparticle effective mass m* of paramagnetic and ferromagnetic two-dimensional uniform electron liquids for metallic densities (1 ≤ rs ≤ 5), carefully controlling finite-size errors with up to 302 electrons. They find that for the paramagnetic case m*≈1 at high density (rs=1) and increases with rs, while for the ferromagnetic case m* decreases with reduced density, and highlight the importance of backflow correlations and careful choice of DMC timestep and extrapolations.
## Methodology
The authors performed real-space quantum Monte Carlo calculations to obtain quasiparticle energy bands and effective masses of the two-dimensional uniform electron liquid (2D-UEL). They used both variational Monte Carlo (VMC) and fixed-node diffusion Monte Carlo (DMC) approaches with Slater–Jastrow (SJ) and Slater–Jastrow with backflow (SJB) trial wave functions. The Jastrow factor included polynomial and plane-wave expansions of interelectronic separations; backflow displacements were represented by polynomials of electron–electron distance to modify determinant coordinates. Variational parameters were optimized by variance minimization followed by linear least-squares energy minimization as implemented in the CASINO code. Quasiparticle energies were computed as total-energy differences when removing or adding an electron from/to a plane-wave orbital: ε−(k)=E0−E−(k) and ε+(k)=E+(k)−E0, with the simulation cell volume held fixed (adjusted rs for N±1 appropriately). Calculations covered paramagnetic (ζ=0) systems with N=146, 218, 302 electrons and ferromagnetic (ζ=1) systems with N=151 across density parameters rs=1–5, evaluating >20 k-vectors up to ~1.7 kF. To extract the quasiparticle effective mass m* they fitted energy-band points with Padé and quartic functions and used m* = kF / (dε/dk)|kF. Finite-size effects were studied by system-size variation and extrapolation (observed scaling with N^{-3/2}), and systematic tests included the influence of nodal topology (SJ vs SJB), reoptimization of trial wave functions at each excited k, choice of fitting function, and DMC time-step dependence (examples: dτ=0.005 and 0.2 a.u., walker count Nw=2560, ~25k steps). The study emphasized control of many-body finite-size errors, reliance on backflow to improve nodal surfaces, and careful assessment of fitting and time-step/systematic biases when reporting m*.

## Findings
- [supported] VMC and DMC calculations of quasiparticle energy bands and effective mass m* were performed for the 2D uniform electron liquid (2D-UEL) in the metallic density range rs = 1–5, using system sizes up to N = 302 (paramagnetic) and N = 151 (ferromagnetic).
- [supported] VMC and DMC effective-mass results agree within statistical error bars when high-quality (SJ-backflow) trial wave functions are used.
- [supported] For paramagnetic 2D-UEL at high density rs = 1 the quasiparticle effective mass m* is very close to 1 (VMC: 0.98(1), DMC: 0.95(1)).
- [supported] In paramagnetic 2D-UEL, m* increases as density is reduced (i.e., m* grows with increasing rs) within the studied range rs = 1–5, when backflow-correlated trial wave functions are used.
- [supported] In ferromagnetic (fully spin-polarized) 2D-UEL, m* is suppressed as density is reduced (m* decreases with increasing rs) within rs = 1–5.
- [supported] Inclusion of backflow (changing nodal topology) in the trial wave function is crucial for obtaining reliable m* values in 2D-UEL, especially at larger rs.
- [supported] Finite-size behavior of the extracted m* shows systematic scaling with N^{-3/2}, enabling extrapolation toward the thermodynamic limit.
- [supported] DMC time-step errors materially affect extracted m* values; the choice of DMC time-step can change the inferred m* and its uncertainty (example: for N=302, rs=5 different dt give different m* estimates).
- [supported] Re-optimizing the trial wave function for each excited k-state has negligible effect on DMC-derived m* but can slightly increase VMC-derived m*.
- [supported] A quartic polynomial fit to the computed energy band gives slightly larger m* than a Padé fit; quartic provided a better fit in this study.
- [speculative] The contrasting dependence of m* on rs between 2D and 3D paramagnetic UELs is attributed to competition between electron-electron correlation (which tends to increase m*) and screening (which is more effective in 3D and tends to decrease m*).
- [speculative] Use of full configuration interaction (FCI) to improve static correlation beyond backflow might lead to larger m* than found here with SJB wave functions (authors' expectation).
- [supported] VMC can be a reliable and cheaper alternative to DMC for excited-state (quasiparticle) studies provided the trial wave function is well optimized and backflow is included for accuracy in 2D systems.

**Results summary:** The authors used variational and diffusion quantum Monte Carlo (VMC and DMC) with Slater–Jastrow and Slater–Jastrow–backflow wave functions to compute quasiparticle energy bands and extract effective masses m* for paramagnetic and ferromagnetic two-dimensional uniform electron liquids over rs = 1–5 and up to N=302 electrons. They find VMC and DMC agree within error when using high-quality backflow trial functions. For paramagnetic 2D-UEL m*≈1 at rs=1 and increases with rs; for ferromagnetic 2D-UEL m* decreases with decreasing density (increases in rs are associated with suppression of m*). Backflow (nodal updates), finite-size extrapolation (N^{-3/2}), careful DMC time-step choice, and fitting choices are important technical factors. The authors interpret differences between 2D and 3D behavior as due to competing correlation and screening effects.

## Limitations
- Many-body finite-size errors remain significant (they were minimized by using several system sizes and extrapolation, but FS errors increase as density is reduced).
- Pathological Hartree–Fock behavior near k = kF (logarithmic divergence of dε(x)/dk) causes large oscillations/derivatives in finite systems and affects QMC bands because DMC does not retrieve all correlation energy.
- Fixed-node (FN) approximation / nodal-surface bias: the quality of the trial wave function's nodes is crucial and residual nodal errors produce variational bias (backflow reduces but does not fully remove this).
- DMC time-step errors: excitation energies (and hence m*) depend on the chosen DMC time step; time-step errors are not guaranteed to cancel in subtraction and full dτ→0 extrapolation is computationally expensive and therefore not performed.
- Dependence on trial-wave-function optimization and fitting choices: VMC m* is sensitive to per-k optimization, the choice of fitting function (Padé vs quartic) affects extracted m*, and the fitting introduces systematic uncertainty.
- Limited density window: calculations are restricted to the metallic density range 1 ≤ rs ≤ 5, so behavior outside this window is not addressed.
- Limited spin cases: only the paramagnetic (ζ = 0) and fully spin-polarized ferromagnetic (ζ = 1) cases were studied; partially polarized (intermediate ζ) states are not investigated.
- Limited direct comparison with experiment: quantitative comparison is inappropriate because real experimental 2D systems have finite thickness and disorder absent from the ideal 2D-UEL model.
## Open questions
- To what extent do remaining nodal-surface errors (even after backflow) bias the computed m*, especially at low density?
- How would a more complete treatment of static correlation (for example via full configuration interaction or multi-determinant expansions) change m*, particularly in the low-density regime?
- What is the precise quantitative balance between electron–electron correlation and screening that causes the opposite rs-dependence of m* in 2D versus 3D paramagnetic UEL?
- Which many-body approximation (e.g., various flavors of GW, self-consistent vs on-shell) most reliably predicts m* for 2D UEL across densities, and how do those compare quantitatively with QMC?
- How do finite-width effects and disorder in realistic experimental 2D electron systems quantitatively modify m*, and can improved models bridge the gap between idealized calculations and experiments?
- What are the optimal strategies (e.g., twist averaging, larger N, improved trial forms, more efficient extrapolation schemes) to eliminate residual finite-size and time-step biases in QP excitation energies at affordable computational cost?

**Future work:**
- Use fuller treatments of static correlation such as full configuration interaction (FCI) or multi-determinant trial wave functions to assess how much m* at low density increases beyond SJB-VMC/DMC results (explicitly mentioned).
- Systematically address DMC time-step bias (dτ→0) for excitation energies — develop or apply more efficient procedures/algorithms to allow reliable time-step extrapolation despite high cost (authors noted the importance and expense).
- Study more realistic 2D electron systems that include finite quantum-well width and disorder to enable more quantitative comparison with experimental measurements (authors point out current mismatch with experiments due to these missing ingredients).
- Further improve nodal surfaces (beyond the SJ and SJB forms used) and trial-wave-function optimization strategies to reduce fixed-node bias, especially at larger rs where correlation effects are stronger.
