## Abstract summary
The study applies a CVaR-augmented Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) to estimate ground-state energies of 50 coarse-grained seven-residue peptides and compares results to 50 ns molecular dynamics (MD) simulations. CVaR-VQE with dense encoding and extensive sampling produced more favorable folding outcomes and higher rates of negative (stable) ground-state energies than MD within the tested settings, suggesting promise for quantum algorithms in addressing protein folding, particularly for disordered regions.
## Methodology
The authors performed a comparative empirical study of protein folding using two approaches: classical molecular dynamics (MD) simulation and a hybrid quantum-classical Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) method employing a Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR) aggregation objective (CVaR-VQE). They constructed 50 synthetic heptapeptide sequences composed from seven disorder-promoting amino acids (D, H, Q, K, S, E, P) by randomly selecting from 5,040 possible permutations. For the MD branch, each peptide sequence was built as an extended structure in Maestro (Schrödinger), processed with Epik and prepared using the OPLS3e force field, solvated in an orthorhombic box with TIP3P water (no counter-ions), and simulated in Desmond for 50 ns. Trajectories were sampled at 0, 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50 ns; extracted frames were minimized and Lennard-Jones / interaction energies and RMSD were computed to identify minimum-energy conformations. For the quantum branch, the coarse-grained lattice folding problem was encoded using a dense encoding / turn-to-qubit mapping which reduced logical bits to qubits (reported as 9 qubits: seven configuration qubits + two interaction/helper qubits). An ansatz with two layers of parameterized single-qubit rotations (Rx, Ry, Rz), conditional rotation gates, Hadamard and CNOT entangling gates was used to prepare trial states. The VQE optimization was run on local/state-vector quantum simulators (Amazon Braket local and SVI statevector simulator), using a CVaR objective that computes the expectation over the lowest-energy tail of the sampled bitstrings (alpha = 0.05 experimentally; alpha = 0.025 for noise-free runs). The classical optimizer used was surrogateopt (Global Optimisation Toolbox; a global optimizer); the algorithm ran for 100 iterations per peptide with 500,000 shots per iteration on the local simulator. At each iteration, bitstrings produced by the circuit were evaluated via the exact Hamiltonian to obtain energies, probabilities were sorted by energy, and the CVaR expectation over the tail was returned as the objective. Results recorded per peptide included minimum (ground-state) energies (kcal/mol), the most probable output bitstring/qubit configuration, and associated probabilities; VQE outcomes were statistically compared to MD results (two-tailed t-test).

**Algorithms used:** Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE), CVaR-VQE (VQE with Conditional Value at Risk aggregation), surrogateopt (global classical optimizer), Molecular Dynamics (Desmond)
**Frameworks:** Amazon Braket (local simulator), SVI state-vector simulator, Desmond (MD), Maestro/Schrödinger (3D builder, Epik), OPLS3e force field, MATLAB Global Optimization Toolbox (surrogateopt)

**Experimental setup:** Quantum experiments ran on local/state-vector simulators accessed via Amazon Braket (local quantum simulator and SVI state-vector simulator). Classical MD was run in Desmond with systems prepared in Maestro (Schrödinger). MD used OPLS3e force field, TIP3P solvent, orthorhombic box, and 50 ns production runs. Access to Amazon Braket was provided via a MeitY QCaL cohort; GPU support (NVIDIA A100) was used for classical computations.

**Results summary:** The paper reports a simulator-based comparison between CVaR-augmented VQE and classical molecular dynamics (50 ns Desmond runs) on a set of 50 seven-residue peptides biased toward disorder-promoting amino acids. CVaR-VQE (run in a state-vector simulator with dense encoding, 100 iterations and 500k shots) is reported to produce ΔG < 0 outcomes for 49/50 peptides, while MD reached ΔG < 0 for 36/50 peptides within 50 ns. The authors present per-peptide examples where VQE found lower-energy conformations than MD (e.g., sequence 16). They emphasize CVaR’s role in reshaping the VQE objective to favor low-energy tails of the distribution and argue that CVaR-VQE shows advantages in sampling and global optimization. However, the manuscript contains internal numerical inconsistencies (reported summary means for MD vs VQE contradict per-peptide negative ΔG claims) and all quantum experiments were simulator-based; scalability to larger proteins and real QPU performance remain untested.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The authors claim that CVaR-VQE outperforms MD in sampling and finding lower-energy conformations for the tested 7-residue peptides, with simulator evidence showing 49/50 peptides reaching negative ΔG under VQE versus 36/50 under 50 ns MD. However, experiments were performed on state-vector simulators (no physical QPU results), the test systems are small (7-residue peptides on a lattice), and the manuscript contains internal numerical inconsistencies (conflicting summary mean energies). Therefore the claimed advantage is suggested by simulator experiments but remains speculative for practical, larger-scale, or hardware-realized quantum advantage.
## Limitations
- Quantum folding simulations are still in early stages of development (author-stated).
- The VQE model has biases related to the choice/definition of qubit configuration and interaction qubits (author-stated).
- Current QPU/qubit limits prevent incorporation of larger peptides or proteins on real quantum hardware (author-stated).
- Comparison between CVaR-VQE and MD is limited by MD sampling/time constraints; a fixed 50 ns MD run did not produce ground states for a substantial fraction of peptides (author-stated).
- CVaR-VQE results were obtained primarily on local/Amazon Braket simulators with very high shot counts (500,000); results may not reflect noisy hardware performance (author-stated/inferred).
- The study used a coarse-grained tetrahedral lattice model and 7-residue peptides, which limits biological realism and generalizability to full atomistic proteins (author-stated/inferred).
- Only a subset of interaction types (primarily 1-nearest-neighbour terms) were considered in the Hamiltonian; longer-range interactions and full atomistic potentials were not modeled (author-stated/inferred).
- Penalty functions and constraint encodings were used to enforce valid folds; these add approximations and may alter the optimization landscape (inferred).
- Selection of peptides was limited to permutations of residues considered disorder-promoting and a random subset of 50 sequences — this sampling may not be representative of broader protein sequence space (author-stated/inferred).
- No empirical quantum hardware experiments (or limited hardware usage) were reported, so scalability and noise robustness remain untested (inferred).
- Optimizer and ansatz choices can substantially affect VQE performance; surrogateopt and specific circuit choices were used here but comparative optimizer benchmarking is missing (author-stated/inferred).
- Data availability statement indicates 'No data was used', which raises reproducibility and transparency concerns (author-stated/inferred).
## Open questions
- How well does CVaR-VQE scale to larger peptides and full-length proteins beyond the 7-residue coarse-grained systems studied?
- How robust is CVaR-VQE to realistic quantum hardware noise and limited qubit counts?
- What is the optimal choice of CVaR cutoff (alpha) across noisy and noise-free regimes for protein folding problems?
- How do different ansatz constructions and classical optimizers affect convergence, barren plateau effects, and final folding quality in this application?
- Can CVaR-VQE (or hybrid quantum algorithms) outperform state-of-the-art enhanced sampling MD methods (e.g., replica exchange, metadynamics, Stochastic Surface Walking) on equivalent models?
- How generalizable are the results obtained on disorder-promoting 7-residue peptides to ordered proteins and to diverse amino-acid compositions?
- What is the impact of encoding choices (dense vs sparse) and penalty-term formulations on solution quality and qubit/resource requirements?
- How to best include longer-range interactions and side-chain detail in the Hamiltonian while keeping qubit/resource demands tractable?
- What are the computational cost and wall-clock time trade-offs between high-shot simulator VQE runs and MD simulations when both are scaled to biologically relevant systems?
- How reproducible are the reported results given the apparent lack of shared data and reliance on simulators?
