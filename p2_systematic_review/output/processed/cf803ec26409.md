## Abstract summary
The paper introduces QB-QAOA, a hard-constraint QAOA method using a novel quasi-binary encoding that represents bounded integer variables with O(log R) qubits and a custom mixer that preserves the sum and bounds constraints. It demonstrates the approach on portfolio optimization problems (incorporating CVaR-QAOA and parameter scheduling) and proposes an iterative precision-increasing scheme to refine solutions when qubit resources are limited.
## Methodology
The paper proposes QB-QAOA, a hard-constraint QAOA variant using a novel quasi-binary encoding for integer/bounded/sum-constrained quadratic optimization (portfolio optimization). Each integer variable yi ∈ [0,Ri] is encoded with at most 2·⌈log2(Ri+1)⌉ qubits via a quasi-binary bit-splitting scheme. The phase-separation Hamiltonian C is constructed by substituting the binary encoding into the quadratic cost and mapping variables to Pauli-Z operators. A bespoke mixing Hamiltonian B (Eq. 12) is introduced that preserves the sum constraint; it is implemented in practice via ring XY mixers (pairwise XY terms among qubits representing the same power-of-two) and ring XYY three-qubit mixers (two lower-power qubits with one higher-power qubit). The initial feasible computational basis state is produced via a greedy allocation and X gates. Objective estimation uses either Normal-QAOA (sample mean) or CVaR-QAOA (mean of best τ fraction, τ=0.05). Classical parameter optimization is done with COBYLA (1000 iterations per start) using several parameter-scheduling strategies (random multi-start Sample20, Optimized Linear Schedule (OLS), Iterative OLS (IOLS), and Iterative QAOA (IQAOA)). A precision-increasing iterative procedure is also proposed: run QB-QAOA at coarse precision α(0), center a narrower bound window around the solution, reduce α by a factor λ (recommend λ with integer reciprocal) and repeat to refine precision while keeping qubit count fixed. Experiments and numerical simulations were performed in Qiskit on portfolio instances (6 NASDAQ assets and random Chinese-stock subsets), exploring p ∈ {1,2,4,8,16}, multiple repeats, measuring approximation ratio, cumulative cost distributions and a mixer "mixability" fidelity metric.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA), Quantum Alternating Operator Ansatz (hard-constraint QAOA / QAOAz), CVaR-QAOA (tail-averaged objective), Quasi-binary encoding (proposed encoding scheme), Ring XY mixer, Ring XYY mixer (proposed), Parameter scheduling: Sample20, OLS, IOLS, IQAOA, Classical optimizer: COBYLA, Brute-force baseline, Precision-increasing iterative refinement (proposed)
**Frameworks:** Qiskit

**Experimental setup:** Simulations were implemented in Qiskit. Key settings: measurement shots K = 1,000,000 for objective estimation; QAOA layer depths p ∈ {1,2,4,8,16}; COBYLA optimizer with 1000 iterations per start (Sample20 uses 20 random starts); CVaR tail τ = 0.05 when using CVaR-QAOA; parameter scheduling strategies (Sample20, OLS, IOLS, IQAOA) applied. Repeated trials: Instance 1 repeated 33 times; Instance 2 ran 320 experiments across parameter configs. Precision-increasing iterative method used λ = 0.5 in the reported example and six iterations.

**Dataset:** Two financial datasets: (1) Daily closing prices for six NASDAQ stocks (AAPL, AMZN, GOOGL, MSFT, NVDA, TSLA) for 2022-12-02 to 2023-02-28 (sourced from Yahoo Finance). (2) Chinese Shanghai and Shenzhen exchange equities: daily data from 2023-05-04 to 2023-08-08 accessed via the QStock package; subsets of 4–8 stocks randomly sampled from 4,836 stocks for Instance 2.

## Findings
- [speculative] Quasi-binary encoding: a novel encoding that represents an integer variable yi in [0, Ri] using at most O(log Ri) qubits (specifically at most 2·ceil(log2(Ri+1)) qubits in worst case) and can represent any integer in the range.
- [supported] Quasi-binary encoding reduces qubit counts compared with naive one-hot/Hodson encodings in the tested examples (authors show qubit-count plots and examples).
- [supported] A tailored mixing Hamiltonian / mixer (combination of ring XY and ring XYY components) can be constructed for the quasi-binary encoding to preserve the sum constraint (hard-constraint QAOA); simulations show transitions only among feasible states.
- [supported] The mixing circuit can be decomposed into CNOTs and multi-control RX gates and its circuit depth can vary between constant and linear depending on problem structure (authors present an explicit circuit construction and depth analysis).
- [supported] CVaR-QAOA outperforms Normal-QAOA for the portfolio instances studied (empirical simulation results reported).
- [supported] Parameter scheduling matters: among the scheduling heuristics tested (Sample20, OLS, IOLS, IQAOA), iterative methods (IOLS/IQAOA) combined with CVaR tend to give the best empirical performance in the authors' experiments; IQAOA performed best in many CVaR experiments.
- [supported] Empirical performance on the presented portfolio instances: Normal-QAOA approximation ratios observed in simulations ranged roughly 0.79–0.91; CVaR-QAOA approximation ratios ranged roughly 0.91–0.99, with performance improving as depth p increased (authors' repeated simulations reported these ranges).
- [supported] Precision-increasing iterative procedure (reduce α, re-center bounds around previous solution with shrink factor λ) empirically improves solution quality while keeping the same qubit count; example: λ=0.5, six iterations on an 18-qubit instance produced progressively better CDFs of cost (authors' simulation).
- [speculative] Claim of uniqueness: the paper asserts quasi-binary encoding is the only encoding satisfying three properties simultaneously (logarithmic qubit scaling, bound-constraint support, and hard-constraint QAOA compatibility).
- [speculative] The approach is presented as broadly applicable to portfolio optimization instances that can be discretized with fixed precision and sum/bound constraints (theoretical applicability claim backed by examples but not large-scale empirical validation).

**Results summary:** The paper introduces QB-QAOA, a QAOA-style algorithm using a new quasi-binary integer encoding plus a custom mixer (ring XY + ring XYY) that enforces bound and global-sum constraints in a hard-constraint manner. The encoding aims to keep qubit counts logarithmic in each variable's range. The authors implement and simulate the approach on small portfolio-optimization instances (Qiskit): they show the proposed mixer preserves feasibility, report that CVaR-QAOA plus iterative parameter scheduling (IOLS/IQAOA) yields higher approximation ratios than Normal-QAOA, and demonstrate an iterative precision-increasing workflow that improves solution quality while holding qubit count fixed. Results are empirical on small instances (up to ~18 qubits in reported examples).

- Parameter optimization is difficult in practice (explicit): classical optimizers (COBYLA) can get trapped in local optima and suffer barren-plateau phenomena for large p (observed degradation at p=16).
- Precision-increasing iterative method can fall into local optima if the iteration factor λ is too small (explicit).
- Quasi-binary encoding requires additional splitting heuristics when some Ri are much larger than others to satisfy mixer design constraints (explicit): encoding needs further improvement in these special cases.

## Open questions
- How does QB-QAOA scale in practice to larger problem sizes (larger n and/or finer precision) on realistic quantum hardware?
- How robust is the proposed mixer circuit and quasi-binary encoding to hardware noise, limited connectivity, and gate errors on NISQ devices?
- What are the optimal strategies (algorithms and hyperparameters) for classical parameter optimization of QB-QAOA at higher p to avoid barren plateaus and local optima?
- What is the best systematic way to choose the iteration factor λ and the number of iterations in the precision-increasing iterative method to balance precision improvement against risk of getting trapped in local optima?
- Can the quasi-binary encoding and the proposed mixer be generalized to arbitrary bound patterns and heterogeneous Ri without ad-hoc splitting heuristics?
- What are the exact gate count and depth scalings (asymptotic resource estimates) for the mixer decomposition in terms of number of qubits and variable ranges?
- Are there theoretical guarantees (connectivity/reachability proofs) that the ring XY and ring XYY mixer construction span the feasible subspace for arbitrary instances under quasi-binary encoding?
- How does QB-QAOA compare empirically and resource-wise with alternative encodings and constrained-QAOA constructions on larger and more diverse financial instances?
- Does the precision-increasing iterative method converge to the global optimum as precision is refined, or can earlier coarse solutions trap later iterations systematically?
- How sensitive are solution qualities to choices like CVaR tail τ, risk factor q, and Hamiltonian scaling η in practical financial use cases?
