## Abstract summary
The paper presents explicit quantum oracles tailored for Grover-based algorithms to select efficient portfolios that match investor preferences, by enforcing thresholds on returns and standard deviations and enabling selection by Sharpe ratio. The authors describe circuits for conditional slicing (greater-than comparisons using phase-encoded values), integrate these oracles into Quantum Exponential Search (QES) and Grover Adaptive Search (GAS), and demonstrate small-scale implementations using Qiskit simulators, discussing qubit scaling and practical limitations.
## Methodology
The authors construct explicit quantum oracles to perform conditional selection (slicing) over a precomputed set of efficient portfolios (expected returns and standard deviations) and use Grover-based search algorithms to retrieve portfolios matching investor preferences. The workflow is: compute the efficient frontier classically (mean-variance optimization with no-short-selling), encode the list values into phase-encoding unitaries U = diag(e^{i2π s_k}) and use quantum phase estimation to obtain t-bit integer approximations of decimal parts, implement a bitwise greater-than (GT) comparison circuit (built from X, CX and multi-controlled-NOT (MCX) gates with uncomputation) to mark items exceeding thresholds, and wrap that oracle in Quantum Exponential Search (QES) to return an index satisfying the condition. For optimization (finding max Sharpe ratio) they use Grover Adaptive Search (GAS) with QES as a subroutine. They also employ quantum counting (phase estimation on the Grover operator) to detect number of solutions and handle the zero-solution case (time-out of O(sqrt(N)) if needed). The implementations and experiments are run on IBM Qiskit simulators using small subsamples (8 portfolios) due to qubit resource limits. Key implementation parameters include a chosen resolving power d = 0.01 for phase encoding and explicit formulae for the number of counting/phase-estimation qubits (m choices given in the paper).

**Algorithms used:** Grover's algorithm, Quantum Exponential Search (QES), Grover Adaptive Search (GAS), Quantum Phase Estimation, Quantum Counting
**Frameworks:** Qiskit

**Experimental setup:** Implementation executed on IBM Qiskit simulators (no physical QPU). Small-scale experiments using a subsample of eight efficient portfolios due to qubit resource limits.

**Dataset:** Efficient frontier values (expected returns and standard deviations) computed classically via mean-variance optimization with no-short-selling. Authors used a subsample of eight portfolio entries; all values scaled to [0,1). Risk-free rate set to 0% for Sharpe ratio calculation.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** theoretical

The paper relies on well-known theoretical quadratic speed-ups of Grover-based algorithms (QES/GAS) — e.g., O(sqrt(N/M)) or O(sqrt(N)) oracle-call scalings — and provides oracle constructions to leverage those algorithms for portfolio selection. However, the work only demonstrates small-scale examples on simulators (N=8) and does not empirically demonstrate a practical quantum advantage on physical hardware or at meaningful problem sizes; thus the claimed advantage is theoretical, not experimentally demonstrated.
## Limitations
- Infeasibility of implementing the complete portfolio optimization problem with continuous asset allocations and positivity (no-short-selling) constraints on current gate-based quantum hardware or simulators (author-stated).
- The implementation assumes that the efficient frontier (risk-return pairs) is precomputed classically and available as input; the quantum routines operate only on selection from that list (author-stated).
- Small-scale experiments only: authors subsampled the efficient frontier to eight values to reduce qubit requirements; larger instances were not demonstrated (author-stated).
- Experiments used simplifying assumptions: all values lie in [0, 1) so integer-part comparisons were not needed, and a 0% risk-free rate was used for Sharpe ratio computations (author-stated).
- The approach requires access to phase-encoded unitaries U = diag(e^{i2π s_k}) for list items (quantum query / state-preparation assumption). Preparing such U efficiently is nontrivial and was assumed available (author-stated).
- Resolving power depends on chosen phase-estimation precision d: the number of phase-estimation qubits (and hence resources) grows with desired precision even if independent of N (author-stated).
- [inferred] Oracle implementations rely on multi-controlled gates (MCX) and many CX/NOT gates; the paper does not detail practical gate decomposition costs or circuit depth on real hardware, which may be large.
- Grover-based subroutines (QES/GAS) yield bounded success probabilities (conventionally ≥1/2) and therefore require repetitions; multiple solutions require multiple runs and the M=0 case is handled via time-outs (author-stated).
- Quantum counting and phase estimation introduce additional resource overhead (number of counting qubits scales as O(log N) for desired accuracies) (author-stated).
- All reported results are from noise-free simulators (Qiskit); no assessment of noise robustness or error mitigation was performed (author-stated).
- [inferred] Total qubit and depth requirements may still be large in practice: e.g., the multi-list circuit used 37 qubits for only eight items; scaling constants and realistic overheads could make implementation impractical on near-term devices.
- [inferred] Phase estimation runtime and the repeated use of controlled operations may yield high circuit depth, challenging feasibility on NISQ devices.
- [inferred] The framework as implemented is tailored to non-negative asset weights / returns (no short positions); handling negative values or short-selling would require additional circuit logic.

- #idea:near-term-feasibility — Provide small-scale implementations on Qiskit simulators (8-item portfolio subsample) and detailed circuit recipes (phase-encoding unitaries, GT-gate construction, qubit counts), but conclude full continuous-weight portfolio optimisation is infeasible on current gate-based hardware.

- #limitation:data-encoding — Phase-encoding of decimal parts (resolving power d = 0.01) and associated phase-estimation register sizes impose significant resource/precision trade-offs; explicit t and m formulae highlight encoding overhead.
- #limitation:simulation-only — All experiments executed on IBM Qiskit simulators (no physical QPU experiments), limiting assessment of noise and real-device overheads.
