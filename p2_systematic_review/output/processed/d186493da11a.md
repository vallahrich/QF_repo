## Abstract summary
The paper proposes a quantum machine learning approach using Variational Quantum Classifiers (VQCs) with optimized quantum encoding/decoding (notably Pauli feature maps) to address class imbalance without resampling. Experiments on several real-world imbalanced datasets (imported food inspection, bank customers, credit card fraud) show improved recall for minority classes and robustness versus classical methods and QSVMs, with performance depending on feature mapping, entanglement, and circuit depth.
## Methodology
The authors investigate using quantum machine learning to mitigate class imbalance without resampling. They select Variational Quantum Classifiers (VQCs) (and compare to QSVMs and multiple classical baselines) and design experiments that: (1) reduce input dimensionality (PCA / MCA) to four features to match a 4-qubit encoding, (2) encode classical features into quantum states using different Feature Maps (ZFeatureMap, ZZFeatureMap, PauliFeatureMap) and test Pauli gate combinations, (3) apply a parameterized variational ansatz (RealAmplitudes) with qubit rotations (RY) and CNOT entanglement (entanglement patterns Linear/Circular/Full were explored; Linear was used in examples), (4) measure Z-axis expectation values and post-process outputs via a mapping g(f) = (1+f)/2 to obtain probabilities, (5) optimize circuit parameters classically using COBYLA (maxiter=100, tol=1e-6) minimizing cross-entropy loss, and (6) evaluate performance primarily by macro-averaged Recall (also reporting Precision, F1, runtime) across multiple datasets (a food inspection seasoning dataset, Bank Customers, Credit Card Fraud) with and without SMOTE and across multiple random seeds. IBM Qiskit and IBM Quantum backends (simulators and hardware) were used; an Nvidia Tesla A100 GPU server hosted classical preprocessing and experiments. Measurement error mitigation resilience was set to 2 when running on hardware. Extensive hyperparameter exploration included Feature Map repetitions, entanglement degree/repetitions, ansatz layers, and selection of Pauli rotations to identify configurations maximizing minority-class Recall.

**Algorithms used:** Variational Quantum Classifier (VQC), Quantum Support Vector Machine (QSVM), Classical SVM, Random Forest, XGBoost, Extra Trees, Gradient Boosted Trees (GBT), AdaBoost, Ensemble (voting), Stacking, COBYLA (classical optimizer)
**Frameworks:** Qiskit, IBM Quantum Platform

**Experimental setup:** AI GPU server with Nvidia Tesla A100 (32-core CPU, 256 GB RAM, 5.76 TB storage) used for classical parts and simulations. Quantum experiments executed on IBM Quantum backends (simulators and available quantum hardware) via Qiskit. Measurement error mitigation resilience set to 2 for hardware runs.

**Dataset:** Multiple datasets: (1) Food import inspection — seasoning products (real-world FDA dataset) highly imbalanced (~98.5% majority vs 1.5% minority); (2) Bank Customers (telemarketing/term-deposit subscription) from Kaggle, imbalance ~85.4%/14.6%; (3) Credit Card Fraud (Kaggle), extreme imbalance ~99.8%/0.2%.

### Process
Pipeline steps: (1) Preprocess data (PCA/MCA to reduce to 4 features). (2) Optionally apply SMOTE for oversampling (experiments both with and without). (3) Map reduced classical features into quantum states using a Feature Map (tested ZFeatureMap, ZZFeatureMap, PauliFeatureMap; PauliFeatureMap chosen as best). (4) Prepare initial superposition (Hadamard gates), then apply the Feature Map. (5) Apply variational circuit (RealAmplitudes ansatz with RY rotations on all qubits and CNOT entanglement; entanglement pattern varied — Linear used in examples). The number of qubits equals number of input features (4). (6) Measure qubits along Z and compute expectation values; post-process using g(f)=(1+f)/2 to obtain class probabilities. (7) Compute cross-entropy loss (binary) and optimize classical parameters using COBYLA (maxiter=100, tol=1e-6). (8) Repeat hyperparameter sweeps over Feature Map repetitions, entanglement degree/pattern, number of ansatz layers, and Pauli gate combinations; record Precision/Recall/F1 (macro averaged) and runtime. (9) Compare VQC performance to QSVM and multiple classical baselines across multiple random seeds and datasets to assess robustness.

### Output
Reported metrics were macro-averaged Precision, Recall (primary metric), F1-score and runtime. Baselines included multiple classical models (Random Forest, XGBoost, Extra Trees, GBT, AdaBoost, SVM, ensemble, stacking) and QSVM. Results presented in tabular form comparing SMOTE vs non-SMOTE conditions and multiple RandomState samplings; key claimed outputs include substantially higher macro Recall for VQC on several datasets without SMOTE and per-gate/feature-map performance tables.

## Findings
- [supported] Variational Quantum Classifiers (VQCs) can outperform several classical ML models on the tested imbalanced real-world food-inspection dataset (seasoning products) in terms of Recall for the minority (hazardous) class.
- [supported] Pauli FeatureMap encoding yielded better performance than ZFeatureMap and ZZFeatureMap in the authors' experiments.
- [supported] Certain Pauli gate combinations (e.g., YZ, ZY, XYZ) produced higher Recall (≈0.87) in the authors' QSVM/VQC experiments on the food dataset.
- [supported] The VQC achieved very high Recall on multiple datasets without applying resampling (SMOTE), e.g., recall values up to 0.98 on the food dataset and up to 0.99 on the Credit Card Fraud dataset (Non-SMOTE runs).
- [supported] Optimizing hyperparameters of the quantum pipeline — feature map choice, number of repetitions (feature map depth), entanglement pattern, and variational circuit layers — materially affected VQC performance.
- [supported] On some benchmark imbalanced datasets (Bank Customers, Credit Card Fraud), the VQC exhibited higher Recall in Non-SMOTE conditions compared with classical SVM and QSVM in the authors' experiments.
- [supported] QSVM performance benefited substantially from SMOTE in some cases (i.e., QSVM showed improved Recall when SMOTE was applied), indicating sensitivity to resampling approaches.
- [speculative] Quantum encoding/entanglement operations can 'restore' or exploit symmetry in data distributions in a way that mitigates class imbalance without resampling (this is proposed as a conceptual explanation by the authors but not proved theoretically beyond experiments).
- [speculative] QML (via appropriately designed circuits) can amplify class differences by rotating qubit axes aligned with data distribution, improving separability (argument illustrated with Bloch sphere visualizations but not generally proved).
- [speculative] VQCs are better suited than QSVMs for NISQ-era devices because their parameterized circuits can integrate measurement noise into optimization and thus reduce errors (authors argue this as a rationale for selecting VQC).
- [speculative] Quantum circuits/gates can accelerate matrix operations and reduce computational complexity relative to classical methods (stated as a general potential benefit of quantum computing).
- [supported] The authors found that tuning Pauli rotations, entanglement patterns, and circuit depth was critical to obtaining the reported high Recall on highly imbalanced datasets.

**Results summary:** The paper reports empirical experiments on a highly imbalanced real-world food-inspection dataset and additional benchmark imbalanced datasets (Bank Customers, Credit Card Fraud), showing that a Variational Quantum Classifier (VQC) using a Pauli FeatureMap and tuned variational circuits can achieve substantially higher Recall for the minority class than several classical models and (in many runs) Quantum SVMs, notably without applying resampling methods like SMOTE. The authors present numeric results (e.g., VQC recall up to 0.98 on a food dataset and up to 0.99 on a fraud dataset in Non-SMOTE experiments), compare different Pauli gate combinations and entanglement patterns, and argue that data encoding choices and circuit hyperparameters are key to performance. They also claim the approach is well-suited to NISQ devices and suggest QML offers a symmetry-aware alternative to resampling for class imbalance.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** demonstrated

The authors present empirical evidence on several real and benchmark imbalanced datasets showing VQC models (with Pauli FeatureMap and tuned circuits) achieving higher minority-class Recall than tested classical models and QSVM runs, notably without resampling. The demonstrated advantage is constrained to their experimental setups, datasets, and hyperparameter configurations rather than a general theoretical proof of broad quantum superiority.
## Limitations
- The optimization process lacks a mathematically rich explanation for why certain quantum circuit states and configurations outperform others.
- Further validation is required across a wider variety of real-world datasets to fully confirm utility and generalizability.
- Dependence on NISQ-era hardware: current noise levels and the absence of error correction constrain algorithm choice, scalability, and may affect real-hardware performance.
- Increasing circuit complexity (more Pauli gate combinations, deeper ansätze, more repetitions) increases training time and resource consumption.
- [inferred] Dimensionality reduction (PCA to 4 features) applied to enable quantum speedups may discard relevant information and affect model generalizability.
- [inferred] Extremely small absolute counts of minority-class instances in some test splits (e.g., only a couple of hazardous items) may limit robustness of evaluation and can inflate perceived performance.
- [inferred] Reliance on a single classical optimizer configuration (COBYLA with maxiter=100, tol=1e-6) may constrain convergence behavior and introduce optimization-specific biases.
- [inferred] Evaluation emphasis on Recall (macro average) with less emphasis on other metrics (precision, ROC-AUC) can mask trade-offs such as low precision or diminished overall F1 in some cases.
- [inferred] Comparative experiments focused on SMOTE-style oversampling; limited systematic comparison with a broader set of algorithm-level imbalance strategies (e.g., cost-sensitive learning, specialized ensembles) was performed.
- [inferred] Use of simulators and a limited set of real quantum backends leaves open questions about performance, noise resilience, and runtime on production-scale quantum hardware.
- [inferred] Scalability concerns: number of qubits required grows with input dimensionality, so applicability to high-dimensional financial datasets and large-scale problems is unclear.
## Open questions
- What is the formal mathematical reason that certain feature maps, Pauli gate choices, rotation axes, and entanglement patterns produce superior separation for imbalanced data?
- How generalizable are the observed VQC benefits across diverse domains (e.g., financial services) and datasets with different imbalance ratios and feature structures?
- How do circuit depth, number of qubits, entanglement topology, and gate selection trade off predictive performance against noise sensitivity and runtime on real NISQ devices?
- Can quantum classifiers (VQCs, QSVMs, etc.) consistently outperform state-of-the-art classical approaches (including cost-sensitive methods, advanced ensembles, and hybrid methods) for class imbalance across tasks?
- What is the best way to combine or compare QML approaches with classical resampling techniques (e.g., SMOTE) — are there hybrid pipelines that yield further gains?
- What are practical resource, latency, and cost requirements to deploy VQC-based models in production environments, especially when real quantum hardware is involved?
- How can we improve interpretability/explainability of VQC learned parameters so that we can link quantum parameters to classical notions of feature importance and decision boundaries?

**Future work:**
- Perform further validation across a wider and more diverse set of real-world datasets to confirm the utility of QML for class imbalance.
- Develop and investigate methods to enhance the explainability of QML optimizations, providing a mathematical understanding of why certain quantum circuit configurations perform better.
## Key ideas
- #idea:quantum-advantage — VQCs (4-qubit RealAmplitudes ansatz with Pauli feature maps) achieved higher macro-Recall on multiple highly imbalanced financial datasets (food inspection, bank customers, credit-card fraud) compared to QSVM and a range of classical baselines without requiring SMOTE oversampling.
- #idea:near-term-feasibility — Experiments were executed on IBM Quantum backends (with measurement error mitigation) and simulators, demonstrating NISQ-era applicability for small-scale (4-qubit) classification tasks.
- #idea:hybrid-approach — The pipeline uses classical preprocessing (PCA/MCA to 4 features), classical optimizers (COBYLA), and classical postprocessing combined with quantum circuits, highlighting a practical hybrid QPU-CPU workflow.
- #limitation:qubit-count — The approach relies on encoding into 4 qubits (after heavy dimensionality reduction), indicating limited demonstration scale and potential constraints for higher-dimensional financial feature sets.
- #limitation:data-encoding — Heavy dimensionality reduction (PCA/MCA to 4 features) is required to fit data into the 4-qubit encoding; this raises concerns about information loss and the generality of the results.
- #limitation:noise — Experiments applied measurement error mitigation (resilience=2), implying hardware noise mitigation was necessary and that results may be sensitive to current device noise levels.
- #idea:quantum-advantage — Model performance depends strongly on feature-map choice (PauliFeatureMap found best), entanglement pattern, and circuit depth, suggesting quantum encoding/ansatz design is critical to observed gains.
- #idea:hybrid-approach — Extensive hyperparameter sweeps (feature map repetitions, entanglement degree/patterns, ansatz layers) and multiple random seeds were used to assess robustness across samplings and datasets.
## Contradictions
- contradiction:scalability — The paper reports improved performance on 4-qubit problems after aggressive dimensionality reduction; it is unclear whether the claimed advantage will scale to realistic, higher-dimensional financial datasets or larger-qubit devices.
- Possible contradiction — The observed gains may be attributable to preprocessing choices (PCA/MCA to 4 features), feature-map/ansatz tuning, or sampling decisions rather than an inherent quantum speedup or superiority over optimized classical pipelines; the paper does not demonstrate scaling or isolate quantum-specific sources of improvement.
