## Abstract summary
This whitepaper proposes efficient quantum algorithms and cloud-based AI models to improve financial prediction and portfolio decision-making, focusing on wealth distribution prediction and extracting price-sensitive information. It presents an empirical comparative analysis of quantum, classical machine learning, and hybrid approaches (including cloud deployment) on real financial datasets—highlighting potential performance gains from AI-operated quantum algorithms and discussing implications, challenges, and future research directions for financial services.
## Methodology
The study conducts a comparative analysis between quantum-enhanced approaches and classical cloud-based AI models on financial prediction and portfolio tasks. The authors report two main experiments: (1) an empirical simulation using equity return data from a cross-section of publicly traded U.S. firms to evaluate portfolio optimization and extreme-event prediction, and (2) a theoretical/comparative study of quantum-inspired algorithms (notably quantum walks) applied to time series and unsupervised clustering. For the empirical work, daily returns for 450 U.S. companies (Feb 1998 – Oct 2005) were used; a subset of the data (reported as 300 trading days producing 47 observations) was employed for certain portfolio-optimization tests. The pipeline described involves computing returns, examining moment properties (means, covariances, skewness, kurtosis), applying mean–variance optimization and downside-risk-aware portfolio constructions, training and testing multiple classical predictive models (logistic regression, decision trees, random forest, SVM, XGBoost, KNN, mean-reverting rules, deep learning/reinforcement learning where noted), and applying quantum algorithms/circuits or quantum-inspired methods to extract hidden information and perform optimization. Performance comparisons were made on error rates and goodness-of-fit metrics; the paper reports that hybrid approaches combining quantum circuits with AI-based gradient boosting produced statistically significant outperformance versus several classical baselines. No low-level quantum gate sequences, specific optimizer settings, or named quantum frameworks are provided in the paper.

**Algorithms used:** logistic regression, decision trees, random forest, support vector machine (SVM), k-nearest neighbors (KNN), mean-reverting algorithm/rule-based, gradient boosting / XGBoost (extreme gradient boosting), deep learning (unspecified architectures), reinforcement learning (unspecified), mean-variance portfolio optimization, quantum circuits (hybrid quantum-classical), quantum walks (quantum-inspired algorithms), quantum annealing (discussed as background)

**Experimental setup:** High-performance cloud computing architecture hosting cloud-based AI models and simulations of quantum algorithms / quantum circuits. The paper references quantum annealing platforms and various qubit technologies (superconducting, ion traps, neutral atoms) in background discussion but does not specify an actual QPU, simulator name, or cloud provider used in experiments.

**Dataset:** Daily price returns for 450 publicly traded U.S. companies covering the period February 1998 to October 2005. For portfolio optimization tests a subset described as 300 trading days (resulting in 47 observations as reported) was used. The dataset exhibits typical asset-return characteristics such as skewness and excess kurtosis.
