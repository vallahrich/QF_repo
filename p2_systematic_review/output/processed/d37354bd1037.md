## Abstract summary
The paper implements a quantum computing approach to multi-asset, multi-period portfolio simulation, using IBM's 127-qubit System One at Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute to generate discretized multivariate normal return distributions and simulate rebalancing strategies. The authors demonstrate proof-of-concept simulations (using 3 qubits per asset) comparing input and output covariance, discuss practical limitations such as circuit build time and hardware/software discrepancies, and emphasize the need for continued quantum software development in finance.
## Methodology
The authors perform a quantum-based multi-period portfolio simulation for a three-asset portfolio (US equities, international equities, global fixed income). They use 20 years of historical index data (S&P 500, MSCI ACWI ex US, Bloomberg Global Aggregate) to obtain expected returns and the covariance matrix, assume a multivariate log-normal return model and set distribution bounds at +/- 3 standard deviations. A quantum circuit is constructed to represent the discretized multivariate normal distribution: each asset is allocated a small qubit register (typical experiments used [3,3,3] qubits per asset) and the registers are combined to produce joint samples. Each execution performs 120 shots (each shot representing one month); thus a single execution simulates 10 years of monthly returns. The measured bitstrings are converted back to returns; the output sample covariance is compared to the input covariance to assess fidelity (example reported ΔΣ entries). Multiple rebalancing rules (monthly, quarterly, semi-annual, annual) are applied to the simulated return paths to evaluate allocation performance. Experiments are run on both an IBM quantum simulator/emulator and on a physical IBM Quantum System One (127-qubit, 'Eagle' processor) accessed via IBM cloud at Rensselaer; the authors also report circuit build and run time behavior and limitations when increasing qubit counts ([4,4,4], [5,5,5]).

**Algorithms used:** Custom quantum circuit for discretized multivariate normal distribution sampling
**Frameworks:** IBM Quantum cloud (simulator and IBM Quantum System One)

**Experimental setup:** Used IBM quantum simulator/emulator for development and testing, and IBM Quantum System One (127 qubits, 'Eagle' processor) hosted at Rensselaer via IBM cloud for physical runs. Quantum circuits constructed to sample discretized multivariate normal distributions; per-execution 120 shots. Qubit configurations tested included [3,3,3], [4,4,4], [5,5,5].

**Dataset:** 20 years of historical monthly index data for three proxies: S&P 500 (US equities), MSCI All Country World Index ex US (international equities), and Bloomberg Global Aggregate (global fixed income). Expected returns were set to 10%, 10%, and 6% respectively. The simulation assumes a multivariate log-normal return distribution.

## Contradictions
- contradiction:scalability — The paper reports failures and degraded fidelity when increasing per-asset qubit registers ([4,4,4], [5,5,5]) and notes long circuit build times and hardware/software discrepancies, contradicting any broad claims that current QPUs can scale to realistic multi-asset, high-resolution simulations without substantial engineering work.
