## Abstract summary
The paper presents a hybrid quantum–classical fraud detection pipeline using CVQBoost on the photonic Dirac-3 processor, integrating a heterogeneous pool of weak classifiers (KNN, LDA, logistic regression, XGBoost). Evaluated on a Kaggle credit-card fraud dataset, the quantum-enhanced ensemble achieved mean AUC-PR > 0.8 — about a 9% relative improvement over the best published classical baseline — and highlights trade-offs between training runtime and detection performance, suggesting photonic QML can improve rare-event detection in finance.
## Methodology
The study implements a hybrid quantum-classical boosting pipeline (CVQBoost, building on QBoost) using the Dirac-3 continuous-variable photonic processor (Quantum Computing Inc.) to perform optimization of weak-classifier selection and weighting for credit-card fraud detection. The publicly available MLG-ULB Kaggle credit-card fraud dataset was preprocessed by removing rows with missing values, mapping labels to ±1, and scaling features to [0,1]. Data were split 70/30 into training and test folds; the training fold was oversampled with SMOTE while the test fold was left unchanged. A heterogeneous pool of weak classifiers (KNN, LDA, logistic regression, and shallow XGBoost) was constructed classically. For each run the weak classifiers were built, a Hamiltonian encoding the ensemble selection problem was constructed (using QCi’s eqc models / QBoostClassifier functionality), and the Hamiltonian was submitted to the Dirac-3 device which returned selection/weight samples. The returned weights were then used by a classical model for inference. Experiments used a one-factor-at-a-time (OFAT) hyperparameter design to explore weak-classifier settings; primary quantum solver knobs explored were relaxation schedule and number of samples (weak-classifier-schedule and lambda regularization were held fixed in reported experiments). Performance was averaged across N=10 independent random-seed runs, and additional repeated resampling of the fixed test fold (random splits into validation and evaluation subsets) was used to calibrate decision thresholds before final evaluation. Reported metrics include AUC-PR (primary), AUC-ROC, precision, recall, and accuracy; runtime was decomposed into weak-classifier construction, Hamiltonian construction, and Dirac-3 runtime.

**Algorithms used:** CVQBoost, QBoost, SMOTE, k-Nearest Neighbours (KNN), Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA), Logistic Regression, XGBoost
**Frameworks:** QCi eqc models (QBoostClassifier), XGBoost, (implied) scikit-learn / standard ML libraries for KNN, LDA, LogisticRegression, SMOTE (imbalanced-learn or equivalent)

**Experimental setup:** Dirac-3 continuous-variable photonic quantum processor (Quantum Computing Inc.) used to solve the Hamiltonian optimization for weak-classifier selection/weighting; classical preprocessing and weak-classifier training on an AMD Ryzen 5 5600G CPU. Job submission to remote Dirac-3 solver with sampling/relaxation parameters; runtimes decomposed into weak-classifier construction, Hamiltonian construction, and Dirac-3 run-time.

**Dataset:** MLG-ULB Kaggle credit-card fraud dataset (public Kaggle dataset). Highly imbalanced fraud data (fraud rate reported <0.2%).

**Results summary:** The paper reports that a hybrid quantum-classical boosting pipeline (CVQBoost) executed with the Dirac-3 photonic processor and a heterogeneous pool of weak classifiers (KNN, LDA, logistic regression, shallow XGBoost) improved precision-recall performance on the MLG-ULB Kaggle credit card fraud benchmark. The best configuration (KNN weak learners) reached AUC-PR 0.8108 and AUC-ROC 0.9821, outperforming a cited classical baseline (CAD) by +0.0685 AUC-PR (~9.2% relative) and +0.0087 AUC-ROC. The study also characterizes a trade-off between predictive quality and training cost—KNN yields the highest detection quality at substantially higher training time, while LDA is fastest but yields weaker PR performance—and finds that tuning Dirac-3 relaxation/sample parameters within tested ranges had limited effect on AUC metrics. The authors interpret these empirical gains as evidence that quantum-assisted optimization can enhance rare-event detection, while noting limitations in generalizability and deployment readiness.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The paper presents empirical improvement in a benchmark AUC-PR when using a hybrid CVQBoost pipeline with the Dirac-3 photonic optimizer, which suggests potential quantum-assisted benefit for this task. However, the experiments are limited to a single public benchmark and hybrid architecture (with substantial classical preprocessing and classical weak learners); the causal contribution of the quantum device vs. ensemble construction and classical elements is not isolated. Thus a general quantum advantage is suggested but not conclusively demonstrated, and broader validation is required.
## Limitations
- Benchmark datasets (Kaggle MLG-ULB) may not capture the diversity and scale of real banking transactions; generalisability to production data is uncertain.
- Integration challenges with existing financial IT infrastructure and the need for robust, production-grade quantum–classical workflows.
- Overall computation includes substantial classical preprocessing (weak-classifier training, Hamiltonian construction) and remote solver overhead (job submission latency, queueing, repeated sampling), which can create hardware access and availability bottlenecks.
- Financial deployments face strict data governance and privacy requirements that are not addressed in this study.
- KNN-based ensembles, while most accurate, incur substantially higher training cost (runtime) which may limit operational applicability.
