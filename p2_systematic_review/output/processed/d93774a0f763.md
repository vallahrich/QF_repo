## Abstract summary
The paper presents a statistical implementation of the Lanczos algorithm within variational quantum Monte Carlo by using the stochastic reconfiguration (SR) method to stabilize the sign problem and enable a few Lanczos steps for large systems. The approach yields systematic, variational improvements to trial Jastrow-Slater wavefunctions (including multi-parameter optimization) and demonstrates accurate energy and correlation estimates on strongly correlated lattice models such as the t–J model. The variance of the energy is used to assess convergence toward the exact ground state and to guide extrapolations.
## Methodology
The paper develops and tests a statistical implementation of the Lanczos algorithm within a Variational Quantum Monte Carlo framework by using the Stochastic Reconfiguration (SR) method to stabilize propagation and control the sign problem. The key idea is to replace the exact propagated many-body state G|psi> with an approximated state psi' determined by enforcing a set of p+1 linear SR constraints (expectation values of chosen operators O_k), which leads to a linear system involving a covariance matrix s_{i,k} whose solution yields coefficients alpha_k that define a multiplicative correction r_x. A pseudo-projector PSR is constructed from these quantities and applied to iterate toward improved variational states. The authors describe two propagation/reference strategies: (i) a fixed-reference dynamic (psi_f fixed to the guiding/variational state psi_G) that yields a rigorous variational method and reproduces the Lanczos Krylov subspace when Ok = H^k; and (ii) a self-consistent dynamic where reference amplitudes are updated (including an interpolation parameter r controlling how much the reference amplitudes vs. nodes are updated). Practical Monte Carlo implementation details include importance sampling, use of a reference Green function G_f with absolute-value matrix elements to reduce variance, a binning scheme (bin length L_b) and large walker population M to control statistical error and bias in estimating the SR linear system, reweighting of walker weights after SR to maintain stable sampling, and a forward-walking scheme (generalized to non-symmetric reference matrices) to evaluate expectation values and correlation functions on the SR-improved states. Multi-parameter variational optimization is performed within the Jastrow-Slater ansatz: many-body Jastrow density/spin factors together with one-body Slater determinant parameters (pairing amplitudes, etc.) are updated by including corresponding operators in the SR conditions. Numerical stability methods include diagonalizing the covariance matrix s to remove near-singular eigenmodes before solving for alpha_k. The approach is tested on strongly correlated lattice models (t-J and Heisenberg) with a small number (p=1,2) of Lanczos steps and variance extrapolation to estimate ground-state energies and correlation functions.

**Algorithms used:** Lanczos algorithm (implemented statistically), Stochastic Reconfiguration (SR), Green Function Monte Carlo (GFMC)/Power method, Variational Monte Carlo (VMC), Fixed-Node (FN) approximation (lattice FN variant), Forward walking for expectation values, Importance sampling, Reweighting/branching, Multi-parameter stochastic minimization (Jastrow-Slater optimization), Covariance-matrix diagonalization (to remove near-singular modes), Simulated-annealing-style bin-length scheduling

## Findings
- [supported] The standard Lanczos algorithm can be implemented statistically and self-consistently using the stochastic reconfiguration (SR) method, enabling a few Lanczos steps on large lattice systems.
- [supported] When the reference state ψf is chosen equal to the guiding wavefunction ψG (so PSR = P†SR), the SR implementation is rigorously variational and equivalent to performing Lanczos in the Krylov subspace spanned by {H^k |ψG>}.
- [supported] The Hellberg–Manousakis form for the reference Green function Gf (Eq.21) reduces statistical fluctuations and makes SR practical for importance sampling.
- [supported] The SR method (with fixed reference) permits computation of expectation values (including off-diagonal ones) via a generalized forward-walking scheme for non-symmetric matrices, allowing accurate estimates of <ψSR|H|ψSR> and other correlators.
- [supported] Applying a small number of Lanczos steps (p = 1, 2) on optimized Jastrow–Slater variational wavefunctions substantially lowers variational energies and often approaches exact results on tested clusters (e.g., 18, 26, 32, 50, 98 sites).
- [supported] Multi-parameter energy optimization (many Jastrow and one-body parameters) is feasible and effective within SR; optimizing both Jastrow and determinant parameters significantly improves long-range correlation functions (e.g., hole-hole correlations) compared to bare VMC.
- [supported] The variance (<H^2> − <H>^2) can be used with few Lanczos steps to extrapolate to near-exact ground-state energies; this procedure yields good estimates even for fairly large systems (examples up to ~100 sites shown).
- [supported] The SR algorithm stabilizes the sign problem by replacing exact propagated wavefunctions with approximations satisfying a small set of conditions (Eq.2); binning (large Lb) or many walkers greatly reduces statistical bias (∝ 1/M or ∝ 1/√Lb).
- [supported] A reweighting parameter r (0 ≤ r ≤ 1) interpolates between the variational Lanczos limit (r = 0) and the original SR/self-consistent amplitude improvement (r = 1); tuning r trades strict variational bounds for potentially better amplitudes and correlation functions.
- [speculative] The self-consistent SR approach with r > 0 can mitigate Lanczos size-consistency limitations and may provide size-consistent energy improvements in the thermodynamic limit (claimed but not rigorously proven).
- [speculative] The SR/Lanczos framework and the fixed-reference technique can be extended straightforwardly to continuous systems using Langevin dynamics (stated as straightforward extension).
- [supported] In tests on the t–J model and Heisenberg model, the combination of optimized Jastrow–Slater states plus one or two Lanczos steps and variance extrapolation produced energies and correlators much closer to exact diagonalization benchmarks than VMC alone.
- [speculative] Few-Lanczos-step extrapolation can be used to estimate physically relevant quantities (e.g., superconducting condensation energy) for models like the t–J model; an estimate consistent with experiment is suggested but not rigorously demonstrated.
- [disputed] None of the paper's central methodological claims are presented as contradicting established literature; results are framed as extensions/improvements of existing GFMC/Lanczos techniques.

**Results summary:** The paper develops and demonstrates a stochastic implementation of the Lanczos algorithm within the stochastic reconfiguration (SR) framework for variational quantum Monte Carlo. By choosing an appropriate positive-definite reference Green function and enforcing a small set of SR constraints, the author shows that a few Lanczos steps (typically p = 1 or 2) can be performed statistically on large lattice systems, improving variational energies and correlation functions. The method supports multi-parameter optimization of Jastrow–Slater wavefunctions, stabilizes the sign problem via binning and reweighting, and generalizes forward-walking to non-symmetric reference matrices to compute expectation values. Tests on the t–J and Heisenberg models (clusters up to ≈98 sites) demonstrate significant energy improvements and that variance-extrapolation using a few Lanczos steps yields near-exact energies in many cases. The paper also introduces an interpolation parameter r to trade strict variational guarantees for improved amplitudes/nodes self-consistently.

## Limitations
- Sign problem remains severe for fermion systems; although SR alleviates instability, a sizable bias remains and is difficult to eliminate because of nodal errors explicitly noted by the author.
- Improving amplitudes alone (without modifying nodes) is often ineffective for fermions — node errors dominate energy and correlation biases.
- The method is strictly variational only when the pseudo-projector PSR is Hermitian (PSR = PSR†), i.e. when the reference wavefunction ψf equals the guiding wavefunction ψG; otherwise ESR does not necessarily bound the ground state energy.
- The r > 0 self-consistent variants (which improve amplitudes) trade off the rigorous variational guarantee; choosing r improperly can raise the estimated energy or produce non-variational behavior.
- For large systems and/or decreasing spectral gaps, power-iteration and few-step Lanczos corrections become less effective; few Lanczos iterations may be insufficient to remove bias in the thermodynamic limit.
- Statistical biases and fluctuations arise from using a finite number of walkers (M) and finite bin length (Lb); accurate determination of SR parameters requires large M or large Lb and can be costly.
- Reweighting schemes introduce a systematic bias (controlled by number of correcting factors L and M) that vanishes only exponentially in L and as 1/M; practical runs must balance cost vs bias.
- Numerical instability can occur when the covariance matrix of SR operators becomes singular or near-singular (operators linearly dependent); special regularization (removing small eigenvalues) is required.
- The fixed-node (FN) reference Green function yields much worse statistical accuracy for determining SR constants compared to the proposed Gf (Eq.21).
- Forward-walking for expectation values is more complex because non-symmetric renormalized reference matrices (¯Gf) must be handled; this increases algorithmic complexity.
- Computational resource limits permit only very few Lanczos iterations for large sizes in practice; this constrains achievable improvement.
- Variance extrapolation as an error estimate can fail if the algorithm becomes trapped in a local minimum or a 'quasi eigenstate' — i.e., variance can be small yet state still incorrect.
- On finite systems with spontaneously broken symmetries, recovering the exact symmetry-broken state accurately with few Lanczos steps is difficult, complicating extrapolation to exact energies and observables.
- [inferred] The procedure's computational cost and wall-clock time scale unfavorably with system size (e.g., reported tens of CPU hours for medium-large clusters), limiting practical scalability.
- [inferred] The effectiveness and numerical stability depend sensitively on the selection and number (p) of SR operators Ok; the optimal choice and its scaling are not fully specified.
- [inferred] The choice and tuning of the interpolation parameter r (and how it should scale with system size) is not prescribed; optimal setting likely requires empirical tuning.
- [inferred] The method is sensitive to the quality of the initial variational wavefunction; poor initial guesses can lead to being trapped in local minima and poorer extrapolations.
## Open questions
- How can nodal errors for fermionic systems be eliminated or further reduced beyond the SR and Lanczos-step strategies?
- What is the optimal choice of the interpolation parameter r that balances variational guarantees and amplitude/node improvement? How should r scale with system size (thermodynamic limit)?
- How many Lanczos iterations (p) are required for reliable convergence of energies and long-range correlation functions in large systems or the thermodynamic limit?
- How to efficiently control statistical fluctuations and finite-walker/bin biases (M and Lb) in practical computations with constrained computational budgets?
- What is the best systematic way to choose the set of SR operators Ok (and their number p) to maximize convergence and numerical stability across different Hamiltonians?
- How does the generalized Lanczos/SR approach scale and compare in accuracy and cost to fixed-node and other projection methods for large lattice fermion systems?
- Can the r > 0 self-consistent approach be made rigorously variational or provably size-consistent, and under what conditions?
- How robust is variance extrapolation for estimating errors in correlation functions, especially when starting from qualitatively incorrect (e.g., symmetry-mismatched) variational states?
- What are the practical implementation details and performance characteristics when extending the method to continuous models (e.g., using Langevin dynamics for Gf)?
- How to efficiently and stably perform forward-walking for non-symmetric renormalized reference matrices at large scale, including for off-diagonal observables?
- How to detect and escape trapping in local minima/quasi-eigenstates during SR/Lanczos optimization—are global strategies (e.g., annealing) sufficient?

**Future work:**
- Extend and apply the generalized Lanczos/SR scheme to continuous models using Langevin dynamics for the reference Green function Gf.
- Perform further extensive testing on strongly correlated models (e.g., larger t-J clusters) and on larger lattice sizes to assess scalability and accuracy.
- Develop and refine many-parameter energy optimization strategies (including generic long-range Jastrow factors and site-dependent orbital determinants) within the SR framework.
- Improve statistical control procedures (e.g., adaptive bin-length Lb, walker number M strategies) to reduce bias and variance in the determination of SR parameters.
- Investigate the self-consistent r > 0 technique further, including its size-consistency properties and effects on long-range correlation functions and phase-separation/bound-state detection.
- Refine forward-walking algorithms for non-symmetric renormalized reference matrices to compute diagonal and off-diagonal correlation functions accurately.
- Address numerical instabilities from near-singular covariance matrices via improved regularization, operator selection, or other stabilization techniques.
- Combine SR/Lanczos approaches with global minimization heuristics (e.g., simulated annealing by varying effective temperature via bin length) to mitigate trapping in local minima.
- Publish further detailed benchmarks and methodological improvements (as indicated by referenced 'in preparation' works) to validate and disseminate practical recipes.
