## Abstract summary
This preprint derives rigorous error bounds and resource estimates for variational quantum algorithms that solve differential equations by combining Runge–Kutta time-stepping with quantum evaluations of the underlying vector field. The analysis treats truncation error and measurement (shot) noise, provides closed-form trade-offs for RKM order, step count, and measurement budget, and applies the framework to a toy ODE and the Black–Scholes PDE, finding optimal RKM orders (four for the simple ODE and two for the option-pricing application) while neglecting representation and hardware noise.
## Methodology
The paper develops a theoretical and numerical methodology to derive rigorous error bounds and resource (measurement / circuit) estimates for variational quantum algorithms applied to differential equations, with special focus on Runge–Kutta (RK) time-stepping methods. Analytically, the authors map linear PDE/ODE problems (in particular dy/dτ = -H y(τ)) to imaginary-time Schrödinger evolution and use a parameterized quantum circuit (ansatz) to represent the evolving state. McLachlan's variational principle is used to obtain an ODE for the circuit parameters θ(τ) (A(θ) · dθ/dτ = C(θ)), which is then solved classically using RK methods of different orders p and s stages. The methodology derives upper bounds on (i) local truncation error (LTE) of RKMs (via standard RK error theory), (ii) global error propagation through RK steps, and (iii) additional errors arising from shot-noise in quantum expectation-value estimations. Statistical noise is modelled using the central-limit theorem and bounded with Chebyshev-type inequalities; inversion/regularization errors for the A matrix and condition-number effects are incorporated via perturbation-analytic bounds. The authors define cost metrics (number of function evaluations and total quantum circuit evaluations Ncirc), derive closed-form and approximate formulas for minimal steps and measurements (Nτ and Nr) needed to meet a target error, and provide asymptotic/approximate analytical solutions. Numerically, they validate and illustrate the theory in two case studies: (1) a simple scalar ODE with known analytic solution (sensitivity analysis over RK order and parameters); (2) option pricing via the Black–Scholes PDE mapped to imaginary-time Schrödinger form. For the numerical studies they (a) estimate quantities (K, M, Lipschitz constants, amax, bmax, condition number bounds) either analytically or via a toy-model where matrix entries of A and C are sampled as small truncated Fourier-like functions (random amplitudes and phases), (b) run parameter sweeps / sensitivity analyses over key parameters (RK order p, time T, Lipschitz constants, target error) and (c) compute resulting cost measures (C and Ncirc) across RK methods to identify resource-optimal orders. The work is primarily analytic/theoretical with numerical parameter studies rather than empirical runs on quantum hardware; no specific quantum software frameworks or publicly released code are specified.

**Algorithms used:** Variational quantum imaginary-time evolution (McLachlan variational principle), Variational quantum algorithm for differential equations (ansatz-based parameter evolution), Runge–Kutta methods (Euler and higher-order RKMs, various stages/orders), Parameter-shift rule (discussed as an alternative for expectation evaluation), Classical numerical analysis techniques (Lipschitz bounds, perturbation bounds, Chebyshev/Chernoff)

**Results summary:** This preprint provides a theoretical and numerical error/resource analysis for variational quantum algorithms that solve differential equations by discretizing parameter dynamics (from McLachlan’s variational principle) with Runge–Kutta methods. The authors derive bounds that combine RKM truncation error with shot-noise-induced measurement error, produce formulas for minimal RKM steps and measurement counts to achieve a target error, and translate these into total quantum circuit-evaluation counts for VQAs. Two numerical case studies are presented: (1) a simple 1D ODE without shot noise, where higher-order RKMs (notably order 4) are most resource-efficient under their parameters, and (2) a Black–Scholes option-pricing PDE solved by the variational algorithm, where a 2nd-order RKM is most efficient under their parameter choices. Crucially, the Black–Scholes instance shows prohibitive resource requirements for realistic shot-noise and conditioning estimates (N_circ ≈ 1.6×10^28, and astronomically many shots per circuit), highlighting matrix inversion and shot noise as the principal bottlenecks. The authors stress that their bounds are conservative and that alternative algorithmic choices that avoid matrix inversion or reduce measurement overhead may materially change feasibility.

## Limitations

- Hardware / circuit errors (gate infidelity, SPAM, bias) ignored in analysis (author-stated).
- Assumed invertibility of A; matrix regularization and the errors introduced by regularization/inversion are not analyzed in detail (author-stated).
- Stability of differential equations and numerical stability of methods not analyzed (author-stated).
- Worst-case (rigorous) upper bounds used throughout; estimates may be overly pessimistic and could significantly overestimate required resources (author-stated).
- Several bounds are loose/not tight (e.g., LTE bounds, shot-noise bounds via Chebyshev), limiting practical sharpness of resource estimates (author-stated).
- Dependence on a toy model to estimate matrix A, vector C, Lipschitz constants, and condition numbers; generality of these estimates is uncertain (author-stated).
- Difficulty of estimating the Runge–Kutta error-constant K in general; K may scale unfavorably with order p and is challenging to bound (author-stated).
- Shot-noise modeled by Gaussian/CLT and bounded via Chebyshev; alternative concentration bounds or more exact noise models were not fully exploited (author-stated).
- Resource estimates (shots N_r and total circuit counts N_circ) can be astronomically large for realistic target errors (example: ~10^28 circuit evaluations), making near-term practical implementation unrealistic for some use cases (author-stated).

## Open questions
- For a given problem instance, which Runge–Kutta order p (and corresponding number of stages s) truly minimizes quantum resources when all error sources (including representation and hardware noise) are considered?
- How can representation errors (Ansatz expressivity and bias) be estimated a priori or bounded to fold into resource estimates?
- What are practical, instance-aware methods to regularize and invert A that balance numerical stability against bias introduced by regularization, and how does that affect shot requirements?
- Can alternative algorithmic formulations avoid the A^{-1} inversion step (reducing shot amplification) while retaining variational advantages? (authors point to Refs. 18, 19 as promising alternatives.)
- How much can one reduce resource requirements by applying tighter concentration bounds (e.g., Chernoff) or advanced measurement reduction techniques (parameter-shift rules, grouping, derandomization)?
- To what extent do the toy-model-derived estimates (for A, C, κ(A), L_fy) generalize to realistic quantum Ansatzes and target PDE/ODE instances?
- What are realistic achievable values and scalings for the Lipschitz constants L_fy and L_{fτ} for common variational Ansätze and problem classes, and how can these be controlled via Ansatz/circuit design?
- Can the differential-equation solver noise be better modeled as a stochastic differential equation (SDE) and would that yield sharper error/resource tradeoffs?
- How do linear multistep methods or general linear methods compare in practice (error vs. number of function evaluations) to RKMs in the variational context with shot noise?
- What are effective strategies for reducing the condition number of A (preconditioning or Ansatz choices) so that shot-noise amplification is mitigated?
- How will device-specific noise sources (coherent errors, drift, correlated readout noise) change these resource estimates?
- Can the recently-proposed techniques that reduce state-preparation counts from Θ(N_V^2) to Θ(N_V) (Refs. 35, 55) be integrated without prohibitive loss in accuracy, and what net resource savings result?

**Future work:**
- Combine the current analysis with recent measurement-reduction techniques (e.g., methods that reduce state-preparations from Θ(N_V^2) to Θ(N_V), Refs. 35 and 55) to tighten resource estimates.
- Adapt the analysis to linear multistep methods (and general linear methods) that reuse evaluations between steps and may lower the number of function evaluations.
- Formulate and analyze the problem within an SDE framework (shot noise as stochastic perturbations) to potentially obtain more realistic error bounds.
- Apply the error/resource framework to other use cases, including other differential-equation problems (including PDEs) and classical algorithms that use noisy data.
- Explore applying these estimates and methods to training of neural networks (suggested application area).

- Tighten and improve the loose bounds identified (LTE, shot-noise bounds), and develop a-posteriori instance-specific bounds to reduce pessimism in resource estimates.

## Contradictions
- This paper challenges optimistic assertions that variational quantum algorithms for PDE-based option pricing are near-term practical: its resource estimates for a Black–Scholes VQA are astronomically large (N_circ ≈ 1.62×10^28), contradicting claims of near-term quantum superiority.
- The work contradicts any simple claim that increasing Runge–Kutta order universally improves resource efficiency: it shows problem-dependent optima (p=4 best for the toy ODE but p=2 best for the Black–Scholes instance), undermining sweeping statements about algorithmic choices and scalability.
