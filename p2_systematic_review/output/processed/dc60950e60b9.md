## Abstract summary
This chapter reviews how quantum computing can transform finance, consumer behavior analysis, blockchain security, digital marketing, and stock-market forecasting. It surveys quantum algorithms (e.g., QAOA, QMC, Shor’s and Grover’s) and applications—portfolio optimization, risk modeling, fraud detection, hyper-personalization, and quantum-safe cryptography—while noting practical challenges such as hardware limitations, error correction, and the need for post-quantum security. This survey reviews recent advances in energy optimization and security for wireless sensor networks (WSNs). It covers energy-efficient protocols and algorithms (e.g., clustering, data-centric routing, duty cycling), energy-harvesting and power-management strategies to extend network lifetime, and security mechanisms including cryptography, key management, intrusion detection and secure routing to protect WSN data integrity and availability.
## Methodology
This work is a narrative review and synthesis of existing literature, industry pilot reports, and public case studies on the intersection of quantum computing and financial / consumer-facing applications. The authors survey academic articles, technical reports, press releases and vendor white papers (e.g., studies and pilots by IBM, D‑Wave, Google, JP Morgan, Goldman Sachs, HSBC, Volkswagen, Save‑On‑Foods), standards and policy documents (e.g., NIST PQC activity), and market/industry analyses. From these sources they extract representative use cases (portfolio optimization, risk modeling, fraud detection, quantum‑safe blockchain, real‑time routing and personalization), summarize relevant quantum algorithms and methods, and discuss technical, ethical and deployment challenges. The methodology emphasizes cross‑case comparison (pilot outcomes, reported speedups, and areas of practical impact), mapping of algorithmic approaches to concrete business problems, and identification of gaps and future research directions. No original empirical experiment is reported in the review; rather the chapter synthesizes and contextualizes secondary evidence and illustrative corporate pilots to highlight opportunities and limitations of quantum approaches in finance, consumer behavior analytics, blockchain security, digital marketing and stock prediction. The authors conducted a structured, PRISMA-guided systematic literature search combined with a narrative (snowballing) step. They queried major bibliographic sources relevant to quantum computing and machine learning (ArXiv, Web of Science, Elsevier, IEEE Xplore, Springer, Physical Review, ACM, Nature, IEEE QTML, IEEE Quantum Week) using security‑focused search terms (examples: “secur*”, “quantum”, “vulnerab*”, “attack”, “privacy”, “defens*”, “model stealing”, “encoding”, “adversarial attack”). A two‑stage approach was used: (1) a systematic automated search and filtering stage (duplicate removal, date filter excluding pre‑2018, and automated exclusion of records not matching scope), and (2) a narrative snowballing stage where references of included papers were hand‑searched. Screening proceeded from title/abstract relevance scoring to full‑text assessment. Inclusion criteria required English language, journal or conference publications, and direct relevance to QML security; exclusion criteria removed non‑QML security works, inaccessible items, or non‑electronic sources. The screening pipeline reported initial identification of 4,658 records, automated/duplicate and date filtering steps that reduced the set, manual screening of ~1,000 records, full‑text assessment of 93 articles, and a final selection of 23 studies from the systematic phase plus 4 additional studies via snowballing (27 total). The review documents filtering steps and basic counts at each stage and focuses on categorizing QML vulnerabilities, attack vectors, and defensive techniques (e.g., adversarial training, differential privacy, formal verification, randomized encodings, and noise‑based defenses).
