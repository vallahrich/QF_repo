## Abstract summary
This review surveys recent advances in entanglement routing, quantum key distribution (QKD), and qubit/resource management for short- and long-distance quantum communication, focusing on optimization techniques such as integer programming, reinforcement learning and collaborative heuristics. It evaluates methods by throughput, scalability and fairness, highlights challenges including decoherence, limited resources, dynamic adaptation and error correction, and advocates for hybrid quantum–classical designs and integration with existing infrastructure to enable practical, secure quantum networks.
## Methodology
This paper is a scoping literature review conducted following PRISMA-ScR guidelines. The authors performed literature identification, duplicate removal, abstract-level screening and full-text inclusion to arrive at a final set of 64 studies (see their Figure 2). Rather than primary experiments, the review organizes prior work into a taxonomy by methodological focus (RL/DRL-driven adaptive routing, resource-aware optimization and multi-objective routing, fidelity/noise robustness and QKD methods, and scalability/deployment strategies). For comparative analysis the authors extracted and compared studies using common criteria: target objectives (fidelity, throughput, request success rate, latency), network assumptions (topology scale, noise/decoherence models, resource constraints such as memory and EPR availability), and methodological mechanisms (RL/DRL, heuristic/optimization-based routing, fidelity/QKD-oriented designs). The review synthesizes findings, highlights trade-offs, reports representative experimental setups and parameters as described in source studies, and provides cross-study takeaways, open challenges, and recommended future research directions.

**Algorithms used:** Q-learning, Deep Q-Network (DQN), Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL), Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), Deep Q-Learning, Deep Quantum Routing Agent (DQRA), Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), Monte Carlo sampling, Dijkstra shortest-path, Yen's K-shortest paths, Integer Linear Programming (ILP), Branch-and-Bound, Upper Confidence Bound (UCB1), Conservative Q-learning (offline RL), Entanglement swapping (protocol-level operation), Entanglement caching (protocol-level optimization), Collaboratively Optimized Selection of Paths (COSP), Multi-Center Approach (MCA), Single-Center Approach (SCA), Quantum Repetition Code (QRC)
**Frameworks:** NetSquid, IBM Qiskit (QASM simulator)

## Findings
- [speculative] Quantum entanglement and quantum interference can provide exponential improvements for certain computational and cryptographic tasks (paper asserts potential exponential gains but does not demonstrate a general, empirical exponential advantage in networks).
- [supported] Quantum networks enable protocols such as QKD, quantum teleportation, and distributed quantum computing and rely on entanglement as a core resource.
- [supported] Entanglement routing differs from classical routing because entanglement creation is probabilistic and constrained by limited quantum resources (memory, qubits, EPR pairs) and fidelity requirements.
- [supported] Reinforcement learning (RL) and deep RL (DRL) methods have been shown in multiple studies to improve adaptive entanglement routing, increasing fidelity, request success rates, and robustness under dynamic conditions compared with heuristic baselines.
- [supported] The qRL (Q-learning-based) approach reported ≈20% higher fidelity in a high-resource configuration and maintained fidelity >0.6 in a low-resource configuration where qDijkstra fell below 0.4; qRL sustained >80% request success under heavy load in reported simulations.
- [supported] Proactive entanglement swapping combined with entanglement caching and deep Q-learning (DQRL) increased request success rates (e.g., SEER +48%, REPS +61% in reported experiments) and benefits depend strongly on entanglement lifetime and swap success probability.
- [supported] The Deep Quantum Routing Agent (DQRA / DRL hybrid) yields high request success (80–90% in qubit-rich settings; 60–75% in severely constrained settings) and scales with polynomial training time and linear routing time in reported simulations.
- [supported] Policy-gradient RL (PPO) routing demonstrated large sample-efficiency improvements over Monte Carlo sampling in specific noise models (papers report up to ~13,000× improvements in steps/actions in certain tests) and maintains robustness under dynamic noise changes.
- [supported] Collaborative, optimization-based methods (e.g., COSP) that combine path selection, linear programming estimates, and MCTS can increase predicted throughput and service rate (reported ≈50% and ≈55% improvements respectively) in high-concurrency simulations.
- [supported] Heuristic repeater-placement strategies (MCA/SCA) significantly reduce computation time compared with ILP approaches and produce feasible placement solutions quickly for networks where ILP is computationally heavy (examples: ILP run times of thousands of seconds vs heuristics in fractions of a second for some test cases).
- [supported] Entanglement- and QKD-focused physical-layer techniques (e.g., GHZ-based multi-bit-per-qubit QKD, encoded Bell pairs with repetition codes) can improve transmission efficiency or fidelity in simulations but often incur additional qubit and operational overhead.
- [supported] QKD provides information-theoretic security in principle, but practical deployments must address device imperfections, finite-key effects, and realistic noise models.
- [supported] Key practical challenges include fragility of quantum states (decoherence, loss), no-cloning constraints, limited repeater/memory performance, noisy hardware, and coordination/control-plane overheads.
- [supported] There is a lack of standardized, reproducible evaluation practices across papers (varying topologies, noise models, traffic models, baselines), which impedes fair cross-study comparison.
- [speculative] Future directions recommended include integrated cross-layer optimization (linking physical-layer fidelity techniques with network-layer routing/scheduling), hardware-aware algorithm design, hybrid quantum–classical algorithms, and deployment-aware benchmarking.
- [speculative] Near-term realistic deployment scenarios most feasible for quantum networking include metro-scale trusted-node QKD integration with existing fiber, satellite-to-ground QKD links complementing terrestrial backbones, inter-data-center secure links, and specialized distributed quantum computing links—these are forward-looking and contingent on hardware advances.
- [supported] Many reported routing and resource-allocation gains from learning-based methods depend on simulator assumptions, noise models, entanglement lifetimes, swap probabilities, and resource budgets; performance generalization requires careful benchmarking.

**Results summary:** This review synthesizes recent literature on entanglement routing, QKD, and qubit/resource management in quantum networks. Empirical simulation studies show RL/DRL approaches (qRL, DQRL, DQRA, PPO) can substantially outperform classical heuristics and sampling methods in fidelity, request success rate, and sample-efficiency in their evaluated scenarios. Optimization and heuristic infrastructure methods (COSP, repeater-placement heuristics) reduce computation time or improve throughput/service rates in large-scale planning tasks. Physical-layer methods (encoded Bell pairs, GHZ-based QKD) improve fidelity or transmission efficiency but at added qubit/operational cost. The review highlights recurring practical obstacles: decoherence/loss, limited repeater/memory hardware, noisy imperfect devices, and inconsistent evaluation methodology. It recommends cross-layer, hardware-aware approaches, standardized benchmarks, and hybrid quantum–classical integration as priorities for advancing deployable quantum networks.

**Performance claims:**
- qRL: ≈20% greater fidelity in Config1 (high-resource); fidelity >0.6 in low-resource Config4 while qDijkstra <0.4; request success >80% under 3000-request demand (reported in [26]).
- DQRL (proactive swapping + caching): SEER success rate increased from 50% to 74% (+48%); REPS from 45% to 73% (+61%); entanglement caching improved success rates by 12–19% when entanglement lasted ≥6 time slots; swap success probability of 0.9 produced ~55% improvement over baseline (reported in [28]).
- DQRA: success rates 80–90% in qubit-rich settings, and 60–75% even when node qubit capacity ci = 2; outperforms baseline approaches by 20–40% in reported tests; training time scales polynomially with network size and routing time remains linear for networks up to 25×25 nodes (reported in [30]).
- PPO-based routing: reported up to ~13,000× fewer steps/actions than Monte Carlo in some noise scenarios; required ~60,000 actions vs Monte Carlo’s ~546 million for amplitude damping in a tested network; 39,000 vs 60 million for reversible phase shifts; retains 93.4% functionality under dynamic noise vs Monte Carlo 33.1% (reported in [27]).
- COSP: ~50% increase in average predicted throughput and ~55% increase in service rate compared with earlier methods in reported simulations (reported in [31]).
- Repeater placement heuristics (SCA/MCA): ILP execution 13,481 s (SURFnet example) vs heuristic/SCA 0.28 s; heuristics produced feasible solutions seconds vs ILP taking hours/days for larger networks (reported in [35]).
- MULTI-R / MR-REC: MULTI-R served all 20 user pairs in reported tests; MR-REC achieved up to 55% higher predicted throughput than FER in reported comparisons (reported in [32]).
- GHZ-based QKD test: 12-bit key transmitted using a 13-qubit GHZ state in Netsquid simulation (reported in [34]).
- Encoded Bell pairs with repetition code require 2k+1 qubits for Quantum Repetition Code encoding (reported in [33]).

## Limitations
- Fragility of quantum states (decoherence and noise) that limit long-distance entanglement distribution and fidelity (author-stated).
- No-cloning theorem prevents duplicating/amplifying quantum information, increasing reliance on error correction and fault tolerance (author-stated).
- Probabilistic and stochastic nature of entanglement creation and link success, making routing and resource planning inherently uncertain (author-stated).
- Limited quantum resources: constrained qubit capacity per node, limited entangled-pair storage, and short memory coherence times (author-stated).
- Quantum repeaters remain immature in practice: limited memory lifetimes, imperfect gates/measurements, and nontrivial coordination overhead across nodes (author-stated).
- Fidelity-preserving techniques (encoding, purification, error correction) typically incur extra qubit and processing overhead that can reduce throughput (author-stated).
- Scalability and computational complexity: exact optimization (e.g., ILP/branch-and-bound) does not scale to large networks; heuristics trade solution quality for runtime (author-stated).
- Learning-based (RL/DRL) approaches introduce training/tuning overhead, require large simulation interactions, and may have growing parameter footprints as network size increases (author-stated).
- Dependence of ML methods on simulator realism and assumed noise/topology models; generalization to different or real hardware conditions is uncertain (author-stated).
- Lack of standardized, reproducible evaluation protocols across studies (topology, traffic, noise models, metrics, baselines), impairing fair comparison and cumulative progress (author-stated).
- Integration challenges with classical control planes: synchronization, timing constraints, control-plane overhead, and interoperability (author-stated).
- Practical deployment constrained to near-term hybrid scenarios (trusted-node metro networks, satellite links, inter-data-center links), limiting immediate universal applicability (author-stated).
- Security and QKD: gap between ideal security proofs and device-level implementation issues (detector inefficiencies, finite-key effects, miscalibration) (author-stated).
- Satellite and space links suffer from intermittency, atmospheric effects, and topology changes that complicate routing and performance guarantees (author-stated).

- How can repeaters and quantum memories be engineered to minimize resource overhead while delivering realistic lifetimes, gate fidelities, and interoperability with telecom infrastructure?
- What are practical, hardware-aware design principles for routing and resource management that account for device imperfections and heterogeneity?
- Which noise and device models should become standardized benchmarks to enable reproducible and realistic evaluation of routing and QKD methods?
- How can ML/DRL methods be trained and validated so they generalize across topologies, traffic patterns, and real hardware noise without excessive training cost?

## Contradictions
