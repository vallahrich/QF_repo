## Abstract summary
The paper proposes a hybrid quantum-classical feature selection method that integrates the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) with the classical Maximum Relevance–Minimum Redundancy (MRMR) approach by formulating MRMR as a QUBO and mapping it to an Ising Hamiltonian. It uses polynomial fitting to avoid division-by-zero in the Hamiltonian encoding and employs a shallow p=2 QAOA circuit; experiments on eight real-world datasets show the method tends to select smaller feature subsets while achieving comparable or better classification accuracy than classical MRMR.
## Methodology
The authors propose a hybrid quantum-classical feature selection method that integrates the classical Maximum Relevance-Minimum Redundancy (MRMR) criterion with the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA). They first reformulate the MRMR maximization objective as a Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) problem. To avoid division-by-zero issues from the MRMR terms 1/S and 1/S^2 when encoding into a Hamiltonian, they approximate those terms with polynomial curve fits (second-order for 1/S and third-order for 1/S^2) obtained via least-squares. The QUBO is then mapped to an Ising-type cost Hamiltonian by using the standard mapping z_i -> (1 - Z_i)/2 so that computational-basis bitstrings correspond to feature-selection vectors. A QAOA circuit (p = 2 layers) is used to prepare a parameterized state: alternating applications of the problem (cost) unitary U_C(γ) and the mixer unitary U_M(β). Parameters (γ, β) are optimized classically using the ADAM optimizer; the optimized circuit is sampled (1000 shots) to obtain candidate feature subsets (measurement bitstrings). Selected subsets are evaluated by training a classical Support Vector Machine (SVM) classifier with 5-fold cross-validation to compute classification accuracy. The classical MRMR heuristic is used as the baseline. Additional diagnostics reported include the fidelity of polynomial fits (MSE and R²) and the empirical probability distribution over sizes of feature subsets obtained by QAOA versus random sampling.

**Algorithms used:** QAOA, MRMR, QUBO, Ising model mapping, Support Vector Machine (SVM), ADAM optimizer

**Experimental setup:** Experiments were run on a simulated quantum computer (no simulator name specified). QAOA used p = 2 layers, with 1000 measurement shots per parameter set. Classical parameter optimization used the ADAM optimizer; convergence required between ~150 and ~800 optimizer generations depending on dataset. Feature-subset evaluations used a classical SVM with 5-fold cross-validation. Eight real-world datasets from the UCI repository were used.

**Dataset:** Datasets drawn from the UCI Machine Learning Repository spanning finance, medicine, and natural hazards. Financial-related datasets mentioned include Australian Credit Approval (14 features) and South German Credit (20 features); other datasets used: Breast Cancer (Wisconsin) (9 features), Heart Failure Clinical Records (12), Algerian Forest Fire (12), Audit Data (17), Cervical Cancer Behavior Risk (19), and Glioma Grading Clinical (23).

## Limitations
- Experiments were performed on a simulated quantum computer rather than real quantum hardware (author-stated).
- The method was implemented with a shallow QAOA circuit (p = 2); increasing layers on noisy hardware may not improve accuracy due to gate errors and fidelity limits (author-stated).
- Encoding the MRMR cost required polynomial curve-fitting to avoid division-by-zero terms; this introduces an approximation to the original objective (author-stated).
- Results are reported for relatively small datasets/features (feature counts in experiments range from 9 to 23), so scalability to much larger feature spaces is not demonstrated [inferred].
- [inferred] The approach requires one qubit per feature (Ising/QUBO mapping), implying potentially large qubit requirements for high-dimensional problems.
- [inferred] The robustness of the algorithm to realistic device noise and gate errors is unknown because the tests were simulated and not run on NISQ hardware.
- [inferred] The polynomial fitting approach depends on the chosen polynomial order and fitting quality; suboptimal fits could bias the Hamiltonian and hence the ground-state search.
- [inferred] The study compares only against classical MRMR; comparative performance versus other classical or hybrid feature-selection methods is not evaluated.
- [inferred] The probability results and classifier improvements are empirical; no theoretical guarantees on finding global optima or bounds on approximation quality are provided.
- [inferred] Use of a particular classical optimizer (ADAM) and fixed sampling (1000 shots) may affect results; sensitivity to optimizer choice and sampling budget was not explored.
