## Abstract summary
This paper examines the threats quantum computing poses to the cryptographic foundations of the financial sector and evaluates quantum-resistant solutions. It reviews vulnerabilities of RSA, ECC and symmetric primitives to Shor's and Grover's algorithms, assesses post-quantum cryptographic families (lattice-, hash-, and multivariate-based), and considers hardware-assisted approaches (QKD, QRNG) alongside hybrid encryption and AI-driven threat modeling to provide a roadmap for transitioning financial institutions to quantum-secure infrastructures.
## Methodology
This study employs a non-empirical, theoretical and analytic methodology grounded in a systematic literature and standards review. The author surveys the current cryptographic landscape and the quantum-threat literature (including analyses of Shor's and Grover's algorithms), performs a comparative evaluation of candidate post-quantum cryptographic primitives (lattice-based, hash-based, multivariate polynomial schemes), and assesses hardware-assisted quantum techniques (QKD and QRNG) with respect to security properties relevant to financial systems (confidentiality, integrity, authentication, and regulatory compliance). The research further synthesizes these findings into design patterns and a transition roadmap for financial institutions, incorporating hybrid encryption strategies and AI-driven threat modeling to describe practical integration steps, risk trade-offs, and policy implications. Emphasis is placed on conceptual system architectures, protocol-level security analysis, and alignment with global regulatory and sovereignty objectives rather than experimental validation.

## Findings
- [supported] The financial sector is highly data-sensitive and technologically dynamic, relying critically on encryption for secure communications, authentication, and record-keeping.
- [supported] Shor's algorithm can factor large integers efficiently on a sufficiently large quantum computer, undermining the security assumptions of RSA and elliptic-curve cryptography.
- [supported] Grover's algorithm gives a quadratic speedup for unstructured search, which reduces the effective strength of symmetric-key schemes (implying a need for larger keys to maintain security).
- [speculative] The rise of quantum computing presents a dual challenge for finance: enabling computational advances in analytics/optimization while posing existential risks to current cryptographic foundations.
- [speculative] Quantum processors promise computational acceleration for portfolio optimization, fraud detection, and financial modeling (potential quantum advantage in analytics and optimization workloads).
- [speculative] Current cybersecurity infrastructure in financial institutions will be rendered inadequate in a post-quantum environment, necessitating adoption of quantum-resistant and hybrid encryption models.
- [speculative] Lattice-based, hash-based, and multivariate polynomial cryptographic schemes are evaluated as viable quantum-resistant solutions for preserving data integrity and transaction confidentiality in finance.
- [speculative] Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) and Quantum Random Number Generation (QRNG) are presented as hardware-assisted techniques capable of providing (near-)unconditional security for financial communications and transaction authentication, subject to practical constraints.
- [speculative] A combined approach—integrating quantum-resistant cryptography, QKD/QRNG where appropriate, hybrid encryption strategies, and AI-driven threat modeling—constitutes a recommended roadmap for financial institutions transitioning to quantum-secure infrastructures.
- [speculative] Quantum cybersecurity should be viewed not only as a defensive necessity but also as an enabler of resilient and sovereign digital finance, supporting stability and sustainable innovation.

**Results summary:** This theoretical, peer-reviewed analysis surveys the implications of advancing quantum computing for the financial sector. It reiterates well-established cryptographic vulnerabilities—principally that Shor's algorithm threatens RSA and ECC and that Grover's algorithm reduces symmetric-key security—and argues the sector must adopt quantum-resistant cryptographic primitives (e.g., lattice- and hash-based schemes) and hybrid models. The paper discusses hardware-assisted approaches (QKD, QRNG) for stronger keying and authentication, and advocates integrating these cryptographic measures with AI-driven threat modeling to form a transition roadmap. While it highlights potential quantum-enabled benefits for optimization and analytics, the work is largely prescriptive/theoretical, emphasizing the need for proactive migration strategies rather than presenting new empirical performance data.

## Open questions
- How should financial institutions operationalize and prioritize migration to post-quantum cryptography across heterogeneous systems (banking, payments, trading, DeFi, blockchain)?
- Which quantum-resistant algorithms are most suitable for specific financial applications considering security, performance, and interoperability?
- What are the practical performance, latency, and cost trade-offs of deploying QKD and QRNG at scale in financial networks?
- How can QKD/QRNG be integrated with existing cryptographic infrastructures and legacy systems without disrupting services?
- What governance, compliance, and regulatory models are needed to ensure safe, auditable transition to quantum-secure infrastructures?
- How should long-lived sensitive data and records be protected today given the risk of retrospective decryption once quantum adversaries exist?
- What are robust verification and validation methods (including standards) for implementations of quantum-resistant algorithms to avoid faulty or vulnerable deployments?
- How can AI-driven threat modeling be effectively combined with quantum cybersecurity measures, and how reliable are such AI models in anticipating quantum-era threats?
- What new attack surfaces or side-channel/vector risks might arise from hardware quantum cryptography (QKD/QRNG) and hybrid systems, and how can they be mitigated?
- What is the realistic timeline for quantum computing capabilities to materially threaten deployed financial cryptography, and how should that timeline drive investment and policy decisions?

**Future work:**
- Evaluate and benchmark quantum-resistant cryptographic protocols (lattice-based, hash-based, multivariate polynomial encryption) in financial use cases.
- Investigate practical deployment models for Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) and Quantum Random Number Generation (QRNG) tailored to financial communications and transaction authentication.
- Design and test hybrid encryption architectures that combine classical and quantum-resistant primitives for phased migration.
- Develop AI-driven threat-modeling frameworks to anticipate and respond to quantum-enabled adversarial capabilities in finance.
- Create a roadmap and best-practice guidelines for financial institutions transitioning toward quantum-secure infrastructures, addressing technical, operational, and regulatory aspects.
