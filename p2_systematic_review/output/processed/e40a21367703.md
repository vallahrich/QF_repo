## Abstract summary
This empirical study compares classical neural networks (an LSTM with dropout and a multi-layer multiple linear regression model) against a variational quantum neural network (implemented with PennyLane and Quantum Dense) for predicting low stock prices of Cooperative Rural Development Bank (CRDB) and National Microfinance Bank (NMB) in Tanzania. Using standard metrics (R2, MSE, MAE, MAPE) on 2,525 data points, the authors report that the MLR and LSTM models outperform the VQNN (average R2: MLR ~0.994, LSTM ~0.967, VQNN ~0.865), concluding that classical models provided more accurate low-price predictions while the quantum approach showed faster computation but less consistent accuracy.
## Methodology
This empirical study compared three models for predicting the low stock price of two Tanzanian banks (CRDB and NMB): a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) neural network with dropout, a Multiple Linear Regression (MLR) neural architecture with multiple hidden layers, and a Variational Quantum Neural Network (VQNN) implemented via variational quantum circuits. Daily stock data for each company were obtained from the Dar Es Salaam Stock Exchange (DSE) (2525 data points per company). Missing trading-day records were cleaned and input features (volume, turnover, deals, close, high, low) were normalized using MinMaxScaler to [0,1]. Data were split 80% training / 20% testing. The LSTM used a time-step/window of 105, stacked LSTM layers (described as up to eight layers, with 50 units per LSTM layer) and dropout (0.2) plus a final dense output; ReLU activations and the Adam optimizer were used. The MLR model used multiple input features, four input streams and a hidden dense layer of 100 neurons with ReLU, trained with a learning rate of 0.001 (max_iter 2000, random_state 1). The VQNN pipeline encoded classical features into a variational quantum circuit (Hadamard and CNOT gates mentioned for state preparation/entanglement), used a parameterized ansatz and classical optimizer (Adam) to train variational angles; reported VQNN training settings included learning rate 0.001, epoch = 1 and batch size = 1. Models were evaluated using MSE, MAE, MAPE, RMSE and R^2; results were reported across multiple epoch settings (reported ranges 15–105 for classical networks) and compared against literature baselines.

**Algorithms used:** Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), Multiple Linear Regression (MLR) (multi-layer dense network), Variational Quantum Neural Network (VQNN) / Variational Quantum Circuit (VQC)
**Frameworks:** PennyLane, Quantum Dense (quantum-dense library), Keras, scikit-learn, pandas, numpy, matplotlib, Jupyter Notebook (Anaconda)

**Experimental setup:** Python 3.11.0 on Jupyter Notebook (Anaconda3). Implementations used Keras for classical DL, scikit-learn for preprocessing/metrics, and PennyLane + Quantum Dense for quantum circuit/VQNN components. No specific quantum hardware or cloud QPU is reported; VQNN was executed via the described quantum software libraries (presumably on a simulator).

**Dataset:** Daily stock market data for Cooperative Rural Development Bank (CRDB) and National Microfinance Bank (NMB) obtained from the Dar Es Salaam Stock Exchange (DSE). Each company: 2525 data points. Features include volume, turnover, deals, close, high and low prices. Target: low stock price.

**Results summary:** The study empirically compared three models (LSTM with dropout, a multi-hidden-layer MLR neural network, and a variational quantum neural network implemented with PennyLane/Quantum Dense) on low-price prediction for two Tanzanian bank stocks (CRDB and NMB). Across multiple epochs and metrics (RMSE, MAPE, MAE, R2), the MLR model achieved the highest reported R2 (up to ~0.9940) and, together with the dropout LSTM, generally outperformed the VQNN on accuracy-related metrics. The VQNN showed inconsistent performance: some RMSE entries are small but overall its MAPE and R2 were worse and more variable, leading the authors to conclude it was less efficient and inconsistent on these datasets. The authors recommend further hyperparameter tuning and hybrid approaches, and note possible computational-speed advantages of quantum approaches without presenting timing evidence.

## Limitations
- VQNN network had less efficiency and showed inconsistency in performance indices due to overfitting of stock datasets (author-stated).
- [inferred] Study used only two Tanzanian bank datasets (CRDB and NMB), limiting generalizability to other stocks, asset classes, and markets.
- [inferred] VQNN was trained with minimal epochs and tiny batch sizes (methods report epoch=1, batch size=1), which likely undertrained the quantum model and affected results.
- [inferred] No comprehensive hyperparameter tuning or evolutionary optimization was applied to the VQNN (and possibly other models), so the comparison may reflect suboptimal configurations rather than intrinsic model differences.
- [inferred] Differences in model architectures and capacities (e.g., LSTM with dropout and multi-hidden-layer MLR versus a relatively shallow VQNN) may bias the performance comparison.
- [inferred] The quantum experiments were implemented with PennyLane/Quantum Dense libraries (likely simulators); no runs on real NISQ hardware are reported, so hardware noise, gate errors and other practical constraints are not evaluated.
- [inferred] The study used only technical features (volume, turnover, deals, close, high, low); omission of fundamental and sentiment features limits the ecological validity and predictive robustness of the models.
- [inferred] Limited reporting of VQNN implementation details (ansatz design, number of qubits, encoding/feature map specifics) reduces reproducibility and prevents assessment of scalability.

## Contradictions
- The paper empirically contradicts claims of quantum superiority for stock price prediction: classical MLR and LSTM achieved substantially higher R2 and lower error metrics than the VQNN on the same datasets.
- The paper raises scalability doubts: the VQNN was trained with minimal epochs (epochs=1 reported for VQNN) and lacked circuit/qubit details yet is reported as faster, which contradicts optimistic claims that quantum ML currently provides practical, scalable advantages for real financial prediction tasks.
