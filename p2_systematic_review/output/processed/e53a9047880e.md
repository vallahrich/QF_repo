## Abstract summary
This paper proposes a Hybrid Quantum-Classical Neural Network (HQCNN) that uses amplitude-based quantum feature encoding and a Quantum Convolutional Neural Network (QCNN) for feature extraction, followed by classical LSTM or Transformer models for cryptocurrency price forecasting. Experiments on historical crypto market data show HQCNN outperforms classical and other quantum-inspired baselines (best MAE 0.023, RMSE 0.036), reduces inference time to about 7 ms, and the authors discuss limitations such as quantum hardware constraints, training overhead, and interpretability along with directions for hardware deployment, reinforcement learning integration, and explainability.
## Methodology
The authors propose a Hybrid Quantum-Classical Convolutional Neural Network (HQCNN) for cryptocurrency price forecasting. The pipeline begins with historical market data (price, percentage changes over 1h/24h/7d, trading volume, market capitalization) which are min-max normalized to [0,1]. Normalized feature vectors are encoded into quantum states using amplitude encoding (|ψ⟩ = Σ xi |i⟩). A parameterized Quantum Convolutional Neural Network (QCNN) / variational quantum circuit (VQC) stack is applied to the encoded state: multiple variational convolution-like layers followed by quantum pooling operations reduce qubit count and extract hierarchical quantum features. The output quantum state is measured (z = ⟨ψ|M|ψ⟩) and converted into a classical feature vector which is fed to a classical sequential predictor (either an LSTM or a Transformer). The hybrid model is trained end-to-end with a combined loss L = α L_quantum + β L_classical, where L_classical is MSE for price forecasting; classical portions are updated by backpropagation while quantum parameter updates use the parameter-shift rule. Experiments explored hyperparameters such as number of quantum layers (2–5), encoding methods (amplitude vs qubit encoding), and choice of classical predictor (LSTM vs Transformer). Evaluation included comparisons to baselines (ARIMA, LSTM, Transformer, TCN, QLSTM, Quantum Transformer), ablation studies (removing quantum encoding/feature extraction, or a given classical module), robustness tests under Gaussian noise, and scalability tests across datasets of varying cryptocurrency counts (10, 50, 100). Results were averaged over five independent runs with different random seeds and small reported run-to-run variance.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum Convolutional Neural Network (QCNN), Variational Quantum Circuit (VQC), Amplitude Encoding, Parameter-shift gradient rule, LSTM, Transformer

**Experimental setup:** Training and quantum-circuit simulation were performed on classical hardware: NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU (24 GB) and Intel Xeon CPU (32 cores). Quantum circuits were simulated (no specific quantum simulator or QPU was named). Reported training times (e.g., HQCNN 2500s, Quantum Transformer 2780s) and inference time (7 ms) are based on this simulated environment.

**Dataset:** A publicly available cryptocurrency market dataset containing historical prices (USD), percentage price changes over 1h/24h/7d, trading volume, and market capitalization for multiple cryptocurrencies. The dataset source is referenced as a CoinGecko scraper example. Experiments used datasets with varying numbers of cryptocurrencies (10, 50, 100).
