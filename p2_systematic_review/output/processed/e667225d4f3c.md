## Abstract summary
This review examines the application of quantum machine learning (QML) methods — including variational quantum circuits, quantum kernel estimation, and quantum-enhanced SVMs — to financial time-series prediction, proposing a hybrid quantum-classical framework that integrates quantum feature mapping with classical optimizers. Empirical benchmarks on historical and synthetic stock data indicate QML can achieve improved prediction accuracy and reduced latency relative to classical models (e.g., LSTM, gradient boosting) in certain small-to-medium dataset regimes with high feature correlation, while also highlighting NISQ hardware limits, data-encoding challenges, and interpretability and scalability concerns.
## Methodology
This review proposes and synthesizes a hybrid quantum-classical methodological framework for applying quantum machine learning (QML) to financial prediction. The core methodological components include: (1) strategies for encoding heterogeneous classical financial data into quantum states (discussing amplitude encoding, angle/basis encoding and other qubit-embedding schemes); (2) design and training of quantum-enhanced predictive models — including variational quantum circuits/parameterized quantum circuits (VQCs), quantum neural networks (QNNs), quantum support vector machines (QSVMs)/quantum kernel methods, and quantum determinantal point processes (DPPs) for sampling/diversity — with classical optimizers controlling trainable parameters; (3) the use of quantum algorithms relevant to finance such as Quantum Amplitude Estimation (QAE) for accelerated Monte Carlo, quantum linear-algebra subroutines for solving large systems and PCA-like tasks (QPCA), and quantum optimization algorithms (QAOA, quantum annealing) for portfolio optimization; (4) practical considerations for NISQ-era deployment including hybrid partitioning of workloads between classical and quantum processors, error mitigation and dynamical decoupling techniques, measurement-error mitigation, qubit-mapping and circuit-depth trade-offs; and (5) proposed evaluation and benchmarking approaches — recommending comparisons against classical baselines (e.g., LSTM, gradient boosting machines), validation on historical market data and synthetic datasets, and robust performance assessment under device noise models. The review emphasizes an integrative experimentation pipeline (data encoding → quantum feature mapping / circuit design → hybrid training loop with classical optimizer → inference and interpretability analysis) and highlights methodological gaps (scalability, data-embedding cost, interpretability, and the need for rigorous real-world benchmarks) that future empirical work should address.

**Algorithms used:** Variational Quantum Circuits (VQCs) / Parameterized Quantum Circuits, Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE), Quantum Neural Networks (QNNs), Quantum Support Vector Machine (QSVM) / Quantum Kernel Methods, Quantum Amplitude Estimation (QAE), Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA), Quantum Annealing, Quantum Principal Component Analysis (QPCA), Quantum Linear Algebra Algorithms (quantum linear systems, SVD routines), Quantum Reinforcement Learning, Quantum algorithms for stochastic optimal stopping, Quantum Determinantal Point Processes (Quantum DPPs)
**Frameworks:** Qiskit, Quantum simulators (general)

**Dataset:** The paper discusses use of historical stock market data and synthetic datasets for benchmarking QML approaches against classical models; it also discusses heterogeneous financial datasets requiring encoding (time-series, tabular transactional data).

- Many studies rely on synthetic or noise-free simulations, limiting generalizability to real-world, non-stationary financial data
