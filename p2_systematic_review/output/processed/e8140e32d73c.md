## Abstract summary
The paper presents a quantum-classical hybrid algorithm designed to solve large-scale linear systems of equations, combining quantum subroutines with classical processing to improve scalability and efficiency. The authors analyze the algorithm's complexity and implementation considerations, and discuss its potential advantages for high-dimensional problems.
## Methodology
This is a theoretical paper that develops and analyses a quantum–classical hybrid algorithmic approach for solving large-scale linear systems of equations. The methodology consists of algorithm design and rigorous analytical evaluation rather than laboratory experiments: the authors construct a hybrid computational framework in which certain computationally intensive subroutines of classical iterative linear solvers are offloaded to quantum subroutines (quantum linear-system primitives and related building blocks), while data preparation, preconditioning and result post-processing remain classical. The work focuses on mathematical derivation of the algorithmic steps, complexity and resource scaling (asymptotic runtime, gate count, and qubit requirements), and error propagation and stability analysis. The methodology includes formulation of the problem in a quantum-accessible representation (state preparation and encoding assumptions), derivation of correctness and convergence properties, proofs of theoretical speedup conditions relative to classical algorithms, and quantitative resource estimates that identify regimes where the hybrid approach is advantageous. No empirical hardware experiments are reported; the results are analytical and conceptual, with discussion of implementation considerations and potential bottlenecks for future empirical validation.
