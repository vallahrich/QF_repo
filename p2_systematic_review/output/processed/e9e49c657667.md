## Abstract summary
The paper explores the use of hybrid quantum–classical architectures for scenario-based macroeconomic and financial forecasting, proposing integration of quantum optimization, probabilistic sampling, and high-dimensional feature mapping to generate and evaluate large scenario trees. It argues quantum-enhanced methods can improve exploration of complex solution spaces and sensitivity to regime shifts and tail risks, while noting practical NISQ-era constraints, interpretability, and governance challenges that limit near-term scalability.
## Methodology
This is a conceptual, theoretical study that investigates scenario-based macroeconomic and financial forecasting using quantum-enhanced models. The paper conducts a methodological analysis of hybrid quantum–classical architectures: classical components perform data preprocessing, parameter estimation, and macroeconomic calibration, while quantum subroutines are proposed to address computational bottlenecks such as nonlinear optimization, high-dimensional feature mapping, probabilistic sampling, and large-scale scenario generation. The study surveys potential quantum subroutines (e.g., amplitude amplification, quantum annealing, variational circuits), quantum-enhanced Monte Carlo for tail-risk estimation (VaR, ES), quantum kernel methods for regime classification, and quantum graph algorithms for identifying contagion channels in network representations. It evaluates theoretical advantages (expanded solution-space exploration, improved scenario sensitivity), computational constraints and trade-offs in the NISQ era (noise, limited qubit counts, error propagation), and implementation challenges including interpretability, model validation, governance and institutional inequality. The methodology is primarily analytical and integrative—synthesizing algorithmic possibilities, application domains (systemic risk, regime shifts, tail-event dynamics), and practical constraints—to propose an architecture and discuss feasibility rather than reporting empirical experiments.
