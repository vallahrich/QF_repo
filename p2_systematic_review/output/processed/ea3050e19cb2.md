## Abstract summary
This chapter reviews variational quantum circuits (VQCs) as a bridge between quantum computing and machine learning, explaining their structure, hybrid quantum–classical optimization, and role as adaptable ML models. It discusses applications across domains such as quantum chemistry, optimization, generative modeling, and finance, highlights current hardware and noise limitations, and outlines future trends toward broader accessibility and practical quantum advantage.

- [supported] Classical optimization algorithms (gradient descent and other optimizers) are routinely used to update VQC parameters; training is conducted in an iterative quantum–classical feedback loop.
- [supported] Hybrid quantum–classical workflows (quantum circuits evaluated on hardware/simulator + classical optimizer updates) are currently the dominant practical paradigm for implementing VQCs.

## Limitations
- Hardware limitations: limited number of qubits, high error rates, limited qubit connectivity, short coherence times, and expensive / limited access to quantum devices.
- Nonconvex variational optimization: VQC parameter landscapes are nonconvex and may exhibit poor convergence or get stuck in local minima (no guarantee of finding global optimum).
- Training data size and encoding: resource requirements (qubits, gates) grow rapidly with data size, making large datasets difficult to handle on current hardware.
- Hybrid classical–quantum integration overhead: coordinating quantum circuits with classical optimization can introduce synchronization and performance overhead that reduces or negates speedups.
- Noise and error sources: decoherence, gate errors, and readout errors degrade the accuracy and reliability of VQC outputs.
- Quantum error correction impracticality on NISQ devices: error correcting codes require many extra qubits and gates, often infeasible on near-term hardware.
- Error mitigation costs: methods to mitigate noise add computational overhead and complexity.
- Scaling challenges: difficulty scaling hardware to larger qubit counts while maintaining low error rates and manageable calibration/control complexity.
- Resource and cost constraints: substantial computational and economic resources required to run nontrivial VQC applications on current platforms.
