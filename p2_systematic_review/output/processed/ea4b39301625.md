## Abstract summary
This preprint proposes a variant of the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) that uses Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) as the objective and an enhanced ansatz with an additional problem-agnostic ZZ layer to solve discrete portfolio optimization (QUBO/Ising) problems. Experiments on historical Nasdaq data show markedly faster convergence and higher probabilities of finding optimal portfolios compared with standard QAOA (e.g., finding the 16-stock optimum in ~35 iterations vs ~700), with advantages growing as problem size increases.
## Methodology
The authors convert a discrete mean-variance portfolio optimization (MVPO) problem into a QUBO / Ising Hamiltonian by binarizing portfolio weights (buy/hold xi ∈ {0,1}), computing expected returns µ and covariance Σ from historical price data, and mapping xi to spins zi for construction of a cost Hamiltonian H_C (sum of single-qubit Z and two-qubit ZZ terms). They apply the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) in a hybrid quantum-classical loop: prepare the uniform superposition state, apply p layers of cost and mixer unitaries, measure, evaluate an objective and update variational parameters via a classical optimizer. Two modifications are used: (1) replace the usual expectation-value objective by a Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) statistic computed over measured sample energies (to emphasize low-energy/tail outcomes and smooth the target function), and (2) introduce an improved ansatz that appends a problem-agnostic layer of nearest-neighbor ZZ gates (with a single shared parameter) and an extra global X-mixer layer (also with a single shared parameter) to the standard p-layer QAOA, yielding 2(p+1) variational parameters. Experiments are performed via exact-state simulation (TensorCircuit) for multiple portfolio sizes (10, 12, 14, 16 qubits), QAOA depths p=1 (p=2 tested but not reported in detail), CVaR confidence levels α in {0.01,0.05,0.1,0.25,0.5,0.75,1}, and classical parameter optimization using COBYLA. Performance is compared between original QAOA (α=1), CVaR-QAOA, and the improved CVaR-QAOA in terms of achieved cost value and probability of measuring the optimal solution; convergence iteration counts are reported.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA), CVaR-QAOA (QAOA objective replaced with Conditional Value-at-Risk), Improved QAOA ansatz (standard QAOA + problem-agnostic ZZ layer + extra mixer)
**Frameworks:** TensorCircuit

**Experimental setup:** All experiments were carried out using exact-state quantum-circuit simulation via TensorCircuit (no physical quantum hardware). Classical optimization of variational parameters used the COBYLA optimizer. Multiple portfolio instances (10, 12, 14, 16 qubits) were simulated; QAOA depth p=1 was primarily reported. CVaR objective used α values from 0.01 to 1.0. Historical stock price data were used to compute µ and Σ for cost/Hamiltonian construction.

**Dataset:** Historical stock data from Nasdaq over a five-year period (2019-04-22 to 2024-04-18) for a selected set of 16 stocks (tickers and company names listed in the paper). Portfolio instances of sizes 10, 12, 14, and 16 were generated from these stocks.

## Findings
- [supported] An improved QAOA ansatz that appends a problem-agnostic layer of n-1 ZZ two-qubit gates (all sharing one parameter) and an additional mixer layer of X gates (sharing one parameter) was implemented and evaluated.
- [supported] Using Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) as the classical objective (replacing the sample mean) speeds convergence and increases the probability of obtaining optimal solutions compared to the standard expectation-value objective (α = 1).
- [supported] In simulated experiments (TensorCircuit), the improved CVaR-QAOA converged to lower cost values faster than the standard CVaR-QAOA and the original QAOA across tested portfolio sizes (10, 12, 14, 16 stocks).
- [supported] For the 16-stock instance the paper reports the improved CVaR-QAOA achieved the optimal cost in ~35 iterations while the original QAOA required ~700 iterations (simulation results).
- [supported] CVaR with small α values (α in the range 0.01–0.1, often α = 0.01) typically performed better than larger α or α = 1 in the tested portfolio optimization instances.
- [supported] QAOA depth p = 1 outperformed or matched p = 2 in the authors' experiments for these portfolio instances (they therefore present only p = 1 results).
- [supported] Experiments used historical stock data from Nasdaq spanning 04/22/2019–04/18/2024 and portfolio instances with budgets and buy/hold binary variables (QUBO formulation).
- [supported] Classical optimization of QAOA parameters used the COBYLA optimizer in the hybrid loop and exact-state simulations (no noisy hardware) via TensorCircuit for evaluation.
- [speculative] The improved QAOA with CVaR is a promising approach for portfolio optimization in practical financial-industry applications, particularly as the number of assets increases.
- [speculative] The additional problem-agnostic ZZ/X layer provides a balance between circuit depth and optimization quality (the claim of general balance is presented as an intended design goal and supported in these experiments but suggested as broadly applicable).
- [supported] The authors did not identify a single universally best α; they note that selecting α may require problem-specific tuning and suggest future work on adaptive α selection.

**Results summary:** The paper proposes an enhanced QAOA ansatz combined with a CVaR-based objective (CVaR-QAOA and an improved CVaR-QAOA) for discrete portfolio optimization (QUBO/Ising formulation). Using exact-state simulations on instances derived from 10–16 stock historical data, the authors report that CVaR objectives with small α (often 0.01) lead to faster convergence and higher success probability than the standard expectation objective. Their improved ansatz (an extra problem-agnostic ZZ layer plus an extra X mixer) further accelerates convergence and increases the probability of finding optimal solutions; e.g., for a 16-stock instance they report reaching the optimal cost in ~35 iterations versus ~700 iterations for the original QAOA. Results are based on simulated (noise-free) experiments using the COBYLA classical optimizer and TensorCircuit.

## Contradictions
- The authors claim that advantages of the improved CVaR-QAOA grow as problem size increases, but all supporting results are from exact-state simulations on small instances (10–16 qubits) with p=1; this contradicts confidence in scalability to larger, hardware-constrained problems where qubit counts, gate depth, and noise would likely negate the observed gains.
- The paper reports superiority over standard QAOA variants but does not compare against strong classical heuristics or exact classical solvers for the same instances; thus the claim of practical advantage versus classical methods is not empirically supported and may be overstated.
