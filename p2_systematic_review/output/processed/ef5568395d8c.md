## Abstract summary
This empirical study models financial performance of 234 European football clubs (2016–2018) using multilayer perceptron and a quantum neural network, with dependent variables RONW and ROCE and predictors spanning financial, sporting, governance and reputation factors. Results indicate that liquidity, leverage and sporting performance are key determinants and that the quantum neural network outperforms the MLP in predictive accuracy, suggesting QNNs can improve forecasting for club managers and regulators.
## Methodology
The authors construct a supervised empirical predictive study of football-club financial performance using two neural-network based methods (a classical multilayer perceptron, MLP, and a quantum-inspired hybrid quantum neural network, QNN). They assemble a cross-country sample of 234 European professional football clubs and extract financial/corporate data from the Amadeus database, sporting data from Transfermarkt, and reputation/social-media counts from official club accounts. Two dependent variables are modeled separately: return on net worth (RONW) and return on capital employed (ROCE). A broad set of independent features spanning corporate-governance, reputation, sporting performance, and financial ratios is used. The dataset is split into train/validation/test subsets (70/10/20). MLP models use sigmoidal activation and standard feedforward backpropagation. The QNN is implemented as a hybrid quantum-inspired network with quantum hidden-neuron initialization (qubit superposition with probability p), rotation gates for weight updates, and a quantum-augmented backpropagation update rule (rotation-angle update ∆θ derived by chain rule), with a stated learning-rate η = 0.1. Variable-importance is assessed via a sensitivity analysis using a Sobol-style variance decomposition and a neural-network sensitivity procedure. Models are evaluated by accuracy, RMSE and MAPE; multi-step iterative forecasting (one- and two-step ahead, t+1 and t+2) is also performed to test robustness. The pipeline yields model accuracies and identifies the most influential predictors via normalized impact scores.

**Algorithms used:** Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) with backpropagation, Quantum-inspired Hybrid Quantum Neural Network (QNN), Sobol sensitivity analysis / neural-network sensitivity analysis

**Dataset:** Cross-sectional dataset of 234 European professional football clubs (clubs participating in first and second national divisions). Financial and corporate data from Amadeus (Bureau van Dijk), sporting performance from Transfermarkt, social-media follower counts from clubs' official social accounts. Time period referenced 2016–2018 with baseline data from 2016 and reserved data for post-estimation (2017, 2018). Features include corporate-governance, reputation (Facebook, Instagram, Twitter followers), sporting metrics (attendance, points, division, wage bill, performance ratio), and ~20 financial ratios (liquidity, leverage, profitability, growth, coverage). Dependent variables: RONW and ROCE.

**Results summary:** This empirical study compared a multilayer perceptron (MLP) and a quantum neural network (QNN) on a cross-country sample of 234 European professional football clubs (2016 baseline, with 2017–2018 held for forecasting). Across training, validation, testing and multi-step forecasts (t+1, t+2), the QNN consistently outperformed the MLP in predictive accuracy for two financial-performance targets (RONW and ROCE). Sensitivity (Sobol) analysis identified liquidity ratios, leverage metrics and sporting-performance variables (notably wage bill and performance ranking) as the most influential predictors; governance and reputation variables (CEO duality, Facebook followers) also appeared significant. Reported testing accuracies ranged roughly from ~90.6% to ~94.5%, with the QNN reaching up to 96.17% (training) on ROCE. The authors interpret these results as evidence that QNN (or quantum-inspired neural methods) can improve financial-performance modeling in this domain, while noting further extensions are possible.

**Performance claims:**
- QNN testing accuracy for ROCE: 94.53% (RMSE 0.51, MAPE 0.30).
- MLP testing accuracy for ROCE: 91.46% (RMSE 0.75, MAPE 0.39).
- QNN testing accuracy for RONW: 93.53% (RMSE 0.58, MAPE 0.34).
- MLP testing accuracy for RONW: 90.57% (RMSE 0.79, MAPE 0.42).
- Highest single reported accuracy: QNN training accuracy 96.17% for ROCE (training RMSE 0.42, MAPE 0.18).
- Multi-step (t+1) forecasting: QNN accuracy for RONW 91.85% and for ROCE 92.38%; MLP accuracies were 88.32% (RONW) and 89.67% (ROCE).
- Multi-step (t+2) forecasting: QNN accuracy for RONW 89.41% and for ROCE 89.41%; MLP accuracies were 86.16% (RONW) and 87.21% (ROCE).
- Sensitivity analysis (normalized impacts reported): examples include F2 (Total Debt/Total Revenue) impact 72% (QNN,RONW), P7 (Performance Ratio) impact 73% (QNN,ROCE), and I4 (CEO duality) impact 51% (QNN,ROCE).
## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** demonstrated

The study empirically demonstrates that a quantum neural network (QNN) variant achieved higher predictive accuracy than a classical multilayer perceptron on this football-club financial dataset and maintained superior multi-step forecast performance. The demonstration is empirical and limited to this application and the authors' QNN implementation (likely quantum-inspired/hybrid rather than execution on large-scale quantum hardware), so the advantage is demonstrated for this dataset but not generalized to broad quantum computational advantage across financial services.
