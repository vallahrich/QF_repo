## Abstract summary
The study evaluates quantum computing methods for stock price movement prediction by extracting technical indicators from real-world stock data and applying dimensionality reduction (PCA) and quantum annealing for feature selection. Quantum Support Vector Machines (QSVM) were trained and compared with classical classifiers across four companies; results indicate quantum annealing can improve feature selection but QSVM did not show a clear advantage over classical SVMs, and dimensionality reduction is important given current hardware limits.
## Methodology
The authors performed an empirical study comparing quantum-enhanced and classical approaches for next-day binary stock-price movement prediction. They collected daily market data (open/high/low/close) for four tickers (AAPL, V, VISA, JNJ, HON) from 25 Dec 2020 to 25 Dec 2022 via the yfinance API, computed technical indicators (SMA/EMA, RSI, MACD, Stochastic Oscillator, ATR, Aroon), and constructed a binary label Change = 1 if Price_T < Price_{T+1} else 0. To reduce dimensionality they applied principal component analysis (PCA) to produce datasets with 3, 5 and 8 components (PCA3/PCA5/PCA8) and they performed feature selection via a QUBO formulation solved with Quantum Annealing to produce corresponding QA3/QA5/QA8 datasets. They trained a suite of classical classifiers (SVM, Decision Tree, Random Forest, KNN, Logistic Regression, Naive Bayes, Gradient Boosting, XGBoost) and quantum-enhanced classifiers (Quantum Support Vector Classifier using quantum kernels implemented with Qiskit ZZFeatureMap and different entanglement schemes: linear, circular, full, pairwise). Model performance was compared using accuracy and F-score averaged across experiments. The pipeline highlights QA for feature selection, PCA for compression, then model training and evaluation on reduced-dimension datasets. The paper notes practical limits (QSVC training failed for 8-feature sets due to simulation resource limits) and contrasts QA-selected features against PCA components.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum Annealing (QUBO), Principal Component Analysis (PCA), Quantum Support Vector Machine / Quantum Support Vector Classifier (QSVM/QSVC) with ZZFeatureMap, Classical SVM, Decision Tree, Random Forest, K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), Logistic Regression, Naive Bayes, Gradient Boosting, XGBoost
**Frameworks:** yfinance, scikit-learn (sklearn), Qiskit (ZZFeatureMap / QSVC), D-Wave (Quantum Annealer)

**Experimental setup:** Data collection via yfinance for four tickers over 25 Dec 2020 – 25 Dec 2022. Feature engineering computed technical indicators (SMA/EMA, RSI, MACD, Stochastic, ATR, Aroon). Dimensionality reduction performed via PCA (3,5,8 components) and feature selection via a QUBO solved using a D-Wave Quantum Annealer. Classical classifiers trained on original and reduced datasets. Quantum kernel-based QSVC models were trained using Qiskit ZZFeatureMap with entanglement schemes (linear, circular, full, pairwise); QSVC training for 8-feature datasets failed due to simulation resource limits. Evaluation metrics were accuracy and F-score. Specific hardware/simulator backends, shots, qubit counts and other low-level parameters are not reported.

**Dataset:** Daily historical stock data (open/high/low/close) from yfinance for Apple (AAPL), Visa (VISA), Johnson & Johnson (JNJ) and Honeywell (HON) spanning 25 Dec 2020 to 25 Dec 2022. Derived technical indicators used as features.

- [speculative] Quantum Neural Networks (QNNs) and Quantum LSTM variants (QLSTM) may offer improved modelling of temporal/complex relationships in financial time series compared to classical neural networks, but this paper does not empirically demonstrate such advantages.

**Results summary:** The authors evaluated quantum-enhanced methods for binary stock-price movement prediction using real stock data and technical indicators. They applied dimensionality reduction (PCA) and quantum annealing (QUBO on D-Wave) for feature selection, and trained classical classifiers and a quantum SVM (QSVC) with ZZFeatureMap variants. Empirically, QA-based feature selection yielded more relevant features than PCA in their experiments, but QSVM did not show a consistent improvement over classical SVM. Practical limitations—insufficient simulation/hardware resources and noise—prevented training of quantum models on higher-dimensional (8-feature) datasets and limited the demonstration of a quantum advantage. Overall, classical methods often matched or outperformed the quantum-enhanced approaches on these datasets; the authors conclude that QA shows promise for feature selection while a general quantum advantage for classification in this context remains unproven and requires further research and better hardware.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The paper provides empirical evidence that quantum annealing can be useful for feature selection on the studied financial data, but it does not demonstrate a consistent or reproducible quantum advantage for classification (QSVM did not outperform classical SVM). Hardware/simulation limits and noise prevented larger-scale quantum experiments, so any claim of a general quantum advantage is tentative/speculative and contingent on future hardware and algorithmic advances.
## Limitations
- Current quantum hardware is in a developing stage: highly sensitive and prone to noise-induced errors, with a limited number of quality qubits (author-stated).
- Dimensionality reduction was necessary because of hardware limitations; available qubits cannot be 'wasted' on high-dimensional raw data (author-stated).
- Insufficient computational resources for simulating quantum feature maps at higher dimensionality: QSVC models with 8 features failed to train due to lack of computational power required to simulate the quantum circuits (author-stated).
- Quantum Support Vector Machine (QSVM) did not show a clear advantage over classical SVM for the datasets used — no significant improvement in binary classification performance was observed (author-stated).
