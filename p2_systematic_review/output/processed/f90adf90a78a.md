## Abstract summary
This preprint applies a variational quantum neural network to short-term demand forecasting for used vehicle financing using a BV bank dataset (2019–2023), with dimensionality reduction (PCA) and data standardization. Results show the quantum model achieved comparable predictive accuracy to a classical recurrent neural network while using fewer trainable parameters and converging in fewer epochs, suggesting promise for quantum machine learning in financial forecasting tasks.
## Methodology
The authors trained variational quantum neural networks (QNNs) to forecast monthly average daily used-vehicle financing using historical economic data from a Brazilian bank. Starting from an original set of 25 features (6 removed due to missing 2019 entries), they used PCA to produce reduced feature sets of 4, 8 and the full 19 features (representing ~68.7%, 92.0% and 100% cumulative variance respectively). All features were standardized (x_hat = (x - mu)/sigma). The QNNs used a parametrized quantum circuit (PQC) pipeline: Hadamard layers to prepare uniform superposition, RY rotations to encode each (standardized) classical feature into a dedicated qubit, layered variational ansätze composed of per-qubit parameterized rotations R(θ,ϕ,ω) interleaved with entanglement layers (two entanglement patterns explored: paired-then-linked and cascade), and a measurement layer returning classical outputs (expectation values). They evaluated three ansatz depths (1, 3, 5 variational layers) and two QNN variants (referred to as experiment 1 and experiment 2) and compared results to classical recurrent neural network (RNN) baselines (recurrent layers with 128 and 1024 neurons). Training was performed via classical optimizers through PennyLane + TensorFlow simulation, using mean absolute error (MAE) as the loss/metric. Models were trained until convergence (empirically <30 epochs; they used 30 epochs for runs), each experiment repeated 10 times to report mean and standard deviation of MAE per month and over the 12-month test period. Execution was done via PennyLane simulators on an HPC cluster (Intel Xeon Platinum 8260L CPUs).

**Algorithms used:** Variational Quantum Circuit / Quantum Neural Network (QNN), RY feature encoding, Principal Component Analysis (PCA), Classical Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) baseline
**Frameworks:** PennyLane, TensorFlow, scikit-learn

**Experimental setup:** Simulations run with PennyLane (Xanadu) interfacing TensorFlow on an HPC cluster with Intel Xeon Platinum 8260L CPUs. 4- and 8-feature simulations used a single core; 19-feature simulations used 17 cores. All quantum experiments were simulated (no physical QPU).

**Dataset:** Proprietary monthly financing dataset provided by Brazilian bank BV, covering January 2019 to April 2023 (52 monthly samples). Original 25 features (6 removed for missing-2019 entries, leaving 19). Target: monthly daily-average used-vehicle financing. Data includes the COVID-19 pandemic period (kept as-is).

## Findings
- [supported] A quantum neural network (QNN) implemented with parameterized quantum circuits can be trained (in simulation) to forecast monthly average daily used-vehicle financing from the provided dataset.
- [supported] In the authors' simulations the QNN achieved predictive accuracy comparable to a classical recurrent neural network (RNN) on the same forecasting task (examples: 4-feature and 8-feature cases).
- [supported] The QNN models empirically converged rapidly in training (most models converged before 30 epochs in the authors' experiments).
- [supported] In several simulated experiments the QNN produced lower or comparable mean absolute error (MAE) than the tested classical RNN when using reduced feature sets (4 and 8 principal components).
- [supported] The QNN models appeared more robust to a single problematic / outlier feature in the full 19-feature dataset (classical RNN results became highly skewed by that feature in the authors' runs).
- [speculative] The authors claim QNNs can offer a practical advantage for demand forecasting in finance by using fewer training parameters and fewer epochs, implying potential computational/resource savings on future quantum hardware.
- [speculative] The paper suggests that integrating quantum computing into predictive analytics can 'revolutionize' financial decision-making and risk management (a forward-looking claim not demonstrated here).
- [speculative] The authors state novelty: that this is the "first time" a QNN was used to predict short-term demand for used vehicles (claim of precedence rather than experimental fact).
- [speculative] The manuscript implies that the observed faster convergence and reduced parameterization in simulation may translate into runtime advantages on real quantum hardware (not demonstrated).
- [supported] PCA-based feature reduction (to 4 and 8 principal components) was used and the reduced datasets (68.69% and 92.03% variance retained) were evaluated with both QNN and classical RNN models.

**Results summary:** The authors trained variational quantum neural network models (simulated via PennyLane) to forecast monthly average daily used-vehicle financing using a BV bank dataset (52 monthly samples, 2019–2023), with PCA-based feature reduction to 4, 8, and 19 components. Across multiple experiments and variational-layer counts, QNNs in simulation reached predictive performance similar to or better than a classical RNN on the reduced-feature tasks, converging rapidly (typically within 30 epochs). QNNs showed empirical robustness to a problematic outlier feature that heavily skewed the classical RNN results in the full-feature case. The work is presented as an exploratory demonstration (simulated) of QNN applicability to financial demand forecasting rather than a demonstration of a provable quantum speedup or empirical runtime advantage on quantum hardware.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** speculative

The authors report simulation results where QNNs achieve comparable or better MAE and faster convergence (in epochs) than a tested classical RNN on reduced-feature datasets. However, all QNN experiments were simulated classically (PennyLane on CPU cores), no runs on quantum hardware were performed, no provable algorithmic speedup is demonstrated, and wall-clock/run-time advantage is not shown on quantum devices. Therefore any claim of practical quantum advantage remains speculative rather than demonstrated.
## Limitations
- Small dataset: only 52 monthly samples (January 2019–April 2023), limiting amount of training data.
- Six features were discarded because they had no data for 2019, reducing available feature information.
- Dataset contains an anomalous period (COVID-19 pandemic); authors retained it due to small sample size, which may affect model behaviour.
- A single feature exhibited a large increase from the 8th test month onwards; this caused strong skewing/bias (notably large errors for the classical model).
- Experiments were performed on a classical quantum simulator (PennyLane) rather than on real quantum hardware; processing times and noise effects on actual devices were not measured.
- Limited availability of quantum resources constrained experiments on real quantum devices.
- Evaluation relied primarily on mean absolute error (and its monthly standard deviation); other performance metrics, statistical tests, and economic loss measures were not reported.
- [inferred] Very limited sample size reduces statistical power and external validity of conclusions; risk of overfitting and limited generalizability to other time periods or markets.
- [inferred] Results on a noiseless/ideal simulator may not translate to NISQ devices where gate errors and decoherence degrade PQC performance.
- [inferred] Encoding/ansatz choice was limited (Hadamard + Ry feature map and the particular layered ansatz used); alternative encodings and circuit architectures were not explored.
- [inferred] Only a small set of variational layer depths (1, 3, 5) and two QNN variants were tested; the hyperparameter/architecture search appears narrow.
- [inferred] Comparison with classical baselines was limited to an RNN (two neuron sizes); no comparisons with other strong classical models (LSTM/GRU, Transformer, XGBoost, ARIMA, ensembles) were presented.
- [inferred] Scalability to larger datasets, longer horizons, or higher-dimensional feature spaces was not demonstrated.
- [inferred] Use of PCA for dimensionality reduction may remove nonlinear structure important to forecasting; impact of this reduction on model expressivity was not fully investigated.
- [inferred] Only 10 repeated runs per experiment were reported; robustness and statistical significance assessment are limited.
- [inferred] Computational cost comparisons between quantum and classical approaches remain inconclusive because simulator runtimes do not reflect execution on quantum hardware.
## Open questions
- How will the QNN architectures evaluated perform on real quantum hardware (including noise) compared to simulator results?
- Can QNNs maintain their predictive performance and training advantages when scaled to larger datasets and longer forecasting horizons?
- Do quantum analogs of recurrent networks (quantum RNNs/quantum LSTMs) outperform the hybrid QNN used here for time-series financial forecasting?
- Which data encoding schemes, ansatz structures, and circuit depths optimize performance for financial time series forecasting?
- How robust are QNNs to anomalous economic events (e.g., pandemic shocks) compared with classical models?
- How do QNNs compare to stronger classical baselines (LSTM/GRU/Transformer, gradient-boosted trees, statistical time-series models) in accuracy, robustness, and resource use?
- What are the best loss functions or evaluation metrics (including economic cost functions and probabilistic forecasts) for training and assessing QNNs in finance?
- How does dimensionality reduction (e.g., PCA) affect QNN vs classical model performance, especially regarding nonlinear feature interactions?
- Can QNNs provide calibrated uncertainty estimates or probabilistic forecasts useful for financial decision-making?
- What are the energy, runtime, and resource-efficiency trade-offs between practical quantum implementations and optimized classical solutions for this task?

## Key ideas
- #idea:quantum-advantage — Variational QNNs (PQC with RY encoding) achieved comparable MAE to classical RNN baselines while using fewer trainable parameters and converging in fewer epochs across 4-, 8-, and 19-feature experiments.
- #idea:near-term-feasibility — Authors argue QNNs show promise for short-term financial forecasting tasks in the NISQ era based on simulation results and reduced-parameter models.
- #idea:hybrid-approach — The pipeline relies on classical preprocessing (PCA, standardization) and classical optimizers (TensorFlow) in a hybrid training loop implemented via PennyLane.
- #limitation:simulation-only — All experiments were run on PennyLane simulators (HPC CPUs); no QPU experiments or noise models were evaluated.
- #limitation:no-empirical-validation — Results are based on simulation over a small proprietary dataset (52 monthly samples) with no public data or real-hardware validation.
- #limitation:qubit-count — Qubit count equals number of features (4, 8, 19), indicating limited scale and potential impracticality for higher-dimensional financial datasets.
- #limitation:data-encoding — Feature encoding used one RY per feature (one-qubit-per-feature mapping), implying linear qubit scaling and potential encoding costs for larger feature sets.
- #limitation:noise — The study does not model or mitigate QPU noise; claims about near-term feasibility do not account for hardware noise or error mitigation overhead.
## Contradictions
- Paper suggests near-term feasibility and a quantum 'advantage' (fewer parameters, faster convergence) but all evidence is simulation-based on a very small dataset (52 samples) and at most 19 qubits, which contradicts any implicit claim of scalability to real-world production forecasting.
- The claimed advantage is primarily in parameter count and convergence speed, while predictive accuracy was only comparable to classical RNNs; this undermines a stronger claim of quantum superiority over classical methods in this task.
