## Abstract summary
The paper presents an advanced methodology for equivalence checking of quantum circuits that leverages quantum-specific properties—particularly reversibility—and the surprising effectiveness of simulation. By interleaving application of gates from the two circuits to keep their concatenation close to the identity and using a small number of random simulations to catch non-equivalence early, the approach substantially outperforms prior decision-diagram and SAT-based methods. An implementation is provided and experiments show dramatic speedups, often finding counterexamples with a single simulation run or proving equivalence far faster than earlier techniques.

## Contradictions
- This paper demonstrates that classical simulation and randomized simulation checks can outperform prior formal methods for equivalence checking, contradicting the common assumption that decision-diagram or SAT-based approaches are necessarily superior for circuit verification (contradiction:classical-vs-quantum).
- By showing that surprisingly few simulations suffice to detect non-equivalence and that many equivalence proofs are found much faster, the paper challenges prevailing beliefs about the scalability limits of classical simulation for verification tasks (contradiction:scalability).
