## Abstract summary
This paper examines the threat posed by quantum computing—particularly Shor's algorithm—to RSA-2048 encryption used in banking, modeling how polynomial-time quantum factorization undermines the hardness assumptions of classical public-key cryptography. It analyzes timelines for quantum feasibility, the ‘harvest now, decrypt later’ risk to financial communications, and evaluates post-quantum cryptographic alternatives and migration strategies for financial institutions.
## Methodology
The study adopts a comparative, theoretical methodology combining a computational-complexity model, an algorithmic vulnerability assessment, and a risk-simulation framework tailored to a banking cryptographic architecture. The system model assumes a national banking environment using RSA-2048 (N = p*q with 1024-bit primes) for TLS handshakes, digital signatures and key exchange. Mathematical modeling contrasts a classical sub-exponential factoring approximation (presented as y = exp(sqrt(x))) with a polynomial quantum approximation (presented as y = x^3) to illustrate the complexity-class transition. The algorithmic methodology is a conceptual three-step pipeline: (1) data interception simulation (harvest-now storage of ciphertext C = M^e mod N), (2) reduction of factoring to period finding using Shor's algorithm with the Quantum Fourier Transform (QFT) to extract the period r, and (3) factor recovery and private-key computation (and subsequent decryption). The study also estimates computational resources required for a cryptographically-relevant attack (order-of-magnitude estimates such as ~4,000 logical qubits and millions of physical qubits after error correction), and simulates risk timelines with discrete scenarios (present/10 years/20 years) to support a Harvest-Now-Decrypt-Later threat model. Validation is literature-based and conceptual: references to small-scale experimental demonstrations (IBM factoring of 15), asymptotic mathematical comparison, and alignment with NIST/NSA migration recommendations; no new empirical quantum experiments or benchmarked implementations are reported.

**Algorithms used:** Shor's algorithm, Quantum Fourier Transform (QFT)
