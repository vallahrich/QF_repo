## Abstract summary
The paper introduces a quantum algorithm to compute the characteristic function of discrete stochastic processes using a number of circuit elements that grows only linearly with the number of time steps. By encoding all process trajectories in superposition, the method reduces Monte Carlo sampling to a Bernoulli trial with optimal variance (eliminating the need for importance sampling) and can be combined with quantum amplitude estimation for a quadratic sampling speed-up. The authors demonstrate applications to option Delta pricing and correlated random walks and present proof-of-principle experiments on IBM quantum hardware.
## Methodology
The authors develop a quantum-classical hybrid methodology to estimate expectation values of functions of discrete stochastic processes (DSPs) by computing point evaluations of the characteristic function φ_X(v)=E[e^{ivX}]. They encode the DSP into a bipartite quantum state comprising an index register (encoding path probabilities) and a single-qubit data register (encoding trajectory-dependent phase/rotation). For independent-increment processes the index state is prepared as a product of level-wise qudit/qubit rotations (Ry) to realize the marginal probabilities; for path-dependent (Markov) processes the index preparation uses controlled rotations to entangle successive levels so joint/path probabilities are encoded. Trajectory values are mapped to controlled unitaries V(x) applied to the data qubit (choices V(x)=Rz(2x) for Pauli measurements or V(x)=Ry(x) for amplitude-estimation-compatible decomposition). Two measurement schemes are used: (i) direct Pauli-σx/σy measurement on the data qubit to obtain E[cos(S_n)] and E[sin(S_n)] (reducing estimation to Bernoulli trials with O(1/√N) shot scaling), and (ii) Quantum Amplitude Estimation (AE) with additional ancillae and QFT to obtain quadratic improvement in sampling complexity. To estimate arbitrary integrable functions f(S_n) they compute Fourier-series coefficients and combine characteristic-function evaluations at harmonics v_l = 2πl/P (l = −L...L). The authors implement proof-of-principle experiments on IBM’s 5-qubit superconducting device (ibmqx2) using Qiskit: circuits are transpiled with pass managers (optimization levels 0 then 3), standard Qiskit error-mitigation is applied, and noisy simulations (Qiskit noise model) are used for comparison. Two example applications are demonstrated: (a) European call option Delta where geometric Brownian motion is approximated by a random walk, and (b) correlated random walks (path-dependent increments). Characteristic functions are sampled at many harmonics (L=100, P=100) and assembled by Fourier approximation to obtain the desired expectation values. Experimental outputs are compared to theoretical predictions and to noisy simulations.

**Algorithms used:** Quantum Amplitude Estimation, Quantum Fourier Transform, Pauli-based (σx/σy) characteristic function estimation, Fourier-series approximation (classical)
**Frameworks:** Qiskit, IBM Quantum cloud (ibmqx2)

**Experimental setup:** Proof-of-principle experiments run on IBM Quantum's 5-qubit superconducting device ibmqx2 via Qiskit (version 0.20.0). Circuits compiled using pass managers (optimization level 0 then 3) to satisfy coupling constraints. Standard Qiskit measurement-error mitigation applied. Noisy-device simulations using Qiskit's standard noise model were performed for comparison. Characteristic function evaluated at harmonics v_l = 2πl/P for l = −L..L (L=100, P=100). For Delta experiments each harmonic evaluation used 8129 total shots (split as multiple executions because device limit is 8192 shots per job); correlated random walk experiments used 32786 total shots per harmonic evaluation.

**Dataset:** Synthetic financial data: geometric Brownian motion for underlying asset (simulated via discrete random-walk approximation) with specified model parameters (e.g., μ=0, σ=0.02, r=0.02, S0=100, t=1, T=10). No external market dataset was used.

**Results summary:** The paper introduces a quantum-classical hybrid method to compute expectation values of functions of discrete stochastic processes by efficiently estimating the process characteristic function on a quantum computer. The core idea encodes all possible trajectories in a joint index–data quantum state so that evaluating the characteristic function reduces to single- or few-qubit measurements. The authors present circuit constructions that scale linearly with the number of time steps, explain how Pauli measurements or quantum amplitude estimation can be used (the latter giving a theoretical quadratic sampling speed-up), and demonstrate proof-of-principle experiments on IBM hardware for option-Delta calculation and correlated random walks that show qualitative agreement with theory and noisy simulations after error mitigation.

## Quantum advantage claim
**Classification:** theoretical

The paper argues (and provides theoretical proofs/constructs) that combining the algorithm with quantum amplitude estimation yields a quadratic speed-up in sampling compared to classical Monte Carlo. This advantage is presented analytically and supported by resource-count discussions but is not empirically demonstrated at scale on hardware; experiments are proof-of-principle feasibility tests rather than demonstrations of a practical quantum speed-up.
## Limitations
- Resource overhead of amplitude estimation (AE): AE requires m ancilla qubits, O(poly(m)) Grover-like iterators and O(m^2) gates for QFT, which may be prohibitive on NISQ devices (author-stated).
- Worst-case state-preparation cost: encoding kn probabilities into amplitude coefficients can be computationally expensive; general state preparation overhead may be substantial (author-stated).
- Experimental hardware limitations: proof-of-principle experiments on ibmqx2 are affected by device noise, limited qubit connectivity (swap overhead), and error sources not captured by simple noise models (cross-talk, drift, non-Markovian noise) (author-stated).
- Fourier approximation constraints: the approach relies on Fourier (periodic) approximation of the payoff/function f; truncation (finite-L) and period choices introduce approximation error and may require many harmonics (author-stated).
- Small-scale demonstration: results are demonstrated only on small devices and classical simulations; scalability to large, practical DSP instances is not demonstrated (author-stated).
- Requirement to realize increments as particular unitaries: Propositions use specific unitary families (e.g., Rz or Ry) — mapping arbitrary increments to such unitaries may be nontrivial (author-stated).
- No explicit circuit for state-dependent increments: the paper leaves explicit circuit design for state-dependent increments as future work (author-stated).
- Multivariate extension complexity: extension to multivariate functions is noted but the practical resource/complexity implications are not detailed (author-stated).
- [inferred] Scalability concerns in practice: although gate count scales linearly in n and k for the controlled operations, total qubit/gate requirements (especially for large k or fine discretizations) may still be impractically large.
- [inferred] Discretization/approximation trade-offs: continuous processes (e.g., Brownian motion) are approximated by discrete walks — required n for a given accuracy and resulting resource scaling is not quantified.
- [inferred] Overhead converting qudits to qubits: practical implementations will encode k-dimensional index registers into qubit registers, likely increasing depth and gate-count beyond the idealized statements.
- [inferred] Dependence on effectiveness of error mitigation: claimed advantages may be reduced or eliminated if error mitigation is insufficient on near-term devices.
