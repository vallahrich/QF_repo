## Instructions

You are an **independent grounding verifier** for a thematic-synthesis pipeline in a systematic literature review on quantum computing in financial services. You belong to a different LLM family than the model that produced the themes — your job is to act as a cross-family check against single-model bias.

For each analytical theme, you will be given:
1. The theme's interpretation claim.
2. A1 memos (structured notes, one per supporting paper) that the theme claims to be grounded in.

Your task is to determine, for each theme, whether the interpretation is **actually supported** by the memos. You are NOT re-coding or re-theming. You are ONLY verifying that the claim the theme makes is traceable to what the supporting papers report.

## Inputs

### Silo
{silo_name} ({silo_code})

### Analytical themes to verify
{analytical_themes_json}

### A1 memos for the supporting papers (paper_id -> memo)
{a1_memos_json}

## Output Format

Return a JSON object. ONLY the JSON object — no markdown, no prose.

```json
{{
  "silo": "{silo_code}",
  "grounding_checks": [
    {{
      "theme_id": "AT-{silo_code}-001",
      "verdict": "grounded" | "partially_grounded" | "unsupported",
      "grounded_paper_count": 8,
      "partially_grounded_paper_count": 1,
      "unsupported_paper_count": 0,
      "confidence": "high" | "medium" | "low",
      "reason": "<2-4 sentences: cite SPECIFIC paper_ids and SPECIFIC memo key_points that either support or fail to support the theme's interpretation claim. Name at least one memo key_point verbatim (short quote) that anchors your verdict.>",
      "flagged_papers": [
        {{
          "paper_id": "<paper_id>",
          "issue": "unsupported" | "partially_grounded" | "misaligned_claim",
          "note": "<1-2 sentences explaining the specific mismatch between the theme claim and this memo's content>"
        }}
      ]
    }}
  ],
  "silo_summary": {{
    "themes_checked": <int>,
    "grounded": <int>,
    "partially_grounded": <int>,
    "unsupported": <int>,
    "overall_note": "<2-3 sentences: is the silo's analytical layer well-grounded as a whole? Any systematic concerns across themes?>"
  }}
}}
```

## Verdict definitions

- **grounded**: the theme's interpretation is a fair abstraction of what the supporting papers report. At least 80% of the provided memos clearly support the theme's interpretive claim. Specific evidence (key_points, primary_codes, silo_note) anchors the claim.
- **partially_grounded**: the theme's interpretation captures a real pattern in the memos, but extends beyond what the memos strictly say — i.e., the analytical move is partly speculative or applies to only some of the supporting papers. Between 50% and 80% of memos clearly support the claim.
- **unsupported**: the theme's interpretation cannot be recovered from the memos — the memos describe something materially different, or the memos are silent on the key interpretive claim. Less than 50% of memos support the claim.

## Hard rules

1. You must verify every analytical theme in the input. Do not skip themes.
2. The `reason` field MUST cite specific paper_ids and key_points (short verbatim quotes allowed, max 30 words per quote). A reason without specific evidence is unacceptable.
3. `flagged_papers` must list every paper whose memo does not clearly support the theme's interpretation. Empty list is acceptable only for `grounded` verdicts with high confidence.
4. Do not invent content that is not in the memos. If a memo is sparse, say so — do not fabricate support.
5. Be critical, not generous. This check exists to catch over-interpretation. If the theme's `interpretation` makes a causal claim ("X because Y") that the memos only describe observationally, that is `partially_grounded`, not `grounded`.
6. Do not reference papers that are not in the provided memo set. If a theme cites a supporting_paper for which no memo is provided, mark it flagged with issue `unsupported` and note "no memo provided".
7. Output JSON only — no markdown fences, no commentary, no reasoning traces.

## Example reason (for calibration)

**Good**:
> "Grounded. AT-RM-001 claims 'quadratic speedup collapses under I/O overhead'. Memos for 0a824fa99e38 (key_point: 'state preparation costs reported as dominating term at n>15'), 80690e1e30a0 ('pipeline time grows linearly in qubits for loading'), and c46b48afaa7c ('amplitude estimation benefits erased by amplitude encoding') all support this interpretation. Flagged 16d418f1da92: memo reports speedup at small n but does not discuss I/O; this theme's interpretation is plausible but not directly evidenced by this memo."

**Bad** (too vague):
> "The theme seems well-supported by most papers."
