## Hard rules

1. **Theme IDs**:
   - Merged descriptive themes use `DT-QML-NNN` (no batch suffix — these are silo-level post-merge).
   - Analytical themes use `AT-QML-NNN`.
2. **Merged provenance**: every merged descriptive theme's `merged_from` list must reference real B1 theme IDs from the input; its `supporting_papers` is the union of the merged inputs; its `support_code_ids` must aggregate the code IDs.
3. **Grounding**: every analytical theme's `grounded_in` must reference at least one merged descriptive theme from your own output.
4. **Supporting papers (analytical)**: the analytical theme's `supporting_papers` must be a subset of the union of papers from its `grounded_in` descriptive themes.
5. **Counter-evidence**: every analytical theme MUST have either (a) at least one entry in `counter_evidence` with a paper_id that is in the silo's scope and a concrete reason, OR (b) a non-null `no_counter_evidence_reason` explaining why the theme is uncontested (e.g., "unanimous finding across all X papers in the silo"). You may NOT leave both empty. Do not invent paper IDs — every cited paper_id must be one of the silo's papers. `evidence_type` is one of: `opposing_claim`, `boundary_condition`, `null_result`, `methodological_critique`.
6. **Analytical theme count**: produce between 4 and 8 analytical themes. More than 8 indicates insufficient abstraction. Fewer than 4 indicates over-merging (unless the silo genuinely has few distinct patterns — justify in interpretation).
7. **Add interpretation**: an analytical theme MUST add something beyond the descriptive theme(s) it is grounded in. Do NOT simply rename a descriptive theme. If you cannot find a genuine interpretation, omit the theme.
8. **Anti-anchoring**: do NOT use Phase 1 taxonomy codes (PD-01..PD-10, SA-01..SA-11) or their labels as theme names. Themes must emerge from the evidence.
9. **Output JSON only** — no markdown fences, no commentary, no reasoning traces.
