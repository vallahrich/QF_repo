  "draft_finance_brief": "In this silo, portfolio optimisation is treated as a family of financial decision problems in which investors, institutions, and regulators choose and rebalance portfolios of assets under uncertainty so as to balance expected return against risk subject to realistic constraints. The canonical starting point is Markowitz-style modern portfolio theory, extended in multiple directions: discrete and cardinality-constrained stock or bond selection, multi-period and transaction-cost-aware rebalancing, index tracking and ETF basket construction, collateral and cash management, insurance and solvency capital optimisation, and diversification or clustering problems over large asset universes. Classical baselines range from convex QP/SOCP solvers and mixed-integer programming through Monte Carlo risk engines to heuristic and metaheuristic search, with classical ML and RL architectures supplying forecasts and policies in more recent studies. Yet once discrete decisions, transaction costs, regulatory measures such as VaR, CVaR, and SCR, ESG constraints, or multi-period trading paths are introduced, these classical approaches are repeatedly described as NP-hard, combinatorially explosive, or operationally slow—sometimes limited to overnight runs or small universes—especially when coupled with high-dimensional, noisy market data. Business stakes are therefore framed not only in terms of incremental Sharpe-ratio or tail-risk improvements, but also in terms of capital efficiency, solvency and regulatory compliance, collateral and liquidity management, and the ability of asset managers, insurers, central banks, and robo-advisors to respond in time to volatile markets. Against this backdrop, the portfolio-optimisation corpus positions quantum and quantum-inspired methods primarily as alternative optimisation engines under these same financial objectives and constraints, with an explicit tension between asymptotic speedup claims and the need to demonstrate end-to-end, institutionally meaningful improvements within real operational and regulatory environments."
