# F1 — per-paper financial-framing extraction (v2)

You are extracting **financial-problem framing** from a single quantum-finance
paper for a systematic literature review. The output of this extraction will
be aggregated across a silo to produce a per-silo finance brief.

## Source

You will be given:
- `paper_id` — a 12-character hex identifier
- `text_path` — the absolute path to the paper's extracted-text markdown file
  in `shared/extracted_text/text/`
- `silos` — the list of P3 silos this paper projects into (1 or more)

You should **read the file at `text_path` directly**, focusing on the parts of
the paper where authors typically state motivation:
- Abstract
- Introduction
- Background / Related Work (when it frames the problem)
- Conclusion (when it restates the problem)
- Limitations (when it states what is hard)

You may skip experimental tables, proofs, and detailed methodology sections.

## Task

Extract what THIS PAPER says about the financial problem and its classical
difficulty. Do NOT import knowledge from outside the paper. Do NOT generalise
from textbooks. If the paper does not address a field, return `null` for that
field — silence is a finding.

## Output schema

Return one JSON object with exactly these fields:

```json
{
  "paper_id": "<provided paper_id>",
  "silos": ["<provided silo list>"],
  "problem_statement": null | "<1-3 sentences in your own words paraphrasing what the paper says the financial problem is>",
  "problem_statement_quotes": ["<verbatim quote 1>", "..."],
  "classical_baseline": null | "<1-3 sentences on what the paper compares against / treats as the classical state of the art>",
  "classical_baseline_quotes": ["<verbatim quote 1>", "..."],
  "classical_difficulty": null | "<1-3 sentences on what the paper says about why the problem is hard classically — complexity, scaling, accuracy, tractability, data, latency>",
  "classical_difficulty_quotes": ["<verbatim quote 1>", "..."],
  "business_stakes": null | "<1-3 sentences on what the paper says about real-world stakes: capital efficiency, latency, regulation, market impact, costs, risk consequences. MOST PAPERS DO NOT MENTION. Default to null>",
  "business_stakes_quotes": ["<verbatim quote 1>", "..."],
  "extraction_confidence": "high" | "medium" | "low",
  "extraction_notes": "<short string flagging anything unusual: 'paper assumes reader knows', 'motivation is one sentence in abstract', 'framing is purely algorithmic with no finance context', 'paper is a review and discusses many problems', etc.>"
}
```

## Rules

1. **Verbatim quotes are mandatory for every non-null field.** Each non-null
   field must include 1–3 quotes (strings) in the corresponding `*_quotes`
   array. If you cannot supply a verbatim quote, the field must be `null` and
   its `*_quotes` must be `[]`.

2. **Quotes must be verbatim substrings of the paper text.** Do not paraphrase
   inside the quotes. Do not collapse whitespace artistically — copy what is
   in the file. The downstream sanitiser will check this and drop any quote
   that fails the substring check.

3. **The prose statement must be fully grounded in the supplied quotes.**
   Every claim in the prose statement (`problem_statement`,
   `classical_baseline`, `classical_difficulty`, `business_stakes`) must be
   directly supported by content in its corresponding `*_quotes` array. Do
   NOT add details that come from methodology, experiments, or your own
   inference — even if true. If you want to make a claim, you must have a
   quote that supports it. If a claim cannot be quoted, drop it from the
   prose. The prose is a careful paraphrase of the quotes, nothing more.

4. **Silence is a valid finding.** If the paper does not state a financial
   problem (e.g. it is a methods paper that just says "we test on portfolio
   data"), set `problem_statement` to `null`. Do not invent context. Set
   `extraction_notes` accordingly.

5. **Do not paraphrase the abstract.** A paraphrase of the abstract is NOT
   acceptable when the paper is silent on motivation. Either the paper says
   something quotable, or the field is `null`.

6. **Do not import method content into framing fields.** This is a common
   failure mode: the paper describes its quantum/classical method in the
   methodology section, and the model attributes those methodological choices
   to the "problem framing." The financial problem and the proposed solution
   are different things. Examples of method content to keep OUT of
   `problem_statement` and `classical_baseline`:
   - the algorithm or encoding the paper uses to solve the problem
   - the dataset, simulator, or hardware used in experiments
   - the paper's own contribution or technique
   These belong in s4 thematic codes, not in s6 framing.

7. **Multi-silo papers**: the financial framing of the paper does not change
   based on which silo it is read from. Extract one consistent framing.

8. **Confidence calibration**:
   - `high`: paper has a clear motivation section with explicit financial
     framing and explicit classical-difficulty statements.
   - `medium`: motivation is present but brief, or split across abstract and
     introduction; some fields are well-grounded and others are weaker.
   - `low`: motivation is largely absent or purely algorithmic; most fields
     end up as `null`.

9. **Output ONLY the JSON object.** No markdown wrapping, no commentary
   before or after.

## Failure modes to avoid

- Returning a paraphrase of the abstract when the paper is silent on
  motivation.
- Importing textbook knowledge ("portfolio optimization is NP-hard") when
  the paper itself does not say so.
- Including methodological choices ("graph-based representation", "iterative
  partitioning", "QAOA ansatz") in the `problem_statement` when these are
  the paper's *solution*, not the *problem framing*.
- Quoting from sections of the file that are not from the paper itself
  (e.g. arXiv metadata, license boilerplate, copyright headers).
- Returning a JSON-like string with smart quotes or unbalanced braces.
- Dropping a `*_quotes` array. Empty array is `[]`, not omitted.
