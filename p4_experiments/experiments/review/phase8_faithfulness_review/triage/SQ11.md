### Concerns raised by Vincent

- Family drift: HHL-based one-class SVM proxied by generic RealAmplitudes ansatz, no HHL/QPE/swap-test structure.
- Compute-uncompute of a fixed-parameter ansatz is ~identity and offers no structural fidelity to the paper's algorithm.
- Label mismatch: circuit.py and instance.json both identify as SQ10 (exp_1, kernel PCA, 'other-gate-based'), but cohort row is SQ11 (exp_4, quantum-svm).
- instance.algorithm_family ('other-gate-based') disagrees with cohort.algorithm_family ('quantum-svm') and with @register algorithm_family ('quantum-svm').
- Operator note in v2 flags SQ8/SQ9/SQ10/SQ11 as 4 redundant labels for the same Liu&Rebentrost theory paper; resource-cost rows from SQ11 will double-count this paper unless deduplicated.

### Recommendations from Vincent

- Either (a) relabel/copy the file to SQ11 with @register(label='SQ11', experiment_id='exp_4', algorithm_family='quantum-svm') and a matching SQ11 instance.json, or (b) drop SQ11 from the cohort as a duplicate of SQ8/SQ9/SQ10 per the operator note.
- If kept, document explicitly in PRE_REGISTRATION/notes that SQ11's circuit is a generic ansatz proxy and therefore its Phase-8 resource estimates should not be interpreted as estimates of HHL-SVM cost.
- Consider replacing the compute-uncompute oracle with a minimal HHL-shaped proxy (QPE on a small Hermitian, controlled rotation, inverse QPE) so the proxy is at least structurally in the HHL family, even at toy scale.

  rationale: |
    Accept Vincent's DRIFT_MAJOR conclusion, with a paper-specific refinement.

    Phase 3 is faithful to the paper for exp_4: the relevant algorithm is quantum one-class SVM for mixed-state anomaly detection. The paper extends the one-class SVM proximity-measure framework to mixed quantum states by defining a valid kernel through the superfidelity between density matrices, then computing the mixed-state proximity measure using kernel/fidelity estimates. This remains a quantum-kernel / one-class-SVM anomaly-detection method, not a variational ansatz method.

    For the pure-state one-class SVM branch, the paper explicitly discusses HHL-style quantum matrix inversion, phase estimation, controlled kernel evolution, and kernel-matrix exponentiation. For the mixed-state one-class SVM branch, the paper is more cautious: it states that the kernel matrix can be prepared and proximity measures can be computed using O(log d) resources, while efficient logarithmic scaling in the number of training states would require additional assumptions and remains an open challenge. Therefore the most faithful SQ11 proxy should preserve the quantum-kernel/SVM/fidelity-estimation structure, and may include an HHL-shaped inversion block only if this is explicitly documented as a stronger structural proxy rather than a fully specified mixed-state implementation.

    The implemented circuit described in Vincent's review is a generic RealAmplitudes ansatz followed by its inverse. That circuit has no mixed-state kernel construction, no superfidelity or fidelity-estimation path, no swap-test ancilla, no SVM linear-system/proximity-measure structure, and no HHL/QPE skeleton. The compute-uncompute pair is therefore structurally trivial relative to the paper's algorithmic content.

    The metadata drift is also severe. The pasted review indicates that circuit.py and instance.json identify the artifact as SQ10 / exp_1 / kernel PCA, while this audit target is SQ11 / exp_4 / quantum-svm. This is a wrong-label and wrong-experiment binding, not just a weak proxy.

    Therefore SQ11 should not be accepted as currently implemented. It should be reauthored as a quantum-kernel one-class-SVM proxy for mixed-state anomaly detection, or otherwise demoted to a theoretical-paper/template-proxy label and excluded from family-faithful quantum-SVM resource aggregates.
